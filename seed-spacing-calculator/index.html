Seed Spacing Calculator — How Many Plants Fit in Your Garden Bed?

Estimate plant positions by dividing the bed length and width by the chosen spacing, then counting whole positions along each direction. This calculator assumes a simple rectangular bed and equal spacing in both directions.

Honest limit: Spacing on the seed packet wins over any calculator; thin seedlings as directed.

How the Plant Count Is Estimated

  1. Convert bed length, bed width, and spacing to one unit.
  2. Plants per row: floor(bed length ÷ spacing) + 1, assuming a plant can be placed at one end.
  3. Rows: floor(bed width ÷ spacing) + 1, using the same edge-position assumption.
  4. Total positions: plants per row × rows.

This is a regular grid estimate. It does not account for paths, border setbacks, staggered planting, crop-specific row spacing, trellises, access, companion planting, or mature plant spread.

Worked Example

A 12-foot by 4-foot bed with 12-inch spacing has 13 possible positions along its length and 5 across its width under the edge-position assumption. That gives 65 theoretical positions. Real planting capacity may be lower depending on the crop and bed layout.

Questions people ask

How many plants fit in a garden bed?

For a simple grid, calculate whole plant positions along the bed length and width using the chosen spacing, then multiply plants per row by the number of rows.

Does seed spacing mean distance between seeds or rows?

Seed packets may list in-row spacing, row spacing, or both. This calculator uses the same spacing in both directions, so use the packet's instructions and treat this result as a simplified grid estimate.

Does the calculator include room around the bed edges?

It assumes a plant position can begin at each edge. It does not include border setbacks, paths, or access space. Reduce the count if plants need clearance from bed edges.

Should I keep every seedling that sprouts?

Not always. Some crops are meant to be thinned to the recommended spacing. Follow the seed packet's thinning instructions and keep the healthiest seedlings at the intended distance.
