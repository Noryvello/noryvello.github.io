Brick Calculator

Enter your wall measurements and pick a brick size, and this calculator works out how many bricks you need with a 10 percent waste allowance, plus an estimate of the mortar volume. The working is shown so you can check every step.

This calculator estimates for a single-skin wall with 10 mm mortar joints. Double-skin walls, piers and patterned brickwork need more bricks, and the mortar figure is an estimate only. Check the actual size of your bricks before ordering, and follow local building practice.

How to use this calculator

Measure the wall length and height in metres. If the wall has doors or windows, work out their total area in square metres and enter it as the openings area, so those bricks are not counted. Pick the brick size closest to the bricks you plan to buy. The picker lists common sizes with their length, width and height in millimetres. Press Calculate to see the brick count and the mortar estimate, with the working shown underneath.

How the calculation works

The wall area is the length multiplied by the height, minus the openings area. Each brick covers its face area plus a 10 mm mortar joint on each side, so the bricks per square metre are one divided by that face area. The total is the wall area multiplied by the bricks per square metre, plus 10 percent for waste. The mortar volume is the wall volume minus the volume of the bricks themselves, which leaves the volume taken up by the joints.

How to use the result

Order the full brick count shown, including the waste allowance, because cutting and breakages use up spare bricks. Buy all your bricks from one batch where you can, so the colour matches across the wall. Use the mortar figure as a rough guide for ordering, and check it against your supplier's advice for the mix you plan to use. If the wall has piers, corners or a patterned bond, allow extra bricks beyond the figure shown.

Questions people ask

Why is a 10 percent waste allowance included?

Cutting bricks to fit, breakages and the odd faulty brick all use up spares. Ten percent is the allowance this calculator uses, and it is shown in the working.

What are openings?

Openings are the parts of a wall that are not brick, such as doors and windows. Enter their total area so the calculator does not count bricks for those spaces.

What does single-skin mean?

A single-skin wall is one brick thick. A double-skin wall has two layers of bricks and needs about twice as many, plus wall ties, so this calculator does not cover it.

How do I measure the openings area?

Measure each door or window opening in metres, multiply its width by its height, and add the areas together. Enter that total as the openings area.

Why check the brick size?

Brick sizes vary by country and maker. The picker lists common sizes, so measure your bricks or check the size on the pack and pick the closest option.
