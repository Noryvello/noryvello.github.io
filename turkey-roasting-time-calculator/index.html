Turkey Roasting Time Calculator

Short answer: At 325°F (163°C), an unstuffed whole turkey takes roughly 13 to 15 minutes per pound, while a stuffed turkey takes roughly 15 to 18 minutes per pound. Regardless of the clock, a turkey is only safe and done when a food thermometer reaches 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part of the breast, the innermost part of the thigh, and the innermost part of the wing (plus the center of the stuffing, if stuffed). Let the turkey rest for 20 to 30 minutes before carving.

Important Note: All roasting times shown above are estimates based on a thawed turkey roasted in an oven set to at least 325°F (163°C). Oven calibration, pan shape, foil tenting, and starting bird temperature all change actual cooking time. Never rely on the clock or pop-up timers alone—the food thermometer reading of 165°F (74°C) decides when the turkey is done.

Questions people ask

What internal temperature must a turkey reach to be safe to eat?

According to USDA food safety guidelines, a turkey must reach a minimum internal temperature of 165°F (74°C) measured with a food thermometer in the thickest part of the breast, the innermost part of the thigh, and the innermost part of the wing. If stuffed, the center of the stuffing must also reach 165°F (74°C).

How long should a turkey rest after roasting?

Let a roasted turkey rest loosely tented with foil for 20 to 30 minutes after removing it from the oven. Resting allows juices to redistribute throughout the meat so it stays moist and is easier to carve.

What oven temperature should I roast a turkey at?

The USDA recommends setting your oven temperature no lower than 325°F (163°C) when roasting a whole turkey.

Do these roasting times work for a frozen turkey?

No. These estimated times apply only to a completely thawed turkey. Roasting a completely frozen turkey takes at least 50 percent longer than the recommended time for a thawed bird.
