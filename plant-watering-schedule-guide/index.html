Plant Watering Schedule Guide

Most houseplants need water every 1–2 weeks, but the right schedule depends on the plant — succulents can go 3–4 weeks, while ferns may need water 1–2 times a week. Pick your plant below to see its watering frequency. This guide covers indoor houseplants only, not garden plants, and all frequencies are guides, not rules.

Frequencies are general guides, not rules. Always check the soil before you water — the finger test below tells you how.

Overwatering kills more houseplants than underwatering. When in doubt, wait a day and check the soil again before reaching for the watering can.

The finger test

Check the soil with your finger — push it in to the first knuckle. If the top few centimetres are dry, water the plant; if the soil is still damp, wait and check again in a few days. Check the soil, not the calendar: pot size, light and room temperature all change how fast soil dries out, so the same plant may need water at different times in different rooms.

Watering through the seasons

Plants usually need less water in winter and more in summer. In the colder, darker months most houseplants grow slowly and the soil stays damp longer, so water less often. In summer the soil dries out sooner and plants are growing, so check the soil more often. The season is a guide, not a rule — the finger test still decides.

Questions people ask

How often should I water a snake plant?

A snake plant usually needs water every 2–3 weeks. Let the soil dry out between waterings, and check the soil with your finger before you water — if it is still damp, wait.

How do I know if my plant needs water?

Push your finger into the soil to the first knuckle. If the top few centimetres feel dry, water the plant; if the soil still feels damp, wait and check again in a few days. Check the soil, not the calendar.

Should I water plants more in summer?

Usually, yes. Houseplants grow faster in the warmer, brighter months and the soil dries out sooner, so they often need more water in summer and less in winter. Still check the soil first — the season is a guide, not a rule.

What happens if I overwater a houseplant?

Soil that stays wet can suffocate the roots and lead to root rot, which is hard to reverse. Overwatering kills more houseplants than underwatering, so when in doubt, wait a day and check the soil again.
