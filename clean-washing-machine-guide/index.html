How to Clean a Washing Machine

A washing machine needs cleaning because detergent residue, damp and lint build up inside it, and that build-up is what causes musty smells and grey marks on laundry. This guide shows you how to clean your machine by type, with the cleaning cycle, the seal and dispenser steps, and a monthly checklist.

It feels odd to clean a machine whose whole job is cleaning, but the inside of a washing machine is a warm, damp place, and that is exactly where residue and mould like to live. Every wash leaves a little detergent and softener behind, and lint from clothes gathers in corners you never see. Over time the drum, the rubber seal and the dispenser drawer collect a film that smells musty and can leave marks on fresh laundry.

Cold washes make the problem worse, because cold water dissolves less residue than hot water. That does not mean you should stop washing in cold water, only that a machine used mostly on cold cycles needs a proper clean more often. A monthly clean takes little effort, and most of it is the machine running an empty cycle while you get on with something else.

Never mix cleaning products, and use only products made for washing machines. Follow your machine manual for the hottest cycle and the filter, and keep the room ventilated while you clean. If the machine has a fault or a leak, stop and call a repairer.

The cleaning cycle

The heart of the clean is an empty hot wash. Take everything out of the drum, add a washing machine cleaner made for the job, exactly as its label says, and run the hottest cycle your manual allows. The hot water and the cleaner work through the drum, the pipes and the parts you cannot reach, dissolving the residue that causes smells.

While the cycle runs, you can clean the parts you can reach. Wipe the outside of the machine with a damp cloth, and clean around the door or lid where dust and detergent gather. When the cycle finishes, leave the door or lid open and let the drum dry fully before the next wash.

If the smell is still there after one cleaning cycle, run a second empty hot cycle with fresh water only. A machine that has gone a long time without a clean sometimes needs two rounds before it smells fresh again. If the smell still does not shift, the drain filter or the drain hose may be the cause, and both are worth checking as your manual describes.

The seal and the dispenser

On a front loader, the rubber seal around the door is the part that needs the most attention. Pull the folds of the seal open gently and wipe inside them with a damp cloth. You will often find lint, hair and a grey film sitting in the folds, and this is where mould starts. Dry the folds with a clean cloth afterwards, and leave the door ajar so the seal can dry between washes.

The dispenser drawer comes out on most machines, usually by pressing a small catch. Pull it out, rinse it under warm water, and scrub away any softener or detergent that has set hard in the compartments. Dry it fully before you slide it back in. A drawer that is left damp grows mould in the same way the seal does.

Most machines also have a drain filter behind a small flap at the front, near the bottom. Put a shallow tray and a towel under it before you open it, because water will come out. Twist the filter out, rinse off lint and any small objects, and put it back firmly. Check your manual for the exact position and for how the flap opens on your model.

Keeping smells away

The simplest habit is also the most effective: leave the door or lid slightly open between washes. A machine that dries out between uses gives mould nowhere damp to grow. If you have small children in the house, leave the door open only a crack, or open it when the room is not in use.

Use the right amount of detergent for the load and the water you have. More detergent does not mean cleaner clothes, and the extra detergent is what builds up inside the machine. Measure the dose rather than pouring freely, and skip the softener now and then, since softener leaves a film of its own.

Take laundry out promptly when a cycle finishes. Clothes left sitting in a damp drum for hours pick up a musty smell that is hard to wash out, and the damp drum stays damp for longer. A quick wipe of the seal after the last wash of the day takes seconds and keeps the worst of the moisture away.

Questions people ask

Why does my washing machine smell?

The usual cause is a build-up of detergent residue and damp inside the drum, the seal and the dispenser. The smell grows when the door stays closed between washes, because the inside never dries out.

Can I use a normal household cleaner in the machine?

No. Use only products made for washing machines, and follow the label. Household cleaners can foam too much, damage seals, or leave residues that end up on your laundry.

How often should I clean my washing machine?

A full clean once a month keeps most machines fresh, with a quick wipe of the seal and dispenser after washes. If you wash mostly in cold water, clean more often, since cold water leaves more residue behind.

What is the black stuff on the rubber seal?

It is usually mould growing in the damp folds of the seal. Wipe the folds dry after washes, leave the door ajar so the seal can dry, and clean the seal as part of your monthly routine.

Should I leave the door open between washes?

Yes, if you can. Leaving the door or lid slightly open lets the drum and seal dry out, which is the simplest way to stop smells from coming back.
