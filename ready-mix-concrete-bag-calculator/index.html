Ready-Mix Concrete Bag Calculator

Enter your slab measurements and pick a bag size, and this calculator works out the concrete volume and how many bags of ready-mix concrete you need, including a waste allowance. The working is shown so you can check every step.

This calculator estimates the volume of a plain rectangular slab. Ground that is uneven, soft or sloping can need more concrete than the maths suggests, and structural slabs need proper design. Check the yield printed on your bag, because it varies by maker.

How to use this calculator

Measure the slab length and width in metres, and the depth in centimetres. Pick the bag size you plan to buy. The picker shows the approximate yield of each bag, which is the volume of mixed concrete one bag makes. Set the waste allowance, then press Calculate to see the concrete volume and the number of bags, with the working shown underneath.

How the calculation works

The volume is the length multiplied by the width multiplied by the depth. The waste allowance is added to that volume, and the total is divided by the yield of one bag. The result is rounded up to a whole bag, because part bags cannot be bought. The steps show the volume, the waste and the division, so you can check the figure before ordering.

How to use the result

Buy the number of bags shown, and check the yield on your own bags before ordering, because yields vary by maker and mix. Mix in small batches if you are working alone, so the concrete does not set before you finish placing it. Keep a couple of spare bags for topping up low spots, and level the ground well before pouring so the depth stays even.

Questions people ask

What is the yield of a bag?

The yield is the volume of mixed concrete one bag makes, once water is added. The picker shows approximate yields, so check the figure printed on your own bags, because it varies by maker.

Why is a waste allowance included?

Uneven ground, spillage and the concrete that stays in the mixer all use up extra material. Ten percent is the default allowance.

What depth should I enter?

Enter the finished depth of the slab in centimetres, as you plan to pour it. A garden path slab is often around 10 cm deep, but check what your job needs.

How is this different from a mix-ratio calculator?

This calculator counts bags of ready-mix, where the cement, sand and stone are already mixed in the bag. A mix-ratio calculator works out separate cement, sand and stone quantities instead.

Can I use this for a shape that is not a rectangle?

Split the shape into rectangles, work out each volume, and add them together. Run the calculator once per rectangle and add the bag counts, or work out an equivalent rectangle for the combined area.
