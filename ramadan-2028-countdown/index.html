When does Ramadan 2028 start?

Ramadan 1449 is expected to begin with the first fast on Friday 28 January 2028, according to the Umm al-Qura calculated calendar. The first taraweeh prayer is expected on the evening of Thursday 27 January, the night before the first fast.

The month is expected to last 29 days, ending with 29 Ramadan on Friday 25 February 2028. Eid al-Fitr is then expected on Saturday 26 February 2028. If the new moon is not sighted on the evening of 25 February, Ramadan completes 30 days and Eid moves to Sunday 27 February.

Why the date can change

The Islamic calendar is a lunar calendar, and each month begins with the sighting of the new crescent moon. The calculated calendar used for these dates is an excellent guide, but the confirmed start of Ramadan depends on the actual sighting, which is why the date can move by a day.

Weather, geography, and local practice all play a part. A country that sights the moon on one evening begins fasting the next morning; a country that does not sight it waits another day. That is why the start of Ramadan is not always the same day everywhere.

India and Bangladesh often begin a day later than the Middle East, because the moon is sighted there a day later in many years. The picker above lets you count down to either date.

The first taraweeh

Taraweeh is the special night prayer of Ramadan, prayed after the night prayer. The first taraweeh is prayed on the night before the first fast — in 2028, the evening of Thursday 27 January for most regions.

Many mosques recite the whole Quran over the course of the month during taraweeh, finishing on one of the last nights. The prayer is voluntary, and people pray as much of it as they are able.

What happens during Ramadan

During Ramadan, adult Muslims fast from dawn to sunset — no food, no drink, and no smoking during the daylight hours. The fast is broken at sunset with the iftar meal, often beginning with dates, and a pre-dawn meal called suhoor is eaten before the fast begins each day.

The month is also a time of extra prayer, charity, and reading the Quran. Many people aim to read the whole Quran during the month, and charity giving rises sharply. The last ten nights of Ramadan are the most blessed, and Laylat al-Qadr, the Night of Decree, is sought among the odd nights of the last ten.
