Candle Wax Calculator

This calculator works out how much wax and fragrance oil you need to fill your candle containers. Enter the container volume and how many candles you are making, pick a wax type and a fragrance percentage. The wax weight and fragrance amount are shown with the working.

Hot wax can burn and spilled wax is a fire risk. Melt wax with care, never leave melting wax or a burning candle unattended, and keep candles away from children, pets and anything that can catch fire. The wax weight here is an estimate using 0.9 grams per millilitre; check your wax supplier's own guidance.

How the amounts are worked out

The calculator starts with the total volume you need to fill. It multiplies the container volume by the number of containers. Four containers of 200 millilitres each give a total volume of 800 millilitres. That volume is then turned into a wax weight using a working figure of 0.9 grams of wax per millilitre, which gives 720 grams of wax for the example.

The fragrance amount is worked out from the wax weight. Multiply the wax weight by the fragrance percentage you entered and divide by 100. With 720 grams of wax and a fragrance percentage of 8, the fragrance amount is 57.6 grams. The total pour weight is the wax weight plus the fragrance amount, which is 777.6 grams in the example.

Wax types at a glance

Soy wax is a common choice for container candles. It pours smoothly, holds fragrance well and is easy to work with for a first batch. Follow your supplier's guidance for melting and pouring, and let the candles set undisturbed so the tops stay smooth.

Paraffin wax is the long-established candle wax and gives a clean, even burn in containers and pillars alike. Beeswax has a natural warm colour and a gentle honey scent of its own, so it suits candles where you want little or no added fragrance. Whichever wax you pick, the calculator's weight estimate stays the same, and your supplier's guidance is the final word on temperatures and pouring.

Getting the fragrance amount right

Fragrance oil is measured as a percentage of the wax weight, not of the total pour. Enter the percentage your fragrance supplier recommends for your wax, and the calculator does the rest. Using more fragrance than the supplier recommends will not make a stronger candle and can cause sweating, poor burning or a greasy feel.

Weigh your fragrance oil on a scale rather than measuring it by eye. Small amounts make a big difference to the scent, and a scale keeps each batch consistent. Stir the fragrance into the melted wax gently and thoroughly so it blends evenly through the whole batch.

Tips for a clean pour

Warm your containers before pouring so the wax cools evenly and grips the sides. Pour slowly and steadily, and leave a little headroom at the top of each container rather than filling to the brim. Centre the wick as you pour and hold it straight while the wax sets.

Let the candles cool slowly at room temperature, away from draughts. Moving them or cooling them quickly can cause sinkholes or a rough top. If a sinkhole appears, a gentle top-up pour of melted wax usually fixes it. Make a little more wax than the calculator shows, because some wax always stays in the pouring jug.

Questions people ask

Why is the wax weight an estimate?

Different waxes weigh slightly different amounts per millilitre, and containers vary too. This calculator uses 0.9 grams per millilitre as a working figure. Weigh your wax and check your supplier's guidance for the wax you use.

How much fragrance oil should I use?

Enter the percentage your fragrance supplier recommends for your wax. This calculator accepts 0 to 15 percent and works the amount out from the wax weight.

Does the wax type change the amount?

No. The wax type picker changes the notes shown, not the maths. The weight estimate is the same for soy, paraffin and beeswax in this calculator.

Should I make extra wax?

It helps to make a little more than the calculator shows, because some wax stays in the pouring jug. Any leftover wax can be cooled, stored and melted again for a later batch.

Can I use this calculator for wax melts?

Yes, if you know the volume of the mould or container you are filling. Enter that volume and the number of melts, and the calculator works the same way.
