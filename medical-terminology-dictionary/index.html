Medical Terminology Dictionary

This dictionary explains common medical terms in plain language, with a short example for each one. It is written for anyone who has heard a word from a doctor, a nurse or a report and wants to know what it means.

This dictionary explains medical words in plain language. It is education only — not medical advice, see a professional. It never diagnoses, never gives doses and never recommends treatment.

How to use this dictionary

Medical words can sound frightening, but most of them are built from a small set of parts. Many come from Greek or Latin, and once you know the parts, the words start to make sense: "cardio" means heart, "derm" means skin, "itis" means inflammation, and "ology" means the study of something. Cardiology is the study of the heart. Dermatitis is inflammation of the skin.

This dictionary explains the terms you are most likely to hear in a clinic, a hospital, or a report, in plain language, with a short example for each one. The terms are grouped by topic: illness and injury, the body and how it works, tests and scans, medicines and treatment, symptoms and feelings, and long-term conditions. Look up the word you need, read the definition and the example, and move on.

A dictionary cannot tell you what is wrong with you, and this one does not try. If you are worried about your health, or if a word in your own report concerns you, ask the doctor or nurse who is caring for you to explain it. They know your situation; this page only explains the words.

Words about illness and injury

Acute. Acute describes a problem that starts suddenly and lasts for a short time. Example: a sudden, severe pain that begins today and lasts a few days is described as acute.

Chronic. Chronic describes a problem that lasts a long time, or that keeps coming back. Example: pain that has been present most days for months is described as chronic.

Benign. Benign means not harmful, and not cancerous. Example: a benign growth is one that does not spread to other parts of the body.

Malignant. Malignant means cancerous — able to grow and spread to other parts of the body. Example: a malignant tumour is one that needs treatment to stop it spreading.

Inflammation. Inflammation is the body's response to injury or infection: the area becomes red, warm, swollen and painful. Example: a sprained ankle becomes inflamed as the body starts to heal it.

Infection. An infection is an illness caused by germs, such as bacteria or viruses, growing in the body. Example: a chest infection is an infection of the airways or the lungs.

Fracture. A fracture is a break in a bone. Example: a fall on an outstretched hand can cause a fracture of the wrist.

Words about the body and how it works

Immunity. Immunity is the body's ability to resist a particular infection, usually because it has fought it before or has been vaccinated. Example: after recovering from an illness, a person may have immunity to it for a time.

Antibody. An antibody is a protein made by the immune system that recognises and helps to fight a specific germ. Example: after a vaccination, the body makes antibodies against the germ the vaccine targets.

Antigen. An antigen is a substance, usually part of a germ, that the immune system recognises as foreign and responds to. Example: the surface of a virus carries antigens that the immune system can recognise.

Metabolism. Metabolism is the set of chemical processes in the body that turn food into energy and build and repair tissues. Example: metabolism slows during sleep and speeds up during exercise.

Blood pressure. Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing against the walls of the arteries as the heart pumps. Example: blood pressure is written as two numbers, such as 120 over 80.

Pulse. The pulse is the regular beat felt in an artery as the heart pumps blood around the body. Example: a nurse may feel the pulse at the wrist to check the heart rate.

Words about tests and scans

Diagnosis. A diagnosis is the name a doctor gives to a person's illness, based on symptoms, examination and tests. Example: after tests, the doctor gave a diagnosis of asthma.

Prognosis. A prognosis is a doctor's reasoned expectation about how an illness is likely to develop. Example: a good prognosis means the doctor expects a good recovery.

Biopsy. A biopsy is a test in which a small piece of tissue is taken from the body and examined under a microscope. Example: a biopsy of a lump can show whether it is benign or malignant.

Ultrasound. An ultrasound scan uses sound waves to make pictures of the inside of the body. Example: an ultrasound scan is often used to look at a baby during pregnancy.

MRI. MRI, short for magnetic resonance imaging, is a scan that uses strong magnets and radio waves to make detailed pictures of the inside of the body. Example: an MRI scan can show soft tissues such as the brain in detail.

CT scan. A CT scan, short for computed tomography scan, uses X-rays and a computer to make detailed cross-section pictures of the body. Example: a CT scan can show a broken bone or bleeding inside the body.

Words about medicines and treatment

Antibiotic. An antibiotic is a medicine that kills bacteria or stops them growing, used to treat bacterial infections. Example: a doctor may prescribe an antibiotic for a bacterial chest infection.

Vaccine. A vaccine is a preparation that trains the immune system to recognise a germ, so the body can fight it if it meets it later. Example: the flu vaccine is given each year before winter.

Anaesthetic. An anaesthetic is a medicine that stops a person feeling pain, either in one part of the body or all over. Example: a dentist uses a local anaesthetic to numb a tooth before filling it.

Therapy. Therapy is treatment intended to relieve or heal an illness or injury. Example: physiotherapy uses exercises and movement to help the body recover.

Prescription. A prescription is a written order from a doctor or other qualified professional for a medicine or treatment. Example: the doctor wrote a prescription for eye drops.

Side effect. A side effect is an unwanted effect of a medicine or treatment, in addition to its intended effect. Example: drowsiness is a common side effect of some medicines.

Words about symptoms and feelings

Symptom. A symptom is a change in the body or in how a person feels that may be a sign of illness. Example: a headache is a symptom that can have many causes.

Syndrome. A syndrome is a group of symptoms that tend to occur together and are known by one name. Example: Down syndrome is the name for a set of features that occur together.

Fever. A fever is a body temperature higher than normal, usually caused by the body fighting an infection. Example: a child with a fever may feel hot and shivery.

Nausea. Nausea is the feeling of being about to vomit. Example: travel sickness often causes nausea.

Vertigo. Vertigo is a feeling of dizziness in which the room seems to spin, caused by a problem with the balance system. Example: a person with vertigo may feel unsteady when they stand up.

Fatigue. Fatigue is a deep tiredness that does not go away with rest, different from ordinary tiredness. Example: fatigue is a common symptom of many long-term conditions.

Allergy. An allergy is a reaction of the immune system to a substance that is harmless to most people, such as pollen or a food. Example: hay fever is an allergy to pollen.

Words about long-term conditions

Diabetes. Diabetes is a condition in which the level of sugar in the blood is too high, because the body does not make enough insulin or does not use it well. Example: a person with diabetes checks their blood sugar regularly.

Hypertension. Hypertension is the medical word for high blood pressure. Example: hypertension often has no symptoms, which is why blood pressure is checked at routine visits.

Asthma. Asthma is a condition in which the airways narrow and swell, making breathing difficult, often in attacks. Example: cold air or exercise can bring on an asthma attack.

Arthritis. Arthritis is inflammation of one or more joints, causing pain and stiffness. Example: arthritis in the hands can make gripping things difficult.

Anaemia. Anaemia is a condition in which the blood has too few red cells, or too little haemoglobin, so less oxygen reaches the body's tissues. Example: a blood test can show whether a person has anaemia.

How to talk with your doctor

A medical appointment is easier when you are prepared. Before you go, write down the words you want to ask about, the symptoms you have noticed, and the questions you want answered. Bring the list with you: it is easy to forget a question in the moment, and a list makes sure nothing is missed.

Ask for plain words. It is always all right to say, "Can you explain that in simple words?" or "What does that word mean?" Doctors and nurses expect these questions, and a good one will be glad to explain. If you do not understand an answer, say so and ask again — understanding your own health is not a favour you are asking, it is part of the care.

Bring someone with you if you can, especially for an important appointment. A second person can listen, take notes, and remind you later of what was said. After the appointment, write down what you understood, and check it against any written information you were given. If something is still unclear, ask again — by phone, at a follow-up visit, or through the clinic's usual route.

Questions people ask

What does acute mean in medicine?

Acute describes a problem that starts suddenly and lasts for a short time. For example, a sudden, severe pain that begins today and lasts a few days is described as acute.

What is the difference between a symptom and a syndrome?

A symptom is a single change in the body or in how a person feels, such as a headache. A syndrome is a group of symptoms that tend to occur together and are known by one name.

What does benign mean?

Benign means not harmful, and not cancerous. For example, a benign growth is one that does not spread to other parts of the body.

What is a prognosis?

A prognosis is a doctor's reasoned expectation about how an illness is likely to develop. A good prognosis means the doctor expects a good recovery.

What does chronic mean?

Chronic describes a problem that lasts a long time, or that keeps coming back. For example, pain that has been present most days for months is described as chronic.

Where can I learn more about a term my doctor used?
