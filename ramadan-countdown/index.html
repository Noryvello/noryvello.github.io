When is Ramadan 2027?

The first fast of Ramadan 2027 is expected on Monday 8 February 2027 in most countries, with the first taraweeh prayed on the evening of Sunday 7 February 2027.

India and Bangladesh often start a day later: the first fast is often on Tuesday 9 February 2027, with the first taraweeh on the evening of Monday 8 February 2027.

Both dates are expected, not certain. The month begins with the sighting of the new crescent moon, so the confirmed date can move by a day. Check your local announcement as the date approaches.

How the countdown works

The countdown ticks every second to midnight at the start of the expected first-fast day, in your local time — the start of Monday 8 February 2027, or Tuesday 9 February 2027 if you choose India or Bangladesh in the dropdown above.

It counts in days, hours, minutes and seconds, and it is computed in your browser, so it always shows the time left from where you are. It does not count to a prayer time.

Once the expected date arrives, the countdown switches to showing how long ago the expected first fast began. The end of the month and Eid depend on the next moon sighting, so this page does not give an end date — check your local announcement.

What is taraweeh?

Taraweeh is a special night prayer held during Ramadan, prayed after the evening prayer. Many people pray it in congregation at the mosque, and it is prayed on each night of the month.

The first taraweeh is prayed on the night before the first fast. For an expected first fast on Monday 8 February 2027, the first taraweeh is on the evening of Sunday 7 February 2027; where the first fast is expected on Tuesday 9 February 2027, the first taraweeh is on the evening of Monday 8 February 2027.

Why the date can differ by country

Ramadan begins with the sighting of the new crescent moon, and the moon may be seen on different nights in different parts of the world, because of the moon's age, the weather and where you are.

Some countries follow their own moon sighting, and some follow another country's announcement. That is why the first fast can fall on different days in different places, and why India and Bangladesh often start a day after most other countries.

The date on this page is the expected date. The confirmed date is the one announced locally after the moon is sighted.
