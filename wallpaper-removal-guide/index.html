How to Remove Wallpaper

Removing wallpaper is mostly about softening the paste and peeling patiently, and the method depends on the wallpaper type. This guide gives you tailored steps for your type, a tools checklist and wall-prep steps for a clean finish.

Pick your wallpaper type to get the method that suits it.

Turn off power to sockets and switches on the wall you are working on, and keep water away from electrics. If the wall is old, or the paper has been painted over many times, test a small area first. If you suspect the wall or paste contains anything hazardous, stop and get professional advice.

Before you start

Start by testing a corner. Find a loose edge, or lift one gently with a scraper, and pull slowly downwards. Some papers come away in satisfying sheets, and some come off in postage stamps. How the paper behaves in that first corner tells you which method to use and how long the job will take, so do not skip the test.

Then protect the room. Lay drop cloths along the wall, move furniture away or cover it, and take pictures and outlet covers off the wall. Turn off the power to any sockets and switches on that wall before you bring water near it. Wallpaper removal is wet work, and water near live electrics is the one real danger in this job.

Finally, check what is underneath. Paper over plaster behaves differently from paper over drywall, and drywall is easier to gouge with a scraper. If you are not sure what your walls are made of, work gently at first and see how the surface responds.

Soaking and peeling, step by step

Work in sections, one strip at a time, from the top of the wall down. The basic rhythm is the same whatever the type: soften the paste, peel the paper, then deal with what is left. Rushing any of these steps makes the next one harder, so patience is the main tool here.

  1. Peel away any loose paper by hand first, pulling slowly downwards at a low angle.
  2. Spray the next section with warm water until it is damp through, then wait a few minutes for the water to reach the paste.
  3. Peel from the top, keeping the angle low so the paper comes away instead of tearing.
  4. Use the scraper only where paper stays stuck, holding it flat against the wall so it does not gouge.
  5. Move to the next section and repeat, working your way across the wall.
  6. When the paper is off, wash the wall with warm water and a sponge to remove paste residue.

Dealing with stubborn patches

Some patches will not budge. Painted-over paper, vinyl-coated paper and paper that was hung with strong paste all resist water, because the water cannot reach the paste. For these, score the surface lightly with a scoring tool or a fork, then soak again so the water can get through the holes. Do not press hard when scoring: you are making holes in the paper, not in the wall.

If soaking and scoring still fail, a steamer is the next step. Hold the steamer plate against the paper for several seconds, then peel while the section is still warm. Work in small areas, keep the steamer moving, and take breaks: steamers are heavy and the work is hot. For very stubborn paper, repeat the cycle rather than forcing the scraper.

Preparing the wall afterwards

The wall is not finished when the paper is off. Paste residue left on the wall will show through paint and stop new paper from sticking, so wash the whole wall with warm water and a sponge, changing the water when it gets cloudy. Run your hand over the dry wall: any slippery or shiny spots still have paste on them and need another wash.

Once the wall is clean and dry, fill any nicks and dents with filler, let it dry fully, and sand it smooth. Then seal the wall with a primer before painting or papering. Skipping the primer is the most common cause of a poor finish after wallpaper removal, and it is also the easiest step to skip, so do not.

Common mistakes

The biggest mistake is rushing. Dry paper tears, wet paper peels, and the difference is a few minutes of soaking. The second is pressing too hard with the scraper, which gouges the wall and creates hours of filling later. The third is skipping the wash: paste residue is invisible when wet and obvious when dry, so wash until the wall feels clean to the touch.

Questions people ask

Do I need a steamer to remove wallpaper?

Not always. Most traditional pasted paper comes off with warm water and patience. A steamer helps with painted-over paper, vinyl-coated paper and paper hung with strong paste, and it is worth borrowing one for a whole room of stubborn paper.

How do I remove wallpaper without damaging drywall?

Soak well, peel slowly at a low angle, and keep the scraper flat against the wall. Drywall is soft and gouges easily, so never force the scraper and never dig at a corner. If a patch will not move, soak it again rather than pushing harder.

What if the paper comes off in tiny pieces?

Soak the section longer, and score the surface lightly first so the water can reach the paste. Work in smaller sections and peel while the paper is still damp. Tiny pieces usually mean the paste has not softened enough yet.

How do I get the paste off the wall?

Wash the wall with warm water and a sponge, changing the water when it gets cloudy. Run your hand over the dry wall afterwards: slippery or shiny spots still have paste on them and need another wash before you paint or paper.

Can I paper over old wallpaper?

It is not recommended. Old paper can bubble, peel and show seams through the new layer, and any loose patches underneath will spoil the finish. Removing the old paper and preparing the wall takes longer, but the result lasts far better.
