How Do You Photograph Products to Sell? A Simple Setup Guide

Short answer: Use soft, even light, a clean uncluttered background, and a sequence of photos that shows the whole item, important details and every flaw. Keep the camera steady, use more than one angle, and make sure the photos represent the item honestly.

A simple setup that works

  1. Choose soft light. Photograph near a bright window, avoiding harsh direct sunlight and mixed color temperatures. Turn off competing room lights if they create odd color casts.
  2. Clear the background. Use a plain wall, sheet or tabletop. Remove unrelated objects so the product is the clear subject.
  3. Stabilize the camera. Hold the phone with both hands or rest it on a stable support. Clean the lens before shooting.
  4. Frame the whole item. Leave a little space around the edges and keep the product straight. Avoid cutting off handles, corners or accessories.
  5. Capture useful detail. Photograph labels, ports, size references where helpful, included accessories and close-ups of wear.

Photo sequence for a listing

1. Main photo

Show the complete item from a clear front or three-quarter angle. Use the cleanest view as the listing thumbnail.

2. Other sides

Show the back, sides, underside and any parts a buyer would reasonably want to inspect.

3. Important details

Capture model labels, dimensions, connectors, material texture or included accessories when relevant.

4. Condition and flaws

Take focused photos of scratches, dents, stains, repairs, missing parts or other wear. Do not hide defects with angles or editing.

Editing without misleading buyers

Crop empty space, straighten the frame and make modest exposure corrections if needed. Avoid filters, artificial blur, object removal or color changes that make the product look newer or different. Keep original files so you can verify what was photographed.

A note before you act: Show flaws honestly; hidden flaws come back as returns and bad reviews. A clear defect photo helps the right buyer make an informed decision.

Questions people ask

What is the best lighting for product photos?

Soft, even light is a reliable starting point. Window light can work well; avoid harsh glare and strong mixed-color lighting.

How many photos should I include in a product listing?

Include enough views to show the full item, its important details, included accessories and its actual condition. The right number depends on the product.

Should I edit product photos before posting?

Basic cropping, straightening and modest exposure correction are fine when they preserve the item's true appearance. Do not edit out defects or change its color.

Do I need a professional camera to photograph products?

No. A phone can produce useful listing photos when the lens is clean, the camera is steady, the light is good and the item is shown clearly.
