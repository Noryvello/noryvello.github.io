DIY Bookshelf Project Guide

This guide takes you through building a simple bookshelf, from measuring and cutting to assembly and finishing. A board-length calculator works out how much board you need from your shelf count and width. Numbered steps and a safety checklist keep the build on track.

Plan your bookshelf

Start by deciding where the bookshelf will stand and how big it should be. Measure the space, including the height, and leave a little room above the top so the shelf does not crowd the ceiling. Think about what will sit on it. Paperbacks need less depth than large art books, and a shelf full of heavy books needs more support than a shelf of ornaments.

Sketch the bookshelf before you buy anything. Draw two side boards, the shelves between them, and a top and bottom if you plan to add them. Mark the width of each shelf and the height of the sides on the sketch. A simple sketch turns a vague idea into a cutting list, and it helps you spot mistakes while they are still free to fix.

Keep the design simple for a first build. A bookshelf with fixed shelves is easier than one with adjustable shelves, and square joints are easier than angled ones. You can always build a fancier bookshelf next time. The goal of this project is a sturdy shelf that holds your books and looks good doing it.

Cutting and joining boards uses sharp tools and power tools. Work slowly, follow your tool maker's safety guidance, and ask an experienced person for help if you are unsure. Always anchor a tall bookshelf to the wall so it cannot tip over.

Materials and tools

For the bookshelf itself you need board for the sides and shelves, screws, wood glue and, if you want one, a thin back panel. A back panel adds a lot of strength and stops the bookshelf racking sideways, so it is worth adding even if you keep it simple. Choose board sold for shelving or furniture, and check each piece is straight and free of large knots before you buy.

For tools, gather a tape measure, a square, a pencil, a saw, a drill, a screwdriver, sandpaper in a few grades, and clamps if you have them. A level helps you check the bookshelf stands straight, and a workbench or a pair of sawhorses gives you a steady surface to cut on. Lay everything out before you start so you are not hunting for tools halfway through the build.

Ask the supplier to cut the board to size if that service is offered. Supplier cuts are usually straight and square, which saves time and gives you clean edges to join. If you cut the board yourself, mark every cut with a square and a sharp pencil, and cut slowly along the line rather than rushing.

Assemble your bookshelf

  1. Lay the two side boards on a flat surface, inner faces up, and mark where each shelf will sit. Measure from the same end on both sides so the shelves line up.
  2. Drill pilot holes at each shelf mark on the side boards. Pilot holes stop the board splitting and make the screws go in straight.
  3. Stand one side board upright and fix the bottom shelf first, using glue on the joint and screws through the pilot holes. Check the joint is square before the glue sets.
  4. Fix the remaining shelves one at a time, working from the bottom up. Check each shelf is level before you tighten the screws.
  5. Fix the second side board in the same way, working from the bottom up. Ask someone to hold the frame steady while you work.
  6. Check the whole frame for square by measuring diagonally across the back. If the two diagonal measurements match, the frame is square.
  7. Fix the back panel if you are using one, starting at one corner and working across. The back panel locks the frame square.
  8. Stand the bookshelf up, check it with a level, and anchor it to the wall before you load it with books.

Finishing

Sand the bookshelf in stages, starting with rough paper and moving to fine paper. Sand with the grain, not across it, and wipe the dust away between stages. Pay attention to the front edges of the shelves, which are the parts you see and touch most. A smooth edge makes the whole bookshelf feel well made.

If you are painting, apply a primer coat first, then two thin coats of paint, letting each coat dry fully before the next. Thin coats dry harder and smoother than one thick coat. If you are staining, test the stain on an offcut first so you know what colour to expect, and wipe off the excess evenly so the finish does not go patchy.

Let the finish dry fully before you load the bookshelf. Paint and stain that feel dry to the touch may still be soft underneath, and books can stick to a finish that has not cured. Leave the bookshelf a day longer than you think it needs. Your patience will show in the finish.

Questions people ask

What board should I use for a bookshelf?

Use board sold for shelving or furniture, cut to your sizes by the supplier if you can. Check the board is straight and free of large knots before you buy.

How do I stop the shelves sagging?

Keep shelves short and thick enough for the books they will hold, and support long shelves in the middle. Heavy books belong on the lowest shelves.

Do I need to anchor the bookshelf to the wall?

Yes, especially for tall bookshelves. A bookshelf can tip forward when a child climbs it or when heavy books are pulled out, so fix it to the wall with a suitable anchor.

Can I build this without power tools?

Yes. A handsaw, a drill or screwdriver, a square and sandpaper are enough for a simple bookshelf. Power tools save time but are not required.

How do I get a smooth finish?

Sand in stages from rough to fine paper, wipe the dust away, and apply thin coats of paint or stain. Let each coat dry fully before the next.
