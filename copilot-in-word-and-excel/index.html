How to Use Copilot in Word and Excel

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

Copilot in Word helps you draft, rewrite, and summarize documents, and Copilot in Excel helps you explore, summarize, and analyze spreadsheet data. You describe what you want in plain words, and Copilot works on the document or data you have open. This guide gives you prompt patterns for each app, with honest notes on what to check.

Copilot inside Word and Excel is different from chatting with a standalone assistant: it can see the document or spreadsheet you have open and work on it directly. In Word, that means drafting a report from an outline or rewriting a paragraph in a different tone. In Excel, it means asking questions about your data in plain words instead of writing formulas.

This guide covers what Copilot can do in each app, the prompt patterns that work best, and the checks you should run before you share anything it produces. The features described here are in general terms, because availability varies by plan, region, and app version.

Always review AI-written text and check any numbers it produces against your own data before you share a document. Never paste confidential information into a prompt unless your workplace allows it.

What Copilot can do in Word

In Word, Copilot can draft a document from a short description, rewrite a paragraph in a different style, summarize a long document, and answer questions about the text you have open. It works best when you give it clear direction: the topic, the audience, the length, and the tone you want.

It is most useful for first drafts and for turning rough notes into clean prose. Give it your bullet points and ask for a draft, then edit the result in your own voice. The draft saves you the blank page, and your editing makes it yours.

Drafting and rewriting in Word

To draft, describe the document you want: draft a one-page welcome letter for new clients, warm and professional. To rewrite, select the text and ask for a specific change, such as make this shorter and simpler or rewrite this in a formal tone. Specific instructions give better results than a vague improve this.

After Copilot produces text, read it as an editor, not a reader. Check that the facts are right, that the tone fits your audience, and that nothing important was left out. AI drafts often sound polished while saying very little, so look for filler and cut it.

Summarizing long documents in Word

For a long report or contract, ask Copilot for a summary with the main points, or ask a specific question about the document, such as what are the three main recommendations? This is faster than reading the whole document when you only need the shape of it.

Summaries can miss caveats and flatten nuance, so treat them as a map, not the territory. If a summary mentions a figure or a commitment, find it in the original text and read the surrounding paragraph before you quote it or act on it.

What Copilot can do in Excel

In Excel, Copilot can answer questions about your data in plain words, suggest formulas, create summaries, and highlight patterns. You can ask things like which month had the highest sales? or summarize this table by region without writing a formula yourself.

It works best with clean, well-organized data: one header row, consistent formats, and no merged cells. If your spreadsheet is messy, Copilot will struggle, so tidy the data first and the answers will be much more reliable.

Asking questions about your data in Excel

Ask one question at a time and name the columns you mean. What is the average order value in the Sales column? is better than analyse this, because it tells Copilot exactly what to compute. When it gives you an answer, ask it to show the formula or the steps, so you can check the working.

For charts and summaries, describe the result you want: show monthly totals as a column chart or list the top five products by quantity sold. Review the result against the raw data before you share it, because a wrong range or a misread column can produce a confident-looking error.

Prompt patterns that work in both apps

Three patterns work well in both Word and Excel. The first is task plus format: summarize this document as five bullet points. The second is task plus audience: explain this table to someone who has never seen it. The third is draft plus constraint: draft a 200-word introduction, plain language, no jargon.

Keep a small list of the prompts that work for you and reuse them. A prompt that produced a good result once will usually produce a good result again on similar material, and building your own library saves time over starting from scratch each time.

Checking AI output before you share it

Before you send any AI-drafted document, check three things: are the facts right, are the numbers right, and does the tone fit the reader? In Word, that means reading the draft end to end and fixing anything that sounds generic or wrong. In Excel, it means spot-checking a few figures against the raw data.

Remember that you are responsible for what you send, not the tool. A quick check takes minutes and prevents the embarrassment of a wrong figure or a misnamed client reaching someone else. Make the check part of your routine, not an afterthought.

When Copilot gets it wrong

Copilot will get things wrong, and the mistakes are not always obvious. In Word, it can invent a detail that sounds plausible, such as a date or a name that was never in your notes. In Excel, it can pick the wrong range, misread a column header, or apply a formula to the wrong rows. The output looks confident either way.

When you spot an error, tell Copilot what is wrong and ask it to try again with the correct information. A message like that date is wrong, the meeting is on the 12th, please fix the paragraph usually corrects it. If it keeps making the same mistake, fix the text yourself: some corrections are faster by hand than by conversation.

Questions people ask

Do I need a special plan for Copilot in Word and Excel?

Copilot in Word and Excel is usually tied to a Microsoft 365 plan that includes Copilot, and availability varies by plan and region. Check the current options in your account or with your workplace administrator.

Can Copilot write a whole report for me?

It can draft a report from your outline and notes, but the result will need your review and editing. Treat it as a strong first draft, and check every fact, figure, and claim before you share it.

Will Copilot change my spreadsheet data?

Copilot generally produces answers, summaries, and suggested formulas rather than editing your data directly. Review anything it suggests before you apply it, and keep a copy of your original data.

How do I ask Copilot about a specific table?

Name the table or the columns in your question, such as asking about the Sales column by name. Clean data with one header row helps Copilot find the right range.

What should I check before sending an AI-drafted document?

Check the facts, the numbers, and the tone. Read the draft end to end, verify any figures against your own data, and make sure names, dates, and commitments are correct.
