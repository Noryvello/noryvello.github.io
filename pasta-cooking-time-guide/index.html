How to test for al dente

Taste one minute before the earliest time in the range — for dried spaghetti, that means tasting at 7 minutes. The pasta should be firm to the bite, not soft. If it is still hard in the middle, give it another minute and taste again.

Salt, water and stirring

Salt the water well before the pasta goes in, and use plenty of water so the pasta has room to move. Stir in the first minute so the pasta does not stick together, then let it cook undisturbed.

Questions people ask

How long should I cook spaghetti?

Dried spaghetti usually takes 8–10 minutes, and fresh spaghetti takes 2–3 minutes. Always check your packet, because the time on the packet comes first, and taste a piece one minute before the earliest time.

How do I know when pasta is al dente?

Taste a piece one minute before the earliest time in the range. Al dente pasta is firm to the bite, not soft and not hard in the middle.

How much salt should I add to pasta water?

Salt the water well before the pasta goes in, and use plenty of water. The right amount depends on how much water you use, but the water should taste seasoned.

Is fresh pasta faster to cook than dried?

Yes. Fresh pasta cooks much faster than dried pasta — for example, fresh spaghetti takes 2–3 minutes while dried spaghetti takes 8–10 minutes. Check the packet for your pasta's own time.
