How to tell a knock-knock joke

Every knock-knock joke follows the same four lines. The teller says "Knock knock", the listener answers "Who is there?", the teller gives a name or word, and the listener asks that name followed by "who?". The teller then delivers the punchline, which turns the name into a pun. The pattern is so well known that even young children can join in after hearing one or two jokes.

The secret is in the delivery. Say the name clearly, pause for a beat after the listener asks who, and then say the punchline slowly enough for everyone to catch the wordplay. If nobody laughs, that is part of the fun too — knock-knock jokes are meant to be a little bit silly, and the groans are half the joke.

Why knock-knock jokes work

Knock-knock jokes work because everyone knows what is coming, and the fun is in the surprise of the wordplay. The setup builds a small expectation, and the punchline breaks it in a harmless way. That is why they are popular with children: the jokes are easy to remember, easy to retell and safe to share with anyone.

They are also good practice for speaking and listening. Telling a joke means speaking clearly, waiting for the other person and watching their reaction. Listening to one means following the pattern and guessing the pun. Teachers often use them as a warm-up for reading aloud, because the call-and-response pattern keeps the whole group involved.

Joke-telling tips for kids

  • Practise the punchline first, so you can say it without stumbling.
  • Speak slowly and clearly, especially on the name in the middle.
  • Wait for the listener to ask who before you give the punchline.
  • Keep jokes kind and clean, and never make a joke about someone in the room.
  • Take turns, so everyone gets a chance to be the teller.
