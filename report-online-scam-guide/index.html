First steps after you spot a scam

Stop all contact with the scammer straight away. Do not reply, do not send more money or details, and do not tell them you are reporting them, because replying only confirms that your contact details work and can lead to more attempts. If you clicked a link or opened an attachment, do not enter any details on the page it led to, and close it. If you entered a password anywhere, change that password now, and change it anywhere else you used the same one.

If you paid money, contact your bank or card provider immediately, before you do anything else. Tell them what happened and ask what can be done. The sooner you report a payment, the better the chance of stopping it or getting it back. Keep a note of who you spoke to and any reference number they give you, because you may need it for your report. Then gather your evidence and report the scam itself.

Where reports go

Reports go to different places depending on the scam, and it is worth reporting in more than one place. Your country's fraud reporting service or cybercrime unit takes reports of online scams in general and uses them to track patterns. The platform where you saw the scam — the social network, the marketplace, or the email provider — has its own report button, and reporting there can get the scam account or advert removed quickly.

If money was involved, your bank or card provider is a reporting route too, and it is the one that can act on the payment. If the scam pretended to be a real company, report it to that company as well, because they will want to warn their customers. You do not need to know exactly which body is the right one: start with the fraud reporting service and the platform, and they will pass the report on if another body handles it.

Protecting yourself afterwards

After a scam, take steps to limit the damage. Change the passwords of any accounts you mentioned to the scammer, and any accounts that share a password with them. Turn on two-factor authentication on your email and bank accounts if you have not already. Check your bank statements for payments you do not recognise, and keep checking for a few weeks, because scammers sometimes wait before using stolen details.

Be ready for follow-up scams. People who have been scammed once are often targeted again, sometimes by someone claiming to help recover the lost money for a fee. That is itself a scam: no genuine recovery service asks for payment upfront. Tell someone you trust what happened, because talking about it helps you spot the next attempt, and it helps them avoid the same scam. There is no shame in being scammed — these schemes are designed to fool careful people.

Spotting the next one

Scams share patterns that are easier to spot once you know them. Urgency is the most common: a message that says you must act now or lose access, miss a deal, or face a fine. Requests for unusual payment methods — gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency — are another warning sign, because those payments are hard to reverse. Offers that sound too good to be true, such as a job with high pay for little work, or an investment that doubles your money, are almost always scams.

Check the sender before you trust a message. Look at the full email address, not just the display name, and hover over links to see where they really go. A genuine company will not ask for your password or your sign-in codes by message. When in doubt, stop and check through a separate route: type the company's web address yourself, or call them on a number you already have, rather than using a number from the suspicious message.

Never share your sign-in codes, passwords, or backup codes with anyone, including someone claiming to be from your bank, your phone company, or a support team. No genuine organisation will ever ask for them. If someone asks, it is a scam — stop contact and report it.

Questions people ask

Should I report a scam even if I did not lose money?

Yes. Reports help authorities and platforms spot scam patterns and shut them down, which protects other people. You do not need to have lost money for a report to matter.

What should I do first if I paid a scammer?

Contact your bank or card provider straight away and tell them what happened. The sooner you report it, the better the chance of stopping or recovering the payment. Then gather your evidence and report the scam.

Should I reply to the scammer or confront them?

No. Stop all contact, do not send more money or details, and do not tell them you are reporting them. Replying only confirms your contact details work and can lead to more scam attempts.

How do I report a scam on social media?

Use the report button on the post, profile, or message. Choose the scam or fraud option, add a short description, and block the account afterwards. Reporting removes the content for others even if you hear nothing back.

Can I get my money back after a scam?

It depends on how you paid and how quickly you report it. Bank transfers and card payments sometimes can be reversed, while gift cards and cryptocurrency usually cannot. Your bank or card provider can tell you what is possible in your case.
