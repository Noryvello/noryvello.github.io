Iftar Recipes for Ramadan 2027

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

Iftar is the meal that breaks the fast at sunset, and the best iftar recipes are the ones that are quick, filling and kind to an empty stomach. This page gathers family-friendly dishes for Ramadan 2027, from soups and salads to one-pot mains and make-ahead meals. Every idea comes with prep tips so the evening runs calmly, even on busy days.

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin on Monday 8 February, which means a month of evening meals to plan. Cooking iftar while fasting is a challenge of its own: you cannot taste as you go, the clock matters, and by late afternoon everyone is hungry. The households that enjoy iftar most are usually the ones that plan simply, cook in advance where they can, and keep the menu to a few good dishes rather than a feast every night.

How to build a balanced iftar plate

A good iftar plate has four things: something to rehydrate you, something gentle to wake up the stomach, something filling, and something fresh. Water and dates handle the first two. Soup or a light starter handles the third. A main dish with protein and a slow-release carbohydrate, plus a salad or vegetables, handles the rest. When all four are there, you feel satisfied without feeling heavy.

Think in proportions rather than piles. A bowl of soup, a modest main portion, a generous salad, and a few dates will serve most people well. The evening is long — there is time for a second helping later, or a light snack before bed. Eating in two stages, with the Maghrib prayer in between, is a natural way to pace the meal.

It also helps to plan the week loosely rather than deciding each day at the last minute. A rough menu — soup on some nights, a one-pot dish on others, a lighter plate when the day has been long — takes the pressure off the cook and keeps the shopping list short.

Quick starters: dates, soup and salad

The classic iftar opening is dates and water, and it is hard to improve on. Dates give gentle, quick energy, and a glass of water rehydrates you after the day. Keep a bowl of dates on the table through the month so the first bite is always ready.

Soup is the great iftar starter: warm, light and easy to make in a big pot. Lentil soup is a favourite in many homes — red lentils, onion, carrot, cumin and lemon, simmered until soft and blended smooth. Vegetable soup, tomato soup and chicken broth all work the same way. Make a large pot, and you have starters for several nights, since soup keeps well in the fridge and freezes well too.

A fresh salad balances the warm dishes. A chopped salad of tomato, cucumber and herbs with lemon and olive oil takes minutes and cuts through richer food. A yoghurt-based salad, with cucumber and mint, is cooling and gentle on the stomach. Keep the dressing light and let the salad sit for a few minutes so the flavours come together.

One-pot mains for busy evenings

One-pot dishes are the iftar cook's best friend: everything goes into one pot, the cooking mostly looks after itself, and there is little washing up. A chicken and vegetable stew, a lentil and rice dish, or a bean and tomato casserole can all be started earlier in the day and left to simmer gently until sunset.

The secret is to build flavour at the start. Fry onions, garlic and spices first, then add the main ingredients and liquid, and let time do the rest. A dish that has simmered slowly all afternoon will taste far better than one rushed at the last minute, and it frees you to set the table and prepare the salad while the pot finishes.

Rice dishes work the same way. A simple pilaf — rice cooked with onions, spices and vegetables, with chicken or chickpeas stirred through — feeds a family from a single pan. Serve it with salad and yoghurt, and the meal is complete. If you cook rice dishes often, keep the spices you use most in one place so the evening routine is quick.

Light iftar ideas for warm evenings

Not every iftar needs a heavy main dish. On evenings when the day has been long or the weather is warm, a lighter plate can be exactly right: a generous salad with bread and dips, a vegetable omelette with salad, or a platter of grilled vegetables, cheese and olives with flatbread.

Wraps are another easy option. Fill flatbreads with grilled chicken or falafel, salad and yoghurt sauce, and let everyone build their own. Children enjoy choosing their fillings, and the cook only has to prepare the components rather than a finished dish. The same fillings work well the next day in a lunchbox or as part of suhoor.

Light does not mean small. A light iftar still needs enough food to carry you through the evening and into the night's prayers. The difference is in the cooking: grilled and fresh rather than fried and heavy, with vegetables doing more of the work than oil and pastry.

Make-ahead dishes that freeze well

The smartest iftar cooking happens before Ramadan even starts. A weekend spent cooking and freezing a few big dishes gives you a head start for the first week, when everyone is still adjusting to the new routine. Soups, stews, curries and rice dishes all freeze well and reheat gently.

Label everything with the dish name and the date, and freeze in family-sized portions so you only defrost what you need. Move the next day's dish from the freezer to the fridge the night before, and it will be ready to reheat when you get home. A slow cooker or a low oven does the reheating while you prepare the salad and set the table.

Some dishes are better made fresh, but their parts can still be prepared ahead. Chop vegetables and store them covered in the fridge, marinate meat or chicken the day before, and make sauces and dressings in advance. Small preparations like these turn a rushed evening into a calm one.

Drinks and desserts to end the evening

Drinks matter at iftar, because rehydration is the body's first need after a day without water. Water is the most important drink of all, and it is worth keeping a jug on the table. Fruit-infused water, with slices of lemon, orange or cucumber, is refreshing without being sweet. Milk-based drinks, such as a simple lassi or a warm milk drink, are filling and gentle.

Very sweet drinks are best kept small. A small glass of fruit juice or a traditional sweet drink is a pleasure, but large amounts of sugar after a fast can leave you feeling uncomfortable. The same goes for desserts: a small sweet treat after the main meal is lovely, but a large rich dessert on a full stomach is often more than anyone needs.

Good iftar desserts are simple ones: fresh fruit with yoghurt, a rice pudding made earlier in the day, or a small piece of a baked sweet shared between everyone. Save the grand desserts for the weekend or for guests, when there is time to enjoy them properly.

Planning the week's iftar menu

A weekly plan is the single best way to make iftar cooking calm. Sit down once a week, choose a main dish for each evening, and write the shopping list from the plan. Include one or two very easy nights — leftovers, a simple soup and salad, or a freezer dish — so the cook gets a rest too.

Rotate the types of dish rather than repeating the same meal every night. A week might hold a soup night, a one-pot night, a grilled night, a rice dish, a light salad night, a family favourite, and a night out or a shared iftar with friends. Variety keeps the table interesting without adding work.

Finally, share the work. Iftar cooking does not have to fall on one person for the whole month. Children can set the table and wash salad, older family members can take a night each, and guests can be asked to bring a dish. A shared iftar is a happier iftar, and the month is long enough for everyone to take a turn.

Questions people ask

What should I eat first at iftar?

Dates and water are the traditional first bite, and they are a gentle way to break the fast. Follow them with soup or a light starter, then the main meal after the Maghrib prayer.

How early should I start cooking iftar?

It depends on the dish. Soups and one-pot meals can be started earlier in the day and left to simmer, while salads and quick dishes need only a short time. Aim to have the main dish ready shortly before sunset, with the table set in good time.

How do I keep iftar light and still feel full?

Build the plate around soup, salad and a modest main portion, and eat slowly. A balanced plate with protein, vegetables and a slow-release carbohydrate fills you up without leaving you heavy.

Can I prepare iftar in the morning?

Yes. Many dishes, especially soups, stews and marinated dishes, are better when prepared ahead. Chop vegetables, marinate meat and make sauces in the morning, then finish cooking in the afternoon.

What do I do with leftovers?

Cool leftovers quickly and store them in the fridge in covered containers. Most cooked dishes keep well for a day or two and make an easy iftar or suhoor the next day. Avoid keeping food out at room temperature for long.

How do I cook iftar for a crowd?

Choose dishes that scale easily, such as one-pot meals, rice dishes and large salads. Serve buffet-style so guests help themselves, and ask guests to bring a dish if they offer. Keep the menu to a few generous dishes rather than many small ones.
