Insulation Rolls Calculator

Enter the area you want to insulate and the coverage printed on one roll, and this calculator works out how many rolls you need, including a waste allowance. The working is shown so you can check every step.

This calculator counts rolls from area alone. It does not check whether the insulation suits your building, and cutting around pipes, wires and joists can use more material than the allowance covers. Follow the maker's fitting guidance and local building practice.

How to use this calculator

Measure the area to insulate in square metres. For a floor or a loft, multiply the length by the width. Find the coverage of one roll on the pack or the product page. It is usually given in square metres per roll. Enter that figure, set the waste allowance, then press Calculate to see how many rolls you need, with the working shown underneath.

How the calculation works

The area is multiplied by one plus the waste percentage, to allow for cutting and fitting. That total is divided by the coverage of one roll. The result is rounded up to a whole roll, because part rolls cannot be bought. The steps show the area with waste, the division and the rounding, so you can check the figure before ordering.

How to use the result

Buy the number of rolls shown, and keep any offcuts for filling small gaps. If your area has many joists, pipes or awkward corners, consider a higher waste allowance. Check the roll width against the gap between your joists before ordering, so the rolls fit without too much trimming. Lay the insulation with no gaps and no squashing, so it works as the maker intends.

Questions people ask

Where do I find the roll coverage?

The coverage is printed on the roll packaging or listed on the product page, usually as square metres per roll. Enter that figure as it is printed.

How much waste allowance should I add?

Ten percent is the default and suits a plain area. Add more for a space with many joists, pipes, wires or awkward corners, because cutting around them uses more material.

Why is the result rounded up?

Rolls are sold whole, so the calculator rounds up to the next full roll. The waste allowance already covers cutting, and rounding up covers the last part roll.

Does this work for insulation batts?

The maths is the same if you enter the coverage of one pack of batts instead of one roll. The result is then the number of packs, not rolls.

What if my area is not a rectangle?

Split the area into rectangles, work out each one, and add the areas together before entering the total. Enter the combined area as a single figure.
