How to Use Perplexity: AI Search Explained

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

Perplexity is an AI search tool: instead of a page of links, you ask a question and get a written answer with citations you can check. You can follow up in the same thread, and focus modes narrow the search to a particular kind of source. This guide shows you how to ask, read, and follow up so you get answers you can trust.

Traditional search gives you ten blue links and leaves the reading to you. Perplexity does the reading first: it searches the web, writes a direct answer, and attaches citations to the sources it used. For a question like what caused a historical event or how a new rule works, that can save a lot of clicking.

It is still a search tool, not an oracle. The answer is only as good as the sources it found, and citations need checking. This guide explains how to ask good questions, how to read a cited answer, how follow-up threads work, and what the free and paid tiers offer in general terms.

Perplexity's interface, modes, and plan details change over time, and cited answers can still be wrong. Always open the cited sources for anything important, and never treat a cited answer as proof on its own. Never share personal or payment details in a search.

What Perplexity is and how it differs from a chatbot

A chatbot answers from what it learned during training, which may be months or years old. Perplexity searches the live web each time you ask, so it can answer questions about recent events, current rules, and new products. The answer comes with numbered citations that point to the pages it used.

That makes it closer to a research assistant than a conversational chatbot. You ask a question, it finds sources, and it writes a summary with the evidence attached. For factual questions, that is a better fit than a chatbot guessing from memory.

Asking your first question

Ask in full sentences, the way you would ask a knowledgeable friend. Why do leaves change colour in autumn? works better than a bare keyword like leaves autumn, because the question tells Perplexity what kind of answer you want. Add context when it matters, such as your country or your level of knowledge.

If the answer misses the point, rephrase the question rather than repeating it. A narrower question gets a narrower answer. For example, what does the new recycling rule mean for flats in my city? will do better than recycling rules on its own.

Reading a cited answer

Read the answer first, then check the citations that matter. Each numbered marker points to a source page, and you can open it to see the original wording. Pay attention to which claims carry a citation and which do not: uncited sentences are the assistant's own wording, not a quoted fact.

Check the date and the publisher of each source before you rely on it. A citation to an official page is stronger than one to a random blog, and a recent source is stronger than an old one for a fast-moving topic. If a claim has no citation, treat it as unproven until you find one.

Follow-up threads: digging deeper

After the first answer, you can ask follow-up questions in the same thread, and Perplexity will remember the topic. Ask it to explain a term, compare two options, or find the original source of a claim. Each follow-up builds on the earlier answers, so the thread becomes a small research trail.

Use follow-ups to test the answer too. Ask what is the strongest evidence against that view? or is there a more recent source? A good research habit is to ask for the other side, not just confirmation of what you already believe.

Focus modes in general terms

Focus modes let you narrow the search to a particular kind of source, such as academic papers, videos, or general web pages. If you are researching a scientific question, an academic focus gives you papers rather than blog posts. If you want a how-to video, a video focus skips the articles.

The available modes change over time, so look at the options on your own screen and pick the one that fits your question. When in doubt, start with a general search and narrow down only if the results feel too broad.

Free vs paid tiers, named without prices

Perplexity offers a free tier and a paid tier. The free tier covers everyday searching with a limited number of advanced searches, while the paid tier adds more advanced searches, access to more powerful models, and extra features such as file uploads. The exact limits and features change, so check the current details on the service itself.

For most casual users, the free tier is enough to answer daily questions and do light research. If you research heavily every day, the paid tier's higher limits may be worth it, but try the free tier first and see where you hit the edges.

Everyday uses: research, shopping, learning, news

For research, Perplexity is good for gathering background on a topic before you write or decide. For shopping, ask it to compare two products by features, then check the cited reviews yourself. For learning, ask it to explain a concept and then ask follow-ups until it clicks. For news, ask what happened and when, and open the cited reporting to read the full story.

In each case, the citations are the point. An answer without checked sources is just a chatbot answer with extra decoration. Build the habit of opening at least one source per answer, and Perplexity becomes a genuinely useful research tool.

Saving threads and building a research habit

When a thread produces something useful, save it. Perplexity lets you keep threads in your account, so you can return to a research trail days later without starting over. Give important threads a clear name, and delete the one-off questions so the useful ones are easy to find.

Build a simple habit around it: ask, check one source, then write down the finding in your own words. The note-taking step matters more than the search itself, because it forces you to decide what you actually learned. A short note with a link to the source is worth more than a long thread you never revisit.

Questions people ask

Is Perplexity a search engine or a chatbot?

It is an AI search tool. Like a search engine, it searches the live web; like a chatbot, it answers in writing. The difference is that its answers come with citations you can check.

Are the citations always accurate?

No. Perplexity can cite the wrong page, misread a source, or present an outdated answer as current. Always open the cited pages for anything important, and check the date and publisher.

Can Perplexity answer questions about recent events?

Yes, because it searches the live web rather than relying only on training data. That makes it better than a plain chatbot for news and current topics, though its answers still need checking.

Do I need an account to use Perplexity?

You can usually ask questions without an account, but signing in unlocks extra features such as saved threads and higher limits. The exact options can change, so check the service itself.

When should I use Perplexity instead of Google?

Use Perplexity when you want a direct, cited answer to a factual question. Use Google when you want to browse many sources yourself, compare pages, or search for something specific like a local business.
