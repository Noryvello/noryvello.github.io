Coffee Grind Size Guide

The right grind size depends on how you brew. A fine grind suits espresso, a coarse grind suits a French press, and each method in between has its own size. Pick your brew method below to see the grind size, what it should look and feel like, and the brew time.

Grind size is only one part of good coffee. Your beans, water and technique matter too, and the brew times on this page are guides — adjust them to your own taste.

Adjusting your grind

Taste is your best guide. If your coffee tastes sour or sharp, the water has not pulled enough out of the grounds — try a finer grind. If it tastes bitter or harsh, the water has pulled out too much — try a coarser grind.

Change one thing at a time and keep everything else the same, so you know what made the difference. A small step finer or coarser is usually enough.

Questions people ask

What grind size should I use for espresso?

Use a fine grind, like table salt. Espresso brews in 25–30 seconds under pressure, so the fine grind gives the water enough coffee to work with in that short time.

What grind size is best for French press?

Use a coarse grind, like breadcrumbs. The coffee steeps for about 4 minutes, and a coarse grind keeps the grounds from slipping through the mesh and making the cup gritty.

How do I know if my coffee grind is too fine?

If your coffee tastes bitter or harsh, your grind may be too fine — the water pulls out too much from the grounds. Try a coarser grind and see if the bitterness eases.

Can I use the same grind for all brew methods?

No. Each method needs a different grind because the brew time and the way water meets the coffee are different. Espresso needs a fine grind for a 25–30 second brew, while cold brew needs an extra coarse grind for 12–16 hours.
