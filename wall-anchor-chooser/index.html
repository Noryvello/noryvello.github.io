Wall Anchor Chooser

The right anchor depends on two things: what your wall is made of and how heavy the load is. Pick your wall type and enter the weight below, and this chooser tells you which anchor to use, with a plain explanation of why.

Heavy loads and overhead fixing need a professional. This page is general guidance, not a guarantee — always check the anchor maker's own load rating before you buy, and if you are unsure, ask a professional.

How the choice is made

The chooser looks at your wall type and your load band. The load bands are: light means under 5 kg, medium means 5 to 20 kg, and heavy means over 20 kg. The table below shows the recommendation for every combination.

Plasterboard is hollow behind the board, so anchors for it work by gripping or spreading behind the surface. Brick and concrete are solid, so anchors work by gripping inside a drilled hole. Wood holds a screw by itself, so no anchor is needed at all — the screw is the fix.

Before you drill

  • Check for pipes and cables. Use a pipe and cable detector, or check any plans you have, before you drill into any wall. Hitting a pipe or a cable is dangerous and expensive.
  • Use the right drill bit for the wall. Use a masonry bit for brick and concrete, and a wood bit for timber. Self-drill plasterboard anchors screw straight in and need no drilled hole.
  • Drill the hole to the right size. The hole must match the plug or anchor you are using — too big and the anchor will not grip, too small and it will not go in.
  • Wear eye protection. Drilling throws up dust and small pieces of wall, so wear safety glasses.
  • Do not drill into a wall you have not identified. If you are not sure what the wall is made of, find out first. The wrong anchor in the wrong wall will not hold.

Questions people ask

How do I know what type of wall I have?

Tap the wall: plasterboard sounds hollow, while brick and concrete sound solid. You can also look at the dust from a small test hole — brick dust is reddish, concrete dust is grey, and plasterboard dust is white and powdery. If you are still unsure, ask someone with experience before you drill.

Can I hang something heavy on plasterboard?

Yes, but not on the board alone. The safe way is to fix into a wooden stud behind the board, or use a specialist heavy-duty hollow-wall anchor made for the weight. Always check the anchor maker's own load rating, and get professional advice for very heavy items.

Do I need an anchor for a wood wall?

No. Timber holds a screw by itself, so a wood screw driven straight into solid wood is the fix. Use a longer screw for a heavier load, and check the timber is sound first — soft, cracked or rotten wood will not hold.

What is overhead fixing, and why does it need a professional?

Overhead fixing means hanging something from a ceiling or above head height, where a failure could drop the load on someone. The forces are different from fixing to a wall, so it needs the right anchor and the right fixing method. Get a professional to do it.
