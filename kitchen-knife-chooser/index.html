How to use this chooser

Pick the task that matches what you are about to do. The card shows the knife type, why its shape suits that job, and one care note to keep it in good shape. If you do not own the recommended knife, the card still helps: it tells you what quality to look for, so you can pick the closest knife you have.

Most kitchens do not need a knife for every job. Three knives cover almost everything: a chef's knife for general work, a paring knife for small work, and a bread knife for anything with a crust. The rest are helpful, but they are extras, not essentials.

Knife care basics

Good care is simple and it makes a bigger difference than the price of the knife. Wash knives by hand with warm soapy water and dry them straight away. Water left on a blade invites rust on carbon steel and spots on stainless steel, and a wet knife left in the sink is a cut waiting to happen.

Cut on wood or plastic boards. Glass, stone and ceramic plates dull an edge quickly and can chip it. Store knives so the blades do not touch each other: a block, a magnetic strip or blade guards all work. A loose drawer full of knives dulls every blade in it and is dangerous to reach into.

Hone your main knife often and sharpen it when honing stops bringing the edge back. Honing straightens the edge; sharpening removes a little metal to make a new edge. Both are skills worth learning, and both are covered in our sharpening guide.

One knife or a set?

A set looks tidy, but it often includes knives you will never use. Buying one good chef's knife, then adding a paring knife and a bread knife as you need them, usually gives you better tools for the same money. Hold a knife before you buy it if you can: it should feel balanced in your hand, with a handle that fits your grip and does not slip when wet.

Weight is a matter of taste. Some cooks like a heavy knife that does the work for them; others prefer a light knife they can control precisely. Neither is wrong. What matters is that the knife feels like an extension of your hand rather than a tool you are fighting.

Questions people ask

Which knife should a beginner buy first?

A chef's knife. It handles most everyday jobs, from chopping vegetables to slicing meat, and it is the knife you will reach for most often. Add a paring knife and a bread knife next.

Can I put kitchen knives in the dishwasher?

It is better not to. Dishwasher cycles knock blades against other items, which dulls the edge, and the heat and detergent can damage handles. Wash by hand and dry straight away.

How do I keep a knife sharp?

Hone it often with a honing rod, sharpen it when honing no longer brings the edge back, and cut on wood or plastic boards rather than glass or stone. Store knives in a block, on a magnetic strip or in blade guards.

What size chef's knife should I choose?

Most home cooks are happy with a blade around 20 cm long. It is long enough for large vegetables and roasts but short enough to control. Hold one before you buy, if you can.

Is a sharp knife safer than a dull one?

Yes. A sharp knife cuts where you aim it with little force, while a dull knife needs pressure and is more likely to slip. Keep your fingers curled away from the blade and cut away from your body.
