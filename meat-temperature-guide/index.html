Meat Temperature Guide

Cook meat to the right internal temperature and it is safe to eat. This guide gives the USDA safe minimum temperature for beef, lamb, pork, poultry and fish, in °C and °F, with rest times. Pick a meat below to see its temperature, or check the full table further down the page.

The thermometer rule

Temperature, not colour, decides whether meat is done. Meat can look browned on the outside while still under the safe temperature inside, so check with a food thermometer in the thickest part. Push the thermometer into the centre of the thickest part, away from bone, and wait for the reading to settle.

Rest time

Beef and lamb steaks and chops, and pork, should rest for 3 minutes after cooking. During the rest the temperature stays high enough to finish the job, so let the meat sit before cutting it. Ground meat, poultry and fish need no rest time — they are ready once they reach their safe temperature.

Questions people ask

What temperature should chicken be cooked to?

Chicken should reach 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part. That is the USDA safe minimum temperature for all poultry, including chicken, turkey and duck — check it with a food thermometer, not by colour.

What is the safe temperature for beef steak?

Beef steak is safe at 145°F (63°C), measured in the thickest part. Let it rest for 3 minutes after cooking before cutting it.

Do I need a meat thermometer?

Yes — temperature, not colour, decides whether meat is done. Meat can look cooked on the outside while still under the safe temperature inside, so a food thermometer in the thickest part is the only reliable check.

How long should meat rest after cooking?

Beef and lamb steaks and chops, and pork, should rest for 3 minutes. Ground meat, poultry and fish need no rest time — they are ready once they reach their safe temperature.
