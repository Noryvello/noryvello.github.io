How Should You Price Garage Sale Items? A Simple Percentage Method

Short answer: Start with the item's original value, choose a percentage that reflects its condition and age, then adjust for completeness and local demand. Treat the result as a starting estimate—not a guaranteed selling price—and record your reasoning in the worksheet below.

A simple percentage method

Use the original purchase value as a reference, not as a promise of what someone will pay. A practical starting framework is:

  • Like-new and complete: consider an estimated 50–70% of the original value.
  • Good, used condition: consider an estimated 30–50%.
  • Well-used but working: consider an estimated 10–30%.
  • Damaged, incomplete or untested: use a lower estimate, or sell clearly labeled for parts.

These ranges are only a planning framework. They are not market data or a promise of resale value. Pick a point within the range after checking the item's condition, age, completeness and usefulness.

Adjust the estimate before setting a tag

  1. Check condition. Note stains, scratches, missing pieces, wear and whether the item works.
  2. Check completeness. Include manuals, accessories, chargers, lids, matching pieces or original packaging only when actually present.
  3. Consider age and relevance. Older styles, outdated technology and seasonal goods may attract less interest.
  4. Compare local alternatives. Similar items nearby provide a better reality check than the original receipt.
  5. Choose a negotiation margin. Decide the lowest amount you would willingly accept before the sale begins.

Before putting out the item

  • Clean it and test it where safe.
  • Group related pieces together and label what is included.
  • Mark visible defects clearly.
  • Keep fragile or valuable items where you can supervise them.
  • Decide in advance whether you will bundle items or accept offers.
A note before you act: Local demand sets the real price; be ready to negotiate. The percentage method is only a starting estimate, and buyers may value the same item differently.

Questions people ask

Should I use the original purchase value to set the sale price?

Use it only as a reference point. Condition, age, completeness and local demand matter more to what a buyer will actually offer.

What percentage should I use for a used item?

Choose a percentage that reflects condition and usefulness. The ranges on this page are planning estimates, not fixed market rules.

Should I leave room for negotiation?

Yes. Decide your acceptable minimum before the sale and set an asking point you can discuss calmly.

How do I price an item with a defect?

Describe the defect clearly, consider how it affects use, and lower your estimate accordingly. Do not present a damaged or untested item as fully working.
