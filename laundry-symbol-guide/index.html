Laundry Symbol Guide

Care labels use small symbols to tell you how to wash, dry and iron a garment. This guide explains each symbol group in plain words, with a finder and a printable chart. When in doubt, follow the label and choose the gentler option.

Every garment carries a care label, and every care label carries a row of small symbols. They look cryptic, but they follow a simple system: a tub for wash, a triangle for bleach, a square for dry, an iron for iron, and a circle for dry clean.

How to read a care label

Care labels sit inside a garment, usually at a side seam or the neck. The symbols run in a fixed order: wash, bleach, dry, iron, then dry clean. Read them left to right, and you have the full care routine for the garment.

Each symbol is a simple picture. A tub means wash, a triangle means bleach, a square means dry, an iron means iron, and a circle means dry clean. Dots inside a symbol mean heat: one dot is low, two is medium, three is high. A cross through any symbol means do not do that step.

If a symbol is unfamiliar, look it up in the finder above before you wash. A minute of checking is cheaper than a shrunken jumper.

Wash symbols in detail

The number inside the wash tub is the hottest water temperature in degrees Celsius. Thirty is cool, forty is warm, sixty is hot. Washing cooler than the label allows is safe; washing hotter is not.

Lines under the tub mean a gentler cycle. One line means a permanent-press cycle, two lines means a delicate cycle. A hand above the tub means hand wash only, in cool water, without wringing.

Dry symbols in detail

The square with a circle inside means tumble dry, and the dots show the heat. One dot is low, two dots medium, three dots high. A cross through the square means do not tumble dry at all.

Other drying symbols are about air drying. A curved line at the top of the square means line dry, and a horizontal line inside the square means dry flat. Dry flat is common for knitwear, which stretches out of shape on a line.

Iron and dry-clean symbols

The iron symbol works the same way as the others: dots show the heat, and a cross means do not iron. A crossed-out steam symbol means you may iron but without steam.

The circle is for the dry cleaner. The letters inside tell the cleaner which solvent to use, so you do not need to memorise them. A crossed-out circle means the garment must not be dry cleaned.

What to do when the label is missing or faded

Labels fade, fray and fall off. If you cannot read the label, wash the garment gently in cool water and let it dry flat. This is the safest option for almost everything except items that are clearly dry-clean-only, such as structured jackets and silk.

If the garment is valuable or you are unsure, ask a dry cleaner. They can usually tell from the fabric what it needs, and a quick question costs nothing.

Common mistakes

  • Washing hotter than the label allows, which shrinks wool and cotton.
  • Tumble drying a garment marked do not tumble dry.
  • Bleaching a coloured garment, which fades or spots the fabric.
  • Ironing on high heat without checking the dots.
  • Ignoring the dry-clean-only label and washing a structured garment.

Questions people ask

What does the tub symbol mean?

The tub stands for washing. The number inside is the hottest water temperature in degrees Celsius. Lines under the tub mean a gentler cycle, and a cross means do not wash at all.

What does the triangle mean on a care label?

The triangle stands for bleach. An empty triangle means any bleach is allowed, diagonal lines mean non-chlorine bleach only, and a cross means do not bleach.

How do I know if I can tumble dry something?

Look for the square with a circle inside. Dots show the heat: one dot is low, two dots medium, three dots high. A cross through the square means do not tumble dry.

What do the dots on the iron symbol mean?

The dots show the iron temperature. One dot is low heat, two dots medium, three dots high. A cross through the iron means do not iron the garment.

What should I do if the care label is missing?

Wash the garment gently in cool water and let it dry flat, or ask a dry cleaner. When in doubt, the gentler option is safer than the hotter one.
