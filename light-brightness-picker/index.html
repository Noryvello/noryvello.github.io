How the Lumen Estimate Works

The calculator multiplies room floor area by a selected target in lumens per square foot. The built-in targets are simplified planning assumptions for general room use: 8 for hallways, 10 for living rooms, 12 for dining areas, 15 for bedrooms, 20 for kitchens, and 30 for study or reading areas. It shows a range around the calculated midpoint.

Lumens describe the light output of a source. The amount reaching a work surface also depends on fixture design, mounting height, beam spread, shades, surface reflectance, and the number and placement of fixtures. Task lighting may be needed even when total room lumens appear sufficient.

Questions people ask

What are lumens?

Lumens measure the total visible light output from a light source. They are different from watts, which measure electrical power.

How many lumens does a living room need?

This guide uses a simplified planning target of 10 lumens per square foot for general living-room lighting, then displays an approximate range. Room finishes and fixture placement can change the result.

Do kitchens and study rooms need more light?

They often need brighter task lighting than relaxed ambient areas. This guide uses higher planning targets for kitchens and study or reading areas, but work-surface lighting should be planned separately.

Does this estimate account for dark walls or high ceilings?

No. Dark finishes, high ceilings, fixture efficiency, shades, and room layout can require more light than the simple area-based guide suggests.
