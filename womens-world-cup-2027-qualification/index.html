Women's World Cup 2027 Qualification

Thirty-two teams will play at the 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup in Brazil, and 29 of those places come through the confederations' own qualifying competitions, with the last three decided by a 10-team intercontinental playoff. This page explains how the places are shared out, lists the 18 teams that have qualified as of November 2026, and sets out what is still to be decided before qualification ends in February 2027.

The 2027 tournament is the 10th FIFA Women's World Cup. It runs from 24 June to 25 July 2027, hosted by Brazil across 8 host cities, with the final at the Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro on Sunday 25 July 2027. It is the first Women's World Cup staged in South America, and it keeps the 32-team field that was introduced at the previous edition.

Qualifying for a place at the tournament is a long process. Qualification began on 19 February 2025 and ends in February 2027, and 179 teams entered. That is far more teams than the 32 that will reach Brazil, so most of the teams that entered will not make it. Each confederation runs its own qualifying competition, and the places are shared out between the confederations in advance, so every team knows how many places its region is playing for.

The list of qualified teams on this page is a snapshot dated November 2026. Playoffs were still running when this page was written, so the list changes as they finish. Check FIFA for the latest picture before relying on it.

How the 32 places are shared out

The slot allocation for the 2027 Women's World Cup was approved by the FIFA Council on 10 December 2024. It gives 29 direct places to the six confederations, and the remaining 3 places go to the winners of a 10-team intercontinental playoff tournament. Added together, that makes the 32-team field.

UEFA has the largest share, with 11 direct places, which is more than a third of the whole field. The AFC has 6 places, while the CAF and CONCACAF have 4 each. CONMEBOL has 2 places plus the host place taken by Brazil, so South America sends 3 teams to the tournament in total. The OFC has a single direct place. The last 3 places are not given to any one confederation: they are decided by the intercontinental playoff, where 10 teams from different confederations compete for them.

Counting the direct places alone gives 29: 11 plus 6 plus 4 plus 4 plus 2 plus the host Brazil plus 1. Add the 3 playoff places and the total is 32. No confederation can send more teams than its allocation allows, which is why the qualifying competitions in each region matter so much: a team has to finish high enough in its own region's competition to take one of that region's places.

How qualification works

Each confederation runs its own qualifying competition. In some regions that competition is the continental championship, where the best-placed teams at the championship take the World Cup places. In other regions it is a set of qualifying groups, played over several international windows, with the group winners and best runners-up taking the places. Either way, the principle is the same: teams earn their places by finishing high enough in their own confederation's competition.

The places that are not filled directly are decided through playoffs. Some confederations run their own playoffs to settle their last places, and the intercontinental playoff settles the final 3 places in the field. Ten teams take part in the intercontinental playoff, and only 3 of them reach the World Cup, so it is the tightest route into the tournament. The intercontinental playoff is played across the November/December 2026 and February 2027 windows, and it is the last part of qualification to be completed.

Because qualification runs for two years, from 19 February 2025 to February 2027, the picture changes gradually. Some confederations finish their qualifying competitions early, so their teams are known well in advance. Others finish later, and their places are only settled in the final months. That is why any list of qualified teams needs a date on it: a list from one month can be out of date the next.

Teams that have qualified so far (as of November 2026)

As of November 2026, 18 of the 32 places have been filled. The teams that have qualified so far are:

Brazil qualified automatically as the host nation, without having to play in qualification. The AFC, CAF, CONMEBOL and OFC have all completed their direct qualifying, so every one of their places is already filled: 6 from the AFC, 4 from the CAF, 2 from CONMEBOL and 1 from the OFC. That accounts for 13 teams plus the host Brazil, which makes 14.

UEFA has filled 4 of its 11 places so far, with Denmark, France, Germany and Spain already through. That brings the total to 18 qualified teams, which leaves 14 places still open: 4 from CONCACAF, 7 more from UEFA, and the 3 places from the intercontinental playoff. So nearly half of the field was still to be decided as of November 2026.

What is still to be decided

Three competitions will settle the remaining 14 places. The first is the CONCACAF W Championship, which runs from 24 November to 5 December 2026 in Texas. Eight teams take part, and the 4 semifinalists qualify for the World Cup, so the championship fills all 4 of CONCACAF's places in a single tournament.

The second is the UEFA playoffs, which will decide the remaining 7 UEFA places. Four UEFA teams have already qualified, so the playoffs settle the rest of Europe's places. The third is the intercontinental playoff, where 10 teams compete for the last 3 places in the field, across the November/December 2026 and February 2027 windows.

Once those three competitions are finished, all 32 teams will be known and qualification will be complete. Qualification ends in February 2027, a few months before the tournament begins on 24 June 2027.

The tournament format

Once the 32 teams are known, they are split into 8 groups of 4. Each team plays the other three teams in its group, and the top 2 in each group reach the knockout rounds. From there it is a straight knockout: lose and you go home. In total the tournament has 64 matches.

The draw that decides which teams go into which group is not yet scheduled. The tournament itself runs from 24 June to 25 July 2027 across 8 host cities in Brazil, and the final is at the Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro on Sunday 25 July 2027.
