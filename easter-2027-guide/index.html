Easter 2027 Guide

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

Easter Sunday in 2027 falls on 28 March, with Good Friday on 26 March and Ash Wednesday on 10 February. This guide explains what Easter means, the key dates around it, the traditions that go with it, and how families celebrate the weekend.

Easter is one of the great festivals of the Christian year, celebrating the resurrection of Jesus Christ. It arrives in spring, when the days are lengthening and the first flowers are out, and the season carries that sense of new life in both its religious meaning and its family traditions. For many people, Easter is both a holy day and a family holiday: church in the morning, a meal together, and time with children hunting for eggs.

The date of Easter changes each year, which is why so many people search for it. In 2027, Easter Sunday falls on 28 March, and the days around it, from Ash Wednesday to Easter Monday, give the season its shape. This guide sets out the key dates, explains what Easter means, and offers ideas for celebrating the weekend well.

The dates here are for Western Easter, followed by most churches in the West. Some churches follow a different calendar and celebrate Easter on another date, so check with your own church if you are unsure. This guide is general information about the festival and its traditions.

Easter 2027: the key dates

Easter Sunday in 2027 is 28 March. The days around it matter too: Ash Wednesday, which opens the season of Lent, falls on 10 February, and Good Friday, the day of the crucifixion, falls on 26 March. The Saturday between Good Friday and Easter Sunday is Holy Saturday, a quiet day of waiting, and the Monday after Easter Sunday is Easter Monday, a public holiday in many countries.

The date of Easter is set by the moon: it falls on the first Sunday after the first full moon on or after 21 March, which is why it moves between late March and late April each year. In 2027 it falls near the end of March, which means the Easter weekend arrives just as spring is properly under way.

What Easter means

For Christians, Easter celebrates the resurrection of Jesus Christ, three days after his crucifixion. It is the most important festival in the Christian year, and the heart of the Christian faith: the belief that death was overcome and new life began. Churches mark the day with joyful services, music, and flowers, often after the quiet and solemn days of Holy Week.

The season leading up to Easter is Lent, a period of forty days of reflection and preparation that begins on Ash Wednesday. Many people give something up for Lent or take up a new practice, such as daily prayer or giving to charity. Holy Week, the last week of Lent, follows the final days of Jesus, from Palm Sunday through Good Friday to Easter itself.

Lent and Holy Week in brief

Lent begins on Ash Wednesday, 10 February in 2027, when many churches hold services at which ash is placed on the forehead as a sign of repentance. The forty days of Lent are a time for slowing down, reflecting, and preparing for Easter. Some people fast or give up a favourite food, while others focus on prayer, reading, or helping others.

Holy Week is the final week before Easter. Palm Sunday remembers the entry of Jesus into Jerusalem, and the days that follow trace the Last Supper, the crucifixion on Good Friday, and the quiet of Holy Saturday. Many churches hold special services through the week, and the Easter Vigil on Saturday night is one of the most beautiful services of the year, ending in the joy of Easter morning.

Easter traditions around the world

Easter is celebrated in many ways around the world, and the traditions vary from place to place. In many countries, eggs are the great symbol of Easter: decorated, hidden, rolled, or exchanged as gifts. Egg hunts are a favourite with children almost everywhere, and chocolate eggs have become a much-loved part of the celebration.

Food is a big part of Easter too. Hot cross buns, marked with a cross, are eaten on Good Friday in Britain and beyond. Lamb is a traditional Easter dish in many countries, and special breads, cakes, and pastries appear in bakeries for the season. In some places, Easter Monday is a day for outdoor gatherings, games, and egg rolling, while in others the weekend is a quiet family time.

Spring itself is part of the celebration. Daffodils, tulips, and blossom appear on tables and in churches, and many families use the weekend for a walk outdoors. The season and the festival go together: after the dark of winter, Easter arrives with light, colour, and new growth.

How families celebrate at home

For many families, Easter at home begins with church in the morning and continues with a meal together. Children wake to Easter baskets or an egg hunt in the garden, and the day unfolds at a gentle pace: cooking, eating, and time together. If you have young children, an egg hunt is easy to arrange: hide chocolate eggs around the garden or the house, give each child a basket, and let them search.

Decorating eggs is a simple activity that children of all ages enjoy. Hard-boiled eggs can be dyed with food colouring or decorated with pens, and the finished eggs make a cheerful table decoration. If you are hosting, set the table with spring flowers and a few chocolate eggs, and let the children help with the laying of the table.

Easter food and the family table

The Easter meal is often a roast, shared with the whole family. Roast lamb is the classic choice, served with spring vegetables and potatoes, though many families choose chicken, beef, or a vegetarian centrepiece instead. Start with something light, such as soup or a salad, and finish with a seasonal dessert: a fruit tart, a sponge cake, or a traditional Easter cake.

Hot cross buns belong to Good Friday, but they are good all weekend, toasted and buttered. For Easter Sunday breakfast, many families enjoy a special spread: pastries, fruit, and eggs cooked any way they like. Keep the cooking simple where you can, so the cook can enjoy the day as well.

If you are feeding a crowd, a one-pot dish or a large roast keeps the cooking manageable. Serve the food in the middle of the table so guests can help themselves, and keep a jug of water and a pot of tea within reach. A relaxed table matters more than a perfect menu.

Planning your Easter weekend

The Easter weekend runs from Good Friday to Easter Monday, giving most people a four-day break. Plan the days loosely: church or a quiet morning on Good Friday, preparations and an egg hunt on Saturday, the main celebration on Sunday, and a relaxed day out or a rest on Monday. If you are travelling, book early, as Easter is a popular time for short breaks.

If you are hosting, write a short plan for the weekend: what you will cook, who is coming, and what the children will do. Buy chocolate eggs and decorations in advance, and prepare what you can the day before. The best Easter weekends are not the busiest ones: a little time together, a good meal, and a garden full of children are enough.

Leave room in the plan for rest. A four-day weekend is a gift, and it is easy to fill it so full that Monday feels like a recovery day. One outing, one good meal, and plenty of unscheduled time together will leave everyone refreshed rather than worn out.

When is Good Friday in 2027?

Good Friday in 2027 falls on 26 March, two days before Easter Sunday. It is the day Christians remember the crucifixion of Jesus, and it is a public holiday in many countries.

What is Ash Wednesday?

Ash Wednesday opens the season of Lent, forty days of reflection and preparation before Easter. In 2027 it falls on 10 February. Many churches hold services at which ash is placed on the forehead.

What are the most common Easter traditions?

How long is the Easter weekend?
