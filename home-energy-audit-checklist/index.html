    How to use this checklist

    Walk through your home room by room with this page open. Tick every problem you spot — the counter keeps track, and the findings summary below the checklist turns your ticks into a short list of actions, in the order the checklist lists them. Start at the top of the findings list and work down.

    The five areas

    The checklist covers five areas of the home: heating and cooling, lights, drafts and windows, appliances, and water heating. Each area has four common problems to look for. If an item does not apply to your home — for example, you have no air conditioning — just leave it unticked.

    After your audit

    Once you have your findings list, pick one or two actions to start with. Small changes you actually do beat a long list you never start. Come back to the checklist after a few weeks and untick the problems you have fixed — they will drop off your findings list.

    Questions people ask

    How do I do a home energy audit?

    Walk through your home room by room and look for the problems on this checklist: heating and cooling, lights, drafts and windows, appliances, and water heating. Tick each problem you find, then work through the findings at the top of your list first. A simple self-check like this one needs no tools and no expert knowledge.

    What wastes the most energy at home?

    It depends on the home, but the usual suspects are heating and cooling, hot water, and appliances left running when nobody is using them. Drafts around doors and windows also let heated or cooled air escape. The checklist above helps you find which of these apply to your own home.

    How can I find drafts in my home?

    On a windy day, hold a hand near doors and window frames and feel for moving air. You can also watch a lit candle or a thin strip of paper near the frame — if the flame or paper moves, air is getting through. Check the front door, back door, and any window that does not close tightly.

    What should I check first in an energy audit?

    Start with the easy ones: lights left on in empty rooms, appliances on standby, and drafts around doors and windows. Then look at heating and cooling, and finish with water heating. The findings summary on this page lists your checked problems in order, so you can start at the top.
