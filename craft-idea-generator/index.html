Setting up a craft session

Cover the table, lay out the materials and read the steps through before you begin. A few minutes of preparation saves a lot of tidying up later, and it lets everyone start making straight away.

For children, keep the steps short and let them make choices, such as colours and decorations. A craft that looks like the maker's own work is worth more than a perfect copy of an example.

Craft safely. Keep scissors, hot glue and sharp tools with adults or older children, protect tables and clothes, and check that paints and glues are safe for the age group using them.

Making crafts last

Let glue and paint dry fully before you move a finished piece. Thin layers dry faster and hold better than thick ones, and a clear varnish or sealant can protect paint on wood and clay.

Photograph anything fragile or short-lived. Paper crafts fade and clay can chip, but a photo keeps the memory. Store finished crafts in a box with tissue paper between them.

Questions people ask

How many craft ideas are in the bank?

Thirty original ideas, covering paper, fabric, clay, wood, recycled materials and paint, in quick, medium and long time bands.

What does each idea include?

Each idea gives you a short steps outline and a materials list, so you can check you have everything before you start.

Can I swap materials?

Yes. Most ideas work with a substitute, such as card instead of wood, or yarn instead of ribbon. The steps stay the same.

Which ideas suit young children?

Pick the quick time band and simple materials such as paper, paint or recycled items. Keep cutting and gluing to adults for the youngest crafters.

How do I display finished crafts?

A washing line with pegs, a shelf, or a photo frame all work well. Photograph anything fragile so the memory lasts even if the craft does not.
