Eid al-Adha 2027 Travel Guide

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

Eid al-Adha in 2027 is expected on Sunday 16 May, subject to the sighting of the moon. This guide helps you plan the journey home, with advice on booking early, travelling by road or by air, and making the trip comfortable for the whole family.

For many families, Eid al-Adha means going home: to parents, to the town where you grew up, or to the house where the whole family gathers. The journey is part of the celebration, but it is also one of the busiest travel periods of the year. Roads fill up, flights sell out, and a little planning makes the difference between a relaxed trip and a stressful one.

The key is to plan early and to plan around the date. Because the date of Eid depends on the moon, it can shift by a day, so flexible bookings and a little slack in your plans are worth more than a rigid schedule. This guide covers booking, road and flight travel, and travelling with children and elders, so that the journey feels like part of the holiday rather than a hurdle before it.

The date of Eid al-Adha depends on the sighting of the moon, so Sunday 16 May 2027 is expected rather than certain. Travel costs change quickly around Eid: check prices with the official seller or airline before you book, and read the change and cancellation terms before you pay.

Why booking early matters

Eid is one of the busiest travel times of the year, and the best options go first. Flights on the days before Eid sell out early, and the most convenient road routes fill with traffic. Booking early usually gives you more choice: better departure times, seats together for the family, and a wider range of options if your first choice is full.

Early booking also gives you time to plan the rest of the trip. Once the journey is fixed, you can arrange who you will visit, what you will bring, and where everyone will sleep. If you are travelling with children or elders, that extra planning time matters even more, because their comfort depends on the details.

Booking early also lets you compare options calmly instead of rushing. Look at departure times, journey length, and how many changes a trip involves, not just the headline option. A slightly longer journey that leaves at a comfortable hour is often better for a family than a faster one that starts before dawn.

Planning around the expected date

Eid al-Adha in 2027 is expected on Sunday 16 May, but the date is confirmed only when the moon is sighted, usually a day or two before. That means your travel dates may need to move by a day. Where you can, choose bookings that allow changes without a heavy penalty, and avoid plans that depend on a single exact day.

A practical approach is to travel a day or two before the expected date, so that a shift in either direction still leaves you home in time. If you must travel on a fixed day, check the terms carefully before you book, and keep an eye on mosque announcements in the days before Eid. Let the family know your plans and the possible change, so nobody is surprised if you arrive a day early or late.

Flying home for Eid

If you are flying, book as early as you can and compare departure times, not just routes. Early morning flights are often less crowded and less likely to be delayed by the build-up of the day, while evening flights can be busy with fellow travellers heading home. Check baggage rules in advance, especially if you are carrying gifts or food, and confirm what can travel in hand luggage.

Arrive at the airport earlier than usual: Eid travel means longer queues at check-in and security. Keep essentials in your hand luggage, including water, snacks for children, and anything you need for the first hours after landing. If your flight is delayed, a little patience and a charged phone make the wait easier.

Driving home for Eid

Driving gives you freedom: you leave when you want, carry what you like, and stop when you need to. It also means traffic, so plan your departure around it. Leaving very early in the morning or later in the evening often beats the worst of the congestion, and a quick check of traffic before you set off can save hours.

Prepare the car before a long drive: check the fuel, tyres, and water, and pack a small kit with water, snacks, a first aid box, and a phone charger. Plan your stops in advance, especially with children, and share the driving if you can. If the journey is long, consider breaking it with an overnight stop rather than driving through the night.

Keep the car comfortable for the journey. A little music, a playlist of something the family enjoys, and regular fresh air make a long drive pleasant. Agree on the route before you leave so there is no debate on the road, and keep a paper note of the destination address in case your phone runs out of charge.

Travelling with children and elders

Children make a journey livelier and a little more complicated. Pack snacks, water, and something to keep them busy, and plan regular stops so they can stretch their legs. Explain the journey to them in advance, including how long it will take and what will happen when you arrive, so they know what to expect.

Elders may need extra care: a comfortable seat, regular breaks, and easy access to water and any medicines they take. If an elder traveller has difficulty walking, think about the journey in stages and allow extra time at every step. Travelling together is worth the effort: Eid is a family occasion, and having everyone together is the point of the trip.

Packing for an Eid trip

Pack for the celebration as well as the journey. Eid clothes, gifts for family, and anything you have promised to bring should be packed carefully, with fragile items protected. Keep a change of clothes and essentials in a separate bag that stays with you, so a delay or a lost bag does not leave you stuck.

If you are bringing food, check what travels well and what the rules allow, especially on flights. Cooked dishes should be packed in sealed containers, and anything perishable should travel in a cool bag. Keep gifts simple to carry, and remember that the best gift is often your presence rather than a parcel.

Keep your travel documents and booking details together in one place, whether on your phone or in a small folder. A quick photo of your booking confirmation and your destination address can save a difficult moment if your phone battery dies or your signal drops on the road.

If plans change

Travel plans change, especially around Eid. A flight can be delayed, a road can close, or the date can shift by a day. The best defence is flexibility: bookings you can change, a spare day in your schedule, and a calm approach when things go wrong. Keep the phone numbers of your airline or transport provider handy, and know where to check for updates.

If you cannot make it home, do not let the distance spoil the day. Call your family, join a video call during the feast, and plan a visit for soon after. Eid is about connection as much as location, and a thoughtful call can carry much of the warmth of being there.

Questions people ask

When is Eid al-Adha in 2027?

Eid al-Adha in 2027 is expected on Sunday 16 May, but the date depends on the sighting of the moon. Check with your local mosque for the confirmed date before you finalise travel plans.

How early should I book travel for Eid?

As early as you can. Eid is one of the busiest travel periods of the year, and flights and convenient routes fill up quickly. Early booking gives you more choice of times and seats, and more time to plan the rest of the trip.

What if the date of Eid moves by a day?

The date can shift by a day when the moon is sighted, so choose bookings that allow changes where possible and travel a day or two before the expected date. Keep an eye on mosque announcements in the days before Eid.

Is it better to drive or fly home for Eid?

It depends on the distance, the number of travellers, and your budget. Driving gives you freedom and room for luggage, while flying saves time on long journeys. Compare the full journey, including travel to and from the airport, before you decide.

What should I pack for travelling with children?

Pack snacks, water, and something to keep children busy, plus a change of clothes and any essentials they need. Plan regular stops on a road journey, and keep the things children need most in a bag that stays with you.

What should I do if my travel plans fall through?

Stay flexible: know how to change your booking and keep your transport provider details handy. If you cannot travel, call your family and join the celebration by video, then plan a visit for soon after.
