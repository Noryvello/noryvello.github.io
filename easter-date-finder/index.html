How the date is calculated

Easter is the first Sunday after the first full moon on or after 21 March, using the Church's ecclesiastical calendar rather than the astronomical moon. The Computus algorithm reproduces that calculation, which is why Easter can fall anywhere between 22 March and 25 April.

Questions people ask

How is the Easter date calculated?

Easter is the first Sunday after the first full moon on or after 21 March, using the Church's ecclesiastical calendar rather than the astronomical moon. The Computus algorithm in this tool reproduces that calculation for the Gregorian calendar.

Can I use this tool for any year?

The tool works for years from 1583 onward, when the Gregorian calendar began. Earlier years used the Julian calendar and need a different calculation.

Why does Easter change every year?

Easter moves because it is tied to the moon: the first Sunday after the first full moon following the spring equinox. That Sunday can fall anywhere between 22 March and 25 April.

Is the date the same for all Christian denominations?

Most Western churches use the Gregorian calculation. Many Orthodox churches follow the Julian calendar, which can give a different date — often one to five weeks later. In 2027, Orthodox Easter falls on 2 May.
