Website Launch Checklist

A website launch goes smoothly when the checks happen before the announcement, not after. This checklist covers content, links, mobile, speed, search and legal pointers so you can launch with confidence.

Most launch-day problems are small things nobody checked: a link that still points to a test address, a page that looks fine on a laptop but breaks on a phone, a form that nobody ever submitted. Each one is easy to fix before launch and embarrassing after it.

This checklist is organised into six groups so you can work through it in stages. Start with content and links, then check mobile and speed, then search and legal pointers. Tick each item as you finish it, and do not announce the launch until the list is done.

General guidance only. The legal pointers here are reminders to check, not legal advice. For anything binding, such as privacy wording or terms, ask a qualified professional.

Why a launch checklist matters

A launch is a promise to your visitors. When someone arrives for the first time, they judge the whole site by what they see in the first minute. A broken link or a page full of placeholder text tells them the site is not ready, and many will not come back to check again. A checklist is how you make sure the promise holds.

The checklist also protects you from the rush of launch week. When several people are editing at once, it is easy to assume someone else checked the forms or the mobile layout. A shared list with ticks makes it clear what is done and what is still open.

After you launch

Launching is the start, not the end. In the first week, check the site daily: look for error pages, read any messages from visitors, and watch how the busiest pages behave under real traffic. Keep a short list of fixes and work through it in order of impact, not in order of complaint.

It is also worth keeping this checklist for the next launch. Sites change, and a redesign or a new section deserves the same checks as the first launch. Save a copy of your completed list so you know what good looked like last time.

Questions people ask

How long before launch should I start checking?

Start the checks at least a week before you plan to announce, so there is time to fix what you find. Content and link checks can begin as soon as the pages exist, while speed and mobile checks are best done on the finished site.

What is the most common launch mistake?

Leaving test content or test addresses on the live site is the classic one: placeholder text, sample images, or links that still point to a staging copy. Reading every page once on the live address catches most of it.

Do I need a privacy page before launch?

If your site collects any personal details, even through a simple contact form, visitors expect to find a privacy page that says what you collect and why. Write it before launch rather than adding it afterwards.

How do I check my site on a phone?

Open the live site on a real phone and use it as a visitor would: tap the menu, fill in the form, read a full page. A resized desktop window is useful, but it does not show how the site feels under a thumb.

What should I do on launch day?

Announce once the checklist is complete, then watch the site for a few hours: check the forms, read messages, and fix anything urgent. Leave bigger improvements for the days after, when you can see what visitors actually use.
