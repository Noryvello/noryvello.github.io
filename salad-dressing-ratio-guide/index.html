Salad Dressing Ratio Guide

Pick a dressing style and a batch size and this guide gives you the oil, acid and seasoning amounts for that dressing. Six flavour variations and storage notes are included, so one ratio turns into many dressings.

How to use this guide

Choose a dressing style and a batch size, then press the button. You will get the amounts for each ingredient, the ratio the dressing is built on, and a storage note. The classic vinaigrette ratio is 3 parts oil to 1 part acid, and most of the styles here build on that ratio with different oils, acids and seasonings.

To mix a dressing, put the acid, salt and mustard in a jar or bowl first and stir until the salt dissolves. Then add the oil slowly while whisking, or put the lid on the jar and shake hard. Taste it and adjust: too sharp means more oil, too flat means more acid or salt, too plain means more seasoning.

The 3 to 1 ratio and how to bend it

The 3 to 1 ratio of oil to acid is a starting point, not a law. Some vinegars are sharper than others, and some oils are milder, so the same ratio can taste different from one bottle to the next. If the dressing tastes too sharp, add a little more oil. If it tastes flat or greasy, add a little more acid or a pinch more salt. A small spoonful of mustard does two jobs at once: it adds flavour and it helps the oil and acid hold together.

Sweetness is the other lever. A little honey, maple syrup or sugar rounds off a sharp dressing and balances bitter leaves such as rocket or chicory. Add it a little at a time and taste as you go, because a dressing that is too sweet is hard to fix. A pinch of salt is essential in every dressing, because it wakes up the flavours of both the oil and the acid.

Six flavour variations

Once you know the ratio, one dressing becomes many. Here are six variations to try, each starting from the classic vinaigrette amounts:

  • Garlic and herb: add 1 crushed garlic clove and 1 tablespoon of chopped fresh herbs such as parsley, dill or chives.
  • Chilli and lime: use lime juice as the acid and add 1 teaspoon of chilli flakes and a pinch of sugar.
  • Tahini: replace half the oil with tahini and add a squeeze of lemon and a clove of garlic for a nutty, creamy dressing.
  • Balsamic: use balsamic vinegar as the acid and add 1 teaspoon of honey for a sweet, dark dressing.
  • Mustard: double the mustard and use red wine vinegar for a sharp dressing that suits potatoes and beans.
  • Ginger and soy: use rice vinegar as the acid, add 1 tablespoon of soy sauce and 1 teaspoon of grated ginger.

These variations work with any batch size: keep the oil and acid amounts from the builder and add the extras on top. Taste before serving and adjust the salt, because soy sauce and tahini both add saltiness of their own.

This page is general cooking guidance. Dressings with yoghurt, cream or fresh ingredients should be kept in the fridge and used within 3 to 4 days. Plain oil and vinegar dressings keep longer, but throw any dressing away if it smells off or looks unusual.

Storing homemade dressing

Store dressing in a sealed jar in the fridge. A plain oil and vinegar dressing keeps for about 2 weeks, because the acid helps preserve it. Dressings with yoghurt, cream, fresh garlic or fresh herbs keep for 3 to 4 days, so make those in small batches and use them quickly. Olive oil can thicken and cloud in the fridge; that is normal, and it clears again at room temperature.

Shake or whisk the dressing well before each use, because oil and vinegar separate when they sit. If a dressing has been in the fridge for a while, taste it before serving: the flavour can sharpen over time as the acid works on the other ingredients. A little extra oil or a pinch of sugar will bring it back into balance.

Questions people ask

What is the classic vinaigrette ratio?

The classic vinaigrette ratio is 3 parts oil to 1 part acid, plus salt and a little mustard to help it hold together. For a small batch of about 120 ml that is 90 ml oil and 30 ml vinegar or lemon juice.

Why does my dressing separate?

Oil and vinegar do not mix on their own, so a dressing separates when it sits. A teaspoon of mustard helps it hold together longer, and a good shake or whisk just before serving brings it back together.

How long does homemade dressing keep?

A plain oil and vinegar dressing keeps in the fridge for about 2 weeks in a sealed jar. Dressings with yoghurt, cream or fresh ingredients keep for 3 to 4 days, so make those in small batches.

Can I use lemon juice instead of vinegar?

Yes. Lemon juice works in the same ratio as vinegar and gives a fresher, lighter flavour. Lime juice works too, and it suits Asian-style and Mexican-style salads especially well.

How much dressing does a salad need?

Less than most people pour. Start with about 2 tablespoons for a salad for two people, toss well, and add more only if the leaves still look dry. The leaves should glisten, not swim.
