Work From Home vs Office Cost

This page compares the monthly cost of working from home with the monthly cost of working in an office, using your own numbers. Enter what you spend on commuting, food, clothing and home energy under each pattern, and the calculator shows both monthly totals side by side.

How the comparison works

Working in an office and working from home move money around rather than simply saving it: the commute disappears at home, but the heating, lighting and cooking move with you. The calculator adds up each pattern separately. The office total is your monthly commute plus food plus clothing. The home total is your extra home energy plus food plus clothing at home. Both totals use only the numbers you enter, so the comparison reflects your own journey, appetite and wardrobe rather than anyone else's. The steps under the result show both sums and the yearly figure for each, so you can see how a small monthly gap grows over a year.

This page compares monthly money cost only. It does not include one-time home office setup, such as a desk or chair, and it cannot weigh time, focus or social contact. If you split your week between home and office, enter the costs of a typical month as it is now.

How to use the result

Start with the figures you know best, then adjust the ones you guessed. The commute is usually the largest office cost, so if you are unsure of it, add up a week's fares or fuel and multiply by the weeks you work in a month. For home energy, enter only the extra cost of working from home, not your whole household bill: the heating and lights you would use anyway do not belong in the comparison. If the two monthly totals are close, look at the yearly figures in the steps, because a small monthly difference still adds up over twelve months.

What the numbers do not show

Money is only part of the picture. The time spent commuting is not in either total, and neither is the value of a quiet room at home or the company of colleagues in an office. Some costs sit outside both patterns, such as childcare, which may change with where you work but is too personal for a simple comparison. Use the result to understand where your money goes, then weigh the rest yourself: the cheaper pattern on paper is not always the one that suits your working day.

Questions people ask

What if I work some days at home and some in the office?

Enter the costs of a typical month as it is now, or run the calculator twice: once for a full office month and once for a full home month, then compare the two.

Should home energy include heating and cooling?

Yes. Enter the extra home energy you use because you work from home, such as heating, cooling, lighting and charging, for a typical month.

What counts as clothing cost?

Enter what you spend on work clothes in a typical month for each pattern. Many people spend less when working from home, but only your own figure tells the story.

Does the calculator include a home office desk or chair?

No. One-time setup costs are not part of a monthly comparison. If you want to include them, spread the cost over the months you expect to use the item and add it to the home energy box.

Does this page say working from home is better?

No. It only compares monthly money cost. Time, focus, comfort and social contact matter too, and they are personal.
