FIFA World Cup Winners List

This page lists every men's FIFA World Cup final from 1930 to 2026, with the winner, the runner-up and the score. Spain are the current champions after beating Argentina 1–0 after extra time in the 2026 final.

How to read the table

The score column shows the result of the final match. Where a final was level after ninety minutes, the table says "after extra time" and gives the score at the end of extra time. Where a final was still level after extra time, the score shows the goals first and then the penalty shoot-out result in brackets. For example, the 1994 final ended 0–0 and Brazil won the shoot-out 3–2, so the row reads "0–0 (Brazil won 3–2 on penalties)".

West Germany appears as the winner in 1954, 1974 and 1990, and as the runner-up in 1966, 1982 and 1986. Those results count towards Germany's record, since the team continued as Germany after reunification. The titles summary below groups them together under Germany.

The 1950 tournament

The 1950 World Cup is the only edition that ended without a final match. Instead of a knockout final, the last four teams played a round-robin group, and the team finishing top of the group won the tournament. Uruguay won the group, and their 2–1 win over Brazil in the last match of the group decided the title. The table shows that decisive match, and the note in the score column makes clear that it was a round-robin, not a final.

The 2026 final

The most recent final was played on 19 July 2026 at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford. Spain beat Argentina 1–0 after extra time, with Ferran Torres scoring the only goal in the 106th minute. The win gave Spain their second World Cup title, after their first in 2010. Argentina, the defending champions from 2022, finished as runners-up.

Titles summary

Eight teams have won the World Cup across the twenty-three editions, and Brazil lead the way with five titles. The full summary is:

  • Brazil — 5 titles (1958, 1962, 1970, 1994, 2002)
  • Germany — 4 titles (1954, 1974, 1990, 2014; the first three as West Germany)
  • Italy — 4 titles (1934, 1938, 1982, 2006)
  • Argentina — 3 titles (1978, 1986, 2022)
  • France — 2 titles (1998, 2018)
  • Uruguay — 2 titles (1930, 1950)
  • Spain — 2 titles (2010, 2026)
  • England — 1 title (1966)

Brazil are the only team to have won five titles, and the only team to have appeared in every edition of the tournament. Uruguay won the first two tournaments they entered, in 1930 and 1950, and have not won since. Spain's two titles both came in the modern era, sixteen years apart. England's single title came on home soil in 1966.

Finals that needed more than ninety minutes

Most World Cup finals have been settled inside ninety minutes, but nine of the twenty-three needed more. Six were decided in extra time: Italy beat Czechoslovakia 2–1 in 1934, England beat West Germany 4–2 in 1966, Argentina beat the Netherlands 3–1 in 1978, Spain beat the Netherlands 1–0 in 2010, Germany beat Argentina 1–0 in 2014, and Spain beat Argentina 1–0 in 2026. Three more went to a penalty shoot-out: Brazil beat Italy in 1994, Italy beat France in 2006, and Argentina beat France in 2022.

The three shoot-out finals share a pattern worth noting. In each case the score was level after extra time, so the trophy was decided by penalties alone. The 1994 final was the first to end goalless, and the 2022 final was the highest scoring of the three, with six goals shared before the shoot-out. Extra time and penalties are part of what makes a final memorable, and the table above shows exactly which finals needed them.

Scores and winners here follow the FIFA record for the men's tournament. The 1950 edition had no final match — Uruguay won a round-robin group. This page covers the men's finals only.

Questions people ask

Who has won the most World Cups?

Brazil, with five titles in 1958, 1962, 1970, 1994 and 2002. Germany and Italy follow with four titles each.

Who won the 2026 World Cup?

Spain beat Argentina 1–0 after extra time in the final on 19 July 2026 at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, with Ferran Torres scoring in the 106th minute. It was Spain's second title.

Why is there no final score for 1950?

The 1950 tournament ended with a final round-robin group instead of a single final match. Uruguay won the group, and their 2–1 win over Brazil in the last match decided the title.

How many teams have won the World Cup?

Eight teams have won it across twenty-three editions: Brazil, Germany, Italy, Argentina, France, Uruguay, Spain and England.

What does after extra time mean?

It means the final was level after ninety minutes, so the teams played thirty minutes of extra time. The score shown is the score at the end of extra time.
