Women's World Cup 2027 Knockout Stage Guide

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

The knockout stage of the Women's World Cup 2027 begins after the group stage, when the top two teams from each of the eight groups reach the round of 16. From there it is single elimination all the way to the final at the Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro on 25 July 2027.

The Women's World Cup 2027 runs from 24 June to 25 July 2027 in Brazil, with 32 teams playing 64 matches across eight cities. The first 48 matches are the group stage; the last 16 are the knockouts, where every match must produce a winner and one bad day ends a team's tournament.

How the tournament is structured

The tournament has two parts. First comes the group stage: 32 teams divided into eight groups of four, with every team playing the other three teams in its group once. Each group therefore plays six matches, and eight groups times six matches gives 48 group matches. The top two teams in each group advance, which means 16 teams reach the knockout stage.

The knockout stage is then a simple single-elimination bracket. The round of 16 has eight matches, the quarter-finals have four, the semi-finals have two, and then there is a third-place match and the final. Eight plus four plus two plus one plus one is 16 knockout matches, and 48 group matches plus 16 knockout matches makes the tournament total of 64. FIFA published the 64-match schedule framework on 20 August 2026, setting the dates and venues for each match slot.

The round of 16

The round of 16 is where the tournament changes character. In the group stage a team can lose a match and still qualify; in the knockouts there is no second chance. Sixteen teams play eight ties, and the winners go through to the quarter-finals. The schedule framework sets which group positions meet in each round-of-16 tie, but the actual teams in those ties depend on the December 2026 draw and on how the group stage plays out.

This round often produces the tournament's most dramatic matches. A group winner that cruised through its group can meet a strong runner-up that finished second in a hard group, and the gap between the two teams is often much smaller than the group tables suggest. It is also the round where the host nation's hopes are most exposed: one off night, and the party ends early.

The quarter-finals

Eight teams reach the quarter-finals, and four matches decide the semi-finalists. By this stage every team left is a serious contender, and the margins are fine. Quarter-finals are often decided by a single moment: a set piece, a goalkeeping error, a flash of skill from a star player, or a penalty shootout after two teams cannot be separated.

For fans planning to attend, the quarter-finals are spread across the host cities in the schedule framework, with dates and venues set per match slot. Kick-off times have not been published yet, so travel plans should stay flexible until FIFA confirms them. The eight host cities are Belo Horizonte, Brasília, Fortaleza, Porto Alegre, Recife, Rio de Janeiro, Salvador and São Paulo, and all eight also staged matches at the 2014 men's World Cup.

The semi-finals and the third-place match

The two semi-finals decide who plays for the trophy and who plays for third place. Semi-finals carry their own pressure: winning one puts a team ninety minutes from a World Cup title, while losing one means the final is out of reach but a medal is still to be won. The losing semi-finalists meet in the third-place match, a fixture that matters more than it is sometimes given credit for, because third place at a World Cup is a proud finish for any team.

The semi-finals and the final are the matches that draw the biggest audiences, and they are the hardest tickets to get. Tickets for the tournament are not yet on general sale, and no prices have been published. Anyone planning to attend should follow the official tournament channels for ticketing news rather than trusting unofficial sellers.

The final at the Maracanã

The final takes place at the Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro on Sunday 25 July 2027. The Maracanã is one of the most famous stadiums in world football, and it also hosts the opening match of the tournament, when Brazil play there on 24 June 2027. A World Cup final at the Maracanã, with the possibility of the host nation involved, is the sort of occasion the whole sport stops to watch.

The final is the 64th and last match of the tournament. By then, the two finalists will each have played seven matches: three in the group stage and four in the knockouts. Fitness, squad depth and the ability to recover between matches all matter as much as talent by the final weekend, and the team that lifts the trophy is usually the one that managed its whole squad best across the month.

What the December 2026 draw decides

The final draw, scheduled for December 2026, is the moment the tournament takes shape. It decides which four teams go into each of the eight groups, and therefore which teams can meet in the round of 16, which half of the bracket the favourites land in, and which route each team must take to reach the final. Until the draw, all of that is guesswork, which is why this guide does not predict match-ups.

Some things are already fixed regardless of the draw. Brazil, as hosts, play their three group matches on 24 June at the Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro, 29 June at Arena Itaquera in São Paulo, and 4 July at Estádio Nacional in Brasília. Qualification is still running, with the last places decided by an inter-confederation play-off in March 2027, so even the full list of 32 teams is not yet final.

How to follow the knockout stage

The best source for confirmed information is FIFA's own tournament channels, which publish the schedule framework, the draw results and ticketing news as they are confirmed. The 64-match framework published on 20 August 2026 gives match dates and venues per slot, so fans can already see which cities host which rounds, even though the teams in each slot are not yet known.

Kick-off times are not yet published, and they matter for anyone planning travel or viewing parties, because matches in Brazil's different time zones and climates can feel very different at midday and in the evening. The sensible approach is to follow the draw in December 2026, note the dates and venues for the rounds you care about, and wait for kick-off times before booking anything that cannot be changed.

What the knockout rounds feel like

There is a particular feeling to knockout football that the group stage never quite matches. Every attack matters, every mistake is magnified, and a match can turn on a single moment: a save, a deflection, a shot from distance. The crowd knows it too, and the noise in a stadium during a tight knockout match is different from anything earlier in the tournament.

The drama comes from the stakes. In the group stage, a team can lose and recover; in the knockouts, one bad evening ends four years of preparation. That is why underdogs thrive in this part of the tournament: with nothing to lose, they can play freely, while favourites carry the weight of expectation. It is also why the knockout rounds produce the matches people remember for decades. The final at the Maracanã on 25 July 2027 will be the last of them, and the two teams that reach it will have earned their place the hard way.

Questions people ask

When does the knockout stage start?

The knockout stage begins after the group stage ends. Exact dates for each round are set in the 64-match schedule framework FIFA published on 20 August 2026, and the final is on 25 July 2027.

How many knockout matches are there?

Sixteen: eight in the round of 16, four quarter-finals, two semi-finals, the third-place match and the final. Added to the 48 group matches, that makes 64 matches in total.

Where is the final?

The final is at the Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro on Sunday 25 July 2027. The Maracanã also hosts the opening match, when Brazil play there on 24 June 2027.

What happens if a knockout match is level after 90 minutes?

Knockout matches must produce a winner on the night. The standard practice at a World Cup is extra time, and a penalty shootout if the teams are still level.

When will we know the knockout match-ups?

The final draw in December 2026 decides the groups, and the group results then decide which teams meet in the round of 16. The bracket fills in round by round as the tournament goes on.

Are kick-off times published yet?

No. FIFA has published the schedule framework with match dates and venues per slot, but kick-off times have not been published yet.
