How the absorption method works

Rinse the rice in a few changes of cold water until the water runs fairly clear, then drain it. Put the rice and the measured water in a pot with a tight lid, bring it to the boil, then turn the heat right down. Leave it covered until the water is absorbed, then take it off the heat and rest it with the lid on.

Adjusting to your own pot

A heavy pot with a tight lid loses less steam than a thin one, so you may need a little less water; a loose lid can mean a little more. If your rice comes out too wet, use slightly less water next time; if it is dry or undercooked, add a little more. Once you find the amount that works for your pot, keep using it.

Questions people ask

How much water do I need for 1 cup of rice?

It depends on the rice. For 1 cup of white long-grain or basmati rice you need 1.5 cups of water; jasmine needs 1.25 cups, brown rice needs 2 cups, and short-grain needs 1.2 cups. These amounts are for the absorption method with rinsed rice.

What is the rice to water ratio for basmati rice?

Basmati rice uses a ratio of 1 part rice to 1.5 parts water, so 1 cup of basmati needs 1.5 cups of water and 2 cups need 3 cups. Use the absorption method with rinsed rice, and let it rest for 10 minutes after cooking.

Should I rinse rice before cooking?

Yes, for the ratios on this page. Rinsing washes off extra surface starch, which helps the grains stay separate. Rinse the rice in a few changes of cold water until the water runs fairly clear, then drain it before cooking.

How long should rice rest after cooking?

Let rice rest for 10 minutes with the lid on after cooking, or 15 minutes for brown rice. Resting lets the steam finish the grains evenly, so fluff the rice with a fork only after the rest.
