The ratio rule

Every recipe on this page follows one rule: 1 part liquid to 1.5 parts fruit. So 1 cup of milk goes with 1.5 cups of fruit, and 2 cups of juice go with 3 cups of fruit. The rule works with any cup or glass as the measure, as long as you use the same one for the liquid and the fruit.

How to blend a smoothie

Pour the liquid in first, so the blades can move freely, then add the fruit and the extra. Blend until the drink looks smooth, stopping once to push down any fruit that sticks to the sides. If the drink is too thick to blend, add a splash more liquid and blend again.

Making it your own

Swap the fruits to change the flavour — banana and mango make a thick, sweet drink, while apple and kiwi make a lighter, sharper one. Frozen fruit blends into a thicker drink than fresh fruit, and water as the base lets the fruit flavour come through most. Try one change at a time until the recipe tastes the way you like it.

Questions people ask

What is the best liquid to fruit ratio for a smoothie?

A good starting point is 1 part liquid to 1.5 parts fruit — for example, 1 cup of milk with 1.5 cups of fruit. If the drink is too thick, add a little more liquid; if it is too thin, add more fruit.

Can I make a smoothie without a blender?

It is hard to get a smooth drink without a blender, because the fruit needs to be broken down fully. A food processor works for soft fruit, and a fork can mash a very ripe banana into milk or yogurt, but the result will be lumpier than a blended smoothie.

What can I add to a smoothie to make it filling?

Oats, peanut butter, yogurt and chia seeds all make a smoothie thicker and more filling. Add one spoonful at a time and blend again, so the drink stays smooth and does not get too thick.

How do I make a smoothie less sweet?

Use less of the sweetest fruit and more of a plainer one, such as apple or kiwi, and skip the honey. Water or plain yogurt as the base also keeps the sweetness down.
