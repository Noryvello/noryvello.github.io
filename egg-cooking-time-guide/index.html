Boiling times by egg size

Size is the main thing that changes boiling time: bigger eggs take longer for the heat to reach the centre. For a medium egg, a soft yolk takes around 6 minutes, a medium or jammy yolk around 8 to 9 minutes, and a hard yolk around 10 to 11 minutes, all timed from the moment the egg goes into already boiling water. Small eggs need roughly a minute less at each stage, while large and extra large eggs need roughly a minute more. These are guide ranges: eggs straight from the fridge, a pan at altitude, or a crowded pot can all shift the result, so use the same method each time and adjust by a minute if needed. Other ways to cook an egg

Boiling is only one method. A fried egg cooks in a minute or two on each side in a hot pan, a scrambled egg comes together in a few minutes over a gentle heat with regular stirring, and a poached egg sets in a few minutes in barely simmering water — never a rolling boil, which tears the white apart. An air fryer can cook eggs in their shells too, with no water at all.

Peeling made easier

Cooling the eggs in cold or ice water straight after cooking helps twice over: it stops the yolk cooking further, and it loosens the shell. Once cool, tap the egg gently all over to crack the shell finely, then peel from the wider end, where the air pocket sits. Peeling under a thin stream of running water can help lift stubborn bits of shell away.

Storage and food safety

Boiled eggs keep best in the fridge, in their shells, and are best eaten within a couple of days. Peeled eggs should go into a sealed container and be eaten sooner rather than later. Do not leave cooked eggs sitting at room temperature for hours — if they have been out for a long while, it is safer to throw them away than to guess. Should eggs go in cold or boiling water?

Either method works, but the times are not interchangeable. This guide assumes eggs lowered into already boiling water, which gives the most repeatable results. If you prefer starting in cold water, bring the pan to the boil and then time from that point — but expect to adjust the minutes, since the egg warms up gradually along the way.

Questions people ask

When should I start timing boiled eggs?

Start timing when the egg is placed into already boiling water, using the same method consistently.

Do larger eggs need more time?

Usually, larger eggs need more time than smaller eggs to reach a similar yolk texture.

Why cool eggs after boiling?

Cold or ice water helps stop carryover cooking and can make peeling easier for some eggs.

Are these times exact for every kitchen?

No. They are guide ranges; starting temperature, altitude, pan conditions, and egg size affect the result.
