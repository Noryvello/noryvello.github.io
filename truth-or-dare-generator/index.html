How to play

Truth or dare is a simple party game that works with any number of players, from two friends to a whole room. Sit together, decide who goes first, and take turns around the group.

  1. On your turn, choose truth or dare, then pick an intensity: easy, medium or spicy-but-clean.
  2. Read the prompt out loud. If you chose truth, answer the question honestly. If you chose dare, do the dare.
  3. If the prompt does not suit you, press Next for a different one, or switch between truth and dare.
  4. When your turn is done, pass to the next player and keep going for as long as everyone is having fun.

The safety rule

Every prompt on this page was written to be clean and safe for a mixed group, including families. No dare asks you to do anything dangerous, painful or embarrassing, and nothing here involves money, alcohol or anything a parent would object to. The truths are light and fun: they ask about favourite things, funny memories and silly what-ifs, never about private matters or anything that could shame someone.

Spicy-but-clean is the cheekiest level, but it is still fully clean. It means playful prompts such as talking in a robot voice until your next turn, or telling the group the silliest thing you ever said to a stranger. If a prompt ever feels too much for someone, the rule is simple: skip it, with no questions asked and no penalty.

Tips for a good game

  • Start on easy while everyone warms up, then move to medium or spicy-but-clean when the group is ready.
  • Let each player pick their own intensity on their turn, so nobody feels pushed.
  • Keep turns short. If a dare is taking too long, agree on a quick finish and move on.
  • Take breaks. A few rounds, a snack and a chat, then a few more rounds keeps the game fun.
  • Agree before you start that anyone can skip any prompt. A game is only fun when everyone is comfortable.

Questions people ask

Is this game safe for kids?

Yes. Every truth and dare on this page is clean and safe: there is no danger, pain, embarrassment, money or alcohol involved, and the truths are light and fun. Younger children may enjoy the easy level most, and an adult can look over the choices before play.

What does spicy-but-clean mean?

Spicy-but-clean means playful and cheeky, but still fully clean. A spicy dare might ask you to talk like a pirate until your next turn, and a spicy truth might ask about the silliest thing you ever said to a stranger. Nothing rude, risky or embarrassing is included.

How do you play truth or dare?

Take turns. On your turn, choose truth or dare and pick an intensity, then read out the prompt the generator gives you. If you chose truth, answer the question honestly; if you chose dare, do the dare. Then pass to the next player.

What if someone does not want to answer or do a dare?

They can skip it, no questions asked. Press Next for a different prompt, or let them switch from truth to dare, or the other way round. The game is only fun when everyone is comfortable, so never pressure anyone to answer or do something they do not want to.
