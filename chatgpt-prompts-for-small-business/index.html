30 ChatGPT Prompts for Small Business Owners

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

This page gives you 30 original ChatGPT prompts for small business owners, organised into five groups: marketing, customer replies, product descriptions, planning, and social posts. Every prompt comes with a one-line note on how to adapt it to your business.

Running a small business means writing all day: replies to customers, descriptions for products, posts for social media, and plans for the week ahead. ChatGPT can draft that writing in seconds, so you can spend your time on the work itself.

The prompts below are written for real small-business tasks. Fill in your product, your offer, or your situation, and the adaptation note under each prompt shows how to shape it to your shop, stall, or service.

Check every AI draft before it reaches a customer. ChatGPT can invent details about your products, promise things you cannot deliver, or use a tone that does not match your business. You know your customers: the AI drafts, and you decide what goes out.

How to Use These Prompts

Replace the words in square brackets with your own product, offer, or situation before you send a prompt. The more detail you give, the better the draft: naming your product and who it suits changes the answer far more than any clever wording.

Read every draft as if you were the customer receiving it. Fix the facts, cut the fluff, and rewrite anything that does not sound like you. A prompt that saves you twenty minutes of drafting is doing its job, even if you rewrite a third of what it gives you.

Marketing

Use these prompts to describe your business and reach new customers.

  1. "Write a short tagline for [business] that says what we do and who we serve, in under 10 words."
    Adapt it: name your business and the customers you serve.
  2. "Give me five headline ideas for a flyer about [offer], each aimed at a different customer worry."
    Adapt it: name your offer and the worries customers raise.
  3. "Help me describe [product] in one paragraph that focuses on the customer's problem, not the features."
    Adapt it: name your product and the problem it solves.
  4. "Suggest three low-cost ways to tell local customers about [new product or service]."
    Adapt it: name the new product or service.
  5. "Write a 50-word introduction for [business] I can use on a market stall sign."
    Adapt it: name your business and where the sign will go.
  6. "List five questions customers often ask about [product], with a short honest answer for each."
    Adapt it: name any product customers ask about.

Customer Replies

Use these prompts to draft replies that are quick, polite, and clear.

  1. "Draft a polite reply to a customer asking when [product] will be back in stock."
    Adapt it: name the product and the date you expect it.
  2. "Write a calm reply to an unhappy customer about [issue], apologising and offering a clear next step."
    Adapt it: describe the issue in general terms, without names.
  3. "Draft a thank-you reply for a customer who left a kind review."
    Adapt it: mention what the review praised.
  4. "Help me reply to a customer asking for a discount, without lowering the price."
    Adapt it: describe what you can offer instead, if anything.
  5. "Write a short reply explaining a delivery delay, with a new date and an apology."
    Adapt it: add the new date and the reason in general terms.
  6. "Draft a reply to a customer asking how [product] works, in simple everyday language."
    Adapt it: name the product and the part customers find confusing.

Product Descriptions

Use these prompts to write descriptions that help customers decide.

  1. "Write a product description for [item] in 60 words, naming who it suits and when to use it."
    Adapt it: name the item and change the length.
  2. "Turn this list of features into benefits a customer would care about."
    Adapt it: paste your own feature list.
  3. "Write three versions of a description for [item]: one playful, one plain, one premium."
    Adapt it: name the item and pick your own three tones.
  4. "Help me write a description for a handmade item that explains the time and care behind it."
    Adapt it: describe how the item is made.
  5. "Write a short description for [item] that answers the top three questions a buyer would ask."
    Adapt it: name the item and the three questions.
  6. "Rewrite this description so it sounds like a person talking, not a catalogue."
    Adapt it: paste your own description.

Planning

Use these prompts to organise your week and think ahead.

  1. "Help me list the five most important tasks for my business this week, in order."
    Adapt it: name your goals and the time you have.
  2. "Turn this rough goal into a simple monthly plan with one focus per week."
    Adapt it: state your goal in plain words.
  3. "Give me a checklist for preparing [event or market day], from setup to pack-up."
    Adapt it: name the event and the venue.
  4. "Help me compare two ideas for growing my business, listing the pros and cons of each."
    Adapt it: describe the two ideas.
  5. "Suggest a simple way to track which products sell best each week, using a plain notebook."
    Adapt it: describe how you sell and record sales now.
  6. "Help me plan a quiet month: three low-cost actions to keep customers coming back."
    Adapt it: name the month and your regular customers.

Social Posts

Use these prompts to keep your pages active without spending all day on them.

  1. "Write a short social post announcing [news], friendly and under 60 words."
    Adapt it: name the news and the platform.
  2. "Give me five post ideas for this week, one for each day, about [business]."
    Adapt it: name your business and the week.
  3. "Write a caption for a photo of [product] that tells a small story about how it is made."
    Adapt it: name the product and how it is made.
  4. "Draft a post asking customers a question about [topic] to start a conversation."
    Adapt it: name a topic your customers care about.
  5. "Write a post sharing a customer story, keeping the customer's details private."
    Adapt it: describe the story in general terms, without names.
  6. "Turn this announcement into three short posts I can share across the week."
    Adapt it: paste your announcement.

Checking Drafts Before Customers See Them

Before any AI draft goes out, read it with a customer's eyes. Are the facts right? ChatGPT can invent features, sizes, and delivery times, so check every claim against what you actually sell. Is the tone right? A playful caption suits some businesses and not others. Does it promise anything you cannot deliver? Cut any promise you are not sure you can keep.

It helps to keep a short list of facts the AI should use: your products, your hours, your delivery area. Paste those facts into the prompt and ask the AI to use only them. When the facts come from you, the draft stays honest, and the AI's job is simply to shape the words.

Making Prompts Your Own

The prompts on this page are starting points. As you use them, you will find the ones that fit your business, and you will start changing them: different lengths, different tones, different kinds of posts. Keep a note of the versions that work, organised by task, and you will soon have a small library that saves you real time every week.

The best test of a prompt is a customer's reaction. If a post gets replies, if a description brings questions, if a reply calms an unhappy customer, the prompt behind it is worth keeping. Build your library from what works in the real world, not from what sounds good on paper.

Questions people ask

Can ChatGPT write my product descriptions?

Yes, it can draft them from the details you give it. Always check the draft for invented features, wrong sizes, or promises you cannot keep, and rewrite it in your own voice before publishing.

How do I keep AI replies sounding like me?

Tell the AI how you talk to customers: friendly and short, or warm and detailed. Give it one example of a reply you wrote yourself, and ask it to match that style.

Should I use customer names in prompts?

No. Keep customer names, addresses, and order details out of your prompts. Describe the situation in general terms and add the personal details yourself afterwards.

Can AI help me plan my week?

Yes. List your goals and ask for a simple plan with one focus per day or week. Treat the plan as a suggestion, and adjust it to fit your real workload.

What if the AI suggests something I cannot afford?

Tell the AI your limits in the prompt, such as no-cost ideas or things you can do in an hour. Clear limits lead to practical suggestions instead of expensive ones.
