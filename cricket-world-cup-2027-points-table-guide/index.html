Cricket World Cup 2027 Points Table Guide

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

The points table decides which teams reach the Super 7 and then the semi-finals at the Cricket World Cup 2027. This guide explains points, net run rate in plain words with a worked example, and how qualification works in this format.

Every World Cup has a table that fans check after every match, and the 2027 tournament has two of them: one for each group of six, and then a single table for the Super 7. The table looks simple, a few columns of numbers, but it decides who goes home and who plays on. Understanding it makes the whole tournament more enjoyable.

This guide explains how points are earned, what net run rate means, and how the table decides qualification at each stage. The worked examples use made-up example numbers with generic team names, so nothing here can be mistaken for real results: the tournament has not started, and no team has any points yet.

How points work

Points are the first thing the table sorts by. The standard convention in one-day international tournaments is two points for a win, and this guide states that as the standard convention. A team that wins earns two points, a team that loses earns none, and when a match cannot produce a result, the usual convention is one point each. The team with the most points sits at the top of the table.

Points are simple, but they do not tell the whole story. Two teams can finish level on points, and that happens often in a round robin where every team plays five matches. When points are equal, the table is decided by net run rate, which is explained below. That is why a team can finish above another team with the same number of wins: the table rewards not just winning, but winning well.

Reading the table: the columns explained

A points table has the same columns at every tournament. Played tells you how many matches a team has completed. Won and Lost tell you the results. Points is the total earned. Net run rate, usually shortened to NRR, is the tiebreaker. Some tables also show tied or no-result matches, but the columns above are the ones that matter most.

Read the table from the top down, but remember what it cannot show. A team that has played fewer matches may sit lower than it deserves, and a team that has played the strongest opponents may sit lower than a team with an easier run. Early in the tournament, the table moves wildly after every match; it only starts to settle when most teams have played three or four matches.

Net run rate, in plain words

Net run rate measures how fast a team scores its runs compared with how fast it concedes them. It is calculated across all of a team's matches: take the average number of runs the team scores per over, and subtract the average number of runs it concedes per over. A positive number means the team scores faster than it concedes; a negative number means the opposite.

That is all there is to it, but the idea is worth sitting with. A team that wins by a big margin improves its net run rate a lot, because it scored quickly and conceded slowly. A team that wins narrowly improves it only a little. And a team that loses heavily sees its net run rate fall sharply, even if it has the same number of points as the teams around it. Net run rate is, in effect, the table's memory of how convincingly each team has played.

A worked example, checked by hand

Here is a worked example using made-up numbers. Imagine Team A scores 250 runs in 50 overs and concedes 200 runs in 50 overs. Team A's scoring rate is 250 divided by 50, which is 5.00 runs per over. The rate at which it concedes is 200 divided by 50, which is 4.00 runs per over. Subtract the second from the first: 5.00 minus 4.00 is 1.00, so Team A's net run rate is plus 1.00.

Now imagine Team B scores 180 runs in 45 overs and concedes 210 runs in 50 overs. Team B's scoring rate is 180 divided by 45, which is 4.00 runs per over. The conceding rate is 210 divided by 50, which is 4.20 runs per over. Subtract: 4.00 minus 4.20 is minus 0.20, so Team B's net run rate is minus 0.20. Team A, with plus 1.00, sits above Team B on net run rate, even if the two teams have the same points.

One standard rule is worth knowing: if a team is bowled out before using its full allocation of overs, its full allocation still counts in the calculation. A team bowled out for 150 in 30 overs of a 50-over match is treated as having faced 50 overs, not 30. That stops a team from protecting its net run rate by getting bowled out quickly, and it is the standard rule in one-day tournaments.

How qualification works

In the group stage, twelve teams play in two groups of six. The top three teams in each group advance to the Super 7, and they are joined by the best fourth-placed team across the two groups, which makes seven teams in total. The best fourth-placed team is decided by the usual table rules: points first, then net run rate.

In the Super 7, the seven teams play a single round robin of 21 matches, and the top four reach the semi-finals. The semi-finals are in Cape Town on 17 November and Centurion on 18 November 2027, and the final is on 21 November. At every stage, the rule is the same: points decide the order, and net run rate separates teams that are level on points.

What the Super 7 means for the table

The Super 7 gives the points table a second life. In older formats, the table mattered once, in the group stage, and then the knockouts took over. In 2027, the table matters twice: first to decide which seven teams reach the Super 7, and then to decide which four of those seven reach the semi-finals. That means a team that qualifies as the best fourth-placed side still has everything to play for, and a team that topped its group cannot afford to relax.

It also means net run rate matters for longer. A heavy defeat in the group stage can follow a team into the Super 7 race for the best fourth place, and a heavy defeat in the Super 7 can end a semi-final campaign even if the team has the same points as the teams around it. Fans who follow the table closely will find the Super 7 the most tense part of the tournament.

Common misunderstandings

The most common misunderstanding is that net run rate is about totals. It is not: it is about rates, runs per over. Scoring 300 runs is not automatically good for net run rate if it took 50 overs to get there while the other team scored 290 in 45 overs. What matters is the gap between the two rates, not the size of the totals.

Another misunderstanding is that early tables mean a lot. They do not. After one match, net run rate swings wildly, because a single result dominates the average. The table only becomes meaningful once teams have played three or four matches, and even then a single heavy result can change it. The sensible way to read the table is to check the points first, then the net run rate, and then how many matches each team has left.

Questions people ask

How many points do you get for a win?

Two points for a win is the standard convention in one-day international tournaments. A loss earns no points, and the usual convention is one point each when a match cannot produce a result.

What is net run rate?

Net run rate is the average runs a team scores per over minus the average runs it concedes per over, across all its matches. A positive number means the team scores faster than it concedes.

What happens if two teams finish level on points?

Net run rate separates teams that are level on points. The team with the higher net run rate finishes above the other team in the table.

How many teams reach the Super 7?

Seven teams: the top three from each group of six, plus the best fourth-placed team across the two groups. The top four in the Super 7 reach the semi-finals.

Why does a big win matter more than a narrow win?

Because net run rate rewards the margin. A big win improves a team's scoring rate and lowers its conceding rate, which lifts its net run rate far more than a narrow win does.

Where can I see the live points table?

The ICC's official channels carry the live points table once the tournament begins on 2 October 2027. This guide explains the system; it is not a live table.
