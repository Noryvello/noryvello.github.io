How to Fix a Running Toilet

Before you start

Find the shut-off valve first. It is usually on the wall or floor behind the toilet, near the base, and it turns clockwise to close. Test that it turns and that the water to the toilet stops. If the valve is stuck, stiff or leaking, stop: you need a working shut-off before you open the tank, and a faulty valve is a job for a plumber.

Lift the tank lid carefully with both hands and put it somewhere safe, on a towel. Tank lids are heavy, slippery and surprisingly easy to break, and a broken lid is a much bigger job than a running toilet. Put old towels on the floor around the base, and keep the lid off while you work so you can see what the parts are doing.

How a toilet tank works

The tank holds water ready for the next flush, and three parts control it. The flapper is a rubber seal at the bottom of the tank: when you flush, it lifts and lets water into the bowl, then drops back to seal the tank so it can refill. The fill valve is the tall part that refills the tank after a flush and shuts off when the water reaches the right level. The chain connects the flush handle to the flapper, lifting it when you press the handle.

A running toilet means one of these three is not doing its job. If the flapper does not seal, water trickles into the bowl and the tank keeps refilling. If the fill valve does not shut off, water keeps rising until it spills into the overflow tube. If the chain is tangled or too long, the flapper cannot drop back into place. Finding which one is the problem is most of the fix.

Diagnose the problem

Watch the tank during a flush before you change anything. Flush once and watch: does the flapper drop back and seal, does the water stop at the right level, does the chain hang with a little slack? The picker above will point you to the right fix for what you see, and the steps below cover all three causes in order.

The fixes, step by step

Most running toilets are fixed with your hands and no tools at all. Work through the fix for your cause, and test after each change rather than changing everything at once.

  1. For a worn flapper: turn off the water, flush to empty the tank, unhook the old flapper and fit a matching new one, then turn the water on and test.
  2. For a faulty fill valve: check the water level first, and adjust the float so the water stops below the top of the overflow tube.
  3. If the fill valve will not shut off at any level, it needs replacing: turn off the water, empty the tank, and fit a new valve in its place.
  4. For a tangled chain: unhook it, untangle it, and refit it with a little slack, about a finger's width, so the flapper can drop fully.
  5. If the chain is too long, move the hook to a link closer to the flapper; if it is too short, it holds the flapper open.
  6. After any fix, flush twice and watch the tank refill and stop on its own.

Testing your fix

Testing is part of the fix, not an afterthought. Flush, then watch the tank refill and stop. Wait a few minutes and listen: the toilet should be silent. Then check the bowl: the water should be still, with no trickle running into it. If the toilet starts running again after a few minutes, the flapper is probably still not sealing, and it is worth checking that it sits flat and that the chain is not holding it up.

One more check is worth doing: look at the water level in the tank. It should sit below the top of the overflow tube, with a clear gap. If the water is at or above the top of the tube, the fill valve needs adjusting down, even if the toilet seems to have stopped running.

When to call a plumber

Call a plumber if water is leaking from the base of the toilet, if the shut-off valve is stuck or leaking, or if the tank or bowl is cracked. Also call one if you have replaced the flapper and adjusted the fill valve and the toilet still runs: the fault may be in the flush valve seat or in the fill valve itself, and both are easier to fix with the right parts and experience. A running toilet wastes water every day it is left, so do not leave a failed fix for weeks.

Questions people ask

Why does my toilet keep running?

Usually one of three causes: a worn flapper that no longer seals, a fill valve that does not shut off, or a chain that is tangled or the wrong length. Each has a different fix, so watch the tank during a flush to find which one it is.

How do I know if the flapper is worn?

Look for water trickling into the bowl when the toilet has not been flushed, and listen for the tank refilling on its own. A flapper that feels stiff, warped or rough to the touch is worn and needs replacing.

Can a running toilet waste much water?

Yes. A steady trickle into the bowl runs all day and night, and over days and weeks it adds up to far more water than normal flushing. Fixing a running toilet promptly is one of the simplest ways to stop wasting water.

Do I need tools to fix a running toilet?

Usually just your hands. Flappers unhook, chains unhook, and fill valves adjust by hand. You may need a wrench if you replace the fill valve, but most running toilets are fixed without any tools at all.

What if the fix does not work?

Recheck the diagnosis: watch the tank during a flush and see what is actually happening. If the flapper and chain look right and the fill valve is adjusted, the fault may be in the flush valve seat, and it is time to call a plumber.
