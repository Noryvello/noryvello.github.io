ChatGPT Keyboard Shortcuts: The Full List and How to Use Them

By the Noryvello team · Published 20 November 2026 · Updated 20 November 2026

Keyboard shortcuts turn ChatGPT from a click-heavy website into a fast tool you can drive without touching the mouse. The handful that matter most start new chats, search your history, copy answers, and control messages while they generate. This guide lists the shortcuts people actually use, shows you where the full current list lives inside ChatGPT, and explains what to do when a shortcut stops working.

Keyboard shortcuts, buttons, and menus change over time, and they differ between the ChatGPT website, the desktop app, and the phone app. The steps in this guide describe how things commonly work; the in-app help and the shortcut list inside ChatGPT itself are the authority when something looks different.

Why shortcuts matter

ChatGPT is a website you visit with a mouse, but it does not have to be used that way. Almost everything you do in a chat has a keyboard shortcut: starting fresh, finding an old conversation, copying an answer, stopping a reply halfway through. Once you know the handful you use every day, the tool stops feeling like a web page and starts feeling like a desk tool.

The gain is not speed for its own sake. It is flow. When you are in the middle of thinking, reaching for the mouse breaks the thread; a keypress does not. A writer drafting with ChatGPT, a student working through homework, or anyone who keeps several chats open will feel the difference within a day. Five shortcuts do most of the work:

  • Start a new chat — the most-used shortcut of all, because a fresh chat is how you start a fresh task.
  • Search your chats — find yesterday's conversation without scrolling the sidebar.
  • Copy the last answer — get the reply into your document in one move.
  • Stop a reply while it generates — cut off a long answer that is going the wrong way.
  • Add a line break without sending — write a two-part message instead of sending half a thought.

Learn those five first and the rest can wait. The sections below give the keys, explain where the full list lives, and cover what to do when a shortcut stops working.

How to open the shortcut list inside ChatGPT

ChatGPT keeps its own shortcut list inside the product, and that list is the authority. It shows the shortcuts for the device you are on, in the version you are using, which matters because the keys differ between Windows and Mac and between the website and the desktop app.

To find it, open the menu in ChatGPT — commonly reached from your profile icon or the settings area — and look for a keyboard shortcuts entry. On the website it is often listed under help or settings; on the desktop app it may appear in the app menu. The exact labels change, so if you cannot find it, use the in-app help search and type "keyboard shortcuts".

Make a habit of checking the list whenever something stops working. A shortcut that did nothing this morning may have moved in an update, and the in-app list will show the new key in seconds. Bookmarking this guide helps too, but the list inside ChatGPT is always the freshest copy.

One more tip: the list usually shows both the Windows and the Mac version of each key. On Windows the modifier is Ctrl; on a Mac it is Cmd. If a guide says Ctrl and you are on a Mac, try Cmd in its place.

New chat and navigation shortcuts

Starting a new chat is the shortcut you will use most, because every new task deserves a clean conversation. On the website, Ctrl+Shift+O on Windows or Cmd+Shift+O on a Mac commonly opens a fresh chat. If that combination does nothing, your browser may be using it for something else — the troubleshooting section below covers that.

Navigation is the other half of moving fast. ChatGPT commonly offers a shortcut to show or hide the sidebar, so you can give the conversation the full width of the screen while you read, then bring the list back when you need it. There is also usually a way to jump straight to the message box, so you can start typing without clicking into it first.

These navigation keys are worth learning as a pair: one to move around, one to start fresh. Together they replace the two clicks most people make most often, and they keep your hands on the keyboard for the whole session.

  • Ctrl+Shift+O (Windows) or Cmd+Shift+O (Mac): open a new chat.
  • Sidebar toggle: show or hide the chat list — check the in-app list for the current key.
  • Jump to the message box: start typing without clicking first.

Searching and switching between chats

After a few weeks of use, most people have dozens of chats, and scrolling the sidebar to find one is slow. Search fixes that. On the desktop app the search shortcut is often Ctrl+K or Cmd+K, which opens a search box where you type a word from the chat you want. On the website, look for the search entry in the sidebar or the shortcut list.

Search matches words in your chat titles and messages, so a distinctive word works better than a common one. Searching for "rent" will find the chat about the flat; searching for "the" will find everything. If you keep important chats, give them clear first messages, because the first message is usually what becomes the title.

Switching between chats is just as useful. Once search is open, the arrow keys commonly move through the results and Enter opens the one you want. That is the whole pattern: search, arrow, Enter, and you are back in the conversation in two seconds.

Copying answers and editing messages with the keyboard

Getting text out of ChatGPT and into your document is a daily job, and the keyboard makes it quick. Every answer has a copy button underneath it, which copies the whole reply in one click — the fastest option when you want everything. For part of an answer, click at the start of the part you want, hold Shift, and use the arrow keys to select, then copy with Ctrl+C or Cmd+C.

Code blocks get their own copy button, which copies just the code without the surrounding explanation. That matters because pasting an explanation into a code file breaks it, and retyping code by hand invites mistakes. If you use ChatGPT for code, the code copy button is the shortcut to learn first.

Editing your own message is a quieter trick. If you spot a typo in the message you just sent, or you want to ask the same question with one word changed, you can edit the message and send it again instead of typing a new one. The edit control sits with your message; change the words, send, and ChatGPT answers the corrected version. It keeps the chat tidy and saves retyping the whole prompt.

Message actions: regenerate, edit, stop generating

While ChatGPT is writing a reply, you are not stuck watching it. Pressing Esc commonly stops the generation, which is useful when the answer is going in the wrong direction or is far longer than you need. Stopping early saves time and lets you rephrase the question before the model has spent a long reply on the wrong reading of it.

If an answer is finished but not right, you have two options. Regenerate asks for a fresh answer to the same question, which is worth trying when the reply is thin or misses the point. Edit, covered in the previous section, changes your question and gets a new answer to the changed question. The difference matters: regenerate keeps the question and changes the answer, while edit changes the question itself.

A practical habit is to stop early and rephrase rather than waiting for a long wrong answer. If the first two lines show the model misunderstood you, press Esc, edit your message to be clearer, and send again. You will get a better answer faster than by reading to the end of a reply that was never going to help.

The shortcuts that save the most time, in a daily routine

Shortcuts pay off in routines, not in single uses. Here is what a shortcut-driven session looks like for someone who uses ChatGPT through the working day. You open the app, search for yesterday's chat with the search shortcut, and pick it up where you left off. You copy the answer you need with the copy button, then start a new chat for the next task with the new-chat shortcut.

Mid-morning you paste in a long document and ask for a summary. The answer is longer than you want, so you stop it with Esc, edit your message to ask for three bullet points, and send again. After lunch you search for the chat about the report, copy the outline into your document, and open a fresh chat for the afternoon's work.

None of these steps is dramatic on its own. The saving is the clicks you never made and the scrolling you never did, repeated twenty times a day. After a week the keys become automatic, and using ChatGPT without them feels slow by comparison.

Shortcuts on the phone app

On a phone, keyboard shortcuts mostly do not apply, because there is no keyboard. The phone app is built around touch and voice: you tap to open chats, swipe to move around, and hold the voice button to talk instead of typing. The shortcuts in this guide are for a computer, and you should not expect them on a phone.

There are two exceptions worth knowing. If you connect a physical keyboard to a tablet and use ChatGPT in the browser, some computer shortcuts may work, because the browser treats the keyboard the way a laptop would. And the phone app has its own speed features — voice mode, the share menu for sending text in, and widgets — which play the same role that shortcuts play on a computer.

If you move between phone and computer, the habits transfer even when the keys do not. Searching for a chat, copying an answer, and starting fresh are the same jobs on both; only the gestures differ. Learn the jobs on the computer and you will find the phone equivalents quickly.

When a shortcut doesn't work

The most common reason a shortcut fails is focus. Your browser sends keypresses to whatever is active, and if the cursor is in the address bar, a search box, or another tab's field, ChatGPT never sees the key. Click once inside the chat page — on the conversation, not on a button — and try the shortcut again.

The next suspect is a conflict. Browsers reserve some combinations for themselves, and extensions can claim others, so the key you press may open a browser feature instead of a ChatGPT one. If a shortcut opens something unexpected, check whether a browser extension is using it, or try the same shortcut in a private window with extensions off.

Finally, remember that the website, the desktop app, and the phone app are different products with different shortcut sets. A key that works on the website may do nothing in the desktop app, and the reverse. When in doubt, open the shortcut list inside the version you are using — it is the only list that is guaranteed to match your screen — and check the tool's own help pages for the current steps.

Questions people ask

How do I start a new chat with the keyboard?

On the ChatGPT website, Ctrl+Shift+O on Windows or Cmd+Shift+O on a Mac commonly opens a new chat. If that combination does nothing in your browser, open the shortcut list inside ChatGPT to check the current key, because shortcuts change and can differ between the website and the desktop app.

Is there a shortcut to search my chats?

Yes, on a computer. The search shortcut is commonly shown in the in-app shortcut list, and on the desktop app it is often Ctrl+K or Cmd+K. On the website you can also click the search box in the sidebar and type, which works the same way even when you forget the key.

How do I copy the last answer quickly?

The simplest way is the copy button that appears under each answer, which copies the whole reply in one click. You can also click into the answer, select the part you want with the mouse or the keyboard, and copy it with Ctrl+C or Cmd+C, or use the copy button on any code block to copy just the code.

Do keyboard shortcuts work on the phone app?

Mostly no, because a phone has no keyboard. The phone app is built around touch, voice, and the share menu instead. If you connect a physical keyboard to a tablet, some shortcuts may work in the browser version, but the app itself does not publish a shortcut list the way the computer version does.

Why doesn't a shortcut work in my browser?

The usual reason is that the keyboard focus is in the wrong place, for example in the address bar instead of the chat page, so the browser takes the key instead of ChatGPT. Browser extensions can also steal a combination, and some shortcuts behave differently in the desktop app and the website. Click once inside the chat page, try again, and check the in-app shortcut list for the current key.

Where is the full list inside ChatGPT?

Open the help or settings menu in ChatGPT and look for the keyboard shortcuts entry, which shows every shortcut for your device. The list is the authority: it shows the keys as they work today, on the version you are using, which is why it is worth checking whenever a shortcut stops working.
