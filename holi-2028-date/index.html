Holi 2028 Date

Holi is the festival of colours, and one of the most joyful days in the Hindu calendar. On the main day, people throw coloured powder and water at each other, share sweets, and celebrate the arrival of spring. The night before, a bonfire is lit for Holika Dahan, marking the victory of good over evil.

In 2028, Holika Dahan falls on Friday 10 March and Rangwali Holi, the day of colours, falls on Saturday 11 March. This page gives the dates, explains how they are set, and shows how the festival is celebrated in different parts of India.

How the date of Holi is set

Holi is set by the Hindu lunar calendar. Holika Dahan falls on the full-moon night of the month of Phalguna, and Rangwali Holi falls the next day, on the first day of the waning moon. Because the lunar calendar moves against the Gregorian calendar, the date of Holi changes each year — it usually falls in March, and sometimes in late February.

The full moon of Phalguna marks the end of winter and the arrival of spring, which is why Holi is also a spring festival. The colours, the outdoor celebrations, and the sense of a fresh start all belong to that season.

The story behind the bonfire is the story of Prahlad and Holika. Prahlad was a devotee of Vishnu, and his aunt Holika, who had a boon protecting her from fire, tried to kill him by sitting with him in a fire. The fire consumed Holika instead, and Prahlad survived — the triumph of devotion and good over evil that the bonfire remembers each year.

How Holi is celebrated

On the evening of Holika Dahan, a bonfire is lit in a public place or courtyard, and people gather around it. Offerings of grain and coconuts are made to the fire in many places, and the evening is a time for families and neighbours to meet before the main day.

On Rangwali Holi itself, the day starts early. Coloured powder, called gulal, and coloured water are thrown at friends, family, and anyone who steps outside. Water balloons, water guns, and buckets of coloured water are all part of the fun. Music plays from every house, and dancing in the street is common.

Food is a big part of the day. Gujiya, a sweet dumpling filled with khoya and dried fruit, is the classic Holi sweet, and thandai, a spiced milk drink, is served in many homes. By the afternoon, the colours are washed off and families sit down together for a meal.

Regional variation

Holi is celebrated across India, but the style varies. In the Braj region, around Mathura and Vrindavan, the festival lasts for days and is linked to the stories of Krishna and Radha — the Lathmar Holi of Barsana and Nandgaon, where women playfully chase men with sticks, is famous far beyond the region. In West Bengal, the festival is known as Dol Jatra, and the day begins with a procession carrying an image of Krishna.

In Punjab, the day after Holi is Hola Mohalla, a martial-arts gathering with displays of horsemanship and mock battles. In Maharashtra, the bonfire night is called Shimga, and in the south, the festival is smaller but still marked with colours and sweets. In Nepal, Holi is celebrated with enthusiasm in the Terai and the hills alike.

Wherever it is celebrated, the spirit is the same: the day is a great leveller, and on Holi, old quarrels are set aside and everyone plays together.

Planning for Holi 2028

If you plan to play Holi, wear old clothes you do not mind staining — the colours do not always wash out. Many people oil their hair and skin before going out, which makes the colours easier to wash off afterwards. Dry colours are gentler than wet ones, and natural, skin-safe colours are the kindest choice for children and for the skin.

Keep phones and valuables in sealed bags, and keep drinking water handy — the day is long and the sun can be strong in March. If you are hosting, stock up on sweets and snacks the day before, and keep a change of clothes and towels ready for guests.

Pets should be kept indoors during the celebrations, and colours should never be thrown at animals. If you are celebrating with young children, stick to dry colours and keep the play gentle.

What is Holika Dahan?

Holika Dahan is the bonfire lit on the evening before the main day of Holi. It marks the burning of Holika and the victory of good over evil, from the story of Prahlad. In 2028 it falls on Friday 10 March.

What is Rangwali Holi?

Rangwali Holi is the main day of the festival, when coloured powder and water are thrown, sweets are shared, and the streets fill with music. In 2028 it falls on Saturday 11 March.

Why does Holi fall on a different date each year?

Holi follows the Hindu lunar calendar. Holika Dahan falls on the full moon of the month of Phalguna, and because a lunar month is shorter than a calendar month, the Gregorian date moves each year, usually landing in March.

Is Holi on the same day everywhere?

The main dates are Friday 10 March and Saturday 11 March 2028 in the widely published calendars. Some regions stretch the celebrations over several days, so check your local calendar for the exact timings in your area.
