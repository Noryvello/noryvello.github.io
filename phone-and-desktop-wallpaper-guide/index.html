Phone and Desktop Wallpaper Guide

A good wallpaper starts with the right size for your screen and a picture from a source you can trust. This guide shows you where to find wallpapers legally, which size each device needs, and how to set a wallpaper on your phone and your computer.

A wallpaper is the picture you see more than any other — every time you unlock your phone or sit down at your computer. It is worth getting right, and getting it right takes two things: a picture from a source you can trust, and the right size for your screen.

This guide covers legitimate sources only. It names the libraries worth using, explains sizes by device, and walks through setting a wallpaper on a phone and on a computer.

Legitimate sources

The free stock libraries are the best place to start for wallpapers: Unsplash, Pexels and Pixabay all carry large, high-resolution photos free of charge, and each photo page states the licence. A photo from one of these libraries can be used as a personal wallpaper without worry, and the choice is wide enough that you need not look elsewhere.

Device makers publish their own wallpaper galleries, and these are worth a look because the pictures are made for the screens they run on. Museums with open-access collections are another honest source: many museums now share high-resolution images of artworks free of charge, and a painting or a photograph from a museum collection makes a distinctive wallpaper.

Be careful with wallpaper sites that ask you to install an app or a browser extension to download a picture. A wallpaper is just an image file, and you should be able to save it without installing anything. Sites that bundle downloads with software are best avoided entirely.

Sizes by device

A wallpaper should match your screen's shape and resolution. A picture much smaller than the screen will look blurry when stretched; a picture much larger will be cropped, which is fine as long as the subject survives the crop. When a source offers several sizes, download the largest — a large picture can always be shrunk, but a small picture cannot be made larger without losing sharpness.

How to set a wallpaper

On a phone, the usual route is through the settings app: open Settings, look for Wallpaper or Display, choose the picture, and set it for the home screen, the lock screen, or both. Most phones also let you set a wallpaper from the photo itself: open the picture in your gallery app and look for the set-as-wallpaper option. When you set it, the phone usually lets you move and scale the picture first, so take a moment to frame the subject where you want it.

On a computer, the route is similar: open Settings, look for Personalisation or Desktop, and choose the picture. Most computers also let you right-click a picture file and choose to set it as the desktop background. If the picture is not the same shape as your screen, the settings will offer a choice — fill, fit, stretch or tile — and fill is usually the best choice, because it crops rather than distorts.

If you use two monitors, most computers let you set a different picture on each, or stretch one wide picture across both. A very wide picture made for two screens can look striking, but a different picture on each screen is easier to get right.

Making a photo work as wallpaper

Not every good photo makes a good wallpaper. A wallpaper sits behind icons, text and windows, so busy pictures fight with everything on top of them. The best wallpapers are simple: a landscape with a clear sky, a single subject with space around it, or a pattern with room to breathe.

Think about where the icons and the clock sit. On a phone, the top of the screen holds the time and the bottom holds the app icons, so a picture with its subject in the middle works best. On a computer, the desktop icons usually sit on one side, so a picture with empty space on that side keeps them readable.

Lock screen and home screen can be different pictures, and it is often worth making them different. A lock screen is seen for seconds, so a bold picture works; a home screen is seen all day, so a calmer picture is easier to live with. Try a picture for a day before deciding — a wallpaper that delights at first sight can tire quickly, and a quiet one can grow on you.

Wallpaper sites that ask you to install an app or a browser extension to download a picture are best avoided. A wallpaper is just an image file, and you should be able to save it without installing anything.

Questions people ask

What size should a phone wallpaper be?

A common phone wallpaper size is 1080 by 2400 pixels, in a tall shape. Check your own screen resolution in your device settings, and download the largest size the source offers.

What size should a desktop wallpaper be?

A common desktop wallpaper size is 2560 by 1440 pixels, in a wide 16 by 9 shape. A 1920 by 1080 picture also works, but it will look softer on a larger monitor.

Can I use any photo as a wallpaper?

You can use a photo you took yourself, or a photo from a legitimate free source such as Unsplash, Pexels or Pixabay. Do not use a photo you found on a site you cannot identify.

Why does my wallpaper look blurry?

Usually because the picture is smaller than your screen and has been stretched to fit. Download the largest size the source offers, and check the picture at full size before setting it.

How do I set a different wallpaper on my lock screen?

On most phones, the wallpaper settings let you set the home screen and the lock screen separately. Choose the picture, then pick lock screen when the phone asks where to apply it.
