The steps here are general guidance for hand embroidery. Stitches take practice, so try each one on scrap fabric first. This page does not cover machine embroidery.

The 12 stitches at a glance

  • Running stitch — outlining, beginner. A simple in-and-out stitch for lines and gathering.
  • Backstitch — outlining and lettering, beginner. A neat, solid line that follows curves well.
  • Stem stitch — outlining, easy. A smooth, slightly twisted line for stems and flowing shapes.
  • Split stitch — outlining, easy. A fine, rope-like line for delicate outlines.
  • Chain stitch — outlining and filling, easy. A linked line that also fills shapes in rows.
  • Satin stitch — filling, intermediate. Flat, parallel stitches that cover an area smoothly.
  • Lazy daisy — flowers and leaves, beginner. Single chain loops that form petals and leaves.
  • French knot — texture and dots, intermediate. Small raised knots for centres and texture.
  • Seed stitch — texture and filling, beginner. Tiny scattered stitches that fill and shade.
  • Blanket stitch — edges and borders, beginner. A looped edge stitch that finishes hems.
  • Feather stitch — edges and flowers, intermediate. Branching stitches for borders and foliage.
  • Cross stitch — filling and lettering, easy. Even crosses worked in rows on counted fabric.

Before you start stitching

Stretch your fabric in a hoop so it stays taut while you sew. A taut surface keeps stitches even and stops the fabric puckering around the design. Choose a needle with an eye large enough for your thread, and cut a length of thread no longer than your forearm so it does not tangle or wear thin as you stitch.

Mark your design lightly on the fabric before you begin. A light pencil line or a washable marker gives you a path to follow and keeps your lines straight. Start with a small knot or a few tiny stitches on the back of the fabric to anchor the thread, and finish the same way so the work does not come undone.

Practising a new stitch

Try every new stitch on scrap fabric first. Work a short row slowly, watching how the thread sits and how the needle moves. If the stitches look uneven, slow down and keep the stitch length the same. Most stitches feel awkward for the first few tries and smooth out quickly with practice.

Keep your tension gentle. Pulling the thread too tight puckers the fabric, while leaving it too loose makes floppy stitches. Aim for stitches that lie flat and even. When you are happy with the practice row, move on to your project with confidence.

Questions people ask

Which stitch should a complete beginner learn first?

Running stitch or backstitch. Both use short, even stitches, need no special handling, and are useful for outlines and simple designs.

What is the difference between stem stitch and backstitch?

Backstitch makes a neat straight line of touching stitches. Stem stitch makes a smooth, slightly twisted line that curves well, which suits stems and flowing outlines.

How do I keep my stitches even?

Mark your line lightly before you start, and keep your stitch length the same. A hoop keeps the fabric taut, which also helps.

What thread should I use?

Stranded cotton thread is a common choice for hand embroidery. Use fewer strands for fine detail and more strands for bold lines.

Why do my French knots keep pulling through?

Hold the working thread taut as you pull the needle through, and keep the knot close to the fabric. Practise on scrap fabric until the knot sits neatly.
