Bottled Water or a Filter Jug: Which Costs Less in a Year?

Short answer: enter daily water use, bottle size, local costs and filter capacity to compare estimated yearly totals and bottle count.

How the yearly estimate works

The bottled-water estimate uses daily litres divided by bottle size, multiplied by 365 days and the bottle cost. The filter estimate uses annual litres divided by cartridge capacity, multiplied by cartridge cost, plus one year of the jug cost. Bottle count is an estimate and assumes the same daily use throughout the year.

Check these details first

  • Use the actual bottle size and the price you pay, not a promotional price you may not get again.
  • Check the filter maker's stated capacity and replacement schedule.
  • Include replacement cartridges and any required cleaning or maintenance.
  • Confirm that the filter is suitable for the water quality and contaminants relevant to your area.
Important limit: Filter cartridges must be replaced on schedule or the savings and quality drop.

Questions people ask

Does a filter jug always make water safe to drink?

No. Filter types remove different substances. Check the product specification and whether it is suitable for the contaminants that may be present in your water.

How often should I replace a filter cartridge?

Follow the manufacturer's stated time or volume limit, whichever comes first. Usage and water conditions can affect cartridge life.

How does the calculator estimate plastic bottles avoided?

It divides annual drinking-water use by the bottle size you enter. The result is an estimated number of single-use bottles, not a measured waste audit.

Does the comparison include electricity?

Most gravity-fed filter jugs do not use electricity, but this calculator does not include energy or other optional equipment costs.
