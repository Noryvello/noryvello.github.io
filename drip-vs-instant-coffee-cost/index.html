Drip or Instant Coffee: Which Costs Less Over a Year?

Short answer: enter your coffee use, prices and preparation times to see estimated yearly spending and time for both methods.

How the comparison works

Annual coffee cost is cups per day multiplied by days per year, grams per cup, and price per gram. The drip total also includes the equipment cost divided by its expected useful life. Annual time is cups per year multiplied by the preparation and cleanup minutes you enter. These are estimates based entirely on your inputs.

Keep the inputs comparable

  • Use the same cup size and strength target for both methods.
  • Use the price you actually pay, not a generic market average.
  • Add filters, electricity, water, and other extras separately if they matter to your routine.
  • Do not count a coffee maker's full cost every year; spread it over the years you expect to use it.
Important limit: Taste is personal; cost is only half the choice.

Questions people ask

Is drip coffee always cheaper than instant coffee?

No. The result depends on the amount of coffee used, the price per unit, and any equipment cost you choose to include.

How do I compare coffee cost per cup?

Enter the amount of coffee used per cup and the price per gram for each type. The calculator multiplies those values by your cups per day and days per year.

Should I include the coffee maker?

Include an annual share of the maker cost if you are buying it for this comparison. Divide its cost by the years you expect to use it.

Does the time estimate include cleanup?

The time fields are your own estimates for preparation and cleanup per cup. The calculator does not measure your actual routine.
