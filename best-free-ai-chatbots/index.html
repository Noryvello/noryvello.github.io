The Best Free AI Chatbots (and How to Pick One)

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

Eight well-known AI chatbots offer free tiers, and each one has a different feel: some are better for writing, some for research, some for quick answers on your phone. This guide gives you one honest paragraph on each free tier, then helps you pick the one that fits what you actually do.

"Free" means different things on different services. Every chatbot below lets you chat without paying, but free tiers usually come with limits — how much you can chat, which features you can use, or how fast replies come. Those limits change often, so treat this guide as a starting point, not a permanent scoreboard.

There is no single winner. The best free chatbot is the one that fits your main task, your device, and your comfort with how it handles your chats. Read the paragraph on each, then use the picker section at the end to match your needs to a shortlist.

Free tiers change without warning: limits, features, and even which model you get can shift from one month to the next. Check the current free offering on the service itself before you rely on it, and never paste private details into any chatbot.

How to Read This Roundup

The eight chatbots below are listed in no particular order, and none is ranked — the point is fit, not a winner. For each one, the paragraph describes what the free tier offers in general terms: what it is good at, what it feels like to use, and where its limits tend to show. "Good at writing" means it drafts readable text; "good at research" means it handles sources and longer documents well. Keep in mind that free tiers are often a lighter version of the paid service, so the newest features may not be included. If you only need one chatbot, pick the one whose strengths match your most common task. If you do several different things, using two is perfectly normal.

ChatGPT

The most widely known chatbot, and a solid all-rounder on its free tier. It handles everyday writing, explanations, brainstorming, and follow-up questions well, and its chat interface is simple to learn. The free tier gives you access to a capable model with limits on how much you can use in a given period, and some newer features may be reserved for paying users. It is a good first chatbot if you want one tool for many small jobs, from drafting a message to untangling a confusing topic.

Claude

Known for careful, well-structured writing and long conversations. On its free tier, Claude is a strong choice for drafting and editing text, working through ideas step by step, and discussing longer documents. Its replies tend to be measured and clear, which suits people who want thoughtful answers rather than fast ones. Free use comes with limits on message volume, so heavy daily use may run into them. If writing quality matters most to you, Claude's free tier is worth trying first.

Gemini

Google's chatbot, and a natural fit if you already use Google's apps. The free tier answers questions, drafts text, and can work with images and voice in general terms. Its strength is everyday usefulness: quick answers, help with planning, and drafting in a familiar style. Because it sits inside Google's world, it feels convenient for people who live in Gmail, Docs, and Search. Free use has limits, and some advanced features are paid, but the free tier covers a wide range of ordinary tasks.

DeepSeek

A capable chatbot whose free tier is generous for everyday chatting. It handles explanations, writing help, and reasoning-style questions, and it offers a reasoning mode that shows its working in plain steps. That makes it a good pick for problems where you want to see how an answer was reached, not just the answer itself. The interface is straightforward, and the free tier covers the core experience. If you like checking the reasoning behind an answer, DeepSeek is a strong free option.

Perplexity

Less a general chatbot and more an answer engine: you ask a question and it replies with a short answer plus the sources it used. That makes the free tier especially useful for research, fact-checking, and questions where you want to see where the information came from. It is less suited to long creative writing or open-ended brainstorming, where a general chatbot feels more natural. If your main need is "answer this, and show me the source," Perplexity's free tier is the one to try.

Copilot

Microsoft's chatbot, built into Windows, Edge, and the web. The free tier answers questions, drafts text, and can generate images in general terms, and it feels most at home if you already use Microsoft's apps. Its strength is convenience: it is already where many people work, so there is nothing new to install or learn. Free use comes with limits on some features, but the everyday chat experience is fully available. If your computer runs Windows, Copilot is the easiest free chatbot to start with.

Meta AI

The chatbot inside WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook, so you can ask it questions without leaving the apps you already use. The free tier handles everyday questions, quick explanations, and image generation in chat, and it can join group chats when invited. Its strength is that it is already in your pocket if you use Meta's apps; its weakness is that it is tied to those apps rather than a dedicated workspace. For quick questions on your phone, it is the most convenient option here.

Grok

The chatbot from X, known for conversational answers and access to current information from the platform. The free tier handles general chat, explanations, and questions about what is happening now, and it can generate images in general terms. Its tone is informal and direct, which suits casual use. Free access has limits, and the experience is shaped by the platform it lives on. If you already spend time on X and want a chatbot in the same place, Grok is the natural fit.

Picking the Right One for You

Start with your main task. If you mostly write and edit, try Claude or ChatGPT first. If you mostly research and check facts, start with Perplexity. If you want to see reasoning step by step, try DeepSeek. If you want a chatbot inside the apps you already use, pick Gemini for Google's world, Copilot for Windows, Meta AI for WhatsApp and Instagram, or Grok for X. Then try your top pick for a week on real tasks — not test questions, but the things you actually do. Pay attention to three things: does it understand your questions, do you trust its answers enough to use them, and do the free limits get in your way? If the answer to any of those is no, try the next one on your list. Most people settle on one main chatbot and one backup, and switching costs nothing.

Questions people ask

Is there one best free chatbot for everyone?

No. The best free chatbot depends on what you do most — writing, research, planning, or quick questions on your phone. Try the one whose strengths match your main task, and keep a second one as a backup.

Do free chatbots limit how much I can chat?

Yes, free tiers usually limit how many messages you can send or which features you can use. Limits vary by service and change over time, so check the current offering if you plan to use a chatbot heavily.

Are free AI chatbots private?

Free chatbots store your chats and may use them to improve the service, depending on the settings you choose. Read each service's privacy page, use the controls it offers, and never paste private details into a chat.

Can I use more than one chatbot?

Yes, and many people do. One chatbot may be better for writing while another is better for research with sources. Using two costs nothing on the free tiers and lets you pick the better answer.

Do free tiers change over time?

Yes, often. Limits, features, and the model behind a free tier can all change without much notice. If a feature matters to you, check that it is still included before you build a habit around it.
