Buy vs Rent Power Tools

Whether to buy or rent a power tool depends on how often you use it. Enter the purchase cost, the rental cost per day, how many times a year you use it and how long you expect to keep it, and this page shows the break-even point and the yearly cost of each option.

This compares purchase and rental cost only. It leaves out servicing, repairs, replacement parts, storage, the value of your money over time, and the fact that a rented tool is usually a newer model — and it assumes the rental cost stays the same.

How it works

The break-even point is the number of uses at which renting has cost as much as buying. It is the purchase cost divided by the rental cost per day, rounded up to a whole use. If you use the tool more often than that over its life, buying costs less overall. If you use it less often, renting costs less.

The yearly figures spread the costs over one year. Buying per year is the purchase cost divided by the years you expect to keep the tool. Renting per year is the rental cost per day times the uses per year. Your total uses over the tool's life are the uses per year times the years of use.

How to use the result

Compare your total uses with the break-even point first. That tells you which side wins over the whole life of the tool. Then look at the yearly costs, which show the same comparison one year at a time.

Be honest about how often you use the tool. A tool bought for one big project and then left in a cupboard rarely reaches its break-even point, while a tool used every month usually passes it quickly. If you are unsure, run the calculator twice — once with a low number of uses and once with a high number — and see where the answer changes.

What this comparison leaves out

Buying costs more than the price tag. The calculator does not count servicing, repairs, replacement parts or storage, and it does not count the value of the money tied up in the tool. A rented tool is usually checked and serviced between hires, and it is usually a newer model than one bought years ago.

It also assumes the rental cost stays the same and that you can always hire the tool when you need it. If the tool is often booked out, or the hire shop is far away, renting has a cost in time and hassle that no number on this page captures.

Questions people ask

What is the break-even point?

The number of uses at which renting has cost as much as buying. Use the tool more often than that and buying costs less overall. Use it less and renting costs less.

What if I use the tool for more than one day at a time?

Enter the rental cost for the whole hire, not per day, or multiply the daily rate by the days you usually hire for.

Does buying include servicing and repairs?

No. The calculator counts the purchase cost only, so allow for servicing yourself if the tool needs it.

What if I already own the tool?

Then the purchase cost is already spent. This page compares buying new against renting.

Why do I enter years of use?

To spread the purchase cost across the tool's life for the yearly comparison. A tool kept longer costs less per year.
