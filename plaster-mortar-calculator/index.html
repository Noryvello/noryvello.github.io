Plaster and Mortar Calculator

Enter the wall area and plaster thickness, pick a mix ratio, and this calculator works out the volumes of cement and sand you need. The working is shown, including the extra allowance for dry volume.

These are general estimates only. Mix ratios, thickness and practice vary by region and by the surface being plastered, so follow local practice and your supplier's guidance. Check the thickness your job actually needs before ordering.

How to use this calculator

Measure the wall area in square metres. If the wall is a plain rectangle, multiply its length by its height. Enter the plaster thickness in millimetres. A single coat is often around 12 mm, but your job may differ, so check what your wall needs. Pick the mix ratio you plan to use, then press Calculate to see the cement and sand volumes, with the working shown underneath.

How the calculation works

The wet volume is the wall area multiplied by the thickness. Dry ingredients take up more volume than the finished wet plaster, so the calculator adds about a third for dry volume. That dry volume is then split by the mix ratio. For a 1 to 4 mix, one part in five is cement and four parts in five are sand, so the cement is the dry volume divided by five and the sand is the rest.

How to use the result

The result gives cement and sand in cubic metres and litres. Use it as a guide for ordering, and round up slightly so you do not run short partway through a wall. Mix small test batches first if you have not used a ratio before, and keep the mix consistent from batch to batch so the finish stays even. Store cement somewhere dry, because damp cement sets in the bag.

Questions people ask

What does a 1 to 4 mix ratio mean?

It means one part cement to four parts sand, measured by volume. A 1 to 4 mix has five parts in total, so one fifth of the dry volume is cement and four fifths are sand.

Why is extra volume added for dry ingredients?

Cement and sand settle and fill the gaps between each other when mixed with water, so the dry ingredients take up more space than the wet plaster. The calculator adds about a third to cover this.

What thickness should I enter?

Enter the thickness of plaster you plan to apply, in millimetres. A single coat is often around 12 mm, but check what your wall and your local practice call for.

Can I use this for mortar between bricks?

The maths works for any cement and sand mix, but this page is written for plastering. For brick mortar, use the brick calculator, which estimates mortar from the wall itself.

Should I buy exactly the amount shown?

Treat the result as an estimate and round up a little. Running short in the middle of a wall is worse than having a small amount left over.
