AI Chatbot Privacy Compared: What Each One Does With Your Chats

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

Every AI chatbot stores your chats, but what happens to them afterwards — whether they train future models, how long history is kept, and what the chatbot remembers — varies by service. This guide compares ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, DeepSeek, and Copilot on the three controls that matter most.

Privacy is the least exciting part of choosing a chatbot and one of the most important. Your chats can contain work ideas, personal plans, drafts of messages, and questions you would not want shared. Knowing what each service does with that text is worth ten minutes of your time.

This guide covers the three controls that matter most: training-data controls (whether your chats help train future models), history controls (how long chats are kept and whether you can delete them), and memory controls (whether the chatbot remembers things about you between chats). Everything here is in general terms, because policies and settings change.

This guide is general information only, not legal advice. Privacy policies and settings change often, so read each service's current privacy page before you rely on anything here, and treat your own habits — what you paste into a chat — as your first line of defence.

The Three Controls That Matter

Before comparing services, it helps to know what you are comparing. Training-data controls decide whether your chats are used to improve future models. Some services let you opt out; others use chats by default with a setting to stop it. History controls decide how long your chats are kept and whether you can delete them — most services keep a history you can view and delete, but deleted chats may be kept for a short time for safety reasons. Memory controls decide whether the chatbot remembers facts about you across chats, such as your name or preferences, and whether you can view or erase those memories. A service can be strong on one control and weak on another, so check all three.

ChatGPT

ChatGPT offers controls across all three areas. You can usually choose whether your chats are used to improve the service, view and delete your chat history, and manage what the chatbot remembers about you. Memory can be switched off, and individual memories can be deleted. The exact names and locations of these settings change between updates, so if a setting is hard to find, check the service's help pages rather than assuming it is gone. As with every service here, the controls only help if you use them — the defaults may not match your preferences.

Claude

Claude offers history and memory controls in general terms similar to other major chatbots: you can review and delete past chats, and you can control whether the chatbot keeps personal context. Its approach is often described as cautious: the service is designed to avoid keeping more than it needs, and it offers settings to manage what is stored. As always, the details live in the current privacy page and settings screens, which change over time. If you use Claude for work documents or long projects, spend a few minutes in the settings before your first upload.

Gemini

Gemini's privacy controls sit alongside Google's wider activity controls, which will feel familiar if you already use a Google account. You can usually review your activity, set how long it is kept, and delete it. Because Gemini is part of Google's ecosystem, your chats may interact with other Google settings, so it is worth checking the activity controls in your Google account as well as the chatbot's own settings. The names of these controls change, and availability can vary by account type and region, so check what your account actually offers.

DeepSeek

DeepSeek offers the standard controls in general terms: you can manage your chat history and delete conversations, and the service describes how chats are handled in its privacy policy. Because DeepSeek is a newer service for many users, its settings screens and policy wording may be less familiar, which is all the more reason to read the current privacy page before you start. Pay particular attention to where data is stored and how long it is kept, and keep sensitive personal details out of your chats, as you should with every service.

Copilot

Copilot's privacy controls are tied to your Microsoft account, so they sit alongside the privacy settings you may already use for Windows or Edge. You can usually manage your chat history and control how your data is used, and the settings are reached through your Microsoft account privacy pages as well as the chatbot itself. Because Copilot appears in several places — web, Windows, Edge, mobile — it is worth checking the settings in the place where you use it most. As with the others, the controls change over time, so a quick review every few months is a good habit.

Habits That Protect You on Any Chatbot

Controls help, but habits matter more. First, never paste anything into a chatbot that you would not want a stranger to read: account numbers, addresses, medical details, passwords, or other people's private information. Second, delete chats you no longer need instead of letting them pile up. Third, check your settings after every major update, because defaults can change. Fourth, if you use a chatbot for work, find out whether your employer or school has rules about it before you upload work documents. Finally, remember that no privacy setting makes a chatbot a safe place for secrets — the safest private detail is the one you never typed.

It also helps to think about who else can see your screen. If you chat on a shared computer or a work device, remember to sign out when you finish, and be aware that your chat history may be visible to anyone who uses the device next. The same goes for notifications: a chatbot app that shows message previews on a locked phone can reveal the topic of a private conversation. Small habits like these cost nothing and close gaps that settings alone cannot. Privacy is not one switch you flip once; it is a handful of ordinary habits, repeated, that keep your chats yours.

Questions people ask

Do chatbots use my chats to train their models?

It depends on the service and the settings you choose. Some services let you opt out of training use, while others use chats by default. Check the training-data control in the settings of the chatbot you use.

Can I delete my chat history?

Yes, major chatbots let you view and delete your chat history. Deleted chats may still be kept for a short period for safety reasons, so check the service's privacy page for the details.

What is chatbot memory, and can I turn it off?

Memory lets a chatbot remember facts about you, such as your name or preferences, between chats. Most services let you view, delete, or switch off memory in the settings.

Is it safe to paste personal details into a chatbot?

No. Avoid pasting account numbers, addresses, medical details, passwords, or other people's private information into any chatbot. Privacy settings help, but the safest detail is one you never typed.

Where do I find a chatbot's privacy settings?

Look in the chatbot's settings menu, usually under privacy, data, or account controls. Setting names and locations change between updates, so the service's help pages are the best guide.
