Aquarium Setup Checklist for Beginners

This checklist walks you through setting up your first aquarium, from the tank and filter to the first week of checks. Tick each item off as you finish it and the progress counter shows how far you have come. It covers general setup only, so always follow the guidance for the species you keep.

Before you start

Choose where the tank will live before you buy anything. Pick a spot away from direct sunlight and draughts, near a power socket, on a surface strong enough to hold a full tank of water. A tank is far heavier once it is filled, so the stand or table must be sturdy and level. Moving a full tank is not practical, so get the position right first.

Decide what you plan to keep before you shop. Different species need different water temperatures, tank sizes and care, and the equipment you need follows from that choice. Buy the tank, filter, heater and substrate first, and get the tank running before you bring any fish home. Rushing to add fish to a brand-new tank is the most common beginner mistake.

The first week

The first week is about watching, not fixing. Check the temperature each day and make sure the filter is running. The water may go cloudy for a while as the tank settles, which is common in a new setup. Resist the urge to change everything at once. Small, steady steps keep the water stable, and stable water is what fish need most.

Feed lightly in the first week. Uneaten food sinks and fouls the water, so give only what the fish eat in a short time and remove anything left over. A small partial water change during the week helps keep the water fresh. If you have a water test kit, use it to learn what your water is doing rather than guessing.

Common beginner mistakes

The most common mistake is adding too many fish too soon. A new tank needs time to settle, and each fish adds to the load on the filter. Add fish gradually, a few at a time, and let the tank adjust between additions. A second common mistake is overfeeding, which fouls the water faster than almost anything else.

Another mistake is cleaning too hard. Rinsing filter media in untreated tap water or scrubbing every surface can undo the good work your tank is doing. Clean gently, follow your filter maker's guidance, and remember that a healthy tank is a living system, not a piece of furniture. Patience in the first weeks pays off for years.

Questions people ask

How long should I wait before adding fish?

Wait until the tank is running steadily and the water is ready. The exact wait depends on your setup and the species you plan to keep, so follow a cycling guide for your tank rather than rushing.

Do I need a heater?

It depends on the species you keep and the room temperature. Tropical species need warm water, while coldwater species may not. Check the needs of your species before you decide.

How often should I change the water?

Regular small partial water changes are part of routine care. How often depends on the tank size and how many fish you keep, so follow the guidance for your setup.

Why is my new tank water cloudy?

Cloudy water is common in a new tank and often clears on its own as the tank settles. If it does not clear, or fish look unwell, check your filter and seek advice from a specialist.

Can I use tap water in my aquarium?

Tap water usually needs a water conditioner first, because it can contain substances that harm fish. Always treat tap water as your conditioner directs before adding it to the tank.
