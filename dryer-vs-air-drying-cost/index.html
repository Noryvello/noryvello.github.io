Tumble Dryer vs Air Drying Cost

Enter your loads per week, your dryer's power and drying time, and your energy rate, and this calculator shows what tumble drying costs you each year against the zero energy cost of air drying, plus how the time compares. Every number comes from you.

Air drying costs no energy, but it needs space and time, and damp weather slows it down. The dryer figure comes only from the numbers you enter, so check your dryer manual and your energy bill for the most accurate figures.

How the comparison works

A tumble dryer is one of the hungriest appliances in a home. It heats air and blows it through the drum for the whole cycle, and that power multiplied by time is what you pay for. The calculator works out the energy per load from your dryer's watts and your drying time, then turns it into money with your energy rate.

Air drying uses no energy at all, so its running cost is zero. The trade is time: clothes on a line or rack take hours instead of minutes. That is why the calculator asks for your air-drying time per load and shows the weekly time for each option beside the money.

The yearly figure multiplies the cost per load by your loads per week and by 52 weeks. A load that costs little on its own can add up to a noticeable yearly total when you dry several loads a week.

What changes the answer

Drying time is the lever you control most easily. A load spun well in the washer holds less water and dries faster. Overloading the dryer stretches the cycle, because the hot air cannot move through a packed drum. Smaller loads dry faster per item, even though they mean more cycles.

Your loads per week set the scale of the yearly figure. A household drying two loads a week pays far less over a year than one drying eight, even with the same dryer. That is why the calculator asks for your own number rather than assuming one.

The energy rate scales everything in proportion. A higher rate raises the yearly cost without changing the energy used, which is why the result shows the energy per load as well as the money: the energy figure stays true whatever your rate does.

Reading your result

Look at the yearly dryer cost first, then at the time comparison. If the yearly cost is small and the time saved matters to you, the dryer is earning its keep. If the yearly cost is large and you have the space and the time, air drying some or all of your loads is the cheapest change you can make to laundry day.

Many households land in the middle: air drying most loads and keeping the dryer for towels, bedding and wet days. To price that mix, enter only the loads you would actually put in the dryer, and the result shows what the dryer really costs you.

Questions people ask

Does air drying really cost nothing?

Air drying uses no energy, so its running cost is zero. It still needs space, a line or rack, and time. This calculator compares energy cost only, so it does not price the space or the waiting.

Where do I find my dryer's watts?

Check the rating plate on the back or side of the dryer, or the manual. It lists the power in watts. A typical tumble dryer uses far more power than a washing machine, which is why drying costs add up.

Why does the calculator ask for air-drying time?

Cost is only half the comparison. Air drying is free but slow, so the calculator shows the time each option takes per week, letting you weigh money against time.

Can I mix drying methods?

Yes. Many households air dry most loads and use the dryer only for towels, bedding or wet days. To price a mix, enter only the loads you would actually put in the dryer.

Does a shorter drying cycle save much?

It saves in direct proportion to the time saved, because the cost comes from power multiplied by time. Spinning the load well in the washer first also shortens the drying time.
