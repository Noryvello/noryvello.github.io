How to Use Gemini: A Beginner's Guide

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

Gemini is Google's AI chat assistant, and you can use it on the web or on your phone to ask questions, draft text, and brainstorm ideas. You type or speak a prompt, read the answer, and keep the conversation going with follow-up questions. This guide walks through the basics so you can start with confidence.

Gemini is the name of Google's AI assistant, and it works much like a chat with a knowledgeable helper. You ask a question or describe a task, it replies in plain language, and you can keep the conversation going until you have what you need. There is no special language to learn and no technical setup — if you can write a message, you can use Gemini.

This guide covers the first steps: where to find Gemini, how to sign in, how to write a first prompt that gets a useful answer, and how to use follow-up questions to improve the result. It also explains the free and paid tiers in general terms and lists good first tasks to try.

Gemini's menus, buttons, and features change with updates, so the steps here describe the general flow rather than a fixed path. If a button has moved or a feature looks different, use the help pages in the app to find the current layout.

What Gemini is and where to find it

Gemini is a conversational AI assistant made by Google. You can use it to ask questions, draft and edit text, summarise information, brainstorm ideas, plan tasks, and more. It understands everyday language, so you do not need to learn commands or special formatting to get started.

You can reach Gemini in two main places: on the web through your browser, and on your phone through the Gemini app or through the Google app. Both versions share the same account, so a conversation you start on your computer can be continued on your phone. The exact way Gemini appears on a phone can vary by device and region, and Google changes the placement from time to time.

Gemini also appears inside other Google products, such as Gmail and Google Docs, where it can help with drafting and summarising. Those uses are covered in a separate guide, and they depend on your account type and region.

Signing in and starting your first chat

To use Gemini you generally sign in with a Google account. Open Gemini on the web or on your phone, sign in, and you will see a chat box ready for your first message. If you already use Gmail or other Google services, you are most of the way there.

Your first chat is just a conversation. Type a question or a task and send it, then read the answer. There is no need to set anything up first, and there is no wrong way to begin. A simple question — asking for an explanation, a summary, or a list of ideas — is a good first step.

Conversations are saved in your account so you can return to them later. Give a useful conversation a clear name if the app lets you, so you can find it again. Starting a fresh chat for a new topic keeps your conversations tidy and helps Gemini stay focused on the current subject.

Writing your first prompt

A prompt is simply the message you send. The best prompts say three things: what you want, some context, and how you want the answer shaped. Instead of asking for help with an email, try describing the situation, who the email is for, and the tone you want. The extra detail takes seconds to add and makes a large difference to the answer.

Here is a weak prompt and a stronger version of the same request. Weak: help me write a birthday message. Stronger: help me write a short, warm birthday message for my grandmother, who loves gardening, in two or three sentences. The stronger prompt gives Gemini something specific to work with, and the result will show it.

If the first answer is not what you wanted, do not start over. Tell Gemini what to change: make it shorter, make it friendlier, use simpler words, or give a different example. Short corrections like these are the fastest way to get a good result, and they are the core skill of using any chatbot well.

Using follow-up questions

The real power of a chatbot is the conversation, not the single answer. After Gemini replies, you can ask it to explain a part in more detail, to give an example, to argue the other side, or to turn the answer into a list or a plan. Each follow-up builds on what came before, so the conversation gets more useful as it goes on.

Follow-ups are also how you check an answer. If a claim surprises you, ask where it comes from or ask for the reasoning behind it. If an answer is vague, ask for specifics. If two answers seem to disagree, point that out and ask Gemini to sort it out. Treat the conversation as a discussion, not a search box.

When a conversation drifts or the answers start repeating themselves, start a fresh chat with a clearer prompt. A new chat is a clean slate, and it often produces a better answer than a long, tangled thread.

Using Gemini on your phone

Gemini on a phone works much like the web version, with a few extras that suit a small screen. You can speak your prompt instead of typing it, which is handy when your hands are busy or you are walking. You can also share a photo and ask questions about it, or ask Gemini to help with something you are looking at.

The phone version is good for quick, everyday tasks: settling a question while cooking, drafting a reply while commuting, or planning the next hour of your day. For longer work, such as writing a document or studying a long text, the larger screen of a computer is usually more comfortable.

Voice conversations are a separate feature called Gemini Live, which lets you talk back and forth in a flowing conversation. It is covered in its own guide, and it is worth trying once you are comfortable with typed chats.

Free and paid tiers, in brief

Gemini has a free tier and a paid tier, and the names of the tiers can change over time. The free tier covers everyday use: asking questions, drafting text, and brainstorming. The paid tier adds higher limits and access to newer models and extra features, and it is aimed at people who use Gemini heavily or want the latest capabilities.

For most beginners, the free tier is the right place to start. It shows you what Gemini can do and where its limits fall for your kind of use. If you later find yourself hitting limits or wanting a feature that is paid-only, you can look at the current plan details and decide then. This guide does not quote prices, because they change — check the current pricing page for the latest details.

Whatever tier you use, the quality of your results depends mostly on your prompts. A clear, detailed prompt on the free tier will outperform a vague prompt on the paid tier, so learning to prompt well is worth more than any upgrade.

Good first tasks to try

If you are not sure where to start, try one of these. Each one is a small, safe task that shows what Gemini does well.

  • Explain something: Ask Gemini to explain a topic you find confusing, in simple words, with an everyday example.
  • Draft a message: Ask for a polite reply to a message you received, then edit the draft to sound like you.
  • Plan your day: List your tasks and ask Gemini to arrange them into a realistic schedule.
  • Brainstorm ideas: Ask for ten ideas for a gift, a party theme, or a blog post, then pick the best three.
  • Summarise a text: Paste a long article and ask for a three-sentence summary and the three most important points.

After each task, notice what worked and what did not. Did the answer need a follow-up? Was a detail wrong? Did a clearer prompt give a better result? These small observations build your skill faster than any guide.

Limits to keep in mind

Gemini can be confidently wrong. It may invent a fact, misstate a date, or give advice that sounds reasonable but is incorrect. For anything important — health, money, legal matters, schoolwork you will submit — check the answer against a trusted source. AI is a starting point, not an authority.

Gemini also has no access to your private life unless you tell it. It cannot see your calendar, your files, or your messages unless you share them in the conversation, and you should think carefully before sharing anything personal. Never paste passwords, financial details, or sensitive personal information into any AI chat.

Finally, remember that Gemini's answers reflect patterns in its training, not your values or your judgement. It can help you write, plan, and learn, but the decisions are yours. Use it as a tool that saves time and sparks ideas, and keep the thinking for yourself.

Questions people ask

Is Gemini free to use?

Yes, Gemini has a free tier that covers everyday use such as asking questions and drafting text. A paid tier adds higher limits and newer features. Prices change, so check the current pricing page for the latest details.

Do I need a Google account?

In general, yes. Gemini is a Google product, and signing in with a Google account lets it save your conversations and work across your devices. The exact sign-in steps depend on where you use it.

Can Gemini search the web for current information?

Gemini can look up current information on the web for many questions, though the details of how it does this change over time. For important facts, always check the answer against a trusted source yourself.

Can I use Gemini without installing an app?

Yes, you can use Gemini on the web through your browser, without installing anything. The app adds convenience on a phone, such as voice input, but the web version covers the same core features.

Does Gemini remember my chats?

Conversations are saved in your account, and you can review or delete them through the activity controls. Check the privacy settings to see what is stored and to adjust what Gemini keeps.
