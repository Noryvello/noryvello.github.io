How to read the scale

Scoville Heat Units (SHU) express chili pungency associated with capsaicinoids. The figures above are commonly cited approximate ranges, not guaranteed values for every pepper. Black pepper's piperine and ginger's gingerols create a different kind of pungency and are not assigned comparable Scoville ratings.

Choosing a heat level

  • Start with a small amount when using an unfamiliar chili.
  • Remove seeds and inner membranes only if that suits the recipe; most capsaicin is concentrated in the white placental tissue, not the seeds themselves.
  • Do not assume two peppers of the same variety have identical heat.
  • Use the heat picker as a reference, not as a promise about a particular pepper.
Important limit: Heat varies by variety and growing conditions; the ranges are guides, not guarantees.

Questions people ask

What is the Scoville scale?

The Scoville scale describes chili pepper pungency in Scoville Heat Units, or SHU. Modern values are generally based on measuring capsaicinoids rather than human taste tests.

Which common chili is the hottest in this guide?

The Carolina Reaper has a commonly reported range around 1.4 million to 2.2 million SHU, although individual peppers vary and records or cultivars can change.

Do black pepper and ginger have Scoville ratings?

No standard Scoville rating applies to black pepper or ginger because their pungent compounds are not capsaicin measured on the chili Scoville scale.

Can I rely on a pepper's heat range?

Use ranges as broad guides only. Variety, growing conditions, maturity and handling can change heat, and individual peppers can differ substantially.
