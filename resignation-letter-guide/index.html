What a resignation letter needs

A short resignation letter needs five parts: a greeting, a clear statement that you are resigning, your last working day, a thank-you line, and a closing with your name. The builder above puts all five together for you. You can add a line offering to help with the handover — it is already included in the draft.

What to do — and what not to do

Do:

  • Keep it short — a few lines are enough.
  • Thank your employer for the opportunity.
  • Offer to help with the handover.

Don't:

  • Don't list complaints or blame anyone.
  • Don't burn bridges — you may need a reference one day.
  • Don't resign in anger — write the letter when you are calm.

Before you send it

Read your letter once more before you send it, and check the date and the notice period against your own contract. Keep a copy for yourself, and hand the letter to your manager in person if you can — a short, polite conversation goes with the letter, not instead of it.

Questions people ask

How do I write a resignation letter?

Keep it short and polite. Say that you are resigning, name your role, give your last working day, thank your employer, and offer to help with the handover. Fill in the builder above and your letter is ready to copy.

How much notice should I give?

Check the notice period in your own contract first — that is the one to follow. If your contract does not state a notice period, ask your employer what they expect before you send your letter.

What should I not say in a resignation letter?

Do not list complaints, blame anyone, or criticise your employer. A resignation letter is not the place for grievances — keep it short, polite and professional, and save any feedback for a conversation if you are asked.

Do I need to give a reason for leaving?

No. You can simply say that you are resigning — you do not have to explain why. If you want to, you can add one short line, such as saying you are moving on to a new opportunity.
