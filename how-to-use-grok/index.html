How to Use Grok: A Beginner's Guide

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

Grok is a chat-based AI assistant you can talk to in plain words, with access to real-time information that can help with current events and fast-moving topics. You can ask questions, get explanations, brainstorm ideas, and generate images from a description. This guide shows you where Grok is available and how to get useful answers from it.

Grok is an AI chatbot with a particular strength: it can draw on real-time information, which makes it useful for questions about current events, breaking news, and fast-moving topics. For everyday questions, it works like any other chatbot: you type a message, it answers, and you can keep the conversation going.

This guide covers where to find Grok, how to write prompts that get good answers, how to use its real-time information wisely, and what to do with its image generation. As with any fast-changing tool, the details here are in general terms, and the interface may look different on your screen.

Grok's features, access, and limits change often, and real-time information can still be wrong or incomplete. Check important claims against a trusted source, and never share private details in a chat. Treat fast-moving news from any AI tool as a starting point, not a confirmed report.

What Grok is and where to find it

Grok is a chat-based AI assistant developed by xAI. You can use it on the web and on your phone, through its own app and website, and it is also available inside the X platform for users there. Wherever you open it, the experience is the same: a chat window where you type questions in plain words.

Access and limits vary, and they have changed over time, so check the current options where you find Grok. Some features may need an account, and some may be limited to certain plans or regions.

Your first conversation

Start with a simple question to get a feel for the answers. Try explain black holes in plain words or give me three ideas for a birthday gift for someone who likes cooking. Grok will answer in the chat window, and you can reply to refine it.

Notice the tone: Grok is known for a direct, sometimes witty style. If you want a straighter answer, say so in your follow-up, such as give me the same answer in a plain, serious tone. The assistant will adjust, and you will learn quickly how to steer it.

Writing prompts that work

Good prompts are specific. Instead of tell me about space, try explain why the moon looks bigger near the horizon, in about 100 words, for a curious adult. The detail about length and audience shapes the answer far more than the topic alone.

Follow-ups are where the value is. After the first answer, ask for an example, a simpler version, or a comparison with something else. Each follow-up builds on the same conversation, so you do not need to repeat the original question.

Real-time information: what it is good for

Grok's access to real-time information makes it useful for questions about things happening now: a sports result, a market move, a breaking story, or a new announcement. Ask what happened, when, and what is known so far, and Grok can summarize the latest available information.

Real-time does not mean reliable. Early reports are often wrong or incomplete, and Grok can repeat an unconfirmed claim as if it were settled. For anything important, check a trusted news source directly, and be extra careful with fast-moving stories where the facts are still changing.

Generating images in general terms

Grok can generate images from a written description. Describe the subject, the setting, the style, and the mood, and it will produce an image you can download and use. Detailed descriptions work better than vague ones, and you can refine the result by adjusting the description and generating again.

The usual limits apply: text inside images often comes out wrong, faces and hands can look odd, and fine details may not match your description. Treat the first image as a draft, and keep the description simple if the results keep missing the mark.

Everyday uses: news, learning, ideas, and fun

For news, Grok is a quick way to catch up on a story before you read the full reporting. For learning, it can explain a concept, quiz you, or walk you through a problem step by step. For ideas, it can brainstorm names, outlines, and plans. For fun, it can write a joke, a story, or a playful take on a topic.

The common thread is conversation. Grok works best when you treat it as a sparring partner: ask, react, push back, and ask again. A single question gives you a single answer, but a short conversation usually gives you something better.

Limits worth knowing

Grok can be confidently wrong, especially about fast-moving events where the information is incomplete. It can also repeat rumours and mix up similar stories. Its witty tone can make a shaky answer sound more certain than it is, so read critically.

It also cannot see your files, your calendar, or your private data unless you share them, and it cannot act on your behalf. Use it for drafting, explaining, and exploring, and verify anything that matters before you rely on it.

Grok for quick research

Grok works well as a first stop for quick research: ask for background on a topic, a summary of a story, or an explanation of a new term. Because it can draw on recent information, it is useful when the topic is still developing and older sources have not caught up.

Treat the first answer as a sketch, not a source. Ask Grok where the information comes from, then open the original reporting or the official page yourself. A two-minute check separates a solid summary from a confident guess, and it is the habit that makes quick research trustworthy.
