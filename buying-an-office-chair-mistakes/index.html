7 Mistakes to Avoid When Buying an Office Chair

The most common mistake when buying an office chair is choosing it for how it looks instead of how it adjusts to your body and your desk. This guide covers the seven mistakes buyers make most often, plus an adjustability checklist to use before you buy.

An office chair is something you sit in for hours every day, which makes it one of the most personal purchases for a home or office. A chair that fits you fades into the background and lets you work; a chair that does not fit nags at you all day long.

The key is adjustability. Bodies and desks come in different sizes, so a chair that adjusts to you is worth far more than a chair that merely looks good. The mistakes below cover what to check and what to adjust.

This guide is general buying advice about fit and comfort. It makes no health claims — comfort is personal, so try a chair before you buy it if you can.

The 7 mistakes to avoid

  1. Buying for looks alone. A chair that matches the room is tempting, but you sit in a chair for hours, not just look at it. A beautiful chair that does not adjust to your body will disappoint you every single day, while a plain chair that fits you well will make working far more comfortable. Judge a chair by sitting in it, not by how it photographs.
  2. Ignoring seat height range. Seat height decides whether the chair fits your desk and your body together. Check the full range of adjustment, not just the middle setting, and check that you can set the seat to a height where you sit comfortably at your desk with your feet supported. A chair whose lowest setting is still too high, or whose highest setting is still too low, will never fit you properly.
  3. Forgetting the armrests. Fixed armrests can push you away from the desk or stop the chair sliding under it, which is a daily annoyance. Check whether the armrests adjust in height, whether they move in and out, or whether they can be removed. If you like to sit close to your desk, armrests that slide under it — or no armrests at all — may suit you better than fixed ones.
  4. Skipping the backrest check. The backrest should adjust to support you where you need it, not where the maker guessed you need it. Check whether the backrest moves up and down, whether it tilts, and whether any lower-back support adjusts in position. Sit in the chair for a few minutes and notice whether the backrest meets your back comfortably or leaves a gap.
  5. Overlooking seat depth. A seat that is too deep presses behind your knees and stops you sitting back properly, while a seat that is too shallow leaves your legs without support. Check the depth of the seat against your legs, and check whether the seat slides forwards and backwards on the chair. Seat depth is easy to overlook and hard to fix later, so check it before you buy.
  6. Ignoring the castors and base. The wheels and the base decide whether the chair rolls smoothly and feels stable. Check that the castors suit your floor — hard wheels can mark hard floors, while soft wheels roll better on them. Give the chair a push and notice whether the base feels steady or tips easily. A stable base and smooth castors make the chair feel solid every time you sit down.
  7. Not trying it before you buy. A chair is one of the few purchases where a few minutes of sitting tells you almost everything you need to know. If you can, sit in the chair, adjust the seat and backrest, and notice how it feels after a few minutes — not just the first ten seconds. If you are buying online, read the returns policy carefully, because a chair that looks right on screen can feel wrong after a day of sitting.

Before you buy

Once the checklist is done, take a final look at the practical details. Measure the space where the chair will live, including room to roll back from the desk and to swivel. Check how the chair is delivered — some arrive assembled, others need putting together — and check the warranty. Then sit in it one more time, adjust everything to suit you, and trust what your body tells you: the chair that feels comfortable after a few minutes is the one to take home.

Questions people ask

What should I check first when buying an office chair?

Check the seat height range first, because it decides whether the chair fits your desk and your body. Then check the armrests, the backrest adjustment and the seat depth.

Do I need armrests on an office chair?

Armrests suit many people, but fixed armrests can push you away from the desk. If you choose armrests, check whether they adjust in height or can be removed.

How do I know if an office chair fits me?

Sit in the chair and check that you can adjust the seat height so you sit comfortably at your desk with your feet supported, and that the backrest supports you where you need it. A short sit tells you more than a photo.

Mesh or padded seat — which is better?

Neither is better for everyone. Mesh feels cooler in warm rooms, while padding feels softer at first. Sit in both if you can and choose the one that feels comfortable to you.

Should I buy an office chair online without trying it?

You can, but check the returns policy carefully first. A chair that looks right on screen can feel wrong after a few minutes of sitting, so knowing you can return it matters.
