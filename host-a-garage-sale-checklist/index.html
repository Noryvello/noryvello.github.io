Garage Sale Hosting Checklist

This checklist takes you from sorting your items to packing up at the end of the day, with steps for pricing, signage and setup. Tick each item off as you go, and check the pricing guide linked below before you set your prices.

Work through the list in order, and tick each step off when it is done.

Check your local rules before you set up. Some areas need a permit for a sale, and some have rules about signs on public land. This page is general planning help, not local legal advice.

Sort before you price

The sorting is the hardest part, and it is worth doing properly. Go through each room in turn and sort items into three piles: sell, keep and give away. Be honest about the keep pile: if you have not used something in a year and have no plan to, it belongs in one of the other two. Sorting first means you price what you actually have, not what you think you have.

Clean everything you plan to sell, and test anything that runs on batteries or plugs in. A clean item with a price label sells far more easily than a dusty one without, and a buyer who finds out something does not work will remember it for the rest of the sale.

Pricing without the guesswork

Pricing is where most garage sales go wrong, in both directions. Price too high and nothing sells; price too low and you give things away. The pricing guide linked at the bottom of this page is a good starting point: it explains how to think about condition, age and what similar items sell for, without guessing.

Price every item one by one, and put a clear label on each one. Buyers do not like asking for prices, and many will walk away rather than ask. A label also stops the awkward moment when two buyers ask about the same item at different times and get different answers.

Signs people can actually read

Signs are how most buyers find you, so make them big, simple and readable from a moving car. The date, the time and the address are the only words that matter, plus an arrow where the road turns. Dark letters on a light background, large enough to read from across the street, will do more than a clever design nobody can read.

Put signs out at nearby junctions the day before, and check them on the morning of the sale: wind and rain knock signs down, and a missing sign at a key junction can cost you half your buyers. Take them all down again when the sale is over.

Setting up on the day

Set up tables and lay items out so people can see and reach them without moving things around. Group similar items together, and put your best items where people see them first. Keep the cash box where you can see it at all times, and prepare small change before the first buyer arrives.

Ask a friend or family member to help. Two people can watch the tables, answer questions and take breaks, where one person on their own has to do everything at once. Keep bags and newspaper handy for buyers who need to carry things home.

After the sale

Decide an end time in advance and stick to it. When the time comes, start packing up: take down the signs first, then sort what is left. Unsold items go to one of three places — given away, stored, or kept for a future sale — and deciding on the day is better than leaving boxes in the garage for months.

Count the money, put it away safely, and take a moment to enjoy the space you have cleared. A garage sale is as much about clearing room as it is about selling, and the empty shelves are part of the reward.

Questions people ask

When is the best time to hold a garage sale?

A weekend morning suits most buyers, when people are free and the light is good. Check the weather forecast as the date gets closer, and have a plan for rain, such as a covered area or a backup date.

How should I price my items?

Start with the garage sale pricing guide linked at the bottom of this page. It explains how to think about condition, age and what similar items sell for. Price every item one by one, and put a clear label on each one.

Do I need a permit for a garage sale?

It depends on where you live. Some areas need a permit, and some have rules about signs on public land. Check your local rules before you set up, and do it early enough to apply if you need to.

What should I do with unsold items?

Sort them on the day: give away what someone else can use, store what is worth keeping, and keep only what you have a real plan for. Leaving boxes in the garage for months undoes the point of the sale.

How do I handle payments?

Prepare small change and a cash box before the first buyer arrives, and keep the cash box where you can see it. Put larger notes away as the day goes on, and decide in advance whether you will accept anything other than cash.
