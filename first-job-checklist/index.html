What Should You Sort Out in Your First Month at a New Job?

Short answer: In your first month, confirm the documents and details your employer requires, understand how and when you are paid, learn the basic workplace rules, and build dependable habits around attendance, communication and record-keeping. Ask your employer when anything is unclear.

Week 1: documents and essential details

  • Read your offer or employment agreement and clarify your role, start date, work location and reporting contact.
  • Ask which identity, tax, bank or work-eligibility documents are required in your country and for your role.
  • Confirm how to submit timesheets, attendance or leave requests.
  • Save copies of documents you are allowed to keep, using a secure location.
  • Learn who to contact about payroll, equipment, scheduling and workplace support.

Week 2: understand the money basics

  • Confirm pay frequency, pay date and how your pay statement is delivered.
  • Check what deductions or contributions may appear and who can explain them.
  • Make a simple budget based on take-home pay once you have reliable figures.
  • Plan for transport, meals, work clothing or other job-related routine costs.
  • Keep a record of hours worked and approved changes using the employer's process.

Weeks 3–4: build habits and confidence

  • Arrive ready to start and learn the process for reporting delays or absences.
  • Keep a short task list and write down instructions you may need again.
  • Ask questions early rather than guessing about safety, quality or deadlines.
  • Send concise progress updates and raise blockers promptly.
  • Request feedback and agree on the next skills to develop.
  • Review your first month: what is clear, what is confusing and what support would help?

Keep a simple personal record

Maintain a secure folder for permitted copies of your agreement, pay statements, training records and important workplace communications. Follow your employer's confidentiality rules; do not copy customer, company or restricted information to personal storage.

A note before you act: Requirements differ by country and employer; confirm with your employer. This checklist is a general starting point, not a substitute for local employment, tax or immigration guidance.

Questions people ask

What documents do I need for my first job?

The required documents depend on your country, employer and role. Ask HR or your manager for the official list and submit documents only through approved channels.

What should I check on my first pay statement?

Check the pay period, recorded hours where relevant, gross pay, deductions and take-home amount. Ask payroll to explain anything you do not recognize.

Should I make a budget before my first paycheck?

You can list expected costs in advance, but build the working budget around confirmed take-home pay and actual expenses once you have them.

What should I do if I do not understand a task at work?

Ask your manager or the appropriate teammate for clarification, repeat back the expected outcome and note the instructions for next time.
