Eid al-Fitr marks the end of Ramadan, the month of fasting, and it is a day for celebration, gratitude and togetherness. Families gather, friends exchange visits and calls, and greetings fly between people near and far. A good Eid message does not need to be long or fancy. What does Eid Mubarak mean?

"Eid Mubarak" means "Blessed Eid". It is the greeting Muslims share at both Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, and it carries a simple wish: that the festival brings blessing, joy and peace to the person you are greeting. It works in a card, a text message, a phone call or face to face, and it needs no special occasion beyond Eid itself.

Replying is easy. You can answer with the same greeting, "Eid Mubarak to you too", which is always welcome, or add a wish of your own, such as "May your Eid be blessed as well". If someone greets you first, a warm reply matters more than a clever one. A short, sincere answer, sent promptly, is the best reply there is.

When to send Eid greetings

Eid greetings are sent from the sighting of the Shawwal moon, which marks the end of Ramadan, through the days of Eid. In practice, most people send their messages on the evening before Eid, once the moon has been sighted, or early on Eid morning. Messages sent during the days of Eid are still welcome, so do not worry if yours arrives a day or two after the first morning.
