How the 50/30/20 rule works

The 50/30/20 rule splits your take-home pay — the amount that actually reaches your bank account — into three parts. Half goes to needs, just under a third to wants, and a fifth to savings. The table below shows what each part usually covers.

PartShareWhat it covers
Needs50%Rent or mortgage, food, utilities, transport to work, insurance, minimum debt payments
Wants30%Eating out, holidays, hobbies, streaming, new clothes
Savings20%Emergency fund, extra debt payments, long-term saving

Adjusting the split

The 50/30/20 split is a starting point, not a fixed law. If your needs cost more than 50% of your pay, raise the needs percentage and lower wants or savings until the split matches your real life. If your needs are low, you can push savings above 20% — the tool shows the new amounts straight away, and it warns you if your percentages no longer add up to 100%.

Questions people ask

What is the 50/30/20 budget rule?

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to split your take-home pay: 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings. It is a starting guide, not a rule you must follow exactly — you can adjust the percentages to fit your own situation.

What counts as a need in the 50/30/20 rule?

Needs are the things you must pay for to live and work: rent or mortgage, food, utilities, transport to work, insurance and minimum debt payments. Anything you could live without, even if you enjoy it, is a want rather than a need.

What if my needs cost more than 50%?

Then the 50% figure does not fit your situation, and that is fine — many people spend more than half their pay on needs. Try trimming wants first, and save what you can, even if it is less than 20% for now. The split is a starting point to adjust, not a pass-or-fail test.

How much of my paycheck should I save?

The 50/30/20 rule suggests 20% of your take-home pay for savings. If 20% is out of reach right now, start with a smaller amount you can keep up every payday — saving something regularly matters more than hitting an exact percentage.
