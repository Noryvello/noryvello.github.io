7 Mistakes to Avoid When Buying a Vacuum Cleaner

The most common mistake when buying a vacuum cleaner is choosing the wrong type for your floors and household, not the wrong brand. This guide covers the seven mistakes buyers make most often, and the picker below matches a cleaner type to your home.

A vacuum cleaner is one of those purchases that seems simple until you start comparing models. Upright, canister, stick, robot, handheld — every type suits a different home, and the one your neighbour loves may be a poor fit for your floors. The good news is that most buying mistakes are easy to avoid once you know what to look for.

The trick is to start with your home, not with the shelf. Think about your floors, the size of your household, whether you have pets, and where the cleaner will live. A cleaner that matches your home gets used often and lasts longer in daily service; a cleaner that fights your home ends up gathering dust in a cupboard.

This guide is general buying advice, not a product review. Cleaner types and features vary between models and makers, so check the details of any model before you buy.

The 7 mistakes to avoid

  1. Buying for suction power alone. Headline suction figures tell you very little about how a cleaner handles your floors. What matters more is the brush head, the seal between the head and the floor, and whether the cleaner suits your floor type. A cleaner that performs well on hard floors can struggle on deep carpet, and a cleaner built for carpet can scatter dirt across a hard floor. Read about the brush head and the floor settings instead of chasing the biggest number on the box.
  2. Ignoring your floor mix. Carpet needs a powered brush head that lifts dirt out of the pile, while hard floors need soft wheels and a head that will not scratch the surface. If your home has both, you need a cleaner that switches between the two easily. Buying a cleaner built for one floor type and using it on the other is one of the fastest routes to disappointment, so be honest about what your floors actually are before you shop.
  3. Forgetting about weight and handling. A heavy cleaner gets used less often, especially if you carry it up and down stairs or between rooms. Before you buy, think about who will use it and where it will go. Can you lift it comfortably? Does it steer easily around furniture? Can it reach under beds and sofas? A cleaner that is easy to push, carry and store will get far more use than a powerful one that feels like a workout.
  4. Overlooking filters and dustbins. Bagged cleaners keep dust sealed away when you change the bag, while bagless cleaners save you buying bags but need regular emptying and filter cleaning. Either way, check the size of the bin or bag against your household — a small bin in a busy family home means constant emptying. Also check whether the filter is washable and how easy it is to reach, because a filter that is hard to clean is a filter that never gets cleaned.
  5. Choosing the wrong power source. Cordless cleaners are wonderfully convenient, but their run time limits how much you can clean in one go. Corded cleaners run as long as you do, but the cord tethers you to a socket and needs winding away after each clean. Think about the size of your home and the length of a typical clean. If a cordless cleaner cannot finish your home on one charge, it will frustrate you every time you use it.
  6. Skipping the attachments. The main head does most of the work, but the attachments decide whether you can clean stairs, furniture, car seats and tight corners. A crevice tool, an upholstery brush and a pet hair head each earn their place if you actually need them. Check what is included in the box before you buy, and check whether extra attachments are easy to find later, because buying them separately can be a hassle.
  7. Not checking noise, storage and spares. Noise matters in flats and shared homes, and in any home where someone sleeps during the day. Storage matters too — measure the cupboard or corner where the cleaner will live, and check whether it stands upright on its own. Finally, check that filters, bags and belts for the model are easy to find. A cleaner whose spares are hard to get becomes a short-lived purchase.

Before you buy

Once you know the type you need, slow down for a few final checks. Measure the storage space and check the cleaner fits, including its height if it stands upright. If you can, lift the cleaner in the shop and push it around for a minute — weight and steering are hard to judge from a description. Read the manual or the maker's page for filter care and emptying, so you know what looking after it involves. And check the returns policy, because a cleaner that seems fine in the shop can feel very different after a week of real cleaning at home.

Questions people ask

Should I buy a cordless vacuum cleaner?

It depends on the size of your home and how long your cleans take. Cordless cleaners suit quick cleans and smaller homes, while larger homes may need a corded cleaner or a cordless model with enough run time to finish the job in one go.

Are bagless vacuum cleaners better than bagged ones?

Neither is better for everyone. Bagless cleaners save you buying bags but need regular bin emptying and filter cleaning, while bagged cleaners keep dust sealed inside the bag when you throw it away.

What matters most for pet hair?

A powered brush head that lifts hair from carpet, a filter that traps fine dust, and tools for furniture and stairs matter most. Check that the brush head is easy to clean of tangled hair.

How often should I clean the filter?

Follow the guidance from the maker of your cleaner. A clogged filter reduces suction, so check it regularly and clean or replace it when it looks dirty.

Can one vacuum cleaner handle carpet and hard floors?

Yes, if it has a head that switches between carpet and hard floors. Check that the switch is easy to reach while you clean and that the wheels suit hard floors.
