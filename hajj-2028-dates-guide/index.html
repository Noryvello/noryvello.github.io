Hajj 2028 Dates Guide

Hajj 2028 is expected to take place in the first days of Dhul-Hijjah 1449, with the Day of Arafah on Thursday 4 May 2028 and Eid al-Adha on Friday 5 May. The month of Dhul-Hijjah is expected to begin on Wednesday 26 April 2028. All dates are expected and depend on the sighting of the moon.

Hajj is the annual pilgrimage to Makkah, and one of the five pillars of Islam. Every year, Muslims from around the world travel to Makkah to perform the pilgrimage in the month of Dhul-Hijjah, the twelfth month of the Islamic calendar. The pilgrimage takes place over several days, with the Day of Arafah at its heart.

This page gives the expected dates for Hajj 2028: the start of Dhul-Hijjah, the Day of Arafah, and Eid al-Adha. It explains each date in plain terms and shows how the dates are set. It is a dates guide only — it does not give detailed ritual instruction.

All dates on this page are expected and depend on the sighting of the moon. The Hajj dates are announced following the sighting of the moon of Dhul-Hijjah, and they can shift by a day. This page gives dates only — for the rites of Hajj, follow the guidance of your scholar or Hajj group, and for the confirmed dates, follow the official announcement.

1 Dhul-Hijjah — Wednesday 26 April 2028

Dhul-Hijjah is the twelfth and final month of the Islamic calendar, and the month in which the Hajj takes place. In 2028, the month is expected to begin on Wednesday 26 April, the first day of Dhul-Hijjah 1449.

The first ten days of Dhul-Hijjah are considered among the best days of the year. Many Muslims who are not on Hajj use these days for extra prayer, fasting, and charity, and the ninth day — the Day of Arafah — is widely observed as a day of fasting.

Day of Arafah — Thursday 4 May 2028

The Day of Arafah is the ninth day of Dhul-Hijjah, and the most important day of the Hajj. On this day, pilgrims gather on the plain of Arafat, near Makkah, and spend the day in prayer and reflection. Standing at Arafat is the heart of the pilgrimage — the Hajj is not complete without it.

For Muslims who are not on Hajj, the Day of Arafah is a day of fasting and dua. It is considered one of the best days of the year, and many people spend it in prayer and in asking for forgiveness.

Eid al-Adha — Friday 5 May 2028

Eid al-Adha, the Festival of Sacrifice, falls on the tenth day of Dhul-Hijjah, the day after the Day of Arafah. In 2028 it is expected on Friday 5 May. The day is celebrated by Muslims around the world, whether or not they are on Hajj.

The day begins with the Eid prayer in the morning. After the prayer, those who are able offer Qurbani, the sacrifice of an animal, and the meat is shared with family, friends, and those in need. The celebrations continue for four days in total — the day of Eid and the three days of Tashreeq that follow it.

How the Hajj dates are set

The Hajj dates follow the Islamic lunar calendar. The month of Dhul-Hijjah begins with the sighting of the new crescent moon, and the Day of Arafah and Eid al-Adha fall on the ninth and tenth days of the month. The calculated calendar gives a strong guide, but the confirmed dates depend on the actual sighting.

In practice, the dates are announced by the authorities in Saudi Arabia, where the Hajj takes place, once the moon of Dhul-Hijjah has been sighted. Most countries follow that announcement for the Hajj dates, and many also follow it for Eid al-Adha, though some countries make their own announcement.

Because the Islamic year is about eleven days shorter than the Gregorian year, the Hajj dates move earlier by about eleven days each year. That is why Hajj falls in May in 2028, having fallen in the middle of the year in earlier years.

What Hajj is

Hajj is the pilgrimage to Makkah that every Muslim who is able is required to perform once in a lifetime. It is one of the five pillars of Islam, alongside the declaration of faith, prayer, charity, and fasting in Ramadan.

The pilgrimage takes place over several days in Dhul-Hijjah. Pilgrims wear simple white garments, circle the Kaaba, walk between the hills of Safa and Marwa, stand at Arafat, and take part in the other rites of the pilgrimage. The rites retrace the story of the Prophet Ibrahim and his family, and they are the same rites the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ performed on his Farewell Pilgrimage.

Hajj is a journey of equality: rich and poor, young and old, from every country on earth, stand side by side in the same simple clothing. For those who perform it, it is often described as the most meaningful journey of their lives.

Planning around the dates

If you are planning to perform Hajj in 2028, the dates on this page are the starting point for planning — but they are expected dates, not confirmed ones. The confirmed dates follow the moon sighting, and the practical arrangements for the pilgrimage are made through the official channels of your country.

Travel for Hajj is arranged well in advance, and the number of pilgrims from each country is set by quota. Anyone hoping to go should begin the process early and follow the guidance of their country's Hajj authority.

For those not travelling, the dates are still worth knowing. The Day of Arafah is a day of fasting and prayer for Muslims everywhere, and Eid al-Adha is a festival for the whole community. Planning time off around Friday 5 May 2028, with the possibility of the date moving by a day, is the sensible approach.
