Train vs Car Trip Cost

The cheaper way to make a trip depends on the distance, the fare and what your car drinks. Enter the distance, the train fare, your car's fuel use and the fuel price, and this page shows the total trip cost for each side by side, with the travel time you enter.

This compares the train fare with the fuel cost of the car only. It leaves out parking, tolls, car servicing and wear, train food, and any ticket booking fees — add those to your own figures if they apply.

How it works

The train side is simple: the total is the fare you enter. The car side is worked out from your distance and fuel use. The calculator divides the distance by 100, multiplies by the fuel your car uses per 100 km or miles, and multiplies that by the fuel price. The travel times you enter are shown next to the costs so you can weigh time against money.

You can use kilometres or miles, as long as you use the same unit for the distance and for the fuel-use figure. Your car's fuel use is in the manual or on the dashboard display, or you can work it out from your own fuelling records.

How to use the result

Compare the totals first, then the times. A cheaper trip that takes much longer is not always the better choice, and a quicker trip is not always worth the extra cost. The page shows both so the trade-off is clear.

Remember that the train fare is per person while the fuel cost is per car. If more than one person is travelling, enter the total fare for everyone in the fare box — the car side stays the same, so the comparison shifts in the car's favour as more people travel together. For a return trip, enter the return fare and the return distance, or double the one-way result.

What this comparison leaves out

Fuel is only one part of driving. The calculator does not count parking, tolls, servicing, tyres or the wear on the car. On the train side it does not count food bought on the journey or any booking fee. If any of those apply to your trip, add them to the matching total yourself before deciding.

The travel times are the ones you enter, so they are only as good as your own figures. Check the timetable for the train and allow for traffic on the road if the journey time matters to you.

Questions people ask

Is this for a one-way trip or a return?

One way as entered. For a return trip, enter the return fare and the return distance, or double the one-way result.

What if more than one person is travelling?

The train fare is per person and the fuel cost is per car, so enter the total fare for everyone travelling to compare fairly.

Does the car side include parking and tolls?

No. The car side counts fuel only. If you pay for parking or tolls, add that amount to the car total yourself before deciding.

Why do I enter travel time?

So you can weigh cost against time. The cheaper option is not always the quicker one, and the page shows both side by side.

Does the train fare include seat reservations?

Only if you include them in the fare you enter. Add any reservation fee to the fare box so the comparison counts it.
