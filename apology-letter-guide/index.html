Apology Letter Guide

A good apology letter is short, specific and honest. Fill in the four boxes below and the builder turns your own words into a draft letter you can copy and send. It keeps the structure simple: what happened, your part in it, and what you will do differently.

A letter is only a starting point. A real apology also needs changed behaviour — the words matter less than what you do next. Some things are better said in person, and a letter cannot undo what happened.

How to set the right tone

A short, honest letter lands better than a long, careful one. Keep these points in mind as you write:

  • Be specific. Name what happened instead of writing a vague sorry — it shows you understand what went wrong.
  • Own it. Say what your part was, plainly, without blaming anyone else.
  • Don't make excuses. A sorry followed by a but stops sounding like a sorry.
  • Keep it short. A few honest lines beat pages of explanation.
  • Don't ask for anything in return. Forgiveness is theirs to give, not yours to request.

Before you send it

Read your draft back once, out loud if you can. Check that you named what happened, owned your part without excuses, and said what will change. If any sentence sounds like you are defending yourself, rewrite it or cut it. Then send it as it is — a plain, honest letter is enough.

Questions people ask

How do I start an apology letter?

Start with the person's name and a plain sorry, for example Dear Sam, I am sorry. Say what you are sorry about in the first line or two, so the reader knows straight away that you mean it.

What should an apology letter include?

Three things: what happened, in plain words; your part in it, without excuses; and what you will do differently. A short closing is enough — you do not need to explain yourself at length.

How long should an apology letter be?

A few short paragraphs is usually enough. Say what happened, own your part and say what will change, then stop. A very long letter can start to sound like a defence rather than an apology.

Should I apologise in writing or in person?

It depends on the situation. A letter gives you time to choose your words and gives the other person time to read them, but for serious matters a face-to-face apology often means more. Sometimes the best approach is a short letter followed by a conversation in person.
