Bike or Bus: Which Commute Costs Less and Takes Less Time?

Short answer: enter your commute schedule, bus fare, annual bike costs and travel times to compare yearly cost and time estimates.

What the calculator counts

Annual bus cost is the one-way fare multiplied by two trips per commute day and the number of commute days. Bike cost is the annual amount you enter. Annual travel time is shown for the same number of round trips, excluding waiting, parking, route changes and unexpected delays.

Include the full commute

  • Include bike purchase cost spread over the years you expect to use it, plus maintenance, lights, locks and other equipment.
  • Use the actual bus fare for the route and include transfers if they apply.
  • Compare door-to-door times, not only the time spent moving.
  • Consider secure bike parking, shower access, traffic and route conditions.
Important limit: Weather and safe routes matter as much as cost.

Questions people ask

Is biking always cheaper than taking the bus?

No. The result depends on bike purchase and upkeep, local fares, commute frequency and how long you use the bike. Enter your own costs to compare.

Does the calculator include waiting for the bus?

No. It uses the travel time you enter. Add typical waiting and transfer time to the bus estimate for a more realistic door-to-door comparison.

How should I include the cost of a bicycle?

Spread the purchase cost across the number of years you expect to use it, then add annual maintenance and equipment costs.

What else should I consider besides cost and time?

Consider safe routes, weather, lighting, secure parking, traffic, accessibility and whether the commute is practical on the days you travel.
