Moving Boxes Calculator

Enter how many rooms you have and how cluttered your home is, and this calculator estimates how many moving boxes you need — split into small, medium and large — plus how many rolls of tape to buy. It is a planning estimate, worked out from typical per-room box counts.

These counts are planning estimates, not exact figures. Fragile items and books need more small boxes than the split suggests, and every home is different — if you own a lot of books, records or heavy items, buy extra small boxes and tape.

How the count is worked out

Each room type has a typical box count, used here as a planning estimate:

  • Bedroom — 8 boxes
  • Living room — 10 boxes
  • Kitchen — 12 boxes
  • Bathroom — 3 boxes
  • Dining room — 6 boxes
  • Study — 8 boxes
  • Garage — 10 boxes

The room counts are added up, then adjusted for how much you own: minimal clutter multiplies the total by 0.75, normal clutter by 1, and cluttered by 1.25. The result is rounded up to a whole number of boxes.

The total is then split by size: small boxes are 30% of the total (rounded), large boxes are 20% (rounded), and the rest are medium. Tape is worked out at one roll per 8 boxes, rounded up.

Worked examples

  1. 2 bedrooms, 1 living room, 1 kitchen, 1 bathroom, normal clutter. 2 × 8 + 10 + 12 + 3 = 41 boxes. Split: 12 small, 21 medium, 8 large. Tape: 6 rolls.
  2. 1 bedroom only, minimal clutter. 8 × 0.75 = 6 boxes. Split: 2 small, 3 medium, 1 large. Tape: 1 roll.
  3. 3 bedrooms, 1 living room, 1 kitchen, 2 bathrooms, cluttered. (3 × 8 + 10 + 12 + 2 × 3) × 1.25 = 52 × 1.25 = 65 boxes. Split: 20 small, 32 medium, 13 large. Tape: 9 rolls.

Packing tips

  • Heavy things go in small boxes. Books, tools and canned food are heavy — pack them in small boxes so the boxes stay liftable and do not break.
  • Label every box. Write the room and a short note of what is inside on at least two sides, so you can find things without opening every box.
  • Fill the gaps. Stuff soft items like towels and clothes around fragile things so nothing rattles or shifts in the box.
  • Pack a first-night box. Keep bedding, toiletries, chargers and a change of clothes in one box you open first, so you are not hunting through boxes on moving day.

Questions people ask

How many boxes do I need for a 2-bedroom home?

About 41 boxes is a typical planning estimate for a 2-bedroom home with a living room, kitchen and bathroom at a normal clutter level: 12 small, 21 medium and 8 large, plus 6 rolls of tape. Your count changes with how much you own, so use the calculator above with your own rooms and clutter level.

What size boxes should I use for books?

Small boxes. Books get heavy fast, so a large box full of books is hard to lift and can break. Put heavy things like books, tools and canned food in small boxes, and light bulky things like bedding and cushions in large ones.

How much tape do I need?

About one roll per 8 boxes is a good planning figure. The calculator works this out for you: it divides your total box count by 8 and rounds up. Tape the bottom of every box twice for strength.

Should I buy extra boxes?

Yes, buy a few more than the count suggests. The estimate is a starting point, and most moves turn up more stuff than expected. Unused boxes can be flattened and kept for next time, or passed on to someone else who is moving.
