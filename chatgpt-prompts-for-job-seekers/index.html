30 ChatGPT Prompts for Job Seekers

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

This page gives you 30 original ChatGPT prompts for job seekers, organised into five groups: resumes, cover letters, interview prep, job search, and networking messages. Every prompt comes with a one-line note on how to adapt it to your own search.

Looking for a job means writing under pressure: resumes, cover letters, applications, and messages to people you hope will help. ChatGPT can draft and polish that writing, so you can focus on the search itself.

The prompts below cover the whole job hunt, from shaping a resume to following up after an interview. Fill in your role, your field, or your situation, and use the adaptation note to make each prompt fit you.

Keep personal details out of your prompts. Never paste your full name, address, phone number, ID numbers, or the names of your current employer and colleagues into a chatbot. Describe your situation in general terms, and add private details to your documents yourself, after the AI has helped with the wording.

How to Use These Prompts

Replace the words in square brackets with your own role, field, or situation before you send a prompt. Give the AI real facts about your experience, because a resume built on invented details will fall apart at the interview. The AI shapes the words; the facts must come from you.

Work through one document at a time. Draft your resume first, then your cover letter, then your interview answers. Each prompt builds on the last, and by the end you will have a set of documents that tell one consistent story about your experience.

Resumes

Use these prompts to shape a resume that shows results, not just duties.

  1. "Turn this list of duties into three strong bullet points that start with action verbs and name results."
    Adapt it: paste your own list of duties.
  2. "Rewrite my professional summary in 40 words, focused on [role] and what I bring to it."
    Adapt it: name the role and change the length.
  3. "Help me describe a career gap in one honest line that focuses on what I learned."
    Adapt it: describe the gap in general terms.
  4. "Suggest five skills from my experience that match a [job title] posting, with one example each."
    Adapt it: name the job title and paste your experience.
  5. "Trim my resume to one page: tell me which lines to cut and which to keep."
    Adapt it: paste your resume without personal details.
  6. "Rewrite this bullet point to show the result of my work, not just the task."
    Adapt it: paste any bullet point that reads like a job description.

Cover Letters

Use these prompts to write letters that sound like you, not like a template.

  1. "Draft an opening paragraph for a cover letter for [role], connecting my experience to the job in two sentences."
    Adapt it: name the role and your key experience.
  2. "Help me explain in one paragraph why I want this role at [company], without sounding generic."
    Adapt it: name the company and what draws you to it.
  3. "Rewrite this cover letter paragraph to sound confident but not boastful."
    Adapt it: paste your own paragraph.
  4. "Give me three ways to close a cover letter with a clear, polite call to action."
    Adapt it: ask for a different tone, such as warm or formal.
  5. "Turn my answer to why I left my last job into one calm, positive sentence."
    Adapt it: describe your reason in general terms.
  6. "Draft a short cover letter for a role I am changing careers into, focusing on transferable skills."
    Adapt it: name the new role and your transferable skills.

Interview Prep

Use these prompts to practise answers before the real interview.

  1. "Give me five likely interview questions for [role], with a hint on what each one is really asking."
    Adapt it: name the role and ask for more questions.
  2. "Help me answer tell me about yourself in 60 seconds, using my experience in [field]."
    Adapt it: name your field and change the length.
  3. "Practise with me: ask me one interview question at a time and give me feedback on each answer."
    Adapt it: name the role first, then answer out loud.
  4. "Help me prepare a short story about a time I solved a problem at work, in three parts: situation, action, result."
    Adapt it: describe your own example in general terms.
  5. "Suggest three thoughtful questions I can ask the interviewer about [company]."
    Adapt it: name the company and what you want to know.
  6. "Help me answer what is your greatest weakness honestly, without hurting my chances."
    Adapt it: name a real weakness you are working on.

Job Search

Use these prompts to search smarter and stay organised.

  1. "Help me list the job titles I should search for, given my skills in [area]."
    Adapt it: name your strongest skill area.
  2. "Suggest five search terms for finding [type of role] jobs in [location or remote]."
    Adapt it: name the role type and the location.
  3. "Help me write a simple weekly job-search plan: how many applications, how much research, how much follow-up."
    Adapt it: state the hours you have each week.
  4. "Turn this job description into a checklist of what the employer wants, so I can match my experience to it."
    Adapt it: paste the job description.
  5. "Help me decide between two job offers by comparing them on pay, hours, growth, and location."
    Adapt it: describe the two offers in general terms.
  6. "Draft a polite follow-up message to send a week after applying for [role]."
    Adapt it: name the role and the company.

Networking Messages

Use these prompts to reach out to people without sounding pushy.

  1. "Draft a short message to someone who works at [company], asking about their experience there."
    Adapt it: name the company and what you want to know.
  2. "Write a friendly message to a former colleague asking if they know of any openings."
    Adapt it: describe your relationship in general terms.
  3. "Draft a thank-you message to send after an informational chat, mentioning one thing I learned."
    Adapt it: name the thing you learned.
  4. "Help me write a short introduction I can use at a networking event, in under 30 seconds."
    Adapt it: name your field and what you are looking for.
  5. "Draft a message reconnecting with an old contact, without making it sound like I only want a favour."
    Adapt it: mention something you remember about them.
  6. "Write a polite message asking a mentor for advice about [career question]."
    Adapt it: name your career question.

Protecting Your Privacy While Job Hunting

A job search involves some of your most sensitive information: your work history, your current employer, your salary, and your contact details. None of that belongs in a chatbot prompt. Before you paste anything into ChatGPT, remove your name, address, phone number, and the names of employers and colleagues. Describe your situation in general terms, such as "a retail manager with five years of experience," and let the AI help with the wording.

Add your private details to the finished documents yourself, after the AI has done its work. The same rule applies to messages: draft the message with the AI, then add the person's name and your own details before you send it. A few seconds of care keeps your job search private.

Making Prompts Your Own

The prompts on this page are starting points. As you use them, you will find the ones that fit your search, and you will start changing them: different roles, different fields, different kinds of messages. Keep a note of the versions that work, organised by the stage of the search they help with.

The best test of a prompt is a reply. If a cover letter gets an interview, if a networking message gets an answer, if a resume gets past the first screen, the prompt behind it is worth keeping. Build your library from what works in the real world.

Questions people ask

Can ChatGPT write my resume for me?

It can draft and improve the wording, but the facts must come from you. Give it your real experience, check every line it produces, and never let it invent jobs, dates, or skills you do not have.

Should I send the same cover letter everywhere?

No. Tailor each letter to the role by naming the company and connecting your experience to the job. A short, specific letter beats a long, generic one every time.

Can ChatGPT help me practise interviews?

Yes. Ask it to play the interviewer, answer one question at a time, and give you feedback on each answer. Practising out loud makes the real interview feel much calmer.

What personal details should I keep out of prompts?

Your full name, address, phone number, ID numbers, and details about your current employer or colleagues. Describe your situation in general terms instead, and add private details later.

How honest should I be about gaps in my work history?

Be honest and brief. One calm sentence about what you did during the gap, focused on what you learned, is better than hiding it or inventing a story.
