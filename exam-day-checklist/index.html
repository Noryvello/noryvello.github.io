The night before

Pack your bag the night before, not in the morning rush. Put in your ID, your pens, a spare pen, and anything else your exam board allows. Lay out your clothes and set two alarms, so one alarm is a backup for the other.

Resist the urge to revise late into the night. A good night's sleep helps more than a last-minute cram session. Read through your notes once if it helps you feel ready, then put them away and rest.

On the morning

Eat a proper breakfast, even if your nerves say otherwise. Check your bag once more, then leave home with time to spare — traffic and queues are the last things you need on exam day.

Arrive early, find your seat, and switch your phone off before the exam starts. A few quiet minutes in your seat beats a breathless dash through the door.

Questions people ask

What should I bring to the exam?

Bring your student card or ID, pens and a spare pen, and a water bottle. Only bring a calculator if your exam board allows it — check the rules before the day.

What if I am running late?

Go straight to the exam hall and tell the invigilator what happened. Most exam boards have rules for late arrivals, so do not give up and go home.

Can I bring water into the exam?

Usually yes, in a clear bottle with the label removed. Check your exam board's rules first, as some halls have their own rules about bottles and snacks.

What if I feel unwell on exam day?

Tell the invigilator before the exam starts if you can. Your school or exam board can advise you about what happens next — do not suffer in silence.

Should I revise on the morning of the exam?

A light look at your notes is fine if it calms you, but avoid learning new material. Trust the work you have already done and keep the morning calm.
