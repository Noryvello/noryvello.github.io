Get Well Soon Messages

When someone is unwell, a few kind words can mean a lot. This page offers 25 original get-well messages, grouped by tone: short, warm and light. Pick one, add your own line, and send it with care.

A get-well message does not need to be long. A few kind words, sent at the right moment, can lift someone's day and remind them they are not alone. The messages below are all original, written for this page.

Choose a tone that fits the person and the situation. Short messages suit a quick text, warm messages suit a card, and light messages suit a friend who would smile at a little humour.

These are messages only, not health advice. If you are worried about someone's health, encourage them to speak to a doctor or another qualified professional. Keep your message gentle, and avoid asking for medical details.

  6. I hope your recovery is smooth and steady. If there is anything I can do, from a visit to a meal, just say the word.
  7. Get well soon. Your strength has always inspired me, and I know you will come through this one day at a time.
  8. Wishing you comfort, rest and good care. I am thinking of you and your family, and I hope to see you smiling again soon.
  9. The days may feel long, but you are not alone. I am thinking of you, and I hope each morning brings a small step forward.

How to write a get-well card

A good get-well card is short and kind. Start with your good wishes, add a line about the person, and finish with an offer of help or a look forward to seeing them again. You do not need to fill the whole card; a few honest lines are enough.

If you know what they enjoy, mention it. A line about a book, a show or a walk you will share when they are better gives them something to look forward to. Keep the tone warm, and sign off with your name so they know exactly who is thinking of them.

When a light message is right

A light message suits a friend with a cold, a minor injury or a short stay in hospital. It shows you care without making a fuss, and a smile is welcome when someone is bored on the sofa.

For a serious illness or a long recovery, choose a warm message instead. Keep it gentle, avoid jokes about the illness, and let them know you are there for the long haul.

Questions people ask

What is a good short get-well message?

A short message works best when it is warm and simple. Say you are thinking of them, wish them rest, and let them know there is no rush. One or two sentences is enough.

Should I ask about their illness?

No. It is kinder to keep the focus on your good wishes. If they want to share details, they will. Avoid questions about symptoms or treatment.

Is a funny message okay?

It depends on the person and how unwell they are. A light message suits a friend with a cold or a minor injury. For a serious illness, keep it warm and gentle instead.

How often should I message someone who is ill?

A message every few days is thoughtful without being tiring. Short notes are better than long ones, and there is no need to expect a reply.

What should I avoid in a get-well message?

Avoid medical advice, stories about other people's illnesses, and phrases like 'you look tired'. Keep it kind, simple and focused on rest and recovery.
