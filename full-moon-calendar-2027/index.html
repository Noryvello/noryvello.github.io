Full Moon Calendar 2027

2027 has exactly 12 full moons, one in every month, from the Wolf Moon on Friday 22 January to the Cold Moon on Monday 13 December. This page lists all 12 dates in UTC with their traditional names, including the year's supermoon in January and the seasonal Blue Moon in May.

A full moon is the moment when the Moon is opposite the Sun in the sky, so the whole face we see is lit. In 2027 there is exactly one full moon in every month — twelve in total — which means there is no calendar Blue Moon this year: a calendar Blue Moon needs two full moons in one month, and that never happens in 2027.

The names in the table below are the traditional North American almanac names. They are not official astronomical names — astronomers do not name full moons — and other cultures use other names for the same moons. The dates are given in UTC, with no clock times, because the exact moment of full moon falls at a different local time everywhere in the world.

The supermoon of 2027

The full moon of Friday 22 January 2027, the Wolf Moon, is a supermoon — and it is the closest full moon of 2027, about 357,644 km away. A supermoon is a full moon that happens when the Moon is near its closest point to Earth in its orbit, so it looks slightly larger and brighter than an ordinary full moon.

The difference is small — you would only notice it if you compared two full moons side by side — but the January moon is the one to watch if you want the biggest full moon of the year. The other eleven full moons of 2027 are ordinary full moons at ordinary distances, so the Wolf Moon is the year's standout.

Is there a Blue Moon in 2027?

It depends on which rule you use. A calendar Blue Moon is a second full moon in one calendar month — and because 2027 has exactly one full moon in every month, there is no calendar Blue Moon in 2027. The months of 2027 are too tidy for that: twelve months, twelve full moons, one each.

But there is a seasonal Blue Moon. By the older rule, a Blue Moon is the third of four full moons in one season, and in 2027 that is the Flower Moon of Thursday 20 May. So the answer is no by the calendar rule and yes by the seasonal rule — and either way, the Moon does not turn blue. The name is about the calendar, not the colour.

The Harvest Moon and the Hunter's Moon

The Harvest Moon is the full moon nearest the September equinox, which falls on 23 September in 2027 — so the Harvest Moon is the full moon of Wednesday 15 September. It is the moon of harvest time, and it is followed a month later by the Hunter's Moon on Friday 15 October.

Both moons fall in the autumn, when the nights are lengthening, and both are named for the season's work: bringing in the harvest, and then hunting. For most people they are simply two of the year's best evening moons — big, bright and worth stepping outside for.

Where the moon names come from

The names in the table are the traditional North American almanac names, passed down through farming almanacs and rooted in the seasons. They are not official astronomical names, and other cultures use other names for the same full moons — the names are a folk calendar, not a scientific one.

In general seasonal terms, the names follow the year. The Wolf Moon and Snow Moon belong to the cold, dark months of midwinter; the Worm Moon and Pink Moon mark the turn toward spring; the Flower Moon and Strawberry Moon belong to late spring and early summer; the Buck Moon and Sturgeon Moon fall in high summer; the Harvest Moon and Hunter's Moon belong to autumn; and the Beaver Moon and Cold Moon close the year as winter returns. The names are a reminder that the full moon was once a calendar in the sky — a way of marking the seasons through the year.

How to watch a full moon

You need no equipment at all to watch a full moon — no telescope, no binoculars, no special glasses. The evening of the full moon is the best show: the Moon rises around sunset, so it is up all night, and it is at its brightest and fullest.

Find a clear view of the eastern horizon at sunset if you can, and the Moon will climb through the evening. A dark sky helps, but a full moon is bright enough to enjoy from a town or city too — it is the one sky event that needs no planning at all.
