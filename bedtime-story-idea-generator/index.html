    Tips for telling a bedtime story

    • Tell the story slowly and softly, and let the pace slow down as the ending comes near.
    • Let your child choose the hero or the twist — choosing makes the story feel like theirs.
    • Keep the ending calm: the hero goes home, the lights go out, and everyone sleeps soundly.
    • If your child asks what happens next, ask them what they think — their answer is the next part of the story.

    Questions people ask

    How do I make up a bedtime story?

    Start with a hero, a place and a feeling, then ask what happens next. Pick a hero your child likes, put them somewhere interesting, and let one small surprise change the evening. You do not need a long plot: three or four sentences are enough to begin, and your child can help you decide what happens next.

    What makes a good bedtime story?

    A good bedtime story is short, gentle and easy to follow, with a hero your child cares about and a calm ending. Familiar places, kind characters and one small surprise work well. The best stories leave your child feeling safe and sleepy, not wide awake.

    How long should a bedtime story be?

    Most bedtime stories take about five to ten minutes to tell, which is usually enough for one small adventure and a calm ending. Shorter is fine for younger children, and older children may enjoy a slightly longer tale. If your child is getting sleepy, a short story is the right length.

    Can I use this for older children too?

    Yes. Older children can use the starters as writing prompts, add their own twists, or tell the story to a younger brother or sister. You can also ask them to change the hero, the setting or the ending and see how the story changes.
