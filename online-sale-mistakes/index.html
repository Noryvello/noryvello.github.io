Online Sale Season: 7 Mistakes That Waste Your Money

Short answer: sale season — 11.11, Black Friday, 12.12 and every flash sale between them — is designed to make you spend fast and think later. The seven mistakes below are where the money actually goes, and the true-discount checker at the bottom settles any "deal" in seconds.

1. Believing the crossed-out price

The crossed-out "original" price is often inflated just before the sale, then cut back to the normal price. The discount looks huge; the saving is zero. Do this instead: judge a deal against what the item cost a month ago, not against the crossed-out number on the page.

2. Buying without a list

Flash deals and countdown timers exist to make unplanned buying feel urgent. A 70% discount on something you would never buy at full price is not a saving — it is 30% spent for nothing. Do this instead: write your list before the sale starts, with a price limit per item, and buy only what is on it.

3. Chasing vouchers past their real value

"Spend 500 more to unlock a voucher" turns a small saving into extra spending. Vouchers with minimum spends, category limits and short expiry dates often die unused. Do this instead: count a voucher only if it discounts something already on your list, and check its expiry and conditions before adding filler items to reach it.

4. Ignoring delivery, fees and the final total

A cheap item with expensive delivery, handling fees or cash-on-delivery charges is not cheap. Do this instead: compare the final total at checkout — item + delivery + fees — against the same total elsewhere. Never compare headline prices alone.

5. Skipping the return policy

Sale items often carry shorter return windows, restocking fees, or no returns at all — and the policy is written on the product page, not at the checkout. Do this instead: read the return terms before paying, especially for clothing sizes, electronics, and anything you cannot test first.

6. Trusting ratings without reading them

Star averages hide the details: a 4.5-star product can still have a hundred recent complaints about the exact fault you care about. Do this instead: read the newest reviews first, filter to the lowest ratings, and look for repeated complaints about the same thing — that pattern is the truth.

7. Paying before checking the seller

Sale season brings new sellers with no history, copied listings, and prices too good to be real. Do this instead: check how long the seller has sold, how many ratings they hold, and whether the listing photos look like the seller's own. If a deal is far below every other price for the same item, treat it as a warning, not a bargain.

Questions people ask

Are sale-season discounts real?

Some are, and some are not. A common trick is raising the price weeks before the sale, then cutting it back to the normal price and calling the difference a discount. The only honest test is the price history: what did the same item cost a month ago?

How do I check if a deal is really a deal?

Compare the sale price against the price the item sold for before the sale, not against the crossed-out price on the page. The true-discount checker on this page works out the real percentage off any two prices you type in.

What is the biggest sale-season money trap?

Buying things you would never buy at full price. A 70% discount on something you do not need is not a saving — it is 30% spent for nothing. A short list written before the sale starts beats every flash deal.

Should I check the return policy before a sale purchase?

Yes, every time. Sale items often carry shorter return windows, restocking fees, or no returns at all. Read the return terms on the product page before paying, not after the parcel arrives.
