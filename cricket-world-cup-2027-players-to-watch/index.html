Cricket World Cup 2027 Players to Watch

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

The Cricket World Cup 2027 will bring together the best one-day players in the world across South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia. This guide picks the stars and rising names to follow, with the reminder that squads are named closer to October 2027.

The Cricket World Cup 2027 runs from 2 October to 21 November 2027, with 14 teams playing 57 matches of 50-over cricket. The format is new, with a Super Series, two groups of six, and a Super 7, which means the players who succeed will need consistency across seven weeks, not just one good week.

This guide introduces the established international stars and widely known rising names to follow, grouped by what they do best. They are described in general terms: their role, their style, and their reputation. Squads have not been named, so treat this as a watchlist, not a squad list.

Squads for the 2027 tournament have not been named. The players below are established international stars, but selection, fitness and form decide who actually plays. Treat this as a watchlist, not a squad list.

The great batters

Every World Cup is defined by its great batters, and 2027 should be no different. India's Virat Kohli remains one of the finest one-day batters the game has seen, a player whose chasing and big-match record have made him a defining figure of his generation. Pakistan's Babar Azam is among the most elegant batters in the world, a player whose cover drives and calm temperament have made him the centre of his team's batting. England's Joe Root brings classical technique and adaptability, and New Zealand's Kane Williamson brings the same qualities with a captain's calm.

What these players share is not just runs but reliability. In a tournament that lasts seven weeks, teams need batters who can be trusted to build an innings when the pressure is on, and these four have done it for years. Whether they are all still at their peak in October 2027 is a question form and fitness will answer, but their class is beyond doubt.

The new generation of batters

Alongside the established greats, a new generation of batters is shaping the one-day game. India's Shubman Gill is a young opener whose timing and elegance have already marked him out as a future star. New Zealand's Rachin Ravindra announced himself on the world stage with a breakthrough tournament in 2023 and has kept growing since. England's Harry Brook is a batter of rare power and range, able to score quickly against any attack.

Australia's Travis Head brings aggression at the top of the order, a batter who can take a match away from the opposition in the first ten overs. These players matter because they change the tempo of a match: where the older generation builds carefully, the new generation attacks early, and the teams that balance both approaches usually go furthest.

The fast bowlers

Fast bowling wins World Cups, and the 2027 tournament will feature the best quicks in the world. India's Jasprit Bumrah is the most complete fast bowler of his generation, able to bowl with the new ball, in the middle overs, and at the death, with yorkers and slower balls that few batters can read. Australia's Mitchell Starc brings left-arm pace and swing, and his World Cup record with the new ball is outstanding. His captain, Pat Cummins, offers pace, bounce and relentless accuracy.

Pakistan's Shaheen Shah Afridi is a left-arm quick who swings the new ball dangerously, and South Africa's Kagiso Rabada combines pace with bounce on home surfaces that should suit him. England's Jofra Archer, when fit, is one of the fastest bowlers in the game. In South African conditions, which often offer pace and bounce, these bowlers could decide the tournament.

The spinners

Spin wins matches in the middle overs, and the 2027 tournament has some of the best spinners in the world. Afghanistan's Rashid Khan is the most feared leg-spinner in limited-overs cricket, a bowler whose variations are almost impossible to pick and who can change a match in a single spell. Australia's Adam Zampa has become a reliable wicket-taker in one-day cricket, and Sri Lanka's Wanindu Hasaranga offers leg-spin with the ability to score quick runs lower down the order.

Keep an eye, too, on Afghanistan's Allah Mohammad Ghazanfar, a young off-spinner who has already made a mark in international cricket and represents the next wave of Afghan spin talent. Spinners matter most in the middle overs, where they slow the scoring and take wickets, and the teams with the best spin attacks often control the tempo of the tournament.

The all-rounders

All-rounders are the most valuable players in one-day cricket, because they contribute in two disciplines and give their captains flexibility. New Zealand's Rachin Ravindra is as useful with the ball as he is with the bat. Zimbabwe's Sikandar Raza has become one of the most reliable all-rounders in the game, a player who scores runs and bowls tight off-spin under pressure. South Africa's Aiden Markram offers top-order batting and handy off-spin, and India's Hardik Pandya brings power-hitting and fast-medium bowling.

All-rounders also matter for squad balance. A team with two genuine all-rounders can play an extra bowler or an extra batter without weakening either department, and that flexibility is worth its weight in gold over a seven-week tournament. Watch for how captains use their all-rounders: the best ones are match-winners with bat or ball, not just fillers.

The wicketkeepers who change games

The wicketkeeper's role has changed in modern one-day cricket: keepers are now expected to score quickly as well as keep tidily. Pakistan's Mohammad Rizwan is a keeper-batter of remarkable consistency, able to anchor an innings or accelerate as the situation demands. Afghanistan's Rahmanullah Gurbaz is a wicketkeeper who attacks from the start, and his fast starts have won matches for his team. Bangladesh's Litton Das offers elegant batting from the top of the order alongside his keeping.

Behind the stumps, a sharp keeper saves runs, takes the half-chances that turn matches, and keeps the fielding side switched on. With the bat, the modern keeper is often the player who turns a good total into a match-winning one. The teams whose keepers fire with the bat tend to post the totals that win tournaments.

Young names who could break out

Every World Cup produces a young player who arrives unknown and leaves famous. South Africa's Dewald Brevis is a young batter of rare power and flair, and a home World Cup could be the stage where he announces himself. England's Jacob Bethell is a young all-rounder whose clean hitting and left-arm spin have already earned him international chances. Afghanistan's Allah Mohammad Ghazanfar, still young, has already shown he can trouble the best batters in the world.

Young players bring fearlessness, and that matters in a tournament where the pressure grows with every round. They also bring uncertainty for opponents, who have less footage and fewer plans against them. The group stage is where these players announce themselves, and the Super 7 is where they become stars.

How to judge form before the tournament

A watchlist is only useful if you know how to update it, and the months before October 2027 will change the picture more than once. The best guide is recent one-day form: the bilateral series and tournaments played in the year before the World Cup, where players build rhythm or lose it. A batter scoring runs in the months before the tournament is a better bet than one living on reputation, and a bowler taking wickets regularly is worth more than one returning from a long break.

Fitness news matters just as much. Fast bowlers in particular are managed carefully before a World Cup, and a player rested for a series is not necessarily a player in trouble. Follow the official team announcements as the tournament approaches, and remember that squads are named closer to October 2027. The players who arrive in South Africa fit, in form and full of confidence are the ones to watch, whatever their names.

Who is the best bowler to watch?

Jasprit Bumrah is the most complete fast bowler of his generation, and Rashid Khan is the most feared spinner in limited-overs cricket. Both are established stars whose form is worth following.

Which young players could break out?

Names to follow include Dewald Brevis of South Africa, Jacob Bethell of England and Allah Mohammad Ghazanfar of Afghanistan. Every World Cup also produces a breakout star few people saw coming.

How do conditions in South Africa suit the players?

South African pitches often offer pace and bounce, which suits fast bowlers, but conditions vary by venue and by the time of day. The 12 venues across three countries will test every type of player.
