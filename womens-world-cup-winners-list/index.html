Women's World Cup Winners List

Nine Women's World Cups have been played, from the first in 1991 to the most recent in 2023, and five different teams have lifted the trophy. The United States lead the way with four titles, ahead of Germany with two, and Norway, Japan and Spain with one each.

The Women's World Cup began in 1991 with just 12 teams, and in that first edition the matches lasted 80 minutes rather than the usual 90. Since then the tournament has grown steadily: it expanded to 16 teams in 1999, to 24 teams in 2015, and to 32 teams in 2023. The 2023 edition was also the first to be staged in two host countries, Australia and New Zealand. Brazil 2027 keeps the 32-team field, and FIFA has approved expansion to 48 teams from 2031.

This page lists every Women's World Cup final played so far, from 1991 to 2023, with the champion, the score, the runner-up, and the venue. It also shows how the titles are shared between the five winning teams, with short notes on each champion's wins drawn from the finals table.

This page covers the nine Women's World Cup finals played so far, from 1991 to 2023, with the final score and venue of each. It lists the finals only — it does not cover semi-finals, scorers, or results from earlier rounds.

Three of the nine finals went beyond 90 minutes. The 1999 final finished goalless after extra time and was decided 5–4 on penalties, the 2003 final was settled by a golden goal in extra time, and the 2011 final finished 2–2 after extra time before being decided 3–1 on penalties. The other six finals were settled inside 90 minutes.

The biggest winning margin in a final is three goals, from the 2015 final, which the USA won 5–2. Every other final was decided by one or two goals, and two finals — 1999 and 2011 — needed penalties to separate the teams. The most recent final, in 2023, was the lowest-scoring final settled inside 90 minutes, with Spain beating England 1–0.

Five champions in nine editions means the trophy has stayed in a small group of hands. The USA have won nearly half of all the Women's World Cups ever played, with four titles. Germany are the only other team with more than one title, having won twice. Norway, Japan and Spain have one title each. Between them, the USA and Germany account for six of the nine titles.

The runners-up tell a similar story of a small group at the top. Norway, Germany, China, Sweden, Brazil, the USA, Japan, the Netherlands and England have all reached the final and lost, and several of them — Norway, Germany, the USA and Japan — have both won the tournament and lost a final. England reached their first final in 2023, where they lost 1–0 to Spain.

Notes on each champion

USA — 4 titles. The USA won the very first Women's World Cup in 1991, beating Norway 2–1 in the final in Guangzhou. They won again in 1999, beating China on penalties after a goalless final at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena, in front of 90,185 people. After a gap, they won back-to-back titles in 2015 and 2019: a 5–2 win over Japan in Vancouver in 2015, the highest-scoring final in the tournament's history, followed by a 2–0 win over the Netherlands in Lyon in 2019. The USA have also lost a final, in 2011, when Japan beat them on penalties after a 2–2 draw.

Germany — 2 titles. Germany won back-to-back titles in 2003 and 2007. In 2003 they beat Sweden 2–1 after extra time, with a golden goal, in Carson. In 2007 they beat Brazil 2–0 in Shanghai. Germany had earlier lost the 1995 final, beaten 2–0 by Norway in Solna, so their two titles came after a defeat in a final.

Norway — 1 title. Norway won the 1995 edition, beating Germany 2–0 in the final at Råsunda Stadium in Solna. They had been runners-up in the first final in 1991, losing 2–1 to the USA in Guangzhou, so their title came four years after their first final appearance.

Japan — 1 title. Japan won in 2011, beating the USA in the final in Frankfurt. The match finished 2–2 after extra time, and Japan won the penalty shootout 3–1. Japan reached the final again in 2015, where they lost 5–2 to the USA in Vancouver.

Spain — 1 title. Spain are the most recent champions, winning the 2023 edition with a 1–0 win over England in the final at Stadium Australia in Sydney on 20 August 2023, in front of 75,784 people. It was Spain's first Women's World Cup title, and the first time a new name had been added to the list of champions since Japan in 2011.

How the tournament has grown

The first Women's World Cup in 1991 had 12 teams, and its matches lasted 80 minutes. The field grew to 16 teams in 1999, the year the final at the Rose Bowl drew 90,185 people. It grew again to 24 teams in 2015, and to 32 teams in 2023, when the tournament was staged in two host countries for the first time, Australia and New Zealand.

The 2027 edition in Brazil keeps the 32-team field, and FIFA has approved expansion to 48 teams from 2031. As the field grows, more teams get their first chance at the tournament — and a first chance to join the list of five champions on this page.

Questions people ask

Who has won the most Women's World Cups?

The United States, with four titles: 1991, 1999, 2015 and 2019. Germany are next with two titles, in 2003 and 2007.

Who won the 2023 Women's World Cup?

Spain won the 2023 Women's World Cup, beating England 1–0 in the final at Stadium Australia in Sydney on 20 August 2023. It was Spain's first Women's World Cup title.

Has England ever won the Women's World Cup?

No. England's best result is reaching the final in 2023, where they lost 1–0 to Spain. England have never won the tournament.

How many teams have won the Women's World Cup?

Five teams have won it in nine editions: the United States (4 titles), Germany (2), Norway (1), Japan (1) and Spain (1).
