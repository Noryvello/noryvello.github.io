Free Photoshop Alternatives: 4 Tools That Are Really Free

Short answer: Photopea runs the full Photoshop-style editor in a browser with no sign-up and real PSD support. GIMP and Krita are free, open-source desktop programs with no paid tier at all. PhotoCraft, the viral open-source Photoshop lookalike, is real software — but it is early alpha and not ready for daily professional work. Checked 14 November 2026.

Two warnings before you start: (1) Download GIMP and Krita only from their official sites (gimp.org, krita.org) — third-party download pages have bundled unwanted extras, and any site selling you GIMP itself is repackaging a free program. (2) PhotoCraft is alpha: try it on copies of your files, never on the only copy of client work. Last verified: 14 November 2026.

1. Photopea — the closest thing to Photoshop in a browser

Photopea is a free image editor that runs entirely in a web browser, built and maintained by solo developer Ivan Kutskir since 2013. It needs no install, no account and no payment: open photopea.com and start editing. A live test in November 2026 confirmed no sign-in screen appears at any step.

  • PSD support is the headline: Photopea opens and saves PSD files with layers, masks, layer styles and text intact, and can also read AI, XD, Figma, Sketch, PDF and RAW files. A file edited in Photopea opens normally in Photoshop.
  • Every editing tool is free. Premium is a one-time payment (for example €8 for 30 days or €15 for 90 days, as shown in November 2026) that removes the ads and adds PeaDrive storage, more history steps and AI credits — it does not unlock extra editing tools.
  • Your files stay on your device: editing is processed locally, so images are not uploaded to a server during normal local editing.
  • Commercial use: Photopea states its editor may be used by anyone for any purpose for free, including work you sell.
  • One honest limit: Photopea is not an Adobe product, and complex smart objects or newer PSD features may render differently than in Photoshop — verify critical files in the original software.

2. GIMP — the veteran free desktop editor

GIMP (GNU Image Manipulation Program) is the long-running free software answer to a professional raster editor: photo retouching, layered compositing, masks, curves and a plugin ecosystem going back decades. The current stable release is GIMP 3.2.6 (10 September 2026).

  • Completely free: open source under the GPL, with no paid tier, no account, no licence key and no watermark. It may be used for paid client work.
  • GIMP 3 brought non-destructive filters, vector layers and link layers, plus better high-resolution display handling.
  • Download only from gimp.org (or the Microsoft Store, or your Linux distribution's packages). GIMP is not sold on the Mac App Store — anything there was built by a third party.
  • Honest limits: GIMP does not copy Photoshop's shortcuts or single-window habits, so switching costs a few days of relearning, and its CMYK support is still weak, which rules it out for most print work.

3. Krita — free, but built for painting, not photo editing

Krita is a free and open-source raster graphics editor designed primarily for digital art and 2D animation — illustration, comics, concept art and texture painting. The current stable release is Krita 5.3.4 (September 2026). It runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android (beta) and ChromeOS, but not on iOS or iPadOS.

  • Free forever: Krita is licensed under the GNU GPL, and the Krita team says it will stay free and open source forever. The full program downloads free from krita.org with no trial and no locked features, and may be used for commercial work.
  • The paid listings on Steam, the Microsoft Store and the Mac App Store contain the same files — they exist to fund development (Krita says the income funds four developers full time) and add automatic updates.
  • Know what it is not: Krita's own manual says it is not meant as a Photoshop replacement for photo manipulation — photo tasks such as stitching photographs are not its focus. Choose Krita for drawing and painting, not for retouching client photos.

4. PhotoCraft (ArtCraft) — viral, real, and still alpha

PhotoCraft is the image editor inside ArtCraft, an open-source creative suite written in Rust that went viral in October 2026 as a set of free Adobe-style apps. It is a clean-room reimplementation: written from public specifications and observed behaviour, with no Adobe code. The repository (github.com/storytold/photocraft) was created on 30 September 2026 and had about 79,000 stars by 12 November 2026, with releases landing every few days (v0.23.x by mid-November).

  • What works: layers, masks, editable type, shape layers and PSD/PSB document work, in an interface Photoshop users recognise immediately. It is free, needs no account, and is dual-licensed MIT or Apache-2.0, so commercial use is allowed.
  • What does not — yet: PhotoCraft is early alpha, and its own documentation says it is not yet a replacement for daily professional work. The authors' self-assessment from October 2026 put feature breadth at about 76% against Photoshop 2026 and readiness for real work at about 45%. Gaps include generative AI tools, missing tools, typography depth, professional workflows and plug-in compatibility.
  • How to try it safely: download from GitHub Releases for macOS, Windows or Linux, and test it on copies of your projects. Expect bugs, half-finished behaviour and surprising differences from Photoshop. Keep a proven editor for deadline work until it leaves alpha.
  • PhotoCraft is an independent project: Adobe does not develop, own or endorse it.

Which one should you pick?

  • Need Photoshop-style editing right now, in a browser: Photopea.
  • Need a full desktop editor for photo retouching, free forever: GIMP.
  • Drawing, comics or concept art: Krita.
  • Curious about the new open-source wave: PhotoCraft — for experiments, not for client deadlines.

Questions people ask

Is Photopea really free?

Yes. Photopea gives every editing tool free in the browser with no sign-up, no install and no card, and says its editor may be used by anyone for any purpose, including work you sell. The free version is supported by advertisements. Premium is a one-time payment that removes the ads and adds storage, more history steps and AI credits — it does not unlock extra editing tools.

Is GIMP free for commercial work?

Yes. GIMP is free and open-source software under the GNU General Public License, with no paid tier, no account and no licence key, and it may be used for paid client work. Download it only from gimp.org or your operating system's official store: any site selling you GIMP itself is repackaging the free program.

Is PhotoCraft ready to replace Photoshop?

No. PhotoCraft is early alpha software, and its own documentation says it is not yet a replacement for daily professional work. The authors' own assessment from October 2026 put readiness for real work at about 45 percent, with gaps in generative AI tools, typography depth, professional workflows and plug-in support. It is fine for testing and simple edits on copies of your files; keep a proven editor for deadline work.

Which free alternative opens PSD files?

Photopea opens and saves PSD files with layers, masks and text intact, and a file edited there opens normally in Photoshop. GIMP opens PSD files too. PhotoCraft works with PSD and PSB documents, but as alpha software it may hit file bugs, so test it on copies. Krita is built for painting rather than Photoshop file workflows, and its own manual says it is not meant as a Photoshop replacement for photo manipulation.
