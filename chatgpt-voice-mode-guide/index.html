How to Use ChatGPT Voice Mode

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

Voice mode lets you talk to ChatGPT out loud instead of typing, and hear its answers spoken back to you. It is useful when your hands are busy, when you want to practise speaking, or when talking feels easier than typing. This guide explains how to start a voice chat, choose a voice, and what voice mode is good at, along with its limits.

Talking to a chatbot feels different from typing to one. A typed chat encourages long, careful questions. A voice chat encourages short, natural ones, the way you would ask a person standing next to you. That makes voice mode good for quick questions, for thinking out loud, and for anything where the conversation itself is the point, like practising a language or rehearsing what you want to say in a meeting.

It also changes what the answers are good for. Spoken answers are easier to follow when they are short, so voice mode tends to work best for explanations, ideas, and back-and-forth discussion. It is less suited to long lists, tables, or anything you need to read carefully. The good news is that you can switch between voice and text in the same conversation, so you can talk when it suits you and read when it does not.

Voice features vary by app version and plan, and the controls and voices available to you may look different from what is described here. If a feature is missing, check that your app is up to date, and remember that some voice features are limited on some plans.

What voice mode is

Voice mode is a way of chatting with ChatGPT by speaking and listening instead of typing and reading. You talk, the chatbot listens and understands what you said, and it replies in a spoken voice. The conversation flows more like a phone call than a text chat: you can interrupt, ask a follow-up, or change the subject mid-sentence, and the chatbot keeps up.

Underneath, three things happen at once. Your speech is turned into text, the chatbot works out an answer, and the answer is turned back into speech. This all happens quickly enough to feel like a conversation. A transcript of the chat is usually kept in your chat history, so you can go back and read what was said, or copy a useful answer into your notes.

Starting a voice chat

To start, open the ChatGPT app and look for the voice or microphone control near the chat box. Tapping it usually starts a voice session right away, sometimes after asking permission to use your microphone. The first time you use it, you may need to allow microphone access in your phone's settings as well. Once the session starts, just speak your question in a normal voice.

There is no need to use special words or commands. Talk the way you would to a helpful colleague. If the chatbot misunderstands you, say so plainly: "That is not what I meant. I meant..." works better than repeating the same sentence louder. To end the session, look for the end or close control in the voice screen. The transcript stays in your chat history afterwards.

Choosing a voice

Voice mode usually offers a choice of voices, each with a different tone and pace. Some sound warm and chatty, others sound calm and measured. The choice is personal: pick the one you find easiest to listen to for a few minutes at a time. If you are using voice mode for language practice, a slower, clearer voice is usually the better pick. For quick questions, any voice will do.

You can change the voice later in the settings if the first one does not suit you. It is worth trying two or three before settling, because a voice that sounds fine for one sentence can feel tiring over a long conversation. The available voices change over time, so the list you see may not match what a friend sees on their account.

What voice mode is good for

Voice mode shines when your hands or eyes are busy. Asking a question while cooking, walking, or driving is the classic case: you get an answer without stopping what you are doing. It is also good for questions that are easier to say than to type, like "how do I get the smell of garlic off my hands?" or "what is a polite way to ask my neighbour to turn the music down?"

It is also useful for thinking out loud. Talking through a problem, even to a chatbot, often clarifies it. You can describe a situation in your own words, hear a summary back, and then ask "what am I missing?" The back-and-forth rhythm of speech makes this feel more natural than typing the same exchange. Many people use voice mode as a rehearsal tool, practising a presentation or a difficult conversation before the real thing.

Voice mode for learning

Language learners get some of the most from voice mode. You can hold a conversation in the language you are learning, ask to be corrected, and hear the correct pronunciation straight away. Try asking the chatbot to play a role, such as a shopkeeper or a hotel receptionist, and hold a short conversation with it. Then ask it to point out your mistakes and say the sentences correctly.

Voice mode also works for learning by ear in general. You can ask it to explain a topic out loud while you walk, or to quiz you with spoken questions and wait for your spoken answers. One useful trick is to ask for a short explanation, then say "now explain that again as if I am twelve years old." Hearing the same idea twice, in two levels of simplicity, helps it stick.

Limits of voice mode

Voice mode has real limits, and knowing them saves frustration. Spoken answers are hard to skim, so anything long, detailed, or full of numbers is better in text. The chatbot can also mishear you, especially with names, technical terms, or in a noisy room, and a misheard word can send the whole answer off track. If an answer sounds wrong, check the transcript to see what it actually heard.

Voice mode is also not the right tool for anything private in a public place. Your questions are spoken out loud, and the answers are too. If you are on a bus or in an office, use headphones, or switch to typing. And like the text chatbot, voice mode can give confident-sounding answers that are wrong, so check important facts afterwards rather than trusting a spoken answer on the spot.

Tips for better voice chats

A few habits make voice chats much smoother. Speak at a normal pace in a quiet spot, and keep your first question short: one question at a time works better than three questions in one breath. If the chatbot gives a long answer, interrupt and ask for the short version. Short answers are easier to follow by ear, and you can always ask for more detail on one point.

It also helps to tell the chatbot how you want it to answer. Saying "give me short answers" or "ask me one question at a time" at the start of a voice chat changes the whole conversation. And when a voice chat produces something useful, remember that the transcript is saved, so you can open the chat later, read it properly, and copy the parts you need.

Questions people ask

Do I need a special app for voice mode?

Voice mode is part of the ChatGPT app on phones, and it is also available on the web in many cases. The exact place to find it changes between versions, so look for a voice or microphone control near the chat box in the app you use.

Can voice mode understand different accents?

It handles many accents well, but no voice system understands every accent equally. If it keeps mishearing you, slow down a little, speak clearly, and move to a quieter place. Repeating a misheard word on its own often fixes it.

Does voice mode work in other languages?

Yes, voice mode works in many languages, not just English. You can speak in your own language and ask for answers in the same language, which makes it useful for language practice and for people who prefer not to type in English.

Can I switch between voice and text in one chat?

Yes. A voice chat leaves a text transcript in your chat history, and you can continue the same conversation by typing. This is handy when you start hands-free and then want to read or copy part of the answer.

Is my voice recorded?

Voice chats are processed so the service can understand what you said, and a transcript is usually kept in your chat history. How voice data is stored and what controls you have can change, so check the current privacy settings in your account.
