New Owner Cat Care Checklist

Bringing a cat home for the first time is easier with a plan for the first days and weeks. This checklist covers settling in, feeding, litter, play and planning vet visits, so nothing important gets missed.

A new cat needs a few simple things: a safe place to hide, food and water in the right places, a clean litter tray, and time to get used to you. The first days set the tone for everything that follows, so a calm start matters more than any purchase.

This checklist covers the first days and weeks in five groups: settling in, feeding routine, litter, play and vet-visit planning. Tick each item as you complete it.

General information only. This page is not veterinary advice. For any question about your cat's health, food or behaviour, ask a vet.

The first days matter most

Most cats are cautious in a new home. Give the cat one quiet room at first, with everything it needs inside, and let it come out in its own time. A cat that hides for a day or two is not unhappy; it is learning that the new place is safe. Sit nearby, talk quietly, and let the cat approach you.

Keep the first days predictable. Feed at the same times, keep the litter tray in the same place, and avoid big changes such as visitors or rearranged furniture. Cats settle faster when the world around them stays steady.

Building a routine

Once the cat has settled, a simple routine keeps life easy for both of you. Feed at set times, play for a few minutes each day, scoop the litter tray daily, and keep an eye on how the cat eats, drinks and behaves. Small changes in routine are often the first sign that something needs attention, and a vet can advise if you are unsure.

Enjoy the slow parts too. The first time a new cat chooses your lap, or greets you at the door, is the payoff for a patient start. Give it time, and the cat will come to you.

Questions people ask

What should I buy before my cat arrives?

A bed, food and water bowls, a litter tray with litter, a scratching post, and a carrier for trips. A few toys and a hiding spot complete the basics. You can add more once you see what your cat likes.

How long does it take a cat to settle in?

Most cats start to relax within a few days, though shy cats can take a few weeks. Give the cat a quiet room, keep the routine steady, and let it approach you. If a cat is still hiding all the time after a few weeks, ask a vet.

How often should I feed my cat?

Adult cats are usually fed once or twice a day, at the same times, with the amount the food packet suggests for the cat's weight. Kittens need more frequent meals. Your vet can advise on portions for your cat.

When should I first visit a vet?

Register with a vet in the first week and book a first check-up soon after arrival, bringing any papers from the shelter or breeder. The vet can advise on vaccinations and answer questions about your cat's health.

How much play does a cat need?

A few short play sessions each day, with a wand toy or a ball, keeps most cats happy and active. Play also builds trust between you and the cat, especially in the first weeks.
