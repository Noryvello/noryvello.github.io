Fabric Needed Calculator

Enter the size of your project, the width of the fabric and your seam and hem allowance, and this calculator works out how many panels you need and how much fabric length to buy. The result is rounded up to the next 10 cm, so you buy enough — with the working shown, step by step.

Pattern repeats and directional prints need more fabric than this simple calculation. If your fabric has a pattern that must line up across panels, or a print with a clear up and down, check the pattern instructions and buy extra.

How the amount is worked out

Fabric is sold by length from a roll of a fixed width, so the calculation has three steps:

  1. Panels. Divide the project width by the fabric width and round up — that is how many widths (panels) you need side by side.
  2. Length per panel. Add the seam and hem allowance to the project length.
  3. Total length. Multiply the panels by the length per panel, then round up to the next 10 cm.

Three examples:

  • A tablecloth 100 cm wide and 200 cm long, fabric 150 cm wide, allowance 10 cm: 1 panel, 210 cm (2.10 m).
  • Curtains 300 cm wide and 250 cm long, fabric 140 cm wide, allowance 10 cm: 3 panels, 780 cm (7.80 m).
  • A cushion cover 90 cm wide and 120 cm long, fabric 110 cm wide, allowance 5 cm: 1 panel, 125 cm rounded up to 130 cm (1.30 m).

Before you buy

  • Round up, and buy a little extra. The calculator already rounds up to the next 10 cm. If you are matching a pattern or sewing something for the first time, buy a little more — mistakes and pattern matching use extra fabric.
  • Wash and pre-shrink first if the fabric shrinks. Some fabrics shrink on the first wash. If yours does, wash and dry it before cutting, or your finished item will end up smaller than planned.
  • Check the pattern repeat. If the fabric has a pattern, find the repeat — the distance after which the pattern starts again. You may need extra length so the pattern lines up across panels.
  • Measure twice. Measure the finished size you want, then add the allowance. Guessing the size is the most common way to buy too little.

Questions people ask

How much extra fabric should I buy?

The calculator already rounds the length up to the next 10 cm. Buy a little extra on top of that if you are matching a pattern or sewing something for the first time — mistakes and pattern matching use extra fabric.

What is seam allowance?

Seam allowance is the extra fabric left around each edge of a piece so the seams can be sewn without making the finished item smaller. Hem allowance is the extra left at the bottom edge to fold under. Add both together in the allowance box.

Why does the width of the fabric matter?

Fabric is sold by length from a roll of a fixed width. If your project is wider than the fabric, you need more than one width — called panels — sewn side by side, and the calculator counts them for you.

Does this work for curtains?

Yes, as a starting point. For curtains, measure the finished drop and width you want, add allowance for the hem and heading, and remember that gathered curtains need much more width than the window — check your curtain heading instructions for the gather ratio.
