New Job Congratulations Messages

A new job deserves a message that matches the moment, and this page offers 25 original ones to choose from. They are grouped by tone: short, warm and formal. Pick one, add a personal detail, and send it with confidence.

Starting a new job is exciting, nerve-wracking and worth celebrating. A short message from a friend, colleague or family member can make the first day feel a little lighter. The messages below are all original, written for this page, and grouped so you can find the right tone quickly.

These messages are starting points. Add a detail only you would know, such as the role, the company or the journey, and the message becomes yours. A short note sent promptly means more than a long one sent weeks later.

  3. You did it! Congratulations on the new role.

How to personalise a message

A personal detail turns a good message into a memorable one. Mention the role they won, the field they are joining, or something you know about their journey. A line about the long application process or the interview they worried about shows you were paying attention.

Keep it natural. You do not need to write a speech. One sentence that only you could write is worth more than a paragraph of general praise. If you know their first day, mention it. If you know what they are nervous about, offer a word of encouragement.

When to send your message

Send your congratulations as soon as you hear the news. A message on the day they share it feels warm and immediate. If you missed the moment, a note on their first day, or at the end of their first week, is still welcome.

A short message by text or chat is fine for friends and family. For a colleague or a professional contact, an email or a card suits the occasion better. The channel matters less than the thought behind it.

Questions people ask

What is a good short message for a new job?

A short message works best when it is warm and specific. Try congratulating them by name, mention the new role, and wish them well on their first day. A single sentence is enough.

How do I congratulate a colleague formally?

Keep it professional and sincere. Mention their achievement, note the qualities that earned it, and wish them success in the new role. Avoid jokes or overly casual language.

Should I mention the company or the role?

Yes, if you know it. Naming the role or the company shows you paid attention and makes the message feel personal rather than generic.

Is it too late to send congratulations after a week?

No. A message in the first week or two is still welcome. You can wish them well on their first days and ask how the new role is going.

What should I avoid in a congratulations message?

Avoid mentioning salary, asking about the hiring process, or joking about their old job. Keep the focus on their achievement and your good wishes.
