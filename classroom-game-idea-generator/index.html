How to use this generator

Choose a subject and a group size, then press the button. You will get three games that fit, each with rules and a materials list. Press again for a different set.

The bank holds twenty-four games across six subjects, for pairs, small groups and the whole class. Read the materials list before you plan the lesson.

Running games smoothly

Explain the rules once, show one round, then start. Children learn a game faster by playing it than by listening to a long explanation, so keep the introduction short.

Set a clear signal for quiet, such as a raised hand, and use it before you explain the next step. A game with clear signals stays fun instead of turning chaotic.

Games support learning, they do not replace it. Keep rules clear, keep every child involved, and check that any game fits your time, your space and your class rules.

Matching games to your lesson

Pick a game that practises what you just taught. A game that revises the same words, numbers or facts as the lesson will feel purposeful, not like a break from learning.

End with a short review. Ask what the children remember from the game, and you will see straight away what has stuck and what needs another look. Keep a short list of the games that worked best, so next term you can reach for a proven game instead of starting from scratch.

Questions people ask

How many games are in the bank?

Twenty-four original games, covering maths, language, science, history, art and general topics, for pairs, small groups and the whole class.

What does each game include?

Each game gives you rules and a materials list, so you can see at a glance what you need and how the game runs.

How long does a game take?

Most games in the bank run in ten to twenty minutes. Shorten or extend them by changing the number of rounds.

Can I adapt a game to another subject?

Yes. Most games are frameworks. Swap the questions or words and the same game works for a different topic.

What if a game gets too noisy?

Set a noise signal before you start, such as a hand in the air, and pause the game until the room is quiet. A calm game is a fair game.
