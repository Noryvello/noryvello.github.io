ChatGPT Privacy Settings: What to Check

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

ChatGPT gives you several privacy controls: data controls, chat history settings, and memory controls. Each one decides what the service keeps and how it is used. This guide explains what each setting does, in general terms, and ends with a quick privacy check you can do in a few minutes.

Most people set up ChatGPT once and never open the settings again. That is understandable, but the settings are worth a short visit. They decide whether your chats are kept, whether the chatbot remembers things about you, and whether your conversations can be used to improve the service. None of the choices are difficult, but they are yours to make, and the defaults may not match what you would choose.

This guide describes the settings in general terms, because setting names and locations change between updates. If a step does not match your screen exactly, look for a similarly named option in the settings area. The ideas matter more than the exact labels: what is kept, what is remembered, and what you can delete.

Setting names and locations change between updates, so the steps in this guide describe the controls in general terms rather than a fixed path. If something looks different on your screen, check the current settings area. This guide is general information only, not legal advice.

Why privacy settings matter

A chatbot is easy to trust because it feels like a conversation. You type naturally, you share context, and the answers improve when you give more detail. That is exactly why the privacy settings matter: the more you share, the more there is to protect. A few minutes in the settings gives you a clear picture of what is kept and what you can control.

The settings also matter because they are not one decision but several. Keeping your chat history is one choice. Letting the chatbot remember your preferences is another. Allowing your chats to be used for training is a third. Each one can be set the way you like, and changing one does not change the others. Understanding the separate controls is the whole point of this guide.

Data controls

Data controls decide how your conversations may be used beyond answering you. The main control here is whether your chats can be used to improve the service, which usually means training future models. There is a setting that lets you opt out of this. Finding it is worth the minute it takes, because it is the control with the widest effect on your data.

Data controls may also cover things like whether your content is used for other product improvements, and how long deleted data is kept before it is removed. The details vary by plan and region, and the wording changes over time. The practical advice is simple: open the data controls, read each option as it is worded today, and set each one to match what you are comfortable with.

Chat history settings

Chat history is the record of your conversations, shown as a list you can reopen at any time. Keeping history is convenient: you can return to an old chat, copy an answer, or continue a conversation days later. But history is also a record of everything you have asked, which is why the settings let you control it.

You can delete a single chat from the list, or clear all chats at once from the settings. Deleting is permanent, so copy anything you want to keep first. Some accounts also offer a way to keep history off for new chats, or to use temporary chats that are not saved at all. A temporary chat is a good choice for a one-off question you do not want kept, or when someone else is using your device.

Memory controls

Memory is separate from chat history. History is the full record of your chats. Memory is a short list of notes the chatbot keeps about you, such as your preferences and ongoing projects, so future chats can be more useful. The memory controls let you view that list, delete single memories, clear them all, or switch memory off entirely.

Memory is a trade-off between convenience and privacy. With it on, the chatbot remembers that you like short answers or that you are learning a language. With it off, every chat starts fresh. There is no wrong choice, only your preference. A good middle ground is to leave memory on but check the list every few months, deleting anything outdated or anything you do not remember telling it.

What each setting actually does

It helps to see the settings side by side. Data controls decide how your chats may be used beyond the conversation itself. Chat history settings decide whether your conversations are kept in a list you can reopen. Memory controls decide whether the chatbot keeps short notes about you between chats. Each control answers a different question, and each can be set on its own.

One common confusion is between deleting history and clearing memory. Deleting your chats removes the conversation list, but it does not necessarily remove the memories the chatbot saved from those chats. Clearing memory removes the notes, but it does not delete your chat history. If you want a clean break, do both: clear the chats and clear the memories.

Sharing, exports, and deleting your data

Beyond the three main controls, there are a few more things worth knowing. Some accounts let you share a chat with someone else through a link. A shared link shows the conversation to anyone who opens it, so only share chats that contain nothing private, and remember that you cannot control who the link is passed to. If you shared something by mistake, look for a way to remove or revoke the link.

You can usually request a copy of your data, which gives you an export of your chats and account information. This is useful if you want a backup before deleting anything. If you decide to stop using the service, look for the account deletion option in the settings. Deleting your account removes your chats and data, though some information may be kept for a short time for legal or safety reasons, as the service's policy describes.

A quick privacy check routine

Here is a five-minute routine you can repeat every few months. First, open the data controls and confirm the training opt-out is set the way you want. Second, glance through your chat list and delete anything you no longer need. Third, open the memory list and remove anything outdated. Fourth, check whether you have any shared links you no longer need.

Fifth, and most important, review your own habits. The settings can only protect what you do not share. Keep identity numbers, account details, passwords, and other people's private information out of your chats entirely. When you need help with something sensitive, use a made-up example with the same shape as your real problem. Good settings plus careful habits are what real privacy looks like.

Questions people ask

Are my chats used to train the AI?

It depends on the settings you choose and the plan you are on. There is a data control that lets you opt out of having your chats used for training. Open the data controls in your settings to see what yours is set to, and change it if you want.

Can I delete my chat history?

Yes. You can delete a single chat from the chat list, or clear all chats at once from the settings. Deleting a chat removes it from your history, and there is no way to bring it back, so copy anything you want to keep first.

What is the safest way to use ChatGPT?

Keep personal details out of your prompts: no identity numbers, account details, passwords, or other people's private information. Use made-up examples when you need help with something sensitive, and check your privacy settings every few months.

Should I turn memory off?

It depends on what you value more. Memory makes the chatbot more helpful over time by remembering your preferences. Turning it off keeps every chat fresh and separate. If you like the convenience but not the idea of stored notes, check the memory list regularly and delete anything you do not want kept.

Can other people see my chats?

No. Your chats are tied to your own account and are not shown to other users. The main risk is someone using your device or your account, so sign out on shared devices and keep your password to yourself.
