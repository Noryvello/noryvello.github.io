How Big Should Your Gift-Wrapping Paper Be?

Short answer: Measure the length, width and height of your rectangular box, then add an overlap allowance. This calculator estimates a practical sheet size; it cannot guarantee a fit for unusual folds or thick paper.

How the sheet estimate works

For a simple rectangular box, a useful starting sheet has a length of about twice the box length plus twice the height, and a width of about twice the box width plus twice the height. The calculator adds your chosen overlap allowance to both dimensions.

  • Measure the box at its widest points, including any lid or raised parts.
  • Keep the box centred and test-fold a paper offcut if the gift is valuable.
  • Thick paper, deep folds, bows and decorative layers may need more material.
  • For very large boxes, check that the roll width is sufficient before cutting.
Important limit: Measure the box first; odd shapes need extra paper.

Questions people ask

What measurements do I need?

Measure the box length, width and height in the same unit, using the widest points.

Does the estimate include overlap?

Yes. Enter an extra allowance and it is added to both suggested sheet dimensions.

Will this work for round or irregular gifts?

No. The estimate is for a rectangular box; round or irregular shapes need a different wrapping approach.

Why might I need more paper than the result?

Paper thickness, fold style, large flaps, uneven boxes and decorative layers can all require extra material.
