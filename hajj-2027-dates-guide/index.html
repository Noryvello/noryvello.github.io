Hajj 2027 Dates

Hajj 2027 is expected to begin on Friday 7 May 2027, with the Day of Arafah on Saturday 15 May and Eid al-Adha on Sunday 16 May. These dates follow the Islamic lunar calendar, so they are expected dates and may move by one day when the moon is sighted.

What each day marks

1 Dhul-Hijjah — Friday 7 May 2027. 1 Dhul-Hijjah is the first day of the twelfth and final month of the Islamic year, the month in which Hajj takes place. Pilgrims arrive in Makkah around this time and prepare for the days of Hajj. The first ten days of Dhul-Hijjah are widely observed as a special time in the Islamic calendar.

Day of Arafah — Saturday 15 May 2027. The Day of Arafah, the 9th day of Dhul-Hijjah, is the most important day of Hajj. Pilgrims gather on the plain of Arafat, near Makkah, for the standing at Arafat. Muslims who are not on pilgrimage often mark the day with fasting and prayer.

Eid al-Adha — Sunday 16 May 2027. Eid al-Adha, the Festival of Sacrifice, falls on the 10th day of Dhul-Hijjah, the day after Arafah. It marks the end of the main days of Hajj and is celebrated by Muslims around the world. The festival remembers the willingness of Prophet Ibrahim to sacrifice his son in obedience to God.

How the dates are decided

The Islamic calendar is a lunar calendar, and each month begins when the new crescent moon is sighted. Because the lunar year is about eleven days shorter than the solar year, the dates of Hajj move earlier each year on the Gregorian calendar. That is why the dates in this guide are expected dates, confirmed only when the moon for Dhul-Hijjah is sighted.
