    How a 30-day challenge works

    A 30-day challenge gives you one small action a day for thirty days. The actions are short on purpose: a few minutes is enough, and a small step you actually do beats a big plan you put off. By the end of the month you will have done thirty things you would not otherwise have done.

    How to pick your area

    Pick the area that sounds most appealing right now, not the one you think you should pick. Kindness is about small good turns for other people, Tidy Home is about one small job a day around the house, and Reading is about a little reading every day. You can always try another area next month.

    Questions people ask

    What is a 30-day challenge?

    A 30-day challenge is a small daily action, one a day for thirty days, in one area you want to work on. Each day has a different action, so you build a habit a little at a time instead of trying to change everything at once.

    What are some easy 30-day challenges?

    Easy challenges use actions that take a few minutes and need nothing special: one kind act a day, one small tidy-up a day, or a few pages of reading a day. This page gives you a full thirty-day plan for each of those three areas.

    Do 30-day challenges cost anything?

    No. Every action on this page is free and uses things you already have at home, like paper, a pen, or a book from your shelf. Nothing on the list asks you to buy anything.

    What if I miss a day?

    Nothing goes wrong. Just pick up where you left off the next day, or skip the missed day and move on to the next one. The plan is a set of ideas, not a test, so a missed day does not spoil the challenge.
