How to read the diagram

  • The strings. The six vertical lines are the strings, as if the guitar is standing in front of you. The thick low E string (6th string) is on the left, and the thin high e string (1st string) is on the right.
  • The frets. The horizontal lines are the frets, numbered on the left. The thick line at the top of an open chord is the nut, the bar at the top of the neck.
  • The dots. A dot shows where to press a string down. The number on the dot is the finger: 1 is the index finger, 2 the middle finger, 3 the ring finger and 4 the pinky.
  • Open and muted strings. An "o" above a string means play it open, without pressing it. An "x" means do not play that string at all.
  • The bars. A solid bar across several strings means one finger presses all of them at once — a barre. The number on the bar tells you which finger bars, usually the index finger (1).

Open chords and barre chords

Open chords sit near the nut and use open strings. Most beginners start with C, G, D, A, E, A minor, E minor and D minor — all open chords on this page. Barre chords press several strings with one finger, usually the index, laid flat across the neck. The sharp and flat chords (F#, Bb, Eb and the rest) are barre chords here, and so is F. Every chord on this page is labelled open or barre under its name, so you know what to expect before you try it.

Tips for clean chords

  • Press close to the fret. Put each fingertip just behind the fret wire, not in the middle of the fret. You need less pressure there, and the note rings more clearly.
  • Arch your fingers. Curl your fingers so the tips press the strings and the rest of the finger stays clear of the neighbouring strings.
  • Strum slowly and listen. Pick each string one at a time. If one buzzes or sounds dead, look at the finger nearest it and adjust.
  • Keep your thumb behind the neck. It should sit roughly opposite your middle finger, giving your fingers something to press against.
  • Change chords slowly. Practice moving between two chords at a slow, steady pace before you try to speed up. Clean changes matter more than fast ones.

Questions people ask

How do I read the diagram?

The diagram shows the guitar neck as if the guitar is standing in front of you. The six vertical lines are the strings, with the thick low E string on the left and the thin high e string on the right. The horizontal lines are the frets, numbered on the left. A dot shows where to press a string, and the number on the dot tells you which finger to use.

What do the numbers on the dots mean?

They are finger numbers: 1 is the index finger, 2 the middle finger, 3 the ring finger and 4 the pinky. A solid bar across several strings means one finger presses all of them at once — that is a barre, and the number on the bar tells you which finger bars.

Why do some chords have an x?

An x above a string means do not play it. That string would add a note that does not belong in the chord, so mute it with a spare finger or leave it out of your strum. An o above a string means the opposite: play that string open, without pressing it.

Are these the only ways to play the chord?

No. Every chord can be played in many positions all over the neck. The fingerings here are the standard beginner shapes — the ones most players learn first. Once they feel comfortable, you can explore other voicings further up the neck.
