Pet Choosing Guide

The right pet depends on your life, not just on what looks cute. Answer five quick questions about your time, space, budget, allergies and experience, and this guide shows which pets fit — with one good point and one hard point for each, so you can decide with your eyes open.

This page is general guidance only, not vet or medical advice. Research any pet properly, and ask a vet before you decide — a pet is a long commitment, and the right choice depends on details this guide cannot cover.

Before you choose

A pet is a commitment that lasts years, not weeks. Before you fall for a cute face, think through the practical side of living with an animal.

  • Think about holidays. Who will feed and care for your pet when you are away?
  • Plan for vet visits. Every pet needs regular check-ups, and a sick pet needs prompt care.
  • Check your home rules. Some landlords and flats do not allow pets — check before you decide.
  • Count the years. Make sure your life can hold a pet for its whole lifetime, not just for now.
  • Talk to everyone at home. A pet affects the whole household, so everyone should agree.

Questions people ask

Which pet is easiest for a first-time owner?

Hamsters, fish and guinea pigs are often suggested for first-time owners because they need less time and space than a dog. But easy does not mean no work — every pet needs daily care, a clean home and attention. Start with the pet that fits the time and space you actually have.

I have allergies — which pets can I consider?

Fish are the only pet in this guide with no fur or feathers, so they are the usual choice for people with allergies. For any other pet, spend time around the animal first if you can, and ask a vet before deciding.

How much time does a dog really need?

A dog needs over an hour a day for walks, play, feeding and training, plus company through the day. Dogs are social animals and do not do well left alone for long stretches. If you cannot give that time every day, a cat or a smaller pet may be a better fit.

Can I keep a pet in a small flat?

Yes — cats, rabbits, hamsters, fish, birds and guinea pigs can all live happily in a small flat with the right setup. A dog needs a garden or easy access to outdoor space for walks and exercise. Always check your tenancy rules before bringing a pet home.
