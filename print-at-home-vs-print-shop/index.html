Print at Home or at a Print Shop: Which Costs Less per Page?

Short answer: enter your printer, ink, paper and print-shop costs to estimate which option costs less for your planned page volume.

How the estimate works

The home-print estimate spreads the printer purchase cost across its estimated useful life and expected pages, then adds ink or toner, paper and a waste allowance. The print-shop estimate multiplies the shop's per-page price by the same page count. The result is only as reliable as your yield and usage estimates.

Costs people often overlook

  • Color pages can use more ink or toner than simple text pages.
  • Print heads, drums, maintenance and replacement parts may add costs.
  • Some pages are reprinted because of jams, alignment or mistakes.
  • Print shops may have minimum charges, different paper options or finishing fees.
Important limit: Home printing costs include ink and paper waste, not just the printer price.

Questions people ask

Is printing at home always cheaper?

No. It depends on printer cost, consumables, print volume, waste and the shop price. Enter your own estimates to compare the cost per page.

How do I estimate ink cost per page?

Divide the cost of an ink or toner set by its estimated page yield. Actual yield varies with coverage, print settings, paper and cartridge conditions.

Should I include the printer purchase price?

Yes. This calculator spreads the purchase cost across the useful-life months and expected pages so the initial cost is not ignored.

Does the print shop price include color and finishing?

Only if your entered per-page price includes them. Add separate shop charges or use a representative all-in per-page estimate for the job you need.
