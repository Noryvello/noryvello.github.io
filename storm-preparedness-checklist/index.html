Prepare before the storm, not during it

Storm preparation works best when it is done calmly, before the weather turns. Once a storm is close, shops sell out of batteries and bottled water, and there is no time to clear gutters or secure the garden. Work through this checklist as soon as a storm is forecast, and keep the finished kit somewhere you can reach it in the dark.

The most important preparation is information. Know where your local official weather service publishes warnings, and follow its advice. Official advice tells you when to stay indoors, when to expect the worst of the storm, and when it is safe to go out again. If the authorities tell you to evacuate, do so — no possession is worth the risk.

Your home: the quick wins

Most storm damage to homes comes from things the wind picks up. A garden chair, a plant pot, or a loose bin lid can break a window. Bringing loose items indoors or tying them down takes minutes and prevents the most common damage. Check windows and doors close properly, and clear gutters and drains so rainwater runs away from the house instead of pooling against it.

Know where your shut-offs are before you need them. If water gets in, you may need to turn off the electricity to the affected rooms. If you smell gas, turn it off and leave the house. These are simple steps, but they are hard to work out in the dark during a storm, so find them now and show everyone in the household.

Supplies: what to have ready

Keep enough drinking water, food, and medication for a day or two without power or shops. Food that needs no cooking — tinned food, bread, dried fruit — is the most practical choice. Charge phones and power banks while the power is on, and keep torches and spare batteries in one place. A battery-powered radio is worth keeping too, in case the phone network goes down.

Do not forget the people and animals who depend on you. Keep baby supplies, pet food, and any regular medication in the kit. If someone in the household uses medical equipment that needs power, plan in advance for what happens if the power goes out, and tell your power company if they keep a register of vulnerable customers.

After the storm passes

Stay indoors until the official all-clear. The calm after a storm can be misleading — winds can pick up again, and damaged trees and power lines may still fall. When you do go out, wear sturdy shoes, watch for broken glass and debris, and stay well away from floodwater and fallen power lines. Floodwater can hide open drains and sharp objects, and it may carry sewage.

Check your home carefully before using rooms again. Look for roof damage, water coming in, and any smell of gas. Take photos of damage for insurance purposes before you start clearing up. And check on your neighbours, especially older people and anyone living alone — a quick knock on the door can make a real difference after a storm.

Questions people ask

When should I start preparing for a storm?

As soon as a storm is forecast for your area. Shops sell out of batteries and bottled water quickly, and jobs like clearing gutters and securing the garden are best done in daylight, before the weather turns.

What should I do if the power goes out during a storm?

Stay indoors and use torches rather than candles where you can. Keep the fridge and freezer closed so food stays cold longer, and use a battery-powered radio for updates if the phone network goes down.

Is it safe to drive during a storm?

Avoid driving during a storm if you can. Strong winds, fallen branches, and flooded roads are all dangerous. Never drive through floodwater — it is often deeper than it looks, and a small amount of moving water can sweep a car away.

What should I keep in a storm kit?

Drinking water, food that needs no cooking, torches and spare batteries, a first aid kit, important documents in a waterproof folder, and any regular medication. Keep the kit somewhere you can reach it in the dark.

What should I do after a storm?

Stay indoors until the official all-clear, then check your home for damage before using rooms again. Stay away from floodwater and fallen power lines, take photos of any damage for insurance, and check on neighbours who may need help.
