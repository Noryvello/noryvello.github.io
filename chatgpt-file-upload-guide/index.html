How to Use ChatGPT File Uploads: PDFs, Spreadsheets, and Images

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

ChatGPT can read files you upload, including PDFs, spreadsheets, and images, and then answer questions about them. This turns the chatbot from a general helper into a working partner for your own documents. This guide explains what you can upload and six useful tasks to try, with a privacy note on files you should never upload.

Uploading a file is simple: look for the attach or upload control near the chat box, pick a file from your device, and then type your question. The important part is what happens next. A file upload is only as useful as the question you ask about it. Asking a vague question gets a vague answer, while a specific question gets a specific one. The six tasks below each show the kind of question that works well.

It also helps to know what uploading does not do. ChatGPT reads the content of your file, but it does not keep your file as a permanent document, and it cannot open links or reach files stored elsewhere. Very long files may be read only in part, and scanned pages or photos of text can be misread. Treat every answer as a draft to check, not as a final result.

Never upload files that contain sensitive information: identity documents, account numbers, medical records, contracts with private details, or other people's personal data. Once a file is uploaded, you cannot control every copy of it. Remove sensitive pages first, or retype the part you need as a made-up example.

What you can upload

The most useful uploads are the everyday files people already have: PDF reports and articles, spreadsheet files with tables of numbers, plain text documents, and images such as photos, screenshots, and scanned pages. PDFs work best when the text inside them is real text rather than a picture of a page, because the chatbot can then read every word. A scanned PDF is really a set of images, so it may be read less reliably.

Spreadsheets are useful because ChatGPT can look at the numbers, spot patterns, and explain what a table means in plain words. Images are useful for a different reason: the chatbot can describe what it sees, read text inside a photo, and explain a chart or a diagram. If a file type is refused, the usual fix is to convert it first, for example by saving a document as a PDF or copying a table into a plain spreadsheet.

Summarize a PDF

Long documents are the most common upload, and summarizing is the most common task. Instead of reading a thirty-page report yourself, you can ask for the main points in a few paragraphs, or for a summary aimed at a particular reader. The trick is to say what the summary is for. A summary for a busy manager is different from a summary for a student revising for an exam.

Try a prompt like: "Summarize this report in five bullet points, then list the three recommendations it makes." Follow up with questions about anything unclear, such as "what does the report say about costs?" or "explain the second recommendation in plain words." Always check the summary against the original for anything important, because a summary can quietly drop a detail or blur a number.

Explain a spreadsheet

A spreadsheet full of numbers is hard to read at a glance. Upload it and ask ChatGPT to explain it in plain words: what the columns mean, which rows matter most, and what the overall picture looks like. This is useful for household budgets, sales records, class results, or any table someone else made and you now have to understand.

Good questions include: "What does this spreadsheet show, in two paragraphs?" and "Which three rows changed the most between the first and last month?" You can also ask for the numbers behind a claim, such as "show me the working for that total." If the numbers matter, check them yourself in the spreadsheet, because the chatbot can misread a cell or add a column wrong.

Read a chart

Charts and graphs are easy to upload as images, and ChatGPT can describe what they show. This is helpful when a chart is complicated, when you want a plain-words explanation of a trend, or when you need to describe a chart to someone who cannot see it. Upload a clear image of the chart and ask what it shows.

Ask questions like: "Describe this chart in plain words, including the highest and lowest points" or "What trend does this line show over time?" The answers are usually good for simple charts and weaker for crowded ones with many lines or tiny labels. If the chart has small text, crop the image to the part that matters before uploading, so the labels are large enough to read.

Extract text from an image

Photos of printed pages, signs, receipts, and whiteboards often contain text you need in editable form. Upload the image and ask ChatGPT to type out the text it can see. This saves retyping and works well for printed text that is sharp and well lit. It is also handy for turning a photo of a notice board into a list you can search later.

Handwriting is a different story. Neat handwriting in a clear photo can often be read, but messy, faint, or slanted writing is frequently misread, and the chatbot will not always tell you when it is guessing. Always compare the extracted text with the original image, word by word, before you copy it anywhere important.

Compare two documents

Uploading two files at once lets you ask how they differ. This is useful for comparing two versions of a report, two quotes from suppliers, two job descriptions, or a draft against a final version. Ask for the differences in a table, grouped by topic, so you can see at a glance what changed and what stayed the same.

A useful prompt is: "Compare these two documents. List what is in the first but not the second, what is in the second but not the first, and where they disagree." For long documents, narrow the question first, such as asking only about the pricing section or only about the dates. The comparison is only as good as the files: if the two documents use different words for the same thing, say so in your prompt.

Find errors in a file

A fresh pair of eyes catches mistakes, and a chatbot can be that pair of eyes for a draft, a table, or a list. Upload the file and ask it to check for spelling errors, inconsistent numbers, missing items, or dates that do not add up. This works well for documents you have read so many times that you no longer see the mistakes.

Be specific about what to check: "Check this document for spelling mistakes and list them with the corrected word" or "Check whether the totals in this spreadsheet match the rows, and show me any row that does not add up." The chatbot will sometimes flag things that are not errors, especially names and specialist terms, so treat its list as a set of suggestions to review, not as a final verdict.

Keeping your files private

Before you upload anything, pause for a moment and look at what is in the file. Reports and spreadsheets often contain more than the part you need: a second sheet with names, a footer with an address, or a comment with a phone number. It takes a minute to check, and it is much harder to undo an upload than to clean a file first.

A good routine is to make a copy of the file, delete the pages or columns you do not need, and upload only the copy. For images, crop out anything that is not part of the question. And remember that your uploads stay in your chat history, so anyone who can open your account can see them. Delete the chat when the task is done if the file was at all sensitive.

Questions people ask

What file types can I upload to ChatGPT?

Common types include PDF documents, spreadsheets, plain text files, and images such as photos and screenshots. The exact list changes over time and varies by plan, so if a file type is refused, try converting it to PDF or plain text first.

Is there a size limit on uploads?

Yes, limits exist on file size and on how many files you can upload in a period, and they change over time. Very long documents may be read only in part. If a file is too large, split it into smaller sections and upload the part you need.

Can ChatGPT read handwritten notes?

Sometimes. Clear, neat handwriting in a well-lit photo can often be read, but messy or faint writing is frequently misread. Always check the extracted text against the original before you rely on it.

Should I upload files with personal details?

No. Do not upload files that contain identity numbers, account details, medical records, or other people's private information. Remove sensitive pages or details from a file before uploading, or ask your question using a made-up example instead.

Can ChatGPT edit my file and give it back?

ChatGPT can produce new text based on your file, such as a rewritten version or a summary, and it can sometimes provide a downloadable file. It does not edit your original file in place, so always keep your own copy and check the result before using it.
