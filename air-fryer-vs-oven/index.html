Air Fryer vs Oven Running Cost Comparison

Short answer: For small to medium portions, an air fryer generally costs less to run because it uses lower average wattage, skips long preheating, and cooks faster in a compact chamber. An oven becomes more practical when cooking multiple trays or large roasts at once, where running an air fryer in several consecutive batches would erase its time and energy advantage.

Important Note: Real-world running costs are estimates and vary by appliance efficiency, thermostat cycling (neither appliance draws full rated wattage continuously once up to temperature), food volume, and your utility tariff structure.

Questions people ask

How do you calculate the electricity cost of an air fryer or oven?

Multiply the appliance wattage by the hours used and divide by 1,000 to get kilowatt-hours (kWh). Then multiply the kWh by your electricity price per kWh.

Does an oven draw maximum wattage the entire time it is on?

No. Both electric ovens and air fryers draw peak wattage during initial heating, then their heating elements cycle on and off to maintain the target temperature, so actual kWh consumption is often lower than continuous peak wattage.

Why does preheating matter so much in the comparison?

A full-size oven cavity often takes 10 to 15 minutes just to preheat before food goes in, whereas a compact air fryer heats up in 2 to 3 minutes or needs no preheat at all.

When is an oven cheaper to use than an air fryer?

If you are cooking a large meal that would require running an air fryer in three or four separate batches, baking everything together on two racks in an oven can take less total time and electricity.
