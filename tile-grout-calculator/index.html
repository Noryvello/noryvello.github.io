Choosing a joint width

The joint is the gap between tiles that the grout fills, and its width shapes both the look of the job and how much grout you need. Narrow joints, around 1/16 or 1/8 inch, suit smooth wall tiles and look almost seamless. Floor tiles usually take wider joints, around 1/8 to 1/4 inch, because floors move slightly underfoot and a wider joint lets the grout flex. Doubling the joint width roughly doubles the grout, so measure before entering it above.

Tile size matters too: small tiles have more joints per square foot, so a mosaic floor uses far more grout than the same area in large tiles. Before ordering, work out how many tiles the job needs with a tile count calculator.

How the coverage math works

Grout fills a network of channels, so the amount depends on five things: tile length, tile width, tile thickness, joint width, and total area. Thicker tiles make deeper channels and smaller tiles make more of them, so both push the weight up. The calculator combines these into an estimate in pounds, with a 10% margin already included for grout lost in mixing and washing. For an irregular floor, split it into rectangles and add them up; a flooring area calculator can help with an awkward shape.

Cement grout vs epoxy grout

Most home tiling uses cement grout, a mix of cement, filler, and pigment mixed with water, pressed into the joints, and wiped clean. It is easy to work with but porous, so it can stain and may need sealing in wet areas. Epoxy grout is a two-part resin that sets very hard, resists stains and water, and needs no sealer. The trade-off is that it sets faster, is stickier to clean off tile faces, and is less forgiving for a first-time tiler. For a shower floor, epoxy's toughness is worth considering; for a dry hallway wall, cement grout is usually the simpler choice.

Should I buy extra grout?

Yes. The calculator already adds 10% for mixing loss, but one spare bag is still wise: grout colour can vary between batches, so a bag from the same batch is worth having for touch-ups.

Why do uneven tiles use more grout?

Uneven tiles leave gaps of different depths, and the grout must fill the deepest point everywhere to sit flush. If your tiles are uneven, treat the estimate as a minimum. A paint calculator can work out how much paint the room needs once the tiling is done.

Questions people ask

How is tile grout quantity calculated?

Grout quantity depends on tile length, width, thickness, joint width, and total area covered.

Why do deep joints and uneven tiles use more grout?

Thicker tiles create deeper grout channels, and uneven or textured tiles require more material to fill gaps correctly.

What joint width should I use for wall tiles?

Wall tiles often use narrow joints like 1/16 inch or 1/8 inch, while floor tiles often use 1/8 inch to 1/4 inch.

Should I buy extra grout?

Yes, always buy 10% extra to account for mixing loss and future touch-ups.
