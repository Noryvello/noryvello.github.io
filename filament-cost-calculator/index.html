Filament Cost Calculator

A 3D print's material cost is just its weight times the price of the filament. Enter the print weight in grams and the price you paid per kilogram of filament, and this calculator shows the material cost with the working, in your currency.

This is the material cost only. It does not include electricity, failed prints, supports you did not weigh, or your time, so the full cost of a print is higher than the number shown.

How the cost is worked out

Filament is sold by the kilogram, but a print weighs a few grams. The calculator converts the weight to kilograms first, then multiplies by the price:

  1. Weight in grams ÷ 1000 = weight in kilograms.
  2. Weight in kilograms × price per kg = material cost.

Worked examples:

  • A 150 g print with filament at 20 per kg: 150 g = 0.15 kg, and 0.15 × 20 = 3.00.
  • A 500 g print with filament at 25 per kg: 500 g = 0.5 kg, and 0.5 × 25 = 12.50.
  • A 75 g print with filament at 18 per kg: 75 g = 0.075 kg, and 0.075 × 18 = 1.35.

What the price does not include

The number above covers the filament that went into the print, and nothing else. It does not include:

  • Electricity. The printer uses power for the whole print, and long prints use more.
  • Failed prints. A print that fails halfway still used filament, and that filament is wasted.
  • Supports and brims you did not count. If you weighed only the finished part, the supports and brim are extra filament. Your slicer's total weight includes them.
  • Wear and your time. Nozzles, build plates and other parts wear out, and setting up and finishing a print takes your time.

Questions people ask

How do I find the weight of my print?

Your slicer shows the estimated filament weight in grams for each print before you start it. If you already printed the part, weigh it on a kitchen scale and use that number instead.

Does this include electricity?

No. This calculator works out the filament cost only. Electricity, failed prints, supports and your time are extra, so the real cost of a print is higher than the number here.

Why do I enter the price myself?

Filament prices vary by brand, material and where you buy it, so there is no single price to use. Enter the price you paid per kilogram and the result will be in your currency.

What if my print used supports or a brim?

Add their weight to the print weight. Your slicer counts supports and brims in its total, so use the total filament weight it reports for the most accurate result.
