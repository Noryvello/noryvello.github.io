Rubik's Cube Beginner Method Guide

The beginner method solves the cube one layer at a time, using a few short move sequences that you repeat until each layer falls into place. Learn the notation first, then work through the seven steps in order.

A Rubik's Cube looks like a puzzle for geniuses, but the beginner method needs no special talent — only a little patience and a few short sequences of moves. The idea is simple: solve the cube one layer at a time, from the bottom up, and use the same moves again and again until each layer is done.

This guide explains the notation first, because every sequence in the method is written in it, then walks through the seven steps of the layer-by-layer method with each standard move sequence stated exactly. Follow each move sequence exactly, holding the cube exactly as described — one missed turn changes the whole pattern, so go slowly and count your moves.

The notation

Every move in cubing is written with a single letter for the face you turn: R is the right face, L is the left face, U is the upper face, D is the down face, F is the front face and B is the back face. A letter on its own means turn that face a quarter turn clockwise, as if you were looking straight at it. So R means turn the right face clockwise, and U means turn the top face clockwise.

A letter followed by a prime mark means turn that face a quarter turn anticlockwise: R' is the right face anticlockwise, U' is the top face anticlockwise. A letter followed by 2 means turn the face twice, a half turn either way: U2 is the top face turned twice, and R2 is the right face turned twice.

That is the whole system. A sequence like R' D' R D is four turns: right anticlockwise, down anticlockwise, right clockwise, down clockwise. Read sequences slowly, one letter at a time, and say each move out loud until the letters feel natural. The front face is whichever face is pointing towards you, so if you turn the whole cube around, F and R change too — keep the cube steady while you work through a sequence.

The seven steps

The layer-by-layer method solves the cube in seven steps, and each step only worries about one part of the cube. Step one is the white cross: turn the four white edge pieces into a cross on the white face, with each edge matching the colour of the side centre above it. There is no sequence to memorise for this step — build it by hand, one edge at a time.

Step two places the four white corners, using the sequence R' D' R D repeated until each corner twists into place. Step three fills in the middle layer edges with two mirror-image sequences, one for edges that go right and one for edges that go left. By the end of step three, the bottom two layers are solved.

Steps four to seven solve the yellow top layer. Step four makes the yellow cross with F R U R' U' F'. Step five turns the whole yellow face yellow with R U R' U R U2 R', a sequence often called Sune. Step six swaps the top corners into their correct positions with U R U' L' U R' U' L. Step seven swaps the top edges into place with F2 U L R' F2 L' R U F2, and the cube is solved.

Do not worry if the cube looks messier in the middle of a sequence — that is normal. A sequence only makes sense when it is finished, so always complete the whole sequence before judging the result. If a step goes wrong, the most likely cause is a missed or extra turn, so go back, count carefully, and try again.

Tips for practice

Learn one step at a time and do not move on until you can do it without looking at the guide. Scramble the cube, solve up to the step you know, then scramble again. A few minutes of practice every day will get you further than one long session at the weekend.

Hold the cube the same way for each sequence, and learn to turn the faces with your fingers rather than your whole hand — it is faster and you are less likely to drop the cube. If your cube is stiff, a few dozen solves will loosen it up; there is no need to take it apart.

Your first full solve may take ten minutes or more, and that is completely normal. Most people who practise regularly get down to a few minutes within a few weeks. Speed comes from knowing the sequences by heart and recognising each situation quickly, not from turning fast.

Questions people ask

What does R prime mean?

R means turn the right face a quarter turn clockwise, as you look at it. R prime means turn the right face a quarter turn anticlockwise. A 2 after a letter, like U2, means turn that face twice.

How long does the beginner method take to learn?

Most people learn the seven steps over a few days of short practice sessions. Your first full solve may take ten minutes or more, and regular practice usually brings that down to a few minutes within weeks.

What if the yellow cross will not form?

Keep repeating F R U R' U' F'. The cross always appears after a few repeats — hold a line of yellow sideways and an L shape at the back-left before each repeat, and count your turns carefully.

Do I need a special cube?

No. Any standard 3x3 cube works for the beginner method. A cube that turns smoothly helps, and a stiff new cube usually loosens up after a few dozen solves.

Can I solve the cube without memorising sequences?

The first step, the white cross, is solved by hand with no sequence. The other steps each use a short sequence, but there are only a few of them, and they are easy to memorise with practice.
