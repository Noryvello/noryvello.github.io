The dried-to-fresh rule

Dried herbs are stronger than fresh ones, because the water is gone and the flavour is packed into a smaller amount. The standard rule is simple: use one third as much dried herb as fresh herb. That means 1 teaspoon of dried herbs equals 1 tablespoon of fresh herbs, and 1 tablespoon dried equals 3 tablespoons fresh. Dried herbs suit long-cooked dishes best — add them early so they have time to soften and release their flavour. For a fresh garnish, though, nothing quite replaces fresh leaves.

How to use a substitute

Start with the amount the finder suggests, then taste and adjust. Add the substitute in two goes if you are unsure — you can always add more, but you cannot take it back. Remember that a substitute changes the dish: oregano in place of basil gives a warmer, more peppery result, and that is fine as long as you expect it. Fresh herbs added at the end of cooking keep the most flavour; dried herbs need time in the pot.

Questions people ask

Can I use dried herbs instead of fresh?

Yes, in most cooked dishes. Dried herbs are stronger, so use one third as much dried herb as fresh herb — 1 teaspoon dried equals 1 tablespoon fresh. Dried herbs work less well as a fresh garnish, where the look and delicate flavour of fresh leaves matter.

What can I use instead of basil?

Oregano is the closest everyday swap — use the same amount. Mint also works in salads and sweet dishes, and parsley gives a milder, greener flavour. For pesto, a mix of parsley and a little spinach is a common stand-in.

How much dried herb equals fresh?

Use one third as much dried herb as fresh herb. So 1 tablespoon of fresh herbs equals 1 teaspoon of dried herbs, and 3 tablespoons fresh equals 1 tablespoon dried. Taste as you go, because dried herbs vary in strength.

What is a good substitute for cilantro?

Flat-leaf parsley with a squeeze of lemon is the best-known substitute — use the same amount of parsley. Basil works in Asian-style dishes, and mint adds freshness, though you should use half as much because it is stronger. If you dislike cilantro's soapy taste, parsley is the safest choice.
