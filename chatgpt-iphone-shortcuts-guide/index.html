ChatGPT iPhone Shortcuts: Automate Chats with Siri Shortcuts

By the Noryvello team · Published 20 November 2026 · Updated 20 November 2026

Apple's Shortcuts app lets you run ChatGPT with one tap or a voice command, instead of opening the app and typing every time. You can ask a question, summarise copied text, translate a phrase, or start a voice chat — all from Siri or your home screen. This guide shows the six most useful shortcuts, how to add them, and how to fix them when they stop working.

App screens and menu names change, and the steps here describe how things commonly work — the in-app help is the authority. The ChatGPT app must be installed and signed in for its shortcuts to run.

What the Shortcuts app is

The Shortcuts app is Apple's automation tool, built into every iPhone. A shortcut is a small recipe: one tap, or one spoken phrase, runs a chain of steps that would otherwise take several taps in several apps. People use shortcuts to send a message, start a playlist, log water, or open a map — and, since the ChatGPT app added its own actions, to talk to ChatGPT without opening it.

Think of it as a speed dial for tasks. Just as speed dial turns a ten-digit number into one button, a shortcut turns a small routine into one tap. The routine can be as simple as "ask ChatGPT a question" or as long as "copy this text, summarise it, and save the summary to my notes".

Shortcuts are free and built in, and you do not need to know anything technical to use them. The app comes with a gallery of ready-made shortcuts, and the ChatGPT app adds its own actions to the list. The rest of this guide shows the six most useful ChatGPT shortcuts and how to set them up.

What you need first

Two things must be true before any ChatGPT shortcut can run. First, the ChatGPT app must be installed on your iPhone — the shortcuts call the app, so without it there is nothing to call. Second, you must be signed in to the app, because the shortcut runs as you and uses your account.

If a shortcut asks you to sign in again, that second condition has broken: the app's sign-in has lapsed, and the shortcut cannot run until you open the app and sign in fresh. The troubleshooting section covers this in detail, because it is the most common failure.

You will also want the Shortcuts app itself, which comes with the iPhone. If you deleted it, you can get it back from the App Store. No other setup is needed: once both apps are installed and ChatGPT is signed in, its actions appear in the Shortcuts app ready to use.

The 6 most useful ChatGPT shortcuts

These six cover the jobs people actually ask for on a phone: a quick question, a summary, a translation, a voice chat, a fresh chat, and help with a photo. Each one is a single action, so each one takes a minute to set up.

  • Ask anything — speak or type a question and get ChatGPT's answer, without opening the app. The everyday shortcut, and the one to set up first.
  • Summarise copied text — copy a long article or message, run the shortcut, and get a short summary. Useful for long emails and news stories.
  • Translate — take text in one language and get it back in another. Handy while travelling or reading messages from abroad.
  • Start a voice chat — open a spoken conversation with ChatGPT, hands-free. Good for the kitchen, the car, or a walk.
  • Open a new chat — jump straight to a fresh conversation in the ChatGPT app, skipping the chat list.
  • Explain a photo — send a picture and ask what it shows, or what the text in it says. Useful for signs, labels, and homework.

Start with "ask anything" and one more that fits your day. Two shortcuts you actually use beat six you forget, and you can always add the rest later.

How to add a shortcut, step by step

Open the Shortcuts app and tap the plus button to create a new shortcut. Tap "Add Action", then search for ChatGPT: the app's actions appear in a list, with names like "Ask ChatGPT" and "Start voice conversation". Pick the action that matches the shortcut you want.

The action appears as a sentence with blanks to fill in. For "ask anything", the blank is the question: tap it and choose "Ask Each Time", so the shortcut prompts you when it runs. For "summarise copied text", the blank is the text: choose the clipboard, so the shortcut reads whatever you copied.

Give the shortcut a short, clear name — the name is what you will say to Siri, so "Ask ChatGPT" beats "My ChatGPT Question Shortcut". Tap Done to save. Then run it once from the Shortcuts app to test it: if it works there, it will work from Siri and the home screen too.

Screens and menu names change, and the steps here describe how things commonly work. If a button has moved, the in-app help is the authority, and the shape of the task is the same: add the action, fill the blanks, name it, test it.

Triggering it with Siri

Once a shortcut has a name, Siri can run it. Say the trigger phrase followed by the shortcut's name — for example, "Ask ChatGPT" — and Siri runs it and speaks or shows the answer. The name is the command, which is why a short, distinct name matters: Siri must hear it clearly and tell it apart from other shortcuts.

If Siri misunderstands the name, rename the shortcut to something simpler and more distinct. One or two words work best, and words that sound different from your other shortcuts help. Test the phrase twice in a quiet room before relying on it in the car.

Voice shortcuts shine when your hands are busy: cooking, driving, walking, holding a child. Ask the question, hear the answer, and carry on. For anything you need to read carefully — a long summary, a translation to copy — run the shortcut by tapping it instead, so the answer stays on screen.

Adding a shortcut to the home screen

A shortcut you use every day deserves a home screen icon. In the Shortcuts app, open the shortcut, tap the share or details control, and choose "Add to Home Screen". You can pick an icon and a colour, then tap Add. The shortcut appears on your home screen like an app, and one tap runs it.

Home screen shortcuts are best for the jobs you run by touch: "ask anything" for quick questions, "summarise copied text" after reading, "open a new chat" when you want a fresh conversation. Voice is better for hands-free moments; the home screen is better for the jobs you do while looking at the phone.

Keep the home screen tidy: two or three shortcuts, not ten. A row of shortcuts you never tap is clutter, and clutter makes the useful ones harder to find. If a shortcut goes unused for a month, remove it from the home screen — it stays in the Shortcuts app, ready to return.

Troubleshooting

The most common failure is a shortcut that asks you to sign in again. It means the ChatGPT app's sign-in has lapsed. Open the ChatGPT app, sign in fresh, and run the shortcut again. If it keeps happening, check that the app is up to date and that you are signed in with the same account the shortcut expects.

A shortcut that does nothing at all usually has a blank it cannot fill. Open it in the Shortcuts app and check each step: a question set to "Ask Each Time" needs you to answer the prompt, and a summary step needs text on the clipboard. Run the shortcut from inside the app once — the app shows where it stops, which tells you what to fix.

No sound on a voice shortcut usually means the phone is muted, the volume is down, or the shortcut is running while another app holds the speaker. Check the silent switch and the volume, close other audio apps, and try again. If Siri says it cannot run the shortcut, check the shortcut's name — Siri may be hearing a different phrase — and rename it to something shorter and clearer.

Android note in plain words

Apple's Shortcuts app is for iPhones, so Android phones do not have it. Android has its own automation — routines in the Google or Samsung apps, and widgets — but the steps in this guide do not transfer directly, and the ChatGPT app's actions are not the same on Android.

On Android the natural route is Gemini, Google's assistant, which is built into many Android phones and can answer questions, summarise text, and work with voice in a similar way. If you use Android, the Gemini guides on this site cover that route in detail — the links at the end of this page point to them.

The idea is the same on both platforms: one tap or one spoken phrase, and the assistant answers. Only the plumbing differs. Pick the route that matches your phone, and the habits from this guide — short names, one job per shortcut, test before relying on it — carry straight across.

Questions people ask

Can I use ChatGPT with Siri?

Yes, through Apple's Shortcuts app. Make a shortcut that calls a ChatGPT action, give it a short name, and Siri will run it when you say the name. The ChatGPT app must be installed and signed in, because the shortcut runs through the app.

How do I add a ChatGPT shortcut on iPhone?

Open the Shortcuts app, tap the plus button, tap Add Action, and search for ChatGPT. Pick an action such as Ask ChatGPT, fill in the blanks, give the shortcut a short name, and save it. Run it once from the app to test it before using it with Siri.

What can a ChatGPT shortcut do?

The most useful ones ask a question, summarise copied text, translate, start a voice chat, open a new chat, or explain a photo. Each shortcut is one action, so complex jobs need several steps — but the everyday jobs are all single actions.

Why does my shortcut ask me to sign in again?

It means the ChatGPT app's sign-in has lapsed. Open the ChatGPT app and sign in fresh, then run the shortcut again. If it keeps happening, check that the app is up to date and that you are signed in with the same account.

Can I put ChatGPT on my home screen?

Yes, two ways. You can add a shortcut to the home screen from the Shortcuts app, so one tap runs it, or you can put the ChatGPT app itself on the home screen like any other app. The shortcut is faster for one specific job; the app is better for general use.

Is there a ChatGPT shortcut for Android?

Apple's Shortcuts app is for iPhones, so Android does not have it. Android has its own routines and widgets, and the natural voice route on Android is Gemini, which is covered in the Gemini guides linked at the end of this page.
