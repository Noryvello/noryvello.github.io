The arrival of a new baby is one of life's happiest moments, and a warm message means the world to tired, proud new parents. The messages below are all original, written for this page, and grouped so you can find the right words for the parents, the grandparents, or the wider family.

Keep it warm and simple. New parents are short on sleep and long on love, so a short message full of joy is perfect. Avoid comments about the baby's looks, the birth, or how the parents should do things.

How to make your message personal

A personal touch makes a baby message special. Mention the baby's name if you know it, or the joy of meeting them soon. A line about the parents, such as how wonderful they will be, means as much as praise for the baby.

Offer practical help if you can. New parents remember the friend who brought a meal or offered to help with the shopping far longer than they remember the flowers. A message that says you are there for them is worth more than a perfect sentence.

What not to say

Some well-meaning comments land badly in the first days. Avoid remarks about the baby's looks, comparisons with other babies, or questions about the birth. Avoid advice about feeding, sleeping or parenting unless you are asked. And never say the parents should enjoy it while it lasts, as if the hard parts are coming.

Keep the message joyful and free of pressure. Congratulations, love and an offer of help are all a new family needs to hear.

Questions people ask

What is a good message for new parents?

Congratulate them warmly, welcome the baby, and wish the family well. A short message with love and an offer of help is perfect. Mention the baby by name if you know it.

What should I write to new grandparents?

Congratulate them on the new grandchild and share in their joy. Grandparents love hearing that the baby is lucky to have them and that a wonderful new chapter has begun.

When should I send a new baby message?

As soon as you hear the news. A message in the first day or two is warmly received. If you visit, keep it short and let the parents rest.

Should I mention the baby's name?

Yes, if you know it and the parents have shared it. Using the baby's name makes the message feel personal and special.

What should I avoid saying to new parents?

Avoid comments about looks, the birth, or parenting advice they did not ask for. Keep it joyful, simple and free of pressure.
