Slow Cooker vs Oven Running Cost

The answer depends on your own appliances: a slow cooker runs for hours at low power, while an oven runs for less time at higher power. Enter the watts and cook time for each, your energy rate and how often you cook, and this page shows the cost per cook and the yearly cost for each.

This compares energy cost only. It does not include the cost of the food, the appliance itself, or any standing charge on your energy bill, and it assumes each appliance draws its full rated watts for the whole cook time — in practice many appliances cycle on and off.

How it works

Electricity use is measured in kilowatt-hours. One kilowatt-hour is the energy used by a 1,000-watt appliance running for one hour. To find the energy for one cook, the calculator multiplies the watts by the hours and divides by 1,000, then multiplies by the energy rate you enter. The yearly figure multiplies the cost of one cook by the number of cooks you do each week and by 52 weeks.

You can find the watts on the rating plate or label on the appliance, or in its manual. Your energy rate is the price per kilowatt-hour on your energy bill. If your bill shows more than one rate, use the one that applies at the time you usually cook.

How to use the result

Look at both figures, not just one. The cost per cook shows the gap for a single meal, while the yearly cost shows what that gap adds up to over a year of cooking the way you do now. If the yearly difference is small, the choice may come down to taste, texture and convenience rather than cost.

Try the calculator with different cook times. A slow cooker left on for a very long cook can cost more than a short oven cook, and a larger oven cook that feeds several people can work out cheaper per serving than a small slow-cooker meal. Change one number at a time to see what moves the result.

What this comparison leaves out

Energy is only one part of cooking. The calculator does not count the food, the cost of the appliance, or the standing charge on your energy bill. It also assumes each appliance draws its full rated watts for the whole cook time. In practice, many appliances cycle on and off once they reach temperature, so the real energy use can be lower than the rated watts suggest.

It also leaves out preheating. If you want the oven's preheating counted, include that time in the oven cook time you enter.

Questions people ask

Does a slow cooker always cost less to run than an oven?

Not always. A slow cooker draws less power but runs for much longer, so a very long slow cook can cost more than a short oven cook. Enter your own watts and times to see which is cheaper for the way you cook.

Where do I find the watts of my cooker and oven?

Check the rating plate or label on the appliance, usually on the back or underneath, or look in the manual. The watts are the power the appliance draws when it is running.

What energy rate should I enter?

Enter the price per kilowatt-hour shown on your energy bill. If your bill has different rates at different times of day, use the rate that applies when you usually cook.

Does the calculator include preheating the oven?

Only if you include it. Enter the full time the oven is switched on, including preheating, in the oven cook time box, and the preheating energy will be counted.

Why is the yearly cost an estimate?

It multiplies your cost per cook by the cooks you do each week and by 52 weeks, and it assumes every cook is the same length. Real cooking varies week to week, so treat the yearly figure as a guide, not a promise.
