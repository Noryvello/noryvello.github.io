Cashback or Points: Which Reward Style Wins for Your Spending?

Short answer: enter your spending, cashback rate, points earning rate, estimated redemption value and fees to compare net yearly rewards.

How to compare reward styles

The calculator estimates annual cashback from eligible spending and the rate you enter. For points, it estimates points earned and converts them using your estimated redemption value per 1,000 points. It subtracts the annual fee entered for each option. It does not account for caps, excluded purchases, redemption restrictions, expiry, changing terms or the effort needed to redeem points.

Questions to check before comparing

  • Does the reward apply to the categories where you actually spend?
  • Are there caps, minimum redemptions, expiry dates or blackout restrictions?
  • Can you reliably redeem points at the value you entered?
  • Would an annual fee outweigh the reward you expect to earn?
Important limit: Rewards never beat paying interest; carrying a balance wipes out any reward.

General education only. This tool compares user-entered reward assumptions and does not recommend or rank credit cards.

Questions people ask

Is cashback easier to value than points?

Usually. Cashback has a stated cash value, while points depend on the available redemption options and the value you can actually obtain.

How do I estimate the value of points?

Use the value you could realistically redeem, not an advertised maximum. Check restrictions, minimums, expiry and whether the redemption you want is available.

Should I include annual fees?

Yes. Enter the annual fee for each option so the comparison shows estimated rewards after those fees.

Can rewards make carrying a credit card balance worthwhile?

No. Interest and fees can outweigh rewards. Paying the statement balance in full and understanding the card terms matter more than reward earning.
