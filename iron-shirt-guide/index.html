How to Iron a Shirt

A shirt irons best in a set order, from the small pieces to the large ones, with the heat matched to the fabric. This guide gives you a fabric and heat picker, the order that works, and a checklist to keep wrinkles away.

Ironing a shirt feels fiddly the first few times because a shirt is not one flat piece of cloth. It has a collar, cuffs, sleeves, shoulders and a buttoned front, and each part needs the iron in a different position. The trick is to work in an order that keeps finished parts out of the way while you iron the next one.

The other half of the job is heat. Too little heat leaves creases behind, and too much heat scorches cotton or melts synthetic fibres. The care label on the shirt tells you the safe heat, and the fabric picker below turns that into plain guidance for the most common shirt fabrics.

An iron is hot enough to burn skin and scorch fabric. Never leave a hot iron face down or unattended, keep it away from children, and always check the care label on the shirt before you choose a heat setting.

Pick the fabric

Choose the fabric of your shirt to see the heat setting and the notes that go with it. If the shirt is a blend, follow the guidance for the more delicate fibre in the blend.

Setting up

Set the ironing board at a height that lets you press down without hunching. Your elbow should be slightly bent when your hand rests on the board. A board that is too low strains your back, and a board that is too high makes it hard to press firmly.

Check the soleplate of the iron before you start. A dirty soleplate can mark a clean shirt, so wipe it with a damp cloth while it is cool. Fill the water tank if you plan to use steam, and let the iron reach the right heat before you touch the shirt. An iron that is still heating up will not press creases out properly.

If your iron has a steam setting, check that it matches the fabric before you start. Steam helps cotton and linen but can spot silk, so turn the steam off for delicate fabrics. If you are ironing several shirts in one session, start with the ones that need the lowest heat and work up to the hottest, so you are not waiting for the iron to cool down between shirts.

Have a hanger ready. A shirt that is hung straight after ironing keeps its shape, while a shirt left in a pile picks up new creases while it is still warm. Button the top button on the hanger to hold the collar in shape.

The order that works

  1. Collar. Lay the collar flat on the board, wrong side up, and iron from the points toward the centre. Turn it over and iron the other side.
  2. Cuffs. Open each cuff flat and iron the inside first, then the outside. Work from the edges toward the middle so you do not press a fold into the cuff.
  3. Sleeves. Lay a sleeve flat with the seam at the edge, iron one side, then turn it over and iron the other side. If you want a crisp crease, line the seams up; if not, keep the sleeve flat and smooth.
  4. Yoke and shoulders. Slip the shoulder of the shirt over the narrow end of the board and iron the yoke, the panel across the shoulders, on both sides.
  5. Back. Lay the back of the shirt flat on the board and iron it in smooth strokes from the centre outward.
  6. Front panels. Iron each front panel, working around the buttons rather than over them. Use the tip of the iron to get between the buttons.
  7. Final check. Lift the shirt, give it a gentle shake, and look for any creases you missed. Touch them up, then hang the shirt straight away.

Fixing common problems

If a crease will not come out, the shirt is probably too dry. Use the steam setting or a light spray of water, then press again. Pressing the same dry crease over and over only polishes the fabric without relaxing the fibres.

Shiny marks on dark shirts come from pressing the right side of the fabric with too much heat. Turn the shirt inside out and iron from the wrong side, or lay a clean cotton cloth between the iron and the shirt. The cloth takes the shine instead of the shirt.

If the iron drags or sticks, the soleplate may need cleaning, or the heat may be too high for the fabric. Let the iron cool, wipe the soleplate with a damp cloth, and check the care label before you start again.

A collar that curls at the points can be tamed by ironing it flat and then pressing the points down with the tip of the iron while the fabric is still warm. For a collar that keeps curling, iron it from the wrong side first, then from the right side, which helps the fold sit flat. When you have finished ironing, empty the water tank before you put the iron away, and stand it upright until it is cool.

Questions people ask

What order should I iron a shirt in?

Collar first, then cuffs, then sleeves, then the yoke and shoulders, then the back, then the front panels. Ironing the small pieces first keeps them out of the way while you do the large flat areas.

Should I iron a shirt when it is dry or damp?

Slightly damp is best, because damp fibres relax and creases come out with less heat. If the shirt is fully dry, use the steam setting or a light spray of water before you start.

Why does my iron leave shiny marks on dark shirts?

Shine comes from pressing the right side of the fabric with too much heat. Turn the shirt inside out, lower the heat, and use a pressing cloth between the iron and the fabric.

Can I iron a shirt without an ironing board?

Yes, on a flat heatproof surface covered with a folded towel. It works for a quick touch-up, but a board is better for a full shirt because you can shape the sleeves and shoulders over the narrow end.

How do I stop shirts creasing after ironing?

Hang the shirt on a hanger straight after ironing, button the top button, and let it cool before you put it in the wardrobe. Folding a warm shirt straight away presses new creases into it.
