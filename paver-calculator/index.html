Paver Calculator — How Many Pavers and How Much Sand Do You Need?

Divide patio area by the face area of one paver and round up to estimate the paver count. Estimate bedding sand by multiplying patio area by the planned sand depth; the result is a volume estimate before compaction or site adjustments.

Honest limit: Add extra pavers for cuts and breakage. Ground preparation matters more than the count.

How the Paver Formula Works

  1. One paver's face area: length × width, converted to square feet or square meters.
  2. Paver count: patio area ÷ paver face area, rounded up.
  3. Sand volume: patio area × planned bedding depth.

The sand figure is a simple geometric volume estimate. It does not add extra material for uneven excavation, compaction, grading, spills, or site-specific base construction.

Worked Example

A 200 sq ft patio using 8 × 4 inch pavers has a paver face area of 32 square inches, or about 0.222 sq ft. The area-only estimate is about 901 pavers before adding a cutting and breakage allowance. A 1-inch bedding layer over 200 sq ft has a geometric volume of about 16.7 cubic feet.

Questions people ask

How do I calculate the number of patio pavers?

Convert the paver length and width to the same area unit as the patio, divide patio area by one paver's face area, and round up to a whole paver.

Does the paver count include cutting waste?

No. The count is the area-based minimum estimate. Add extra pavers for cuts, breakage, pattern layout, and any future replacements.

How much sand do I need under pavers?

Multiply the patio area by the planned bedding-sand depth to estimate volume. This geometric estimate does not account for compaction, uneven ground, or site preparation.

Is bedding sand enough to prepare a patio base?

Not necessarily. A durable patio depends on excavation, drainage, a suitable compacted base, edge restraint, and the correct bedding layer for the site and paver system.
