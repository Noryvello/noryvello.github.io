Flight vs Train Trip Cost

A flight can look cheaper than a train until you add the cost and time of getting to and from the airport. Enter the fare, transfer cost and travel time for each, and this page shows the total cost and the door-to-door time for both.

This compares the fares and transfer costs you enter only. It leaves out baggage fees, food, seat selection, overnight stays and the value of your time, and it takes your travel times as given — check timetables for the real journey.

How it works

Each side is added up the same way: the fare plus the transfer cost at both ends. The flight total is the flight fare plus what you pay to get to and from the airport. The train total is the train fare plus what you pay to get to and from the station. The door-to-door times you enter are shown next to the totals, so cost and time can be weighed together.

Door-to-door time means the full journey, from leaving home to arriving at your destination. For a flight that includes getting to the airport, check-in and security, the flight itself, and getting from the airport at the other end. For a train it includes getting to the station, waiting and the journey itself.

How to use the result

Look at the totals first, then the times. A flight is often quicker in the air but slower door to door, because airports sit further from city centres and check-in adds time. A train can win on the full journey even when the flight itself is shorter.

Make sure both sides cover the same trip. Compare one-way with one-way, or return with return, and count the same number of travellers on each side. If your plans change, change the figures and run the comparison again — the page follows whatever you enter.

What this comparison leaves out

Fares are only part of a trip. The calculator does not count baggage fees, food, seat selection, overnight stays, or the value of your time. If any of those apply, add them to the matching side yourself before deciding.

The travel times are the ones you enter, so they are only as good as your own figures. Timetables, connections and delays all change real journey times, so check the timetable for the day you plan to travel.

Questions people ask

What counts as transfer cost?

Whatever you pay to get to and from the airport or station at both ends, such as a taxi, bus, parking or a lift you pay for.

What is door-to-door time?

The full time from leaving home to arriving at your destination, including getting to the airport or station, waiting and the journey itself.

Does the flight fare include baggage?

Only if you include it in the fare you enter. Add any baggage fee to the fare box so the comparison counts it.

Why can a train win even when the flight is faster?

Because the airport is often further away and check-in and security add time. The page compares your own totals so you can see both sides.

Should I compare one-way or return?

Compare the same thing on both sides. Enter one-way fares and times for a one-way comparison, or return figures for a return trip.
