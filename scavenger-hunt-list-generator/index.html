How to run a scavenger hunt

Print the list for your setting and age group, then split into teams or play on your own. Set a time limit before you start — around half an hour works well for most groups — and agree how the winner is decided: first to find all 15 items, or the most items when time runs out. Tick off each item as you find it, and take a photo of anything you cannot pick up.

Tips for a fair hunt

Keep everyone in the same area so nobody has an unfair head start. Agree before you start whether a photo counts as a find — it should, for anything alive, fragile or fixed in place. For younger children, read the list out loud first and pair each child with an adult. If two teams disagree about an item, the hunt leader has the final say.

Questions people ask

How do I run a scavenger hunt?

Pick a setting and an age group, print the list, and split into teams or play on your own. Set a time limit, then head out and tick off each item as you find it. The first team to find all 15 items — or the team with the most when time runs out — wins.

What makes a good scavenger hunt item?

A good item is easy to recognise, possible to find where you are playing, and just hard enough to make you look properly. The best hunts mix easy finds with a few tricky ones, and use things you can spot without picking them up or buying anything.

How long should a scavenger hunt last?

Around half an hour works well for most groups, and younger children do best with a shorter hunt. Set the time before you start, and agree what happens when time runs out — the most items found usually wins.

Can adults play scavenger hunts?

Yes. Scavenger hunts work for any age — the adult lists on this page ask for things to identify and notice, not just to spot. They work well for team days, parties and family outings, and you can make them harder by shortening the time limit.
