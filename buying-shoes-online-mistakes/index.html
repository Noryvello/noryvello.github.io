7 Shoe-Fit Mistakes to Avoid When Buying Online

Short answer: The most common online shoe-buying mistakes are guessing your size, ignoring width and shape, overlooking socks and intended use, and skipping the return policy. Use the fit-check questions below before choosing a pair.

The seven size and fit mistakes

  1. Using one remembered size everywhere. Sizing and shape can differ between brands and even between models.
  2. Measuring only one foot. Feet may differ; use the larger foot as a practical reference when comparing measurements with the brand chart.
  3. Ignoring width. Length alone does not tell you whether the shoe will feel cramped across the forefoot.
  4. Skipping toe-box shape. A pointed or narrow front can feel different from a rounded design even at a similar length.
  5. Forgetting socks and use. Thick socks, running, work and casual wear can change what fit feels appropriate.
  6. Trusting star ratings alone. Read comments about sizing, heel slip, rubbing and width.
  7. Not checking returns. Confirm whether worn or tried-on shoes can be returned and who pays return shipping.

A useful try-on check

Try shoes indoors on a clean surface. Check toe room, heel hold and pressure points while standing and walking. Stop if the fit causes pain or numbness.

Important limit: Sizes vary by brand; check the brand's own size chart.

Questions people ask

Should I order my usual shoe size from every brand?

No. Sizing and shape can vary by brand and model, so compare your measurements with the brand’s own size chart.

Should I measure both feet?

Yes. Feet can differ in length or width; measure both while standing and use the larger measurement as a practical reference.

Why does shoe width matter?

A shoe can have enough length but still feel tight across the forefoot or toes if its width or shape does not suit your foot.

What should I check before ordering shoes online?

Check the brand size chart, width and toe-box details, intended use, customer fit notes and return conditions.
