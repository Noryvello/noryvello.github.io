Pet Sitter Instructions Builder

Fill in your pet's feeding times, walks, medication notes and vet contact, and this builder turns them into a neat instruction sheet you can print and leave for your sitter. A clear sheet means your pet's routine stays the same while you are away, and your sitter never has to guess.

A pet sitter does their best work when they know exactly what your pet needs. Written instructions remove the guesswork: the sitter can see the feeding times, the walk route and the vet's number at a glance, instead of trying to remember everything from a hurried chat at the door.

This builder puts the sheet together for you. Fill in as much as you can, then build the sheet and print it. Leave it somewhere obvious, such as on the kitchen table, and go through it with your sitter once before you leave.

This builder does not give veterinary advice. Medication notes here are only the instructions you have already been given for your own pet — write them exactly as your vet explained them, and never add a medicine or a dose from another source.

What to include in the sheet

Start with the daily routine: what your pet eats, how much, and when. Write the amounts in the words you use at home, so there is no confusion about scoop sizes or tin sizes. Add the walk times and the usual route, plus anything your pet is nervous about, such as fireworks or busy roads.

Add the vet's name and phone number, even if your pet is healthy. If your pet takes medicine, write down exactly what your vet told you — the medicine, the amount and the time — and nothing else. The sitter should follow your written instructions and call you or the vet if anything seems wrong.

Finish with house notes: where the food is kept, where the lead and bowls live, and anything about the house the sitter should know. Keep the sheet to one page if you can, so it is easy to follow at a glance.

Before you hand the sheet over

Walk your sitter through the sheet in person, ideally during a trial visit. Show them where everything is, introduce them to your pet, and watch how they get on together. A short trial visit answers more questions than any sheet can.

Leave your own phone number on the sheet and agree how often you would like updates. Most sitters are happy to send a short message each day. That way you can relax, knowing your pet is in good hands and your sitter can reach you if anything comes up.

Questions people ask

What should I put in the feeding section?

Write the food name, the amount and the feeding times, plus any treats and where the water bowl is. Use the words you use at home, so the sitter measures the food the way you do.

Can I include medication instructions?

Yes, but only the instructions you already have from your vet. Write them exactly as your vet explained them, and tell the sitter to call you or the vet if anything seems wrong.

What if my sitter has never looked after my pet before?

Arrange a trial visit before the real sit. Walk through the sheet together, show the sitter where everything is, and let your pet get used to them.

Should I leave an emergency contact?

Yes. Leave your own number and the vet's number on the sheet, plus a neighbour or friend the sitter can call if they cannot reach you.

Can I print the sheet?

Yes. Press the print button under the builder to print the instruction sheet. Leave it somewhere obvious, such as the kitchen table.
