Diwali 2027 Date

Diwali 2027 falls on Friday 29 October 2027 in India, with the five-day festival running from Dhanteras on Wednesday 27 October to Bhai Dooj on Sunday 31 October. Diwali is the festival of lights, marked by lamps, worship, family gatherings, sweets, and fireworks. Diwali is one of the most widely celebrated festivals in the Hindu calendar, and in 2027 its main day — Lakshmi Puja — falls on Friday 29 October. But Diwali is not a single day: it is a five-day festival, and each of the five days has its own name and place in the celebration. The festival opens with Dhanteras on Wednesday 27 October and closes with Bhai Dooj on Sunday 31 October.

The date of Diwali moves between October and November each year, because it follows the Hindu lunar calendar rather than the Gregorian one. That is why the same festival can fall in late October one year and in mid-November the next. Families planning gatherings or travel can use the full five-day span to decide which days matter most to them — the main day of Lakshmi Puja on Friday 29 October is the centre of the celebration, with two days of festival on either side of it.

What Diwali is

Diwali is the festival of lights. Homes are lit with small oil lamps called diyas, and the rows of lamps are the sight the festival is named for — light set against the dark of the new-moon night. The lamps, the decorations, and the gatherings all belong to the same celebration: a festival of home, family, and welcome.

At the centre of the festival is worship: Lakshmi is worshipped together with Ganesh, for well-being. The main day of the festival takes its name from this worship — Lakshmi Puja — and it is the evening of that day, Friday 29 October 2027, when the lamps are lit and the worship is offered.

Around the worship, Diwali is a family festival. Families gather, sweets are shared, and fireworks light up the night. The five days give the celebration its shape: it opens with Dhanteras, builds through Choti Diwali to the main day, and closes with Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj. Each day adds its own part to a festival that is, above all, about light, home, and togetherness.

How the Diwali date is decided

Diwali follows the Hindu lunar calendar. It falls on the Amavasya — the new moon — of the month of Kartik, which is called Ashwin in the amanta reckoning. The Amavasya is the darkest night of the lunar month, when the Moon is not visible, and that is the night the lamps of Diwali are lit against.

The rule that fixes the day is about the evening. The correct day for Diwali is the one whose evening — Pradosh Kaal, the early part of the night — falls within the Amavasya tithi, the lunar day of the new moon. The tithi is a lunar measure, not a fixed 24-hour day, so it can begin and end at different clock times in different years. The day whose Pradosh Kaal falls inside the Amavasya tithi is the day of Diwali.

This is why the Gregorian date of Diwali moves between October and November each year. The lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar do not line up: the lunar year is shorter than the solar year, so a festival fixed to a lunar date drifts earlier through the Gregorian calendar from year to year, and is brought back into line by the extra months of the lunar calendar. In 2027 the Amavasya of Kartik falls in late October, giving Diwali on Friday 29 October. In other years it can fall well into November.

The same rule explains why the five days sit where they do. Dhanteras and Choti Diwali fall on the two days before the Amavasya, and Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj fall on the two days after it. The whole five-day festival is built around the new-moon day at its centre.

Regional variation: Deepavali on 28 October

Not everyone celebrates on the same day. Some communities and regions — including parts of South India and the diaspora in Malaysia, Singapore, and South Africa — observe Deepavali on Thursday 28 October 2027, a day earlier than the date given above. This is a real difference in observance, not a mistake on either side: their almanacs reckon the tithi differently, so the day whose evening falls within the Amavasya tithi comes out a day earlier for them.

The difference comes from the way the tithi is reckoned. Because the Amavasya tithi does not start and end at midnight, and because different almanac traditions apply the Pradosh Kaal rule in different ways, two honest readings of the same lunar month can give dates a day apart. The festival is the same, the new moon is the same, and the worship is the same — only the reckoned day differs.

The practical advice is simple: check your local panchang. If you live in a region or community that observes Deepavali on 28 October, follow that date; if you follow the almanacs that give 29 October, follow that one. And if you are wishing friends or family a happy Diwali, remember that their date may be a day earlier than yours — the greetings are welcome on either day.

Planning for Diwali 2027

The five days of Diwali 2027 run from Wednesday 27 October to Sunday 31 October, with the main day on Friday 29 October. Anyone planning around the festival — a family gathering, a trip home, or time off — can work from that span: the festival opens mid-week and closes on the Sunday, with the main celebration on the Friday evening.

Two things are worth checking before you fix any plans. First, the regional difference: if you or the people you are celebrating with follow a tradition that observes Deepavali on Thursday 28 October, plan around that day instead.
