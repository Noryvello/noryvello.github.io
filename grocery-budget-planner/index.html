How the split works

The default split is produce 25%, protein 25%, dairy 15%, pantry 20% and household 15%, which adds up to 100%. Each category amount is its percentage of your weekly budget — for example, with a budget of 120, produce at 25% gives 30.00. The cost per meal is your weekly budget divided by the number of meals you cook each week.

You can change any percentage to match your own household. If your percentages do not add up to 100%, the planner tells you the total and still shows the amounts for the percentages you entered, so you can see the effect before you adjust them.

Making the most of your weekly shop

A budget works best when it follows a plan. Decide the week's meals first, then shop from a list built around those meals — it keeps the basket focused and cuts down on food that goes to waste. Leftovers from one meal can become the next day's lunch, which stretches the same budget further.

Check the split against your real shopping every few weeks. If one category keeps running over, move a few percent from a category that has room — the planner updates the amounts as soon as you change a number.

Questions people ask

How much should I spend on groceries each week?

There is no single right amount — it depends on your household size, where you live, and how often you cook at home. A good starting point is to look at what you actually spent over the last few weeks, then set a weekly figure you can live with and adjust it as you go. This planner splits whatever budget you enter, so start with a number that fits your own household.

What percentage of my budget should go to groceries?

There is no fixed rule, because incomes and household sizes vary so much. Some households spend a small share of their income on food and others a much larger share, and both can be sensible. What matters is that the amount covers the meals your household needs without crowding out your other essentials.

How can I cut my grocery bill?

Plan your meals before you shop, make a list and stick to it, and buy the basics you use every week in larger packs when that saves money. Cooking at home more often, using up what is already in the cupboard, and wasting less food all lower the weekly shop without changing what you eat.

How do I plan meals on a budget?

Start with the meals you already cook well, then build a week of meals around them and shop for the ingredients on one list. Repeating a few reliable meals keeps the list short, and leftovers from one meal can become lunch the next day. This planner then shows how your weekly budget splits across food categories.
