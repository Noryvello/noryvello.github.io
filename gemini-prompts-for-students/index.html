30 Gemini Prompts for Students

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

These 30 ready-made Gemini prompts help students study smarter — from understanding a hard topic to planning a revision week. Each prompt is written to copy, paste, and adapt with your own subject and level. Remember to check Gemini's answers against your course material, because AI can be wrong.

A good prompt is the difference between a useful answer and a waste of time. Students who ask Gemini a vague question get a vague answer; students who ask a specific question, with their own subject and level included, get something they can actually use. The prompts below are written to be specific from the start.

They are organised into five groups that match a student's week: understanding new material, planning and improving written work, preparing for exams, organising research, and keeping study life on track. Each prompt is original, and each one comes with a short note on how to adapt it to your own subject.

These prompts are study aids, not shortcuts — use them to understand your subject, not to pass off AI work as your own. Check your school's rules on AI use, and always verify facts and quotes against your course material before you rely on them.

How to use these prompts

Each prompt below is written to copy, paste, and adapt. The words in the prompt are a starting point: replace the general phrases with your own subject, topic, and level, and the answer will be far more useful. A prompt that names your topic always beats one that does not.

Every prompt comes with a short note on how to adapt it. The note tells you what to add — your notes, your thesis, your deadline — so Gemini has something real to work with. Prompts without your own material in them produce generic answers, and generic answers rarely help with a specific assignment.

Use the prompts in order within a group, or jump to the one you need. The studying prompts help you understand, the writing prompts help you plan and improve, the exam prompts help you practise, the research prompts help you organise, and the planning prompts help you stay on track. Together they cover a full study week.

One habit matters more than any single prompt: check the answer. Gemini can be wrong about facts, dates, and definitions, and a confident answer is not always a correct one. Check important claims against your course material before you rely on them, and treat every AI answer as a draft to verify.

Studying: understand any topic

  1. Explain this topic to me as if I were hearing about it for the first time, using one everyday example and no jargon.
    Adapt it: Swap in any topic from your course.
  2. I have just read this chapter. Ask me five questions about it, one at a time, and tell me whether each answer is right.
    Adapt it: Paste your chapter summary before the first question.
  3. Turn these notes into a set of flashcards, with a question on one side and a short answer on the other.
    Adapt it: Paste your notes after the prompt.
  4. What are the three most common misunderstandings about this topic, and what is the correct idea in each case?
    Adapt it: Name the topic you are studying.
  5. Compare these two ideas side by side, and tell me one example where each one applies.
    Adapt it: Name the two ideas you keep mixing up.
  6. Explain this concept in three levels: a one-sentence version, a short paragraph, and a full explanation with an example.
    Adapt it: Use the level that matches your exam.

Writing: plan and improve your work

  1. Help me brainstorm ten possible angles for an essay on this topic, then pick the three strongest and say why.
    Adapt it: Name your essay topic and subject.
  2. Here is my thesis statement. Tell me what is strong about it and suggest two ways to make it sharper.
    Adapt it: Paste your thesis after the prompt.
  3. Turn these bullet points into an essay outline, with a heading and two supporting points for each section.
    Adapt it: Paste your bullet points after the prompt.
  4. Read this paragraph and tell me where the argument is weakest, without rewriting it for me.
    Adapt it: Paste one paragraph at a time.
  5. Suggest three different ways to open this essay, each in a different style, so I can choose the one that fits.
    Adapt it: Name your essay topic.
  6. Give me five strong linking phrases I can use to connect these two ideas in my essay.
    Adapt it: Name the two ideas you are joining.

Exam prep: practise and revise

  1. Make me a ten-question practice quiz on this topic, with a mix of easy and hard questions, and mark my answers.
    Adapt it: Name the topic and your level.
  2. I have three days before my exam. Build me a revision plan that covers these topics, with breaks included.
    Adapt it: List your topics and exam date.
  3. Ask me questions about this topic until I get three in a row right, then move on to the next topic.
    Adapt it: Name the topic to start with.
  4. Turn this chapter into a one-page revision summary, with the key terms in bold.
    Adapt it: Paste the chapter text or your notes.
  5. Give me a past-paper style question on this topic, then mark my answer and tell me how to improve it.
    Adapt it: Name the topic and exam board if you know it.
  6. What are the most likely exam questions on this topic, and what would a strong answer include for each one?
    Adapt it: Name the topic and your course.

Research: find and organise ideas

  1. Help me turn this broad topic into a focused research question that I can answer in one essay.
    Adapt it: Name your broad topic and word count.
  2. List five different viewpoints on this question, with one key argument for each side.
    Adapt it: Name the question you are researching.
  3. I found this source. Help me judge whether it is reliable, and tell me what to check.
    Adapt it: Paste the source title and author.
  4. Summarise this article in five bullet points, then list two questions it leaves unanswered.
    Adapt it: Paste the article text after the prompt.
  5. Help me organise my research notes into themes, and tell me which theme has the least evidence so far.
    Adapt it: Paste your notes after the prompt.
  6. Suggest five search terms I can use to find more sources on this topic in my library.
    Adapt it: Name your topic and subject area.

Planning: organise your study life

  1. I have four assignments due this month. Help me order them by deadline and difficulty, and build a weekly plan.
    Adapt it: List your assignments and due dates.
  2. Build me a daily study timetable for this week, with my classes, two hours of study, and time to rest.
    Adapt it: List your class times first.
  3. Help me break this big project into ten small tasks, each one I can finish in under an hour.
    Adapt it: Describe your project and its deadline.
  4. I keep putting off this task. Help me find the smallest possible first step, and a plan to start today.
    Adapt it: Name the task you are avoiding.
  5. Review my study plan for this week and tell me where it is unrealistic, then suggest a version I can actually follow.
    Adapt it: Paste your current plan.
  6. Suggest three study goals for this month, each one specific enough that I will know when I have reached it.
    Adapt it: Name your subjects and what matters most.

Questions people ask

Can I use these prompts for any subject?

Yes. Every prompt is written with a placeholder for your topic, subject, or assignment, and the adaptation note tells you what to fill in. Swap in your own subject and the prompt will work the same way.

Should I paste my homework into Gemini?

It is better to ask for explanations and feedback than to paste a whole assignment and ask for the answer. Use Gemini to understand the topic, plan your work, and check your own draft — and follow your school's rules on AI use.

What if Gemini's answer is wrong?

Check it against your course material, and ask Gemini to explain its reasoning or give a source. If the answer still looks wrong, trust your textbook and your teacher over the chatbot.

How do I make a prompt fit my level?

Say your level in the prompt: name your year, your course, and whether you are a beginner or advanced in the topic. Gemini will pitch its answer to match, and you can ask it to simplify or go deeper.

Can Gemini help me write my essay?

Gemini can help you plan an essay, sharpen a thesis, outline arguments, and get feedback on a draft you wrote yourself. It should not write the essay for you, and passing off AI text as your own work breaks most schools' rules.
