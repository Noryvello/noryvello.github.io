7 Mistakes to Avoid When Buying a TV

A TV is a long-term buy, so the mistakes are expensive to live with. This guide covers the 7 mistakes buyers most often make, from size and room light to sound and ports, and ends with a needs checklist to take to the shop.

A television stays in your home for years, usually in the room you use most, so a wrong choice is one you notice every single evening. The mistakes in this guide come from shopping in a bright showroom instead of thinking about your own room: the size that looks modest under shop lights can overwhelm a small living room, and the picture that dazzles on a demo loop can disappoint with everyday viewing. Measure your room, think about what you actually watch, and work through the seven mistakes before you buy. The needs checklist at the end is worth taking to the shop with you.

This guide is general buying advice with no brand recommendations. It does not calculate viewing distance — measure your room and seating distance yourself, and trust your own eyes in the shop.

Mistake 1: Buying a size that overwhelms or underwhelms the room

Size is the first decision and the easiest to get wrong. A screen that is too large for the room dominates the wall and forces you to turn your head to follow the action, while a screen that is too small leaves you squinting from the sofa. Showrooms make every screen look smaller than it will at home, because the walls are large and the lights are bright. Measure the wall space where the TV will go and the distance from your usual seat, and mark the outline of the screen on the wall with tape if it helps. Choose a size that fills your view comfortably without taking over the room.

Mistake 2: Ignoring the room's light

A bright window opposite the screen causes glare that no picture setting can fully fix, and it turns dark scenes into a mirror of your own room. Before you buy, look at where the light falls in your room at the times you usually watch. If the screen will face a window, check how the screen handles reflections, and think about whether curtains or a different position are possible. Some screens cope with bright rooms far better than others, and the difference matters more in daily use than most of the numbers on the box. A screen that suits your light is worth more than a screen that only suits the showroom.

Mistake 3: Chasing the highest resolution without checking what you will watch

The highest resolution only shows its worth when what you watch matches it. Everyday channels, older shows and most streaming at standard quality will not use the top resolution, so a screen bought for its biggest number may look no better than a simpler one for most of your viewing. What matters more is how the screen handles the content you actually watch: how it upscales ordinary pictures, how it shows dark scenes, and how it copes with fast movement. Match the resolution to your sources, and spend the money you save on a better picture overall rather than a bigger number.

Mistake 4: Forgetting the sound

Thin screens leave little room for speakers, and built-in TV sound is often the weakest part of the set. Dialogue can sound thin or muddy, and quiet scenes can vanish entirely in a noisy room. Listen to the TV in the shop at a normal volume, not the loud demo level, and pay attention to speech rather than music. If the sound disappoints, plan for separate speakers or a soundbar from the start, and include that in your thinking about the total cost. Good sound changes how much you enjoy a film far more than a small step up in picture quality.

Mistake 5: Not counting the ports

Every device you plug into the TV needs a connection, and the count adds up quickly: a streaming stick, a games console, a player, a sound system. Count them before you buy, and check the TV has enough ports of the right kind, placed where you can actually reach them. Ports on the back of a wall-mounted TV can be hard to reach, and some ports support different features from others, so check the details rather than assuming they are all the same. A TV with too few ports means swapping cables or buying a switch, which is an annoyance you will feel every time you switch devices.

Mistake 6: Overlooking the smart features

The apps and menus on a TV decide how pleasant it is to use every day, and they vary far more than the picture does. Check that the apps you actually use are available and work well, that the menus respond quickly, and that the remote is comfortable and easy to read. Ask how long the TV will receive software updates, because a smart TV that stops being updated can lose apps over time. If the smart features disappoint, an external streaming device can replace them, but that is an extra cost and an extra remote to live with.

Mistake 7: Skipping the delivery and setup check

A television is large, fragile and awkward to move, and the journey into your home is where damage happens. Before delivery day, measure every doorway, hallway and stair turn the box must pass through, and clear the route. On the day, check the screen for damage before the delivery team leaves, and confirm that all parts, the stand and the remote are included. Find out in advance who sets the TV up, whether wall mounting is included, and who takes the packaging away. A few minutes of checking on delivery day saves a difficult argument later.

Before you buy

Once the checklist is done, shortlist two or three sets and compare them in the shop at your usual seating distance, with ordinary viewing rather than the demo loop. Ask to see a dark scene and a dialogue scene, because those show a TV's weaknesses faster than bright action. Check the price includes everything you need, including any stand, mount or extra cable, and confirm the delivery and return terms in writing. The right TV is the one that suits your room on an ordinary evening, not the one that shines brightest under showroom lights.

Questions people ask

What size TV should I buy?

It depends on your room and how far you sit from the screen. Measure the wall space and the seating distance first, then choose a size that fills your view without forcing you to turn your head.

Does room light really matter?

Yes. A bright window opposite the screen causes glare that no setting fully fixes. If your room is bright, check how the screen handles reflections before you buy.

Do I need the highest resolution?

Only if your viewing matches it. Everyday channels and older content will not use the top resolution, so match the screen to what you actually watch.

Why does TV sound matter?

Thin screens leave little room for speakers, so built-in sound is often weak. Listen in the shop, and plan for separate speakers if dialogue matters to you.

What should I check on delivery day?

Check the screen for damage before the delivery team leaves, confirm all parts and the remote are included, and keep the receipt and warranty details together.
