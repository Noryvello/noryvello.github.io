Solar Eclipse 2027 Guide

A total solar eclipse crosses southern Spain, Morocco, Libya, Egypt and Saudi Arabia on Monday 2 August 2027. This guide explains the path, the best places to watch, what totality means, and how to view it safely.

The path of totality on 2 August 2027

  • Southern Spain
  • Morocco
  • Libya
  • Egypt
  • Saudi Arabia

Only places inside the path see the Sun fully covered.

Never look at the Sun without proper eclipse glasses — ordinary sunglasses are not safe. This guide gives only the published path and the Luxor duration; exact local timings vary along the path, so check a local eclipse listing for your chosen spot.

Where the eclipse will be visible

The path of totality runs through southern Spain, Morocco, Libya, Egypt and Saudi Arabia. People inside the path will see the Sun fully covered for a few minutes, while people outside it will see only a partial eclipse.

Best places to watch

The best place to watch is anywhere inside the path of totality, where the Sun is fully covered. Southern Spain, Morocco, Libya, Egypt and Saudi Arabia all lie on the path. Pick a spot with a clear view of the sky and check the weather forecast closer to the day.

What totality means

Totality is the part of a total solar eclipse when the Moon fully covers the Sun. It lasts for a few minutes, during which the sky darkens as if it were dusk. Totality is the moment eclipse watchers travel for, because it is the only time the Sun is fully covered.

The Luxor record

In Luxor, in Egypt, totality lasts 6 minutes 23 seconds — the longest totality on accessible land until 2114. That long stretch of darkness is what makes Luxor stand out on the 2027 path.

How to watch safely

  • Never look at the Sun without proper eclipse glasses, even during a partial eclipse.
  • Ordinary sunglasses are not safe for watching an eclipse.
  • Supervise children at all times.
  • If you do not have proper eclipse glasses, do not look directly at the Sun.
