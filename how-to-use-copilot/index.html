How to Use Microsoft Copilot: A Beginner's Guide

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

Microsoft Copilot is an AI assistant built into Microsoft products: you can find it on the web, in Windows, in Edge, and on your phone. You ask questions in plain words, and it can answer, draft text, summarize pages, and generate images. This guide shows you where Copilot appears and how to get useful answers from it.

Copilot is Microsoft's AI assistant, and unlike a chatbot that lives on one website, it shows up in many places: in your browser, on your Windows desktop, and on your phone. Wherever you meet it, the idea is the same: type or speak a request in plain words, and it will answer, draft, summarize, or create something for you.

If you already use Microsoft products, Copilot is probably closer than you think. This guide explains where to find it, how to write your first prompts, what it is good at, and the limits worth knowing before you lean on it.

Copilot's placement, features, and availability change often and vary by device, region, and plan. The steps here are described in general terms, so expect menus and buttons to look different on your screen. Check important facts before you rely on them, and never share sensitive details in a chat.

Where Copilot appears

Copilot appears in several places, and the experience differs slightly in each. On the web, you can open it in your browser as a chat page. In Windows, it is built into the system, often reachable from the taskbar or a keyboard shortcut. In Edge, it sits in a sidebar next to the page you are reading. On your phone, it is available as an app.

You do not need to use all of these. Pick the one that fits your habit: the browser version for research, the Windows version for quick questions while you work, or the mobile app for questions on the go. Your chats may or may not carry across devices, depending on your account and settings.

Your first conversation

Start with something simple, such as explain what a budget is in plain words or give me five ideas for a weekend activity with kids. Copilot will answer in the chat window, and you can reply to refine the answer. Short follow-ups like make it shorter or give me more detail on the second idea work well.

Notice how Copilot handles a vague question: it may answer with several angles at once, or ask you to narrow it down. Either way, the first answer is a starting point. The conversation improves when you tell it what you liked and what you want changed.

Writing prompts that get better answers

The same rules that help any chatbot help Copilot: be specific, give context, and say what format you want. Draft a polite email to a client explaining a one-week delay will do better than write an email, because it tells Copilot the purpose, the tone, and the situation.

If you are working on a document or a page, mention it in your prompt. Copilot in Edge can see the page you have open and summarize or explain it, which is handy for long articles. Ask it to pull out the main points, explain a tricky paragraph, or compare the page with what you already know.

Summarizing pages and documents

One of Copilot's most useful jobs is summarizing. In Edge, you can ask it to summarize the page you are reading, which saves time on long articles and reports. In Windows or on the web, you can paste text and ask for a summary, a list of key points, or an explanation in simpler words.

Always read the summary with a sceptical eye. Copilot can leave out a caveat, flatten a nuanced argument, or misread a figure. For anything important, open the original and check the parts the summary highlights, especially numbers and dates.

Generating images in general terms

Copilot can generate images from a written description. Describe the subject, the setting, the style, and the mood, and it will produce an image you can use for a presentation, a social post, or a personal project. A detailed description gives a better result than a single word.

Image generation has honest limits: text inside images often comes out garbled, hands and faces can look wrong, and fine details may not match your description. Treat the first image as a draft, refine the description, and generate again until the result is close to what you need.

Everyday uses: work, study, and home

For work, Copilot can draft emails, outline a presentation, summarize meeting notes, and brainstorm ideas for a project. For study, it can explain a concept, quiz you on a topic, and help you plan an essay. For home, it can suggest recipes from the ingredients you have, plan a weekend, or help you write a message.

The common thread is that Copilot is best at first drafts and quick answers. It saves you the blank page, and then you shape the result with your own judgement. The more you treat it as a drafting partner, the more useful it becomes.

Limits worth knowing

Copilot can be confidently wrong, and it can mix up facts from different sources. It does not know your files or your inbox unless you share them, and its knowledge has limits on very recent events. Features also vary by plan: some abilities are only available with certain subscriptions or in certain regions.

Keep your expectations realistic. Use Copilot for drafting, summarizing, and brainstorming, and verify anything that matters. That balance gives you the speed of AI without the risk of trusting it blindly.

Copilot on your phone

The mobile version of Copilot is the same assistant in a smaller window, and it is useful for questions that come up away from your desk. You can ask a question by typing or by speaking, and you can take a photo and ask about what is in it, such as a sign in another language or a plant you cannot name.

Keep phone sessions short. A quick question, a summary, or a draft message is a good fit for a small screen, while long documents and detailed analysis are easier on a computer. If a phone answer needs more work, pick the conversation up later on the web or on your desktop.

Questions people ask

Do I need a Microsoft account to use Copilot?

You can usually try Copilot without signing in, but an account unlocks more features, longer conversations, and access across devices. The exact options depend on your region and the version you use.

Is Copilot free?

Copilot has a free tier that covers everyday chatting, summarizing, and image generation, with limits. Paid plans add higher limits and deeper integration with Microsoft apps, and the details change over time.

Can Copilot see my files?

Copilot cannot see your files unless you share them or use a version connected to your documents, such as Copilot in Microsoft 365 apps. The standalone chat only knows what you type or paste into it.

How is Copilot different from ChatGPT?

Copilot is built into Microsoft products, so it can work alongside your browser, your desktop, and your documents. ChatGPT is a standalone chatbot with its own app and website. Both answer questions in plain language.

Can I turn Copilot off?

Yes. On Windows you can usually hide or disable the Copilot entry point in settings, and in Edge you can hide the sidebar button. The exact steps vary by version, so check the settings on your own device.
