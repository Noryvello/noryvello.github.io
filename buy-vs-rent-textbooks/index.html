Buy or Rent Textbooks: Which Costs Less This Semester?

Short answer: enter book prices, book count, expected resale value and rental fees to compare estimated semester totals.

How to compare the semester cost

The buy estimate subtracts your expected resale value from the purchase cost. The rent estimate adds the rental price and required fees. Multiply each per-book amount by the number of books. This assumes the books are returned or resold in the condition and timeframe you expect.

Check before you decide

  • Confirm the exact title, author, edition and ISBN required by the course.
  • Check whether digital access codes, workbooks or online homework are included.
  • Read rental return deadlines, condition rules and late fees.
  • Use a conservative resale estimate; you may not find a buyer.
Important limit: Resale value and edition changes affect the buy side.

Questions people ask

Is renting textbooks always cheaper?

No. Rental costs and fees may be lower for one semester, but buying and reselling can cost less in some cases. Compare the actual prices and expected resale value.

How should I estimate textbook resale value?

Check recent local listings for the same title and edition, then use a cautious estimate. A resale value is not guaranteed.

What if the professor changes textbook editions?

Confirm the required edition before buying or renting. A new edition can affect whether an older copy is accepted and whether it can be resold.

Are access codes included in textbook rentals?

Not always. Check the listing and course requirements carefully because digital access, workbooks and online codes may be separate.
