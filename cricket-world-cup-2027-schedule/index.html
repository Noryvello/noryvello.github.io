Cricket World Cup 2027 Schedule

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

The Cricket World Cup 2027 runs from 2 October to 21 November 2027 across South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia, with 14 teams playing 57 matches. This guide explains the schedule stage by stage, with the confirmed key dates: the semi-finals on 17 and 18 November and the final on 21 November.

The 2027 Cricket World Cup is the biggest the game has staged in Africa since 2003, and the first to be hosted by three nations: South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia. The theme is Three Nations, One Heartbeat, and Namibia hosts World Cup matches for the first time. Fourteen teams will play 57 matches of 50-over cricket across 12 venues over seven weeks.

This guide explains the schedule as the ICC has confirmed it: the three stages, the two groups, the venues, and the key dates. It does not list every match by date, because the honest way to follow the day-by-day fixture list is through the ICC's official schedule, which is the authoritative source.

The tournament at a glance

The tournament opens on Saturday 2 October 2027 and ends with the final on Sunday 21 November 2027. Fourteen teams take part, playing 50-over one-day international cricket. There are 57 matches in total, played at 12 venues: eight in South Africa, three in Zimbabwe and one in Namibia. The format has three rounds before the semi-finals, and it is a new shape for a World Cup, so it is worth understanding before the tournament begins.

One warning for readers of older guides: the format described in some older pages, with Super 6s and 54 matches, is outdated. The 2027 tournament uses the Super 7 format described below, with 57 matches. If a page you read mentions Super 6s, it is describing an old plan, not the tournament that will actually be played.

Stage one: the Super Series

The tournament begins with the Super Series, a short opening round played in Namibia. The three lowest-ranked qualifiers play each other once in a round robin, which produces three matches, and only the winner advances to the group stage. The other two teams go home. It is a short, sharp stage: three teams, three matches, one survivor.

The Super Series matters because it decides the last place in the group stage and sets the tone for the tournament. The team that comes through it arrives in the group stage battle-hardened, having already played competitive cricket, while the other teams are playing their first match. Namibia hosts this stage, which makes it a fitting start for a country hosting World Cup matches for the first time.

Stage two: the group stage

Twelve teams take part in the group stage, divided into two groups of six. Each group plays a round robin, with every team playing the other five teams in its group once. A six-team round robin produces 15 matches, so the two groups together produce 30 matches. The groups are confirmed as follows.

Group A is India, Australia, Pakistan, Afghanistan, Zimbabwe and Qualifier A. Group B is New Zealand, South Africa, Sri Lanka, England, Bangladesh and Qualifier B. The two qualifiers are the teams that come through the qualification process, including the winner of the Super Series. The top three teams in each group advance to the Super 7, along with the best fourth-placed team across the two groups.

Stage three: the Super 7

The Super 7 is the heart of the new format. Seven teams, the top three from each group plus the best fourth-placed team, play a single round robin, with every team playing the other six once. That produces 21 matches, and the top four teams at the end of the Super 7 reach the semi-finals.

The Super 7 changes how the tournament feels. In older formats, the group stage could feel like a long warm-up before the knockouts. Here, the Super 7 is a second group stage where every match matters, because only four of the seven teams survive. A team that scrapes through its group as the best fourth-placed side still has a genuine route to the final, and a team that topped its group can still be caught. Net run rate, which decides teams level on points, becomes important, and the points table is worth following from the first match.

The semi-finals and final

The semi-finals are the two biggest dates before the final. The first semi-final is in Cape Town on 17 November 2027, and the second is in Centurion on 18 November 2027. The winners of the two semi-finals meet in the final on Sunday 21 November 2027. The final is the 57th and last match of the tournament.

Seven weeks is a long tournament, and by the final weekend the two finalists will have played a demanding schedule of group and Super 7 matches. Squad depth, injury management and the ability to peak at the right time matter as much as raw talent. The team that lifts the trophy is usually the one that managed its players best across the whole tournament, not just the one with the brightest stars.

The 12 venues

South Africa provides eight venues: Johannesburg (Wanderers Stadium), Tshwane and Centurion (SuperSport Park), Cape Town (Newlands), Durban (Kingsmead), Gqeberha (St George's Park), Bloemfontein (Mangaung Oval), Paarl (Boland Park), and KuGompo City, formerly East London (Buffalo Park). Zimbabwe provides three: Harare (Harare Sports Club), Bulawayo (Queens Sports Club), and Victoria Falls (Mosi-oa-Tunya International Cricket Stadium). Namibia provides one: Windhoek (Namibia Cricket Ground).

The venues span a wide range of conditions, from the high bounce of Johannesburg to the coastal grounds of Durban and Cape Town, and the new stadium at Victoria Falls, close to one of the great natural sights of the world. For travelling fans, the spread of venues is part of the appeal: a World Cup that takes in three countries and some of the most famous grounds in the game.

How the 57 matches add up

The match total is easy to check by adding the stages together. The Super Series has 3 matches. The group stage has 30 matches, 15 in each group of six. The Super 7 has 21 matches. The semi-finals add 2 matches, and the final adds 1. Three plus 30 plus 21 plus 2 plus 1 is 57, which is the confirmed total for the tournament.

That arithmetic is a useful check when you read other guides. Any schedule that adds up to 54 matches is describing the outdated Super 6s format, not the 2027 tournament. The confirmed format is the Super 7 with 57 matches, and the confirmed dates are 2 October to 21 November 2027, with semi-finals on 17 and 18 November and the final on 21 November.
