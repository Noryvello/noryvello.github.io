Ebook or Paperback: Which Costs Less for a Year of Reading?

Short answer: enter how many books you read, your average prices and any device cost to compare estimated yearly totals.

How the yearly comparison works

The ebook estimate multiplies the number of books by the average ebook cost, then adds one year of the device cost. The device amount is spread evenly across the number of years you enter. The paperback estimate multiplies the same book count by the average paperback cost. Enter your own local currency values; the tool does not assume a currency or current market price.

Make the comparison fair

  • Compare the same titles or equivalent editions where possible.
  • Include an e-reader, tablet or phone purchase only if it is needed mainly for reading.
  • Account for library borrowing, subscriptions, sales and books you reread.
  • Do not count a device you already own as a new purchase unless you want to allocate part of its cost.
Important limit: Device cost belongs in the ebook side for a fair comparison.

Questions people ask

Does an ebook always cost less than a paperback?

No. Individual ebook and paperback prices vary by title, edition, seller and promotion. Enter the prices you can actually access.

Should I include the cost of my phone or tablet?

Include a device cost when buying or keeping a device is part of your reading decision. If you already own it for other uses, choose a reasonable allocation rather than automatically charging its full cost to reading.

What if I read more books next year?

Change the books-per-year input. The yearly comparison scales both formats with the same reading volume.

Does this calculator include library books or subscriptions?

No. It compares per-book ebook and paperback costs. Add subscription or borrowing costs separately if they apply to your reading habits.
