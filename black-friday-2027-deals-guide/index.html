Black Friday 2027 Deals Guide

Black Friday 2027 is Friday 26 November 2027, with Cyber Monday following on Monday 29 November. Not every deal on the day is a genuine bargain, so this guide explains what the research found about crossed-out prices and sets out five checks to run before you buy.

Black Friday falls on the day after US Thanksgiving, and it has become one of the biggest shopping events of the year, with sale prices advertised for days around the day itself. The offers look generous: a crossed-out "was" price, a lower sale price, and a saving worked out for you. The problem is that the crossed-out price is not always what it seems. Research that tracked real prices over a full year found that many Black Friday deals were not the cheapest price of the year at all — and some items were the same price or cheaper at other times. This guide sets out what that research found, and the five checks that tell a real bargain from a fake discount.

This guide is general shopping advice, not financial advice. It names no shops and recommends no products — the checks here work for any retailer. Prices and offers change constantly, so always check the price on the day you buy.

When is Black Friday 2027?

Black Friday 2027 is Friday 26 November 2027, the day after US Thanksgiving. Cyber Monday, the online-focused sale day that follows the weekend, is Monday 29 November 2027. Many retailers now run their Black Friday promotions for longer than a single day, so the sale period can stretch across the week before and the days after the Friday itself. That longer window is one more reason to check prices rather than trust the label: a "Black Friday deal" may be on sale for weeks, at a price that has gone up and down more than once.

What the research found

The clearest evidence about Black Friday pricing comes from the UK consumer group Which?, which tracked real prices over a full year. In its study around Black Friday 2024, Which? followed 175 products at 8 retailers for a full year and found that 83% were the same price or cheaper at least once outside the four-week sale period. Just as striking, none of the deals was at its cheapest price of the year on Black Friday itself. In other words, for every one of those 175 products, a shopper who had bought at another point in the year would have paid the same or less.

The pattern was not a one-off. Which?'s Black Friday 2023 study, which looked at 227 deals at the same 8 retailers, found that 92% were the same price or cheaper at other times of the year. Two years running, the same picture emerged: most of the deals on offer were not the lowest price the item had sold for.

It is important to say plainly what these figures are and what they are not. The Which? studies are UK studies, based on products sold by UK retailers. They are a warning about how "was" prices work, rather than a local statistic for every country. The exact percentages will not match every market, but the mechanism they expose — a crossed-out price that makes an ordinary price look like a bargain — is the same wherever sale prices are advertised. Treat the numbers as a reason to check, not as a prediction about any single deal in your own country.

There is also a legal standard behind the idea of a fair "was" price. The US Federal Trade Commission's Guides Against Deceptive Pricing (16 CFR 233.1) say that a former price counts only if the item was openly and actively offered at that price for a reasonably substantial period of time — otherwise the advertised bargain is a false one. In plain terms: a crossed-out price is only honest if the item really sold at that price, in the open, for a meaningful stretch of time. A price that was briefly listed and never really charged does not make a discount real.

The five checks

Run these five checks on any Black Friday deal before you buy. They take a few minutes each, and together they are the difference between a genuine bargain and a price that only looks like one.

  1. Ignore the crossed-out "was" price. Judge the deal by what the item actually sold for in the last 30–90 days. The "was" price is the retailer's claim; the recent selling price is the fact. If the sale price is close to what the item has been selling for all along, the discount is mostly decoration.
  2. Look back six months or more where possible. A price dip that only appears after a September or October price rise is not a bargain — it is a return to the normal price, dressed up as a sale. A longer view shows whether the discount is real or whether the price was raised first so it could be cut again for Black Friday.
  3. Use a price-history tool. For Amazon listings, CamelCamelCamel or Keepa show the price history of a product over time. Elsewhere, Google Shopping price insights can show how a price has moved. These tools do the looking-back for you, and they are the fastest way to see whether a deal is genuinely the lowest price of the year.
  4. Match the exact model number. Similar models carry different prices, and retailers sometimes sell a slightly different version of a product for the sale. Before you compare prices, check that the model number is exactly the same as the one you researched — a missing letter or a different suffix can mean a different product at a different price.
  5. Watch for fake stores and scam sites. Fake shops peak at sale time, when shoppers are in a hurry and deals look too good to question. Buy from retailers you know, and be wary of a store you have never heard of offering a famous product far below the price everywhere else. If a deal looks impossible, the safest choice is to walk away.

What to do if you miss the day

If Black Friday passes and you did not buy, the research suggests there is usually no need to regret it. The Which? studies found that most of the deals they tracked were the same price or cheaper at other times of the year, so the price you missed is often available again later. Cyber Monday, on Monday 29 November 2027, is the next sale day, and prices on many items return to similar levels in later sales. The better habit is the one this guide recommends all year round: decide what you want, note its usual price, and buy when the price genuinely drops below it — whether or not that happens to fall on Black Friday.

Questions people ask

Are Black Friday deals real?

Some are, but research shows many are not the cheapest price of the year. The UK consumer group Which? tracked 175 products around Black Friday 2024 and found 83 percent were the same price or cheaper at least once outside the four-week sale period.

How do I check if a Black Friday deal is genuine?

Ignore the crossed-out was price and check what the item actually sold for in the last 30 to 90 days, look back six months or more where you can, use a price-history tool, and match the exact model number before you buy.

What is a fake discount?

A fake discount is a deal where the crossed-out was price was never the real selling price for a reasonable period. The US Federal Trade Commission's Guides Against Deceptive Pricing say a former price counts only if the item was openly and actively offered at that price for a reasonably substantial period of time.

When is Black Friday 2027?

Black Friday 2027 is Friday 26 November 2027, the day after US Thanksgiving. Cyber Monday follows on Monday 29 November 2027.

Should I wait until after Black Friday to buy?

The Which? studies found that most tracked deals were the same price or cheaper at other times of the year, so waiting often costs nothing. But there is no guarantee for any single item, so check its price history before deciding.
