How Much Coffee Should You Use for Each Brew Method?

Short answer: choose a brew method, enter your water amount, and get an estimated coffee dose using a practical starting ratio.

Common starting ratios

  • Pour-over: 1:15 to 1:17
  • Drip: 1:15 to 1:17
  • French press: 1:14 to 1:16
  • AeroPress: recipes vary; 1:10 to 1:15 is a useful range
  • Espresso: often around 1:2 by beverage weight
  • Cold brew concentrate: around 1:8 to 1:10 before dilution

Grams and cup estimates

For water, 1 ml is approximately 1 gram. A common kitchen cup is about 240 ml, but cup sizes differ. For a 300 ml pour-over at 1:15, use 20 g coffee and about 1.25 cups of water. Ground-coffee cup measures are especially approximate because grind size and packing change the weight.

Important limit: Taste is personal; use the ratio as a starting point and adjust. Bean freshness, grind size, roast, water and brewing time all affect the cup.

Questions people ask

How much coffee do I need for 300 ml of water?

At a 1:15 ratio, use 20 g of coffee for 300 ml of water. This is a starting estimate, not a universal rule.

How many cups of water are in 500 ml?

Using a 240 ml cup as an estimate, 500 ml is about 2.1 cups. Actual cup sizes vary.

Is a 1:2 coffee ratio for espresso?

A 1:2 espresso ratio means about 2 g of beverage for every 1 g of dry coffee, such as 36 g out from 18 g in.

Can I use the same ratio for every coffee bean?

No. The ratio is a starting point. Change it to suit the beans, roast, grind, equipment and taste.
