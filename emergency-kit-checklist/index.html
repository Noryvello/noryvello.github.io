What an emergency kit is for

An emergency kit is a small store of the things you would need if you had to manage without power, water, or shops for a day or two. It is not a survival bunker and it does not need to be expensive. Most of what you need is already in your home — the kit is about gathering it in one place, checking it works, and knowing where it is.

Keep the kit somewhere you can reach it in the dark, in a bag or box you could carry if you had to leave the house. Tell everyone in the household where it is. A kit that only one person knows about is only half a kit.

Build it category by category

Start with water and food, because those are the things you cannot improvise. Sealed bottles of drinking water, food that needs no cooking, and a manual tin opener cover the basics. Then add light and power: torches, spare batteries, a charged power bank, and a battery-powered radio. Then documents: copies of your ID and insurance papers in a waterproof folder, and emergency numbers written on paper.

First aid basics come last in the list but matter just as much. A stocked first aid kit, a few days of any regular medicines, and a simple first aid guide cover most situations. Check the expiry dates on medicines and first aid supplies when you build the kit, and again at every review.

Review the kit twice a year

A kit that is never checked slowly stops being a kit. Batteries run flat, food goes out of date, and medicines expire. A good routine is to review the kit twice a year — pick two dates you will remember, such as the start of summer and the start of winter. The review reminder above works out your next check date from the date you last checked.

At each review, open the kit and check everything. Replace food and water that is out of date, test the torches, charge the power bank, and check that the documents are still current. If anyone in the household has new medication or a new allergy, update the medical note. A ten-minute review twice a year keeps the kit ready when you need it.

Questions people ask

What should be in an emergency kit?

Drinking water, food that needs no cooking, torches and spare batteries, a phone charger, copies of important documents, and first aid basics. The checklist above covers every category, so you can build your kit step by step.

How often should I check my emergency kit?

Twice a year is a good routine. At each review, check expiry dates on food, water, batteries, and medicines, test the torches, and replace anything used or out of date. The review reminder above works out your next check date.

Where should I keep my emergency kit?

Somewhere you can reach it in the dark, in a bag or box you could carry if you had to leave the house. Tell everyone in the household where it is — a kit that only one person knows about is only half a kit.

How much water should I store?

Store enough sealed drinking water for each person for a couple of days, and do not forget pets. Keep clean containers you can fill quickly if you get warning of a disruption to the water supply.

Do I need a battery-powered radio?

It is worth keeping one. If the power and the phone network go down, a battery-powered radio is often the only way to hear official advice. Keep spare batteries for it in the kit.
