What a mix ratio means

A mix ratio such as 1:2:3 describes the proportions of cement, sand and gravel in a batch of concrete, measured by volume. The first number is cement, the second is sand and the third is gravel. A richer mix with more cement is generally stronger, while a leaner mix suits bulk filling and footings where strength matters less. For structural work, the right ratio is a decision for a professional, not a calculator.

Working out how much concrete a slab needs

Start with the volume of the slab: length × width × thickness, with the thickness converted into the same unit first. A slab 10 feet long, 10 feet wide and 4 inches thick holds about 33 cubic feet of wet concrete. The calculator does this arithmetic and converts the result into cubic yards, the unit ready-mix is usually ordered in. For an L-shaped slab, split it into rectangles and add the volumes.

Ordering and waste allowance

Concrete is almost always ordered with a little extra. The ground underneath is rarely perfectly level, formwork can bow outward under the weight, and some concrete is lost to spills. The calculator already adds 10% for waste, a sensible allowance for a straightforward slab. Running short halfway through a pour means a second delivery, which costs more than the extra material would have. If the job needs a base layer, work that out separately with the gravel and mulch calculator.

A worked example

Example: a garden shed base 8 feet long, 6 feet wide and 4 inches thick, using a 1:2:3 mix. The volume is 8 × 6 × (4 ÷ 12) = 16 cubic feet, or about 0.6 cubic yards before waste. Adding the 10% allowance brings it to roughly 0.65 cubic yards. The calculator then splits the dry volume between cement, sand and gravel in the ratio you chose. These are example numbers for a simple slab — check your own measurements first.

Curing basics

Concrete does not dry — it cures. The cement reacts with water over days and weeks, and the slab only reaches its strength if it stays damp while that happens. In hot or windy weather, cover it with damp sheeting and keep it moist for the first few days. Do not walk on it or load it too soon.

Questions worth asking before you pour

What if my ground slopes?

A slab needs a level base. On sloping ground, dig the high side down or build the formwork up on the low side, and check with a long straight edge before you pour. Concrete also fixes fence posts in place, so the fence panel calculator is a natural next step if your slab borders a new fence — and if a slab will be tiled once cured, the tile grout calculator covers the grout for that finish.

Questions people ask

How is concrete volume calculated for a slab?

Multiply slab length, width, and depth to get total cubic volume.

Why should I add 5-10% extra for concrete?

Extra concrete accounts for sub-grade unevenness, spillages, and formwork deflection.

What is a standard concrete mix ratio?

A standard nominal mix is 1:2:3 (1 part cement, 2 parts sand, 3 parts gravel by volume).

When should I hire a professional for concrete work?

Structural work, load-bearing foundations, and large pours require professional engineering and placement.
