How to write a Diwali greeting

A good Diwali greeting mentions light, warmth and the year ahead. Wish the person health and happiness, and add a line about the festival itself: the diyas, the sweets or the time spent with family.

Keep it warm and respectful. Diwali is a religious festival for many people, so avoid jokes about the festival and focus on light, gratitude and good wishes.

When to send Diwali wishes

Send your wishes on the day of Diwali or in the days around it. A message on the morning of the festival is a lovely way to start the celebrations.

If you are sending a card, post it a few days early so it arrives in time. A message sent after the festival is still welcome, especially if it looks forward to the year ahead.

Questions people ask

What is a good Diwali wish for family?

A good family wish mentions home, light and togetherness. Wish them health and happiness for the year ahead, and add a line about the festival you share.

Can I send Diwali wishes to someone who does not celebrate?

Yes, if you know them well. A simple 'Happy Diwali' with warm wishes is kind. If you are unsure, a general greeting about the season is a safe choice.

Should Diwali wishes mention light?

Light is at the heart of Diwali, so it is natural to mention it. You can also wish for joy, health, prosperity and new beginnings.

Is it okay to send Diwali wishes by text?

Yes. A text or message is fine for friends and colleagues, and a card suits family. Send it on the day or in the days around the festival.

What should I avoid in a Diwali message?

Avoid jokes about the festival or about fireworks. Keep the message warm, respectful and focused on light and good wishes.
