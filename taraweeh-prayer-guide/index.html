Taraweeh Prayer Guide

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

Taraweeh is the special nightly prayer of Ramadan, prayed after the Isha prayer in the evening. Some traditions pray 20 rakats and others pray 8, and both are widely practised. This guide explains what Taraweeh is, how it works, and how to join in at the mosque or pray at home.

For many Muslims, Taraweeh is the sound of Ramadan: the evening prayer that fills mosques after iftar, night after night, for the whole month. It is a prayer of long standing, prayed in congregation, in which the Quran is recited at length. For newcomers it can seem mysterious — how long does it last, how many rakats are there, and what if you cannot keep up? This guide answers those questions plainly.

Taraweeh is prayed differently in different traditions, and this page describes those differences neutrally. The aim is to help you understand what happens and how to take part, whether at the mosque or at home, whatever your local practice may be.

What Taraweeh is

Taraweeh is the extra nightly prayer of Ramadan, prayed after the Isha prayer. The word comes from an Arabic root meaning rest or relaxation, because the prayer is traditionally broken into sets with short pauses between them. It is a Sunnah prayer, which means it is strongly encouraged and richly rewarded, but not obligatory like the five daily prayers.

In most mosques, Taraweeh is prayed in congregation, with an imam leading and the congregation following. The imam recites from the Quran, often working through a portion each night, and many mosques complete a recitation of the whole Quran over the course of the month. The atmosphere is calm and focused, and the long recitation is part of what makes Ramadan nights special.

Taraweeh is prayed in units of two rakats at a time. After each set of two, the worshipper rests briefly, and after every four rakats there is usually a slightly longer pause. The prayer ends with Witr, the odd-numbered night prayer, which is prayed after Taraweeh in Ramadan.

How many rakats: 20 and 8

The best-known difference in Taraweeh practice is the number of rakats. In many traditions, Taraweeh is prayed as 20 rakats, a practice with a long history going back to the early generations of Muslims. In other traditions, Taraweeh is prayed as 8 rakats, also with a long history and wide following. Both are practised in mosques around the world, and both are respected.

If you attend a mosque, you will simply follow whatever that mosque prays. There is no need to worry about which number is right: the congregation follows the imam, and the prayer is complete either way. If you pray at home, you may pray the number your family or tradition follows, or as many as you are able to pray with focus and calm.

What matters more than the number is the quality of the prayer. A smaller number of rakats prayed slowly and attentively is better than a larger number rushed through while tired. If you are new to Taraweeh, it is perfectly fine to pray part of the congregation's prayer and sit out the rest, or to pray a few rakats at home until you build up stamina through the month.

Taraweeh at the mosque

Going to the mosque for Taraweeh is a Ramadan experience in itself. Arrive a little early if you can, especially on the first nights and in the last ten nights, when mosques are busiest. Find a place, pray Isha with the congregation, and then stay for Taraweeh. The imam will lead, and you follow along as you would in any congregational prayer.

If you are new, a few practical points help. Wear comfortable clothes and bring nothing you do not need. Keep your phone silent and out of sight. If you need to leave early, do so quietly between sets of rakats rather than in the middle of one. And if you lose your place in the recitation, do not worry: simply follow the imam's movements.

Mosques often serve tea or light refreshments after Taraweeh, and the walk home with family or friends is part of the pleasure of the month. If you are attending for the first time, going with someone who knows the mosque will make the evening much easier.

Praying Taraweeh at home

Taraweeh can be prayed at home, alone or with family, and many people do so — because of distance, health, childcare, or simply preference. Praying at home follows the same shape: after Isha, pray in sets of two rakats, resting briefly between sets, and finish with Witr. You may pray as many rakats as you wish.

At home you choose your own recitation. Read the chapters you know, even if they are short ones, and take your time. Some people read from a copy of the Quran while praying, following the practice of their tradition. Others listen to a recitation and pray along. The aim is a calm, attentive prayer, not a performance.

Praying with family at home has its own warmth. One person can lead, with the rest following, and children can join for a few rakats before bed. Set a regular time, keep the space tidy and quiet, and treat the home prayer with the same care you would give the mosque.

Following the Quran through the month

One of the beauties of Taraweeh is the recitation. In many mosques the imam recites roughly one thirtieth of the Quran each night, so that the whole Quran is completed by the end of the month. Hearing the Quran in order, night after night, gives a sense of the whole book that reading alone rarely provides.

If your mosque follows this pattern, you may like to follow along with a copy of the Quran or a translation during the day, reading the portion that will be recited that night. Even a few verses read with understanding can change the evening prayer, because the words become familiar and their meaning comes alive.

If you pray at home, you can set your own reading plan: a few pages each night, or a chapter you love, read slowly. There is no requirement to finish the whole Quran, and no penalty for reading less. The point is to spend time with the Quran during its month, in whatever measure suits you.

Taraweeh with children and elders

Children are welcome at Taraweeh in most mosques, and many families bring them for part of the prayer. Young children will not manage the whole night, and nobody expects them to. Let them pray a few rakats, sit quietly for a while, and leave when they are tired. The aim is for them to love the mosque and the month, not to endure it.

Elders, and anyone with health difficulties, should pray in whatever way is comfortable. Sitting for part or all of the prayer is accepted when standing is difficult, and praying a smaller number of rakats is entirely fine. Mosques usually provide chairs, and there is no shame in using one. The prayer is for everyone, at every stage of life.

If you are bringing children or accompanying an elder, choose a spot near the edge of the congregation so you can step out quietly if needed. A short, happy visit is better than a long, difficult one, and the door is always open the next night.

The last ten nights

The last ten nights of Ramadan hold a special place in the month. Muslims believe that Laylat al-Qadr, the Night of Power, falls within these nights, and many fasters give extra time to prayer, recitation and supplication then. Mosques stay open later, and the night prayers grow longer and more heartfelt.

Many people try to pray Taraweeh every night in the last ten, and some stay for extra prayers into the night. If you can manage it, even one or two of these nights can be a memorable part of the month. If you cannot, do what you can: a few extra rakats, some quiet reading, and a sincere supplication are all of value.

As the month draws to its close, Taraweeh ends with the last night of Ramadan. The final night often carries a particular sweetness, and many mosques mark the completion of the Quran's recitation with a special supplication. It is a fitting end to a month of nightly prayer.

Questions people ask

How many rakats is Taraweeh?

It depends on tradition. Many mosques pray 20 rakats, and others pray 8. Both are widely practised and respected. If you attend a mosque, simply follow the imam.

Can I pray Taraweeh at home?

Yes. Pray after Isha in sets of two rakats, resting briefly between sets, and finish with Witr. You may pray as many rakats as you wish, and read the chapters you know.

Is Taraweeh obligatory?

No. Taraweeh is a Sunnah prayer, which means it is strongly encouraged but not obligatory. The five daily prayers are obligatory; Taraweeh is an extra prayer of Ramadan.

Do I have to finish the whole Quran in Taraweeh?

No. Many mosques complete a recitation of the whole Quran across the month, but this is not required of every person. Read what you can, at your own pace.

What if I miss a night of Taraweeh?

Nothing is owed. Taraweeh is not obligatory, so a missed night needs no making up. Simply join again the next night.

Can women pray Taraweeh at the mosque?

Yes, in mosques that provide space for women. Many women pray Taraweeh at the mosque, and many others pray at home. Both are fine.
