How to Use Claude: A Beginner's Guide

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

Claude is a conversational AI chatbot you talk to by typing: ask a question, get an answer, and keep the conversation going with follow-ups. It is widely used for writing, explaining, planning, and working through long documents. This guide walks you through signing up, writing a first prompt, and the habits that make it most useful.

If you have never used an AI chatbot before, Claude is a friendly place to start. There is no technical knowledge needed. You type a message in plain words, the chatbot replies, and you reply back. The whole skill is learning how to ask well: giving enough context, saying what you want back, and following up when the first answer is close but not quite right.

What Claude is

Claude is a large language model with a chat interface, made by Anthropic. In plain words, it is a program trained on a huge amount of text that can understand what you write and reply in natural language. You can ask it questions, ask it to draft and edit writing, explain ideas, summarize documents, help with code, and brainstorm. It does not browse the web in the way a search engine does, and it does not know things that happened after its training, so it is best treated as a thinking partner rather than a source of current facts.

People often compare Claude with other chatbots, and the honest answer is that each one has strengths. Claude is often praised for long, careful writing and for handling long documents without losing the thread. The best way to judge it is to try it on a real task of your own, rather than trusting anyone else's ranking.

Signing up and starting

Getting started takes a few minutes. Create an account with an email address, confirm it, and you will see a chat screen with a box at the bottom. That box is where everything happens. Type a message, send it, and the answer appears above. Your chats are saved in a list, usually on the side, so you can return to an old conversation later.

There is no setup to complete before your first chat. You do not need to fill in a profile or change any settings. Just open a new chat and type something. If you are unsure what to ask first, try a real task you already have: a message you need to write, a topic you want explained, or a list you need to plan. Real tasks make better first prompts than test questions.

Writing your first prompt

A good first prompt has three parts: what you want, some context, and the shape of the answer. Instead of "write a birthday message", try "write a short, warm birthday message for my grandmother, who is turning eighty and loves gardening. Keep it to three sentences." The extra detail takes seconds to add and changes the answer completely.

It also helps to say what the answer is for. A draft email for your boss needs a different tone from a message to a friend. A summary for revision needs different detail from a summary for a meeting. Tell Claude who the answer is for and how it will be used, and the reply will fit the purpose far better than a bare question would.

Using follow-up questions

The first answer is rarely the final answer, and that is normal. The real skill with a chatbot is the follow-up. If the answer is too long, ask for a shorter version. If it is too formal, ask for a friendlier tone. If part of it is wrong, point to that part and ask for a fix. Each follow-up narrows the result toward what you actually wanted.

Useful follow-ups include: "make this shorter", "give me three versions of the second paragraph", "explain the part about the deadline in simpler words", and "what would you change about this draft?" You can also ask Claude to check its own work: "read this back and tell me anything that sounds unclear." Treat the conversation as a collaboration, not a single question.

New chats vs continuing a chat

Each chat is a separate conversation with its own memory. When you continue a chat, Claude remembers what you discussed earlier in that chat and builds on it. When you start a new chat, it knows nothing about the old one. Choosing well between the two is a small habit with a big effect.

Continue a chat when the task is one piece of work: drafting and revising the same letter, planning one trip, or working through one document. Start a new chat when the topic changes completely, or when the old conversation has wandered so far that Claude keeps bringing up things you no longer care about. A fresh chat is also the right move when an answer has gone badly wrong and follow-ups are not fixing it.

Free vs paid tiers

Claude has a free tier and a paid tier, and the free tier is generous enough for everyday use. The paid tier adds higher usage limits, access to the newest models, and extra features such as larger Projects. This guide does not state any price, because prices change; if you are considering the paid tier, check the current pricing page.

Most beginners should start free. Use it for a few weeks on real tasks and notice whether you hit limits or miss features. If you use Claude every day for long documents or heavy writing work, the paid tier may be worth it. If you use it a few times a week for short questions, the free tier will likely serve you well for a long time.

Good habits for beginners

A few habits make Claude much more useful. First, give context before asking: a sentence about who you are and what the answer is for. Second, ask for the format you want, such as a list, a table, or three short paragraphs. Third, check the answer before you use it, especially for facts, numbers, and quotes. Fourth, keep personal details out of your chats: no identity numbers, account details, or passwords.

Finally, remember that Claude is a tool, not an authority. It can draft, explain, and suggest, but the judgement is yours. When an answer matters, read it properly, check it against a reliable source, and edit it in your own voice before you send it anywhere. Used that way, it becomes a genuinely useful daily helper.

Questions people ask

Is Claude free to use?

Yes, there is a free tier that covers everyday chatting, writing help, and questions. There is also a paid tier with higher limits and extra features. Prices change, so check the current pricing page if you are thinking about the paid tier.

What is Claude best at?

Claude is widely used for writing and editing, for working through long documents, and for careful explanations. It is also popular for brainstorming and for coding help. Like any chatbot, it can make mistakes, so check important facts before you rely on them.

Can Claude remember things between chats?

Each chat is separate, so a new chat does not know what you discussed in an old one. For long-running work, Projects let you keep files and instructions in one place so you do not have to repeat yourself. Otherwise, paste the context you need into the chat.

Does Claude make mistakes?

Yes. It can give confident-sounding answers that are wrong, invent details, or misread a document. Treat its answers as drafts to check, especially for facts, numbers, quotes, and anything you plan to publish or send to someone else.

Can I use Claude on my phone?

Yes. Claude works in a phone web browser, and there is a mobile app as well. Your chats are tied to your account, so you can start a conversation on your phone and continue it on a computer.
