7 Bicycle Buying Mistakes — and What Frame Size Might Fit?

Short answer: The right bicycle depends on riding style, fit, components and intended terrain—not frame size alone. Choose a bike category first, use the height picker as a rough starting point, then confirm with the maker’s chart and a test ride.

Height-based frame-size starting point

The seven bicycle buying mistakes

  1. Buying the wrong bike category. Road, mountain, hybrid and city bikes are designed for different riding conditions.
  2. Choosing by looks alone. Consider riding position, gearing, brakes, tires and intended use.
  3. Ignoring fit. Height charts are only a starting point; reach, inseam and proportions also matter.
  4. Skipping a test ride. Check comfort, control, braking and handling before committing.
  5. Overlooking maintenance. Ask about service history on used bikes and inspect wear items.
  6. Forgetting accessories. Consider lights, lock, helmet and other equipment appropriate to your riding.
  7. Not checking assembly and returns. Confirm setup requirements, warranty and return conditions.

Questions people ask

Can height alone determine bicycle frame size?

No. Height gives only a rough starting point; inseam, reach, geometry, riding style and model-specific sizing also matter.

Are road and mountain bike sizes the same?

No. They use different geometry and sizing conventions, so compare the exact model’s chart rather than transferring a size between categories.

Should I test ride a bicycle before buying?

Yes, when possible. A test ride helps you assess reach, comfort, control, braking and handling.

Is the frame-size picker a manufacturer size chart?

No. It gives broad illustrative starting ranges only and does not replace the bicycle maker’s model-specific chart or a professional fit.
