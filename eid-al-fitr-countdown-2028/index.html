Why the date can move by a day

The Islamic calendar is a lunar calendar, and each month begins with the sighting of the new crescent moon. Ramadan is either 29 or 30 days long, and the length is only known when the moon of Shawwal is sighted — or not sighted — on the evening of 29 Ramadan.

In 2028, the Umm al-Qura calculated calendar expects a 29-day Ramadan, which puts Eid on Saturday 26 February. But the confirmed date in each country follows its own moon-sighting announcement, so it is possible for one country to celebrate on Saturday and another on Sunday. This is normal, and it happens in many years.

If you are planning time off or travel for Eid, it is worth planning around both dates until the announcement is made.

What is Eid al-Fitr?

Eid al-Fitr is the festival that marks the end of the month of fasting. The day begins with the Eid prayer, prayed in congregation in the morning, usually at the mosque or in a large open space. Before the prayer, Muslims give Zakat al-Fitr, a charity given on behalf of each member of the household so that those in need can also celebrate the day.

After the prayer, the day is spent with family and friends. Visiting relatives, sharing meals, and giving gifts — especially to children — are all part of the day. In many cultures, the celebrations continue for two or three days.

Eid al-Fitr is one of the two Eids in the Islamic calendar, the other being Eid al-Adha, which falls during the Hajj season. In 2028, Eid al-Adha is expected on Friday 5 May.
