Cake Baking Troubleshooting Finder

This finder helps you work out what went wrong with a cake. Pick the problem from the list and it shows the likely causes and plain steps to fix it next time. Twelve common cake problems are covered.

These are likely causes and general baking fixes, not certainties. Every oven and recipe behaves a little differently, so change one thing at a time and keep notes on what works.

The 12 problems at a glance

  • Sunk in the middle — often underbaked, or the oven door opened too early.
  • Dry cake — often overbaked, or too much flour in the batter.
  • Dense, heavy crumb — often under-mixed, over-mixed or too little raising agent.
  • Big dome on top — often an oven running too hot.
  • Cracked top — often an oven running too hot, or too much raising agent.
  • Burnt or dark edges — often a dark pan, or the cake sitting too close to the heat.
  • Underbaked centre — often an oven running too cool, or a pan that is too deep.
  • Tough, rubbery texture — often over-mixed batter.
  • Uneven rise — often an unlevel shelf or uneven heat.
  • Sticky top — often underbaked, or covered while still warm.
  • Shrank from the pan — often overbaked, or cooled too fast.
  • Stuck to the pan — often a pan that was not greased or lined.

Before you bake: habits that prevent problems

Read the recipe all the way through before you start, and measure every ingredient before you mix anything. Baking moves fast once the wet and dry ingredients meet, and stopping to measure halfway through can cost you the rise. Use room-temperature butter, eggs and milk unless the recipe says otherwise, because cold ingredients do not blend evenly.

Preheat the oven fully and check the shelf is level. An oven that is still heating up bakes unevenly, and a tilted shelf gives a lopsided cake. Grease and line the pan as the recipe directs, and use the pan size the recipe asks for. A few minutes of care before you start prevents most of the problems in the list above.

When to start over

Some cakes can be saved and some cannot. A domed cake can be trimmed level, a cracked top can hide under frosting, and a sunk cake can be filled with cream or fruit. A burnt cake or a raw centre is harder to rescue, and starting over is often the kinder choice.

When a cake fails, write down what happened before you forget. Note the recipe, the pan, the oven setting and the time. Next time, change one thing only, so you know what fixed it. Every baker has failed cakes. The bakers who improve are the ones who keep notes.

Questions people ask

Why did my cake sink in the middle?

A cake usually sinks because it was underbaked or the oven door was opened too early. Bake until a skewer comes out clean and keep the door closed for the first part of the bake.

How can I tell if my cake is done?

Press the top lightly: it should spring back. A skewer pushed into the centre should come out clean or with a few crumbs, and the cake should start to pull away from the pan sides.

Does the pan size really matter?

Yes. The wrong pan size changes the batter depth, which changes how the cake bakes. Use the pan size your recipe asks for, or check a pan substitution guide.

Should I open the oven door while a cake bakes?

Avoid opening the door during the first part of the bake, when the cake is still rising. A sudden drop in heat can make it sink.

Can I fix a cake that has already sunk?

You cannot un-sink a baked cake, but you can still serve it. Trim the top level, or fill the dip with cream or fruit and call it a pudding cake.
