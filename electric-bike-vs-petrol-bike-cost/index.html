Electric Bike vs Petrol Bike Running Cost

An electric bike and a petrol bike can cost very different amounts to run, but the gap depends on how far you ride and what you pay for electricity and fuel. Enter your weekly distance, each bike's energy use and your rates, and this page shows the cost per 100 km or miles and the yearly cost for each.

This compares energy cost only. It leaves out buying the bike, servicing, tyres, insurance, charging equipment and any battery replacement, and it assumes your energy use stays the same all year.

How it works

The calculator works everything out per 100 km or miles, so the two bikes can be compared on the same distance. For the electric bike it multiplies the electricity used per 100 by your electricity rate. For the petrol bike it multiplies the fuel used per 100 by the fuel price. The weekly figure scales the per-100 cost to the distance you ride each week, and the yearly figure multiplies the weekly cost by 52 weeks.

You can use kilometres or miles, as long as you use the same unit for the distance you ride and for the per-100 figures. Your electricity rate is on your energy bill, and your fuel price is what you pay at the pump.

How to use the result

Start with the cost per 100 km or miles — it shows the running cost of the two bikes on equal terms. Then look at the yearly cost, which shows what the gap means for the distance you actually ride. The more you ride, the more the per-distance gap matters.

If the yearly difference is small, other things may matter more: how far each bike goes on a charge or a tank, how long charging takes, and where you can charge or refuel. This page only compares running cost, so use the result alongside those practical questions.

What this comparison leaves out

Energy is only one part of running a bike. The calculator does not count buying the bike, servicing, tyres, brakes, insurance, charging equipment, or a replacement battery. It also assumes your energy use stays the same all year — in practice hills, wind, cold weather and how heavily you load the bike all change how much energy you use.

Charging an electric bike also takes time, and a petrol bike needs trips to a fuel station. Neither is counted here, because this page compares cost only.

Questions people ask

Should I use kilometres or miles?

Either. Use the same unit for the distance you ride each week and for the per-100 energy figures, and the comparison will work out the same.

Where do I find my bike's energy use?

Use the manufacturer's figures, or work it out from your own records: divide the electricity or fuel you used by the distance you covered, then scale it to 100 km or miles.

Does this include charging losses or fuel wasted while idling?

No. The calculator uses the figures you enter. If you know your real use is higher than the manufacturer's figure, enter the higher figure instead.

What if I ride different distances each week?

Enter your average week. The yearly figure multiplies your average week by 52, so it stays fair as long as the average is honest.

Does the calculator include the cost of the bike itself?

No. It compares the energy cost of riding only. Buying, servicing and insurance are separate and are not counted here.
