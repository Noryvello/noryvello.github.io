7 Mistakes to Avoid When Buying an Air Fryer

The most common mistake when buying an air fryer is choosing the wrong size for your household, which means cooking in batches or wasting counter space. This guide covers the seven mistakes buyers make most often, plus a capacity picker for your household.

An air fryer can be a genuinely useful kitchen helper — quick, handy for small batches, and great at crisping things up. But it is also one of the most returned kitchen gadgets, usually because the buyer chose the wrong size or expected it to do more than it can.

The trick is to buy for the cooking you actually do: how many people you feed, how much counter space you have, and what you will cook most often. The mistakes below will help you get that right.

This guide is general buying advice, not a product review. Capacities and features vary between models and makers, so check the usable basket size of any model before you buy.

The 7 mistakes to avoid

  1. Buying the wrong size. Size is the single biggest air fryer mistake. Too small, and you cook in batches while the first batch goes cold; too big, and the fryer swallows counter space for a household of one. Use the capacity picker below as a starting point, and remember that the usable space inside the basket matters more than the headline size — a crowded basket cooks unevenly, so a little extra room is better than too little.
  2. Ignoring the shape of the basket. A wide, shallow basket cooks more evenly than a deep, narrow one for many foods, because hot air reaches the food more easily and nothing sits buried at the bottom. Think about what you will cook most: flat foods like fish and vegetables suit a wide basket, while a deep basket suits foods you shake as they cook. Shape matters as much as size.
  3. Forgetting about counter space. Air fryers are bulkier than they look, and they need more room than their footprint suggests. Measure the spot where the fryer will live, including room to pull the drawer fully open and space around it for steam to escape. A fryer that has to be dragged out of a cupboard every time you cook will not get used nearly as often as one that lives on the counter.
  4. Skipping the cleaning check. You will clean the basket and tray after almost every use, so how easy that is matters a great deal. Check whether the basket and tray remove easily, whether they can be washed simply, and whether food gets stuck in awkward corners. A fryer that is a chore to clean is a fryer that slowly gets used less and less.
  5. Assuming it replaces your oven. An air fryer is wonderful for small batches and quick cooking, but it does not replace an oven for large meals, big joints or several dishes at once. Buying one expecting it to do everything leads to disappointment. Think of it as a handy extra that handles everyday cooking fast, and keep the oven for the jobs that need real space.
  6. Overlooking the controls. Some cooks love simple dials, others prefer presets and a digital display. Neither is better — what matters is that the controls make sense to you. Check that you can set the temperature and time easily, that the display is readable, and that you can tell at a glance how long is left. Controls that confuse you in the shop will confuse you at dinnertime too.
  7. Not checking the noise and smell. Air fryers use a fan, and fans make noise. Cooking smells also spread further from an air fryer than from a closed oven. Think about where you will use it and whether noise matters in your home — especially if you cook early or late. A quick check of reviews for noise will tell you what to expect before the fryer arrives.

Before you buy

Once you know the size you need, take a final look at the practical details. Measure the counter space and check the fryer fits with room to open the drawer. Read the manual or the maker's page for cleaning guidance, so you know what looking after it involves. And check the returns policy, because a fryer that seems right in the shop can feel too small — or too big — after a week of real cooking at home.

When the fryer arrives, start with something simple and forgiving — a batch of vegetables or frozen snacks — so you learn how it cooks before you try a full meal. Check what accessories come in the box, such as a baking tray or a grill rack, and whether more are available for the foods you cook most. A fryer that earns its counter space in the first week will earn it for years.

Questions people ask

What size air fryer do I need?

It depends on how many people you cook for. Use the capacity picker on this page as a starting point, and remember that a crowded basket cooks unevenly, so a little extra room is better than too little.

Can an air fryer replace an oven?

An air fryer suits small batches and quick cooking, but it does not replace an oven for large meals. Think of it as a handy extra that handles everyday cooking fast, not as a full replacement.

Do I need oil in an air fryer?

Most foods need little or no oil, though a light coating helps some foods crisp up. Follow the guidance that comes with your air fryer and the recipe you are using.

How do I clean an air fryer?

Let it cool, then remove the basket and tray and wash them as the maker advises. Wipe the inside with a damp cloth, and clean up spills promptly so they do not bake on.

What can I cook in an air fryer?

Air fryers handle a wide range of foods, from vegetables and fish to chicken pieces and frozen snacks. They suit foods that benefit from hot circulating air and a crisp finish.
