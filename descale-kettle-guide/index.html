How to Descale a Kettle

Limescale is the white crust that builds up inside a kettle in hard-water areas, and descaling means dissolving it away so the kettle boils faster and the water tastes clean again. This guide shows you two methods, how to rinse properly, and how often to descale for your water.

If your kettle has a white or grey crust around the bottom and the element, or white flakes floating in boiled water, it has limescale. The crust is made of minerals left behind when hard water boils. Each boil leaves a little more behind, and over time the layer grows thick enough to slow the kettle down and change the taste of the water.

Descaling is simple and needs nothing special. Both methods below use something you probably already have in the kitchen, and the whole job takes less time than a load of washing. The key is to rinse well afterwards, because the descaling mixture should never end up in your next cup of tea.

Unplug the kettle and let it cool before you clean it, and never immerse the base or the plug in water. Use only the methods below or a descaler made for kettles, and rinse thoroughly before you use the kettle again.

Method 1: vinegar

Vinegar is the quickest way to clear limescale, and it works on even a thick crust. The smell is strong while it works, so open a window before you start.

  1. Empty the kettle and fill it halfway with water.
  2. Add the same amount of white vinegar, so the kettle is about half water and half vinegar.
  3. Boil the kettle, then switch it off and leave the mixture to sit inside.
  4. Leave it until the fizzing stops and the crust has softened or dissolved.
  5. Pour the mixture away and rinse the inside of the kettle well.
  6. Boil a full kettle of fresh water and throw it away. Repeat if the kettle still smells of vinegar.

For a thick crust, you may need to repeat the boil and soak once more. Do not scrape the inside of the kettle with a knife or a metal scourer, because scratches damage the surface and give limescale somewhere new to grip.

Method 2: citric acid

Citric acid works just as well as vinegar and leaves almost no smell behind, which makes it a good choice if you dislike the smell of vinegar in the kitchen.

  1. Empty the kettle and fill it with water.
  2. Add a spoonful of citric acid and stir until it dissolves.
  3. Boil the kettle, then switch it off and leave the mixture to sit inside.
  4. Leave it until the fizzing stops and the crust has softened or dissolved.
  5. Pour the mixture away and rinse the inside of the kettle well.
  6. Boil a full kettle of fresh water and throw it away before you use the kettle again.

Citric acid is sold as a powder for cooking and cleaning. Use it only as the packet describes, keep it away from children, and never mix it with vinegar or any other cleaner.

How often should you descale?

How fast limescale builds up depends on how hard your water is and how often you boil the kettle. Use the checker below for a rough guide, then trust your eyes: if you can see crust or flakes, descale now rather than waiting for the schedule.

Rinsing and aftercare

Rinsing is the step people rush, and it is the step that matters most. After either method, rinse the kettle under running water, then boil a full kettle of fresh water and throw it away. If the water still smells of vinegar or tastes odd, boil and discard another full kettle. Only make a drink when the boiled water smells and tastes like plain water.

Wipe the outside of the kettle with a damp cloth while you are at it, and dry the base before you put the kettle back on it. A damp base can leave marks on the worktop and is not good for the electrical contacts underneath.

Check the filter in the spout, if your kettle has one. Limescale flakes gather there, and a quick rinse under the tap keeps them out of your drinks. Some spout filters lift out for cleaning, and your kettle manual will show how yours comes out.

Slowing limescale down

Empty the kettle after use instead of leaving water sitting in it. Water left in the kettle evaporates slowly and leaves its minerals behind on the element, so an emptied kettle builds up scale more slowly than one that is always half full.

Boil only as much water as you need. Less water per boil means less mineral left behind, and the kettle boils faster too. If you use filtered water for the kettle, the filter will catch some of the minerals before they reach the kettle at all.

Finally, descale on a schedule rather than waiting for a thick crust. Thin limescale dissolves quickly and rinses away easily, while a thick crust needs a longer soak and sometimes a second round. A regular descale keeps the job small.

Questions people ask

What is the white crust inside my kettle?

It is limescale, the mineral left behind when hard water boils and the water evaporates. It is common in hard-water areas and it builds up faster the more the kettle is used.

Is limescale harmful?

Limescale in a kettle is a nuisance rather than a danger: it slows boiling, can flake into drinks, and shortens the life of the heating element. Descaling regularly keeps the kettle working well.

Which method works better, vinegar or citric acid?

Both dissolve limescale well. Vinegar is easy to find and works fast, while citric acid leaves less smell behind. Pick whichever you have, and rinse thoroughly either way.

How do I know the kettle is rinsed enough?

Boil a full kettle of fresh water after descaling and throw it away. If the water still smells of vinegar or tastes odd, boil and discard another full kettle before you make a drink.

Can I stop limescale building up?

You cannot stop it completely in a hard-water area, but emptying the kettle after use and descaling on a regular schedule keeps it thin and easy to remove.
