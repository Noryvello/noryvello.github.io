How the dates are set

Islamic months begin with the sighting of the new crescent moon. The dates in this calendar come from the Umm al-Qura calendar, which is a calculated calendar used to predict the months in advance. Because it is calculated rather than observed, it is an expectation, not a confirmation.

Countries and communities can differ by a day. Some follow the moon sighting in their own country, others follow the announcement from Makkah, and others follow a calculated calendar. That is why Ramadan can begin on different days in different places, and why the dates on this page may not match the dates announced by your local mosque.

This difference is normal and expected. It does not mean one calendar is wrong and another right — it means different communities follow different ways of deciding when the month begins. The practical answer is simple: follow the announcement of the mosque or authority you pray with.

If Ramadan is 30 days

The month is expected to have 29 days, but the Islamic month can also complete 30 days if the new moon is not sighted at the end of the 29th day. If that happens, Ramadan ends on Tuesday 9 March 2027, and Eid al-Fitr moves to Wednesday 10 March 2027.

In practice, this means the last few days of the calendar are the least certain. The first days of Ramadan are fixed once the month begins, but whether the month ends after 29 or 30 days is only known when the moon is sighted — or not sighted — at the end of the month. Plan Eid celebrations with this in mind, and wait for your local announcement before fixing the day.

How to use this calendar

The calendar is laid out day by day so you can follow Ramadan from its first fast to its last. Use it to plan the month ahead: to know when the last ten nights begin, to mark the odd nights for extra worship, and to see when Eid al-Fitr is expected.

The last ten nights deserve the most planning, because they hold Laylat al-Qadr. Knowing in advance that they begin at sunset on Saturday 27 February 2027 lets you arrange time off, family commitments, and mosque visits before the nights arrive, rather than scrambling once they have begun.

If you are travelling during Ramadan, remember that the dates can differ by a day between countries. Check the announcement in the place you will be, rather than assuming the dates from home apply everywhere.
