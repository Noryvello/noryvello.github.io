Subscription Audit Checklist

List your subscriptions, mark each one keep, cancel or undecided, and see your monthly and yearly totals at a glance. The tool also shows how much you would save in a year if the cancelled ones go. Use any currency — just type the amounts as they appear on your statements.

The three rows below are examples to show how the tool works — remove them and add your own subscriptions.

This page does not connect to any account. You type your own subscriptions, and nothing you type is saved or sent anywhere — it stays in your browser. Always check the subscription's own settings before you cancel, so you know what happens to your access.

How to run your audit

  1. Find every subscription you pay for — check your statements, your phone's subscriptions page and your email receipts.
  2. Add each one above with its monthly cost, in your own currency.
  3. Mark each one keep, cancel or undecided, and watch the totals change.
  4. Cancel the ones you marked cancel, then come back in a few months and audit again.

What the totals mean

The monthly total is the sum of every subscription in your list. The yearly total is the monthly total multiplied by 12. The yearly saving is the monthly cost of the subscriptions marked cancel, multiplied by 12 — what you would keep in a year if those subscriptions go.

Questions people ask

How do I find all my subscriptions?

Check your bank or card statements for the last few months and look for regular payments, then check the subscriptions page in your phone's settings and in the app stores you use. Email receipts for renewals are another good place to look. Write each one down here as you find it.

How much do people spend on subscriptions?

It varies widely — some people have one or two subscriptions, others have many, and the amounts differ from person to person. There is no single typical figure that fits everyone. The best way to know your own number is to add up your own subscriptions, which is what this page helps you do.

Which subscriptions should I cancel first?

Start with the ones you have not used in the last month, and the ones you forgot you had. Then look for duplicates — two services that do the same job — and keep the one you use most. Mark the rest as undecided and review them again next month.

How often should I audit my subscriptions?

A quick audit every few months is enough for most people, and once a year at least. It is also worth checking before a yearly renewal comes around, so you are not caught by a payment you no longer want.
