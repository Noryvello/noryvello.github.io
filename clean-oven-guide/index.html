How to Clean an Oven

Cleaning an oven is mostly a matter of working through it in order: the racks first, then the interior, then the glass door. This guide shows you the steps for your oven type, with a cleaning checklist and safety notes.

An oven collects grease and spills every time it is used, and each hot cycle bakes the last lot a little harder onto the walls. Left alone, the build-up starts to smoke when the oven heats up, and the smell can find its way into whatever you are cooking. A clean oven heats more evenly and is simply nicer to use.

The job looks worse than it is. Most of the work is waiting: the racks soak while you clean the interior, and the cleaner sits on the walls while you clean the glass. Work in the order this guide gives, and the whole clean takes less effort than putting it off for another month.

Let the oven cool fully before you start, and never spray cleaner on a hot oven. Open a window and wear gloves, and never use oven cleaner on self-cleaning surfaces unless your manual says it is safe. If the oven has a self-cleaning cycle, follow the manual exactly.

The racks

Take the racks out first, because they need the longest soak. Lay them in a sink or a large tub of hot soapy water and leave them while you clean the rest of the oven. The soak softens the baked-on grease so that a scrub afterwards takes far less effort.

When the interior is done, scrub the racks with a brush or a scouring pad, rinse them well, and dry them before you put them back. If a rack still has stubborn patches, soak it again rather than scraping hard, because scraping can damage the coating on the rack.

Never put racks through a self-cleaning cycle unless your manual says they can stay in. The very high heat of the cycle can dull or damage the rack finish, and some racks warp. When in doubt, take them out and clean them by hand.

The interior

Start by wiping out loose crumbs and flakes with a dry cloth or a dustpan. Loose food on the oven floor is what smokes first, so getting it out makes a real difference before you even touch the walls.

Apply an oven cleaner made for the job, exactly as its label says, or spread a paste of baking soda and water over the interior walls. Leave it to sit for the time the label or the paste needs to soften the grease. Work from the top down when you wipe, so loosened dirt falls onto the floor you have not cleaned yet rather than onto clean surfaces.

Wipe the interior with a damp cloth, rinsing the cloth often, until no cleaner or paste is left. Rinse well: cleaner left on the walls will smoke the next time the oven heats up. Dry the interior with a clean cloth, and leave the door open for a while so the oven airs out before you use it again.

The glass door

The inside of the glass door clouds over with grease and steam marks. Spread a paste of baking soda and water over the inside of the glass and leave it to sit while you finish the interior. Then wipe it away with a damp cloth and polish the glass with a dry cloth.

Some oven doors have two layers of glass with a gap between them, and grease can work its way into the gap. If your door is one of these, check the manual for how the glass comes apart on your model. Do not try to force the door apart, and never use a sharp tool on the glass.

For the outside of the door, a damp cloth and a dry polish are enough. Avoid spraying cleaner directly onto the control panel, since liquid can run into the controls. Spray the cloth instead, wipe the panel, and dry it straight away.

Keeping the oven cleaner for longer

Wipe small spills when the oven is cool, before the next time you cook. A spill wiped today is a damp cloth job; the same spill baked on through three more roasts is a scrubbing job. Keep a cloth nearby and make the wipe part of putting the kitchen away.

Use a baking tray or a sheet of foil under dishes that bubble over, such as pies and casseroles. Catching the drip is far easier than cleaning it off the oven floor, and the tray can go in the wash with the dishes.

Finally, give the oven a quick wipe of the door glass every few weeks. The glass shows dirt first, and keeping it clear makes the whole oven look and feel cleaner, which makes the full clean feel less like a chore when it comes round.

Questions people ask

How often should I clean my oven?

Clean it when you see baked-on spills or smell smoke when it heats up, and give it a full clean every few months. Wiping small spills when the oven is cool stops them baking on and makes the full clean much easier.

Can I use oven cleaner on the glass door?

Only if the cleaner label says it is safe for glass. A paste of baking soda and water, left to sit and then wiped away, is a gentler way to clear a cloudy door.

What should I do with the racks?

Take them out and soak them in hot soapy water, then scrub and rinse them. Never put racks through a self-cleaning cycle unless the manual says they can stay in, because the heat can damage the finish.

Is it safe to use the self-cleaning cycle?

Yes, when you follow the manual. Remove loose food first, keep the kitchen ventilated, and stay in the house while the cycle runs. The oven gets very hot, so keep children and pets away.

Why does my oven smoke when I use it?

Smoke usually comes from old spills burning off the oven floor or the heating element. A full clean of the interior and the floor will usually stop it, but if the smoke continues, stop using the oven and call a repairer.
