This page makes practice sheets for general handwriting practice. It does not replace teaching or assessment. If a child finds writing painful or very difficult, speak to their teacher for advice.

Letter-formation notes

Most letters start at the top and are formed with smooth strokes in one direction. Lowercase letters sit between the baseline and the midline, except for tall letters such as b, d, f, h, k, l and t, which reach up to the top line, and letters with tails such as g, j, p, q and y, which drop below the baseline. The dashed midline on the ruled sheet shows where the main body of each letter should sit.

Trace the grey letters slowly before writing on the blank lines. Follow the shape of each letter rather than rushing to finish the row. Keep the pencil grip relaxed and the wrist resting on the page. If a letter feels awkward, slow down and trace it again rather than pressing harder.

How to use the sheet

Start with the trace lines. Go over each grey letter or word with a pencil, following the shape carefully. When the trace lines feel comfortable, move to the blank lines and write the same letters or words without the guide. Compare your free writing with the traced rows to see which letters need more work.

For letter sets, each row practises one letter repeated across the line. For your own words, each word gets its own row. Print a fresh sheet whenever you want a clean start, and keep finished sheets to see how the writing improves over time.

Tips for regular practice

Short regular sessions work better than one long session. A few minutes of focused practice each day builds steadier handwriting than an hour once a week. Sit up straight with both feet on the floor and the paper slightly angled, and take a short break if the hand gets tired.

Mix letter practice with word practice. Letters build the shapes, but words build the flow between letters. Once a letter feels easy on its own, practise it inside short words. Praise steady progress rather than speed, and keep the practice relaxed so it stays enjoyable.

Questions people ask

What words should I practise?

Short everyday words work well, such as names, colours and simple nouns. Keep the list short so each word gets a full row of practice.

Which line style should I choose?

Ruled lines with a midline help with letter height. Plain lines suit confident writers. Wide lines suit younger children or anyone who writes large.

How do I print the sheet?

Press the Print button on the sheet. The sheet prints on its own, without the rest of the page.

Can I use my own words?

Yes. Choose My own words, type up to 40 characters, and press Make my sheet. Use letters, numbers and spaces only.

How often should I practise?

Short regular sessions work better than one long session. A few minutes of focused practice each day is a good habit to build.
