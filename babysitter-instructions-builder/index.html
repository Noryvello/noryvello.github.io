Babysitter Instructions Builder

Fill in your child's routines, meals, bedtime and emergency contacts, and this builder turns them into a clear instruction sheet you can print for your babysitter. A written sheet keeps the evening calm and means nothing important is forgotten.

Even the best babysitter cannot read your mind. Children have their own routines, their own likes and dislikes, and their own way of winding down at night. Writing it all down once means the sitter can follow your family's rhythm instead of improvising.

This builder puts the sheet together for you. Fill in the routines, the meals, the bedtime steps and the contacts, then build the sheet and print it. Go through it with your sitter when they arrive, and leave it where they can find it.

This page is general information only. It does not give medical or childcare advice. If your child has a health condition or takes medicine, write down exactly what your doctor has told you and talk it through with the sitter in person.

What to include in the sheet

Start with the routines: meal times, snack times, quiet time and outdoor play. Then add the bedtime steps in order — bath, pyjamas, stories, lights out — because bedtime is the part of the evening where a clear order matters most.

Write down any allergies or foods to avoid in plain words, and say where the food is kept. If your child takes medicine, write down exactly what your doctor told you and talk it through with the sitter in person. Never leave medicine instructions to memory.

Finish with the house rules and the emergency contacts: your number, a second contact, and the doctor's number. Keep the sheet to one page so the sitter can read it at a glance.

Before the babysitter arrives

Show the sitter around the house when they arrive: the kitchen, the bathroom, the children's rooms, and where the first aid kit lives. Point out anything unusual, such as a stair gate or a window that sticks.

Agree how you will stay in touch — a short message when the children are in bed is usually enough — and tell the sitter what time you expect to be home. A calm handover at the start makes for a calm evening all round.

Questions people ask

What routines should I include on the sheet?

Include meal times, snack times, playtime, quiet time and the bedtime steps in order. Short notes are enough — the sitter needs the rhythm of the evening, not a long essay.

What about allergies?

Write allergies in plain words on the sheet, and say which foods to avoid. Show the sitter where safe food is kept, and talk through what to do if a child eats something they should not.

How much detail is too much?

Aim for one page. Cover routines, meals, bedtime, contacts and house rules, and leave out anything the sitter does not need. If the sheet is too long, the important parts get lost.

Should I leave my phone number?

Yes, always. Leave your number, a second contact, and the doctor's number. Tell the sitter to call you first if anything is unclear.

Can I print the sheet?

Yes. Press the print button under the builder to print the instruction sheet. Leave it on the kitchen table or pin it to the fridge.
