How to Wrap a Gift

Measuring your paper

Most wrapping problems start with the wrong amount of paper. Too little paper means a gap you cannot hide, and too much paper means thick, bulky folds at the ends. The simple way to measure is to roll the box across the paper: set the box on its side, roll it over once in each direction, and mark where the paper needs to reach. Add a little extra on each side for the fold. For the length, wrap the paper loosely around the box and mark where it overlaps by a few centimetres.

Cut the paper with long, smooth scissor strokes rather than short choppy ones, and cut on a flat surface. If the paper has a pattern, check which way up it goes before you cut, so the pattern is the right way up on the finished gift. If you are wrapping several gifts in the same paper, measure and cut them one at a time rather than cutting a stack of sheets at once, because each box is a slightly different size.

The basic wrap, step by step

Place the gift upside down in the centre of the paper, so the seams end up on the bottom. Bring one long edge of the paper up and over the gift and tape it down, then bring the other long edge over it and tape that down too. Fold the raw edge of the top layer under by a finger width before taping, so the seam looks clean. Crease the paper firmly along each edge of the box with your fingers or the flat of a ruler, because sharp creases are what make wrapping look tidy.

For the ends, push the two sides of the paper inwards to form flaps, crease them flat, then fold the top flap down and the bottom flap up. Tape the top flap down and finish with the bottom flap. Work one end at a time, and keep the paper pulled snug but not so tight that it tears. If a fold goes wrong, unfold it and start that end again rather than piling tape over a wrinkle.

Wrapping awkward shapes

Round and soft items are the hardest to wrap, and the honest answer is that a box makes them easy. If the gift is a scarf, a toy, or anything soft, put it in a box first and wrap the box. If there is no box, gather the paper around the item like a pouch and tie it with ribbon, which looks deliberate rather than messy. Cylinders are easier than they look: roll the gift in the paper, twist or fold the ends, and tie them with ribbon.

Bottles are a common awkward shape. Stand the bottle in the centre of a square of paper, gather the paper up around the neck, and tie it with ribbon. A second layer of tissue paper under the wrapping paper softens the shape and protects the label. Very large items, such as a picture frame, are best wrapped flat on the floor, where you have room to move around the gift instead of trying to turn it on a table.

Finishing touches: ribbon and bows

Ribbon turns a plain wrap into a finished gift. Cut a length of ribbon about five times the length of the gift, wrap it around the box in both directions, and tie a knot on top. For a bow, make two loops the way you tie shoelaces and pull them snug, then trim the ends at an angle. Curling ribbon can be curled by running it firmly over a scissor blade, but keep your fingers clear of the blade and work slowly.

Keep the finishing simple. One ribbon and one bow is enough for most gifts, and a gift tag tucked under the ribbon is neater than a tag stuck on with tape. If the paper is busy, choose a plain ribbon, and if the paper is plain, a patterned ribbon adds interest. Write the tag before you attach it, so a mistake does not mean unwrapping the gift.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is using too much tape. Tape should hold folds in place, not hold the whole gift together. Use short pieces, and place them where they will be hidden by the next fold. The second most common mistake is wrapping on a surface that is too small. Clear a table or work on the floor, because wrapping in a cramped space leads to creased paper and torn corners.

Another mistake is wrapping a gift the right way up. Always wrap with the top of the gift facing down, so the seams and tape end up underneath. Finally, do not rush the ends. The ends are the part people notice, so take an extra minute to fold them neatly. A gift wrapped slowly and carefully always looks better than one wrapped in a hurry.

These steps assume ordinary wrapping paper, tape, and ribbon. Very heavy or fragile gifts should be packed in a sturdy box with padding first, and wrapping alone will not protect them. Keep scissors and blades away from children, and never leave a young child alone with ribbon or string.

Questions people ask

How much wrapping paper do I need for a box?

Roll the box across the paper to measure the width, then make sure the paper reaches up both sides with a little extra. For the length, the paper should reach around the box and overlap by a few centimetres. It is better to cut too much than too little.

What is the easiest way to wrap a gift neatly?

Put the gift in a box first, even if it does not need one. A box gives you straight edges to fold against, and straight edges are what make wrapping look neat.

How do I wrap a gift without tape showing?

Use small pieces of double-sided tape under the folded edges instead of tape on top. Fold the cut edge of the paper under by a finger width first, so the fold sits flat and hides the tape.

What can I use instead of wrapping paper?

A gift bag, a fabric wrap, a paper bag, or plain brown paper all work well. A drawstring pouch is a good choice for small or awkwardly shaped items.

How do I tie a simple ribbon bow?

Wrap the ribbon around the gift and tie a knot, then make two loops the way you tie shoelaces and pull them snug. Trim the ribbon ends at an angle and fluff the loops so the bow sits evenly.
