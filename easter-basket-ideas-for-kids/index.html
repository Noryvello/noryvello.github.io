Easter Basket Ideas for Kids

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

A great Easter basket mixes a little chocolate with toys, crafts, and surprises that suit the child. This page has basket ideas by age, from toddlers to older children, plus DIY basket and decoration ideas and plenty of non-chocolate fillers.

Easter morning has a magic of its own, and the basket is at the centre of it. Children wake to find a basket filled with treats, and the joy is in the unwrapping as much as in the chocolate. A good basket does not need to be expensive or elaborate: it needs a few things the child will love, chosen with their age and interests in mind.

The best baskets mix treats with things to do. A little chocolate, a small toy, something to make or draw with, and a surprise or two will keep a child happy long after the chocolate is gone. This page gives ideas by age, shows how to make a basket yourself, and offers plenty of fillers that are not chocolate at all.

Check age guidance on toys and treats before you buy, and keep small parts, hard sweets, and small chocolate eggs away from toddlers and young children. Ask about allergies before giving food, and supervise young children with craft supplies such as scissors and glue.

What makes a great Easter basket

A great basket starts with the child, not the shop. Think about what they love: drawing, building, stories, animals, or being outside. A basket built around one interest feels personal, while a basket of random treats feels like a lucky dip. Pick a theme if it helps: a garden basket with seeds and a watering can, a craft basket with paints and paper, or a story basket with a book and a cuddly toy.

Balance matters. A little chocolate goes a long way, especially for young children, and the basket is better for having things to do as well as things to eat. Keep the basket itself simple: a woven basket, a bucket, or a decorated box all work, and the container can be part of the gift. Fill the bottom with shredded paper or tissue so the treats sit on top and look generous.

Think about the morning as well as the basket. A basket that arrives with a small egg hunt, or with a note from the Easter Bunny, feels like an event rather than a present. A short handwritten note, hidden under the treats, is a keepsake children remember long after the chocolate is gone.

Basket ideas for toddlers

Toddlers love colour, texture, and things they can hold. A toddler basket can include soft toys, board books, bath toys, and simple stacking toys, alongside a few small treats that are safe for their age. Avoid small parts, hard sweets, and small chocolate eggs, which are a choking risk for very young children.

Keep the basket small and the contents chunky. A soft bunny, a ball, a set of chunky crayons, and a board book about spring make a lovely toddler basket. Add a few slices of fruit or a small biscuit if you want a food treat, and let the toddler unpack the basket at their own pace: the unwrapping is half the fun.

Basket ideas for younger children

Children of school age enjoy baskets with things to make and do. Crayons, colouring books, stickers, bubbles, skipping ropes, and small building sets all fit well, and a chocolate egg or two adds the Easter feeling. A small puzzle or a card game gives them something to enjoy with the family after the hunt.

Personal touches make the basket special. A book about their favourite subject, a packet of seeds to grow, or a small craft kit chosen for them shows that you thought about them. If they have a hobby, such as drawing or football, add something small for it. Keep the chocolate modest: a few eggs and a small bunny are plenty.

Basket ideas for older children

Older children may feel they have outgrown the Easter basket, but a well-chosen one still delights them. Think about their interests: a book by an author they love, a puzzle or a board game, art supplies, sports gear, or a small gadget accessory. Chocolate still works, but choose good chocolate rather than lots of it.

Let older children help with the Easter egg hunt for younger siblings: it gives them a role and keeps them part of the morning. A basket with a voucher for a family outing, a film night, or a trip to a place they love can be more exciting than anything in a shop. The message of the basket matters more than its contents: you are thought of, and you are loved.

DIY baskets and decorations

Making the basket yourself is part of the fun, and children can help. A simple basket can be made from a cardboard box decorated with coloured paper, or from a bucket wrapped in fabric and ribbon. Add a handle of ribbon or pipe cleaners, and fill it with shredded paper in spring colours.

Decorations are easy too. Paper eggs cut from coloured card, tissue paper flowers, and hand-drawn cards all add a personal touch. Let children decorate their own baskets the day before Easter: it builds excitement and gives them something to show off on the morning. Keep glue and scissors supervised, and protect the table with newspaper.

Name tags are a small touch that children love. A folded card with each child's name, tied to the basket handle with ribbon, makes the basket feel made for them. If you have more than one child, use a different colour for each basket so there is no confusion on the morning.

Beyond chocolate: non-sweet fillers

Not every child wants chocolate, and not every parent wants a basket full of sugar. Non-sweet fillers can be just as exciting: small toys, craft supplies, stationery, seeds, bubbles, and outdoor games all work well. A basket of non-sweet treats can be themed around an activity, such as gardening, painting, or exploring the outdoors.

Food fillers need not be chocolate either. Dried fruit, popcorn, cheese crackers, and fruit pouches are good options for children who cannot eat chocolate or who prefer savoury snacks. If you are filling baskets for other children, ask their parents about allergies and preferences first.

A small book is one of the best non-sweet fillers of all. A picture book for a young child or a short story collection for an older one will be read long after Easter, and it makes the basket feel thoughtful rather than hurried.

Putting the basket together

Put the basket together the night before Easter, when children are asleep. Start with the filler at the bottom, arrange the larger items at the back, and place the smaller treats in front where they can be seen. A few chocolate eggs scattered on top make the basket look festive, and a small card or note adds a personal touch.

On Easter morning, hide the basket somewhere the child can find it, or leave it at the end of their bed. If you are doing an egg hunt as well, keep the basket separate from the hunt, so the child has two moments of joy: the basket first, then the hunt. Take a photo before the unwrapping begins: the morning goes quickly.

After the morning, the basket itself can stay in use. A woven basket becomes a toy box, a bucket becomes a garden toy, and a decorated box can hold craft supplies. A basket that keeps being used is a gift that lasts well beyond Easter Sunday.

Questions people ask

What age is an Easter basket for?

Easter baskets suit children of any age, from toddlers to teenagers, as long as the contents match the child. Choose age-appropriate toys and treats, and check age guidance on packaging before you buy.

What should go in a toddler Easter basket?

Soft toys, board books, bath toys, chunky crayons, and a ball make a lovely toddler basket. Avoid small parts, hard sweets, and small chocolate eggs, which are a choking risk for very young children.

How do I make an Easter basket myself?

Decorate a cardboard box, bucket, or woven basket with coloured paper, ribbon, and tissue paper. Add a handle of ribbon, fill the bottom with shredded paper, and let children help decorate it the day before Easter.

What can I put in a basket instead of chocolate?

Small toys, craft supplies, stickers, bubbles, seeds, stationery, and outdoor games all work well. Dried fruit, popcorn, and cheese crackers are good food fillers for children who cannot eat chocolate.

How much should I spend on an Easter basket?

Spend what suits your household. A thoughtful basket with a few well-chosen items is better than an expensive one, and homemade baskets and craft fillers keep costs down while adding a personal touch.

When do children get their Easter baskets?

Baskets are usually given on Easter Sunday morning, often before or after an egg hunt. Some families leave the basket at the end of the bed, while others hide it for the child to find.
