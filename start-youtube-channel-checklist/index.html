Start a YouTube Channel Checklist

A YouTube channel starts with a clear idea, a simple setup and a plan for the first videos. This checklist takes you from channel setup to your first upload, with a progress counter to keep you moving.

Starting a channel can feel like a big project, but the first steps are small and ordinary. You need a name, a subject you can make more than one video about, a way to film, and a plan for the first video. Everything else — the design, the posting rhythm, the way you present — improves with practice. The channels that grow are usually the ones that kept publishing, not the ones that waited until everything was perfect.

YouTube's features, rules and requirements change over time. Check the platform's current guidance before you publish, and only upload video you have the right to share.

How to use this checklist

Work through the list in order, ticking each step as you finish it. The counter at the top of the checklist shows your progress. If a step does not apply to your channel, tick it and move on. The goal is a first video that is published, not a first video that is flawless. Your tenth video will be better than your first, and that is how it should be.

Channel setup

Start with the channel itself. Pick a name that is easy to say and easy to remember, and check that a similar name is not already in use by another channel in the same area. Write a short channel description that says what viewers will find and who it is for, in one or two sentences. A simple channel image and a clear profile picture help viewers recognise your channel at a glance. Keep both simple: a readable name and a clean image do the job.

Decide on the subject of the channel before you film anything. A channel about one clear subject is easier to plan and easier for viewers to understand than a channel about everything at once. The test is the same as for a blog: can you think of ten video ideas without straining? If you can, the subject has room to grow. Think also about who is watching. A video made for beginners sounds different from a video made for people who already know the subject, and knowing your viewer shapes every choice that follows.

First-video plan

Your first video does not need to be long or complicated. A short video that does one thing well — answers a question, shows a process, or introduces the channel — is a better start than a long video that tries to do everything. Plan the video in a few short notes: what it is about, what the viewer will learn or enjoy, and the order of the main points. A rough plan keeps you on track while filming and saves time later.

Plan the next two videos as well, even if only as rough ideas. Having a short list of ideas stops the stall that follows many first uploads, when the excitement fades and the second video feels like a mountain. Decide how often you can realistically publish, and pick a rhythm you can keep. One video a week, kept up steadily, is a strong start for most new channels.

Filming

Film with the camera you already have. For most new channels that is a phone, and it is good enough to start. Film in a quiet, well-lit place, with the light falling on your face or your subject rather than behind it. Record a short test clip and play it back before the real take, checking the picture and the sound. Keep the camera steady, frame the shot with a little space above your head, and speak clearly and at a natural pace.

If you make a mistake while filming, pause for a moment and start the sentence again. The pause makes the mistake easy to find and remove later. Film a little more than you need at the start and end of each part, so you have clean places to cut. A calm, unhurried filming session produces better video than a rushed one.

Upload steps

When the video is ready, give it a clear title that says what it is about, in words a viewer would actually search for. Write a short description that explains the video in a sentence or two. Choose a thumbnail image that is clear and honest about what the video contains. Before you publish, watch the video once more from start to finish and check the sound, the picture and the spelling of the title and description.

After publishing, share the video with a few people whose opinion you trust, and read any comments with an open mind. Early feedback is useful, but remember that a first video is a starting point. The habit of publishing, learning and improving matters more than any single upload.

After your first video

The first video is a milestone, but the channel is built by the videos that follow. Keep a running list of video ideas and add to it whenever something catches your interest. Watch your own videos back with a critical eye: where did you lose energy, where did the sound dip, where could the video have been shorter? Small improvements, one video at a time, add up faster than any single big change. And be patient. Channels grow slowly at first, and a steady publishing habit is the most reliable way to build an audience.

Questions people ask

Do I need expensive equipment to start a channel?

No. A phone camera, a quiet room and good light are enough to start. Viewers care more about clear sound, a steady picture and a video that delivers what its title promises than about expensive gear.

How long should my first video be?

As long as the idea needs, and no longer. A short video that does one thing well is a better start than a long video that wanders. Aim to keep the viewer interested from the first minute to the last.

Do I need to show my face on camera?

No. Many channels work well with voiceover, on-screen demonstration, or filming hands and objects rather than a face. Choose the style you are comfortable with and that suits your subject.

How often should I upload?

Pick a rhythm you can keep for months. One video a week, kept up steadily, is a strong start for most new channels. Regular publishing builds an audience more reliably than bursts followed by silence.

What should my channel be about?

Pick a subject you can make many videos about and that you enjoy enough to keep going. The test is whether you can list ten video ideas without straining. A clear subject also helps viewers know what to expect.
