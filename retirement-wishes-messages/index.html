Retirement Wishes and Messages

Retirement is a milestone worth marking with the right words, and this page offers 25 original messages to help. They are grouped by recipient: colleagues, teachers and family members. Choose one that fits, add a memory of your own, and send it with warmth.

After years of early mornings, deadlines and dedication, retirement is a well-earned rest and a fresh beginning. The right message honours the work that came before and wishes the person well for what comes next. The messages below are all original, written for this page, and grouped by who they are for.

These messages are starting points. Add a memory, a thank you for something specific, or a wish for a plan you know they have, and the message becomes yours. A card with one honest line beats a long message with none.

How to write a retirement card

A good retirement card has three parts: a thank you, a memory and a wish. Thank them for something specific, such as their patience, their humour or their years of steady work. Share one memory, even a small one, because memories show the card was written for them alone. Then wish them well for the days ahead.

Keep the tone warm and upbeat. Retirement is a happy occasion, even if the workplace will miss them. Avoid jokes about old age unless you know the person enjoys them. A sincere line about what they meant to you will be read more than once.

What to say when you do not know them well

If the retiree is a colleague you knew only slightly, a short formal message is perfectly fine. Congratulate them, wish them health and happiness, and sign with your name and team. Nobody expects a long message from an acquaintance, and a brief kind note is always welcome.

For a group card, one or two sentences from each person works well. Short personal notes from many people mean more than one long message from the organiser.

Questions people ask

What do you write in a retirement card?

Thank them for their work, share one memory, and wish them well for the future. A short, sincere card with one personal detail means more than a long generic message.

What is a good message for a retiring colleague?

Mention something you valued about working with them, such as their humour, patience or skill, and wish them a happy, restful retirement. Keep it warm and professional.

What should I write to a retiring teacher?

Thank them for their teaching and mention what they meant to you or your child. Teachers treasure knowing their work made a difference, so a specific memory is a lovely touch.

Is it okay to joke about old age in a retirement message?

Only if you know the person enjoys that kind of humour. For most people, a warm and respectful message is safer and better received.

When should I send a retirement message?

Around their last day or the retirement celebration is ideal. If you missed it, a message in the first weeks of retirement is still very welcome.
