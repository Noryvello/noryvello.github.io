Carry-on Liquids Guide

The 100ml rule is simple: each container of liquid in your carry-on must hold 100ml or less, all containers must fit in one clear resealable bag, and you may take one bag per passenger. Leave a box empty if you have fewer containers. Empty boxes are ignored. The checker only checks container sizes — your bag must still be clear and resealable, one bag per passenger, and that part is up to you.

The 100ml rule in plain words

The rule has three parts, and all three must hold at the same time:

  1. Each container must hold 100ml or less. A container of exactly 100ml passes. A container of 101ml or more fails.
  2. All containers go in one clear resealable bag. The bag must be clear so security can see what is inside, and resealable so it closes properly.
  3. One bag per passenger. Each traveller gets one bag, not one bag per family or per group.

One detail catches many people out: the limit is on the size of the container, not on how much liquid is left inside it. A 200ml bottle with only 50ml of shampoo left still counts as a 200ml container, so it fails the rule. If you want to take that shampoo, pour it into a container of 100ml or less before you travel.

Liquids, gels, creams and pastes are usually treated the same way at security. Toothpaste, shampoo, sun cream, jam and similar items normally count as liquids, so put them in the bag too.

Packing the bag

Put every liquid container in the bag before you reach the airport, and keep the bag somewhere you can reach it easily — you will usually be asked to take it out at security. If a container does not fit, or if you have more containers than fit in one bag, the extra ones belong in your checked luggage, not in a second bag.

Containers over 100ml go in checked luggage too. If you have no checked luggage, leave them at home or buy a smaller size for the trip.

The checker above only checks container sizes. Whether your bag is clear and resealable, whether everything fits inside it, and whether your airport follows the usual rule are all things only you can check — the checker cannot see your bag or your airport's rules.

Worked examples

Container 2: 50ml — pass (50ml is 100ml or less).
Container 3: 75ml — pass (75ml is 100ml or less).
Bag rule: 3 containers entered, 3 pass, 0 fail. Every container is 100ml or less, so the bag rule is met.
Result: 3 containers entered. All 3 pass: each is 100ml or less. Bag rule met — these containers may go in your one clear resealable bag.

Example 2 — containers of 100ml and 150ml.
Container 1: 100ml — pass (100ml is 100ml or less).
Container 2: 150ml — fail (150ml is over 100ml by 50ml).
Bag rule: 2 containers entered, 1 pass, 1 fail. Not every container is 100ml or less, so the bag rule is not met.
Result: 2 containers entered. 1 passes, 1 fails. Bag rule not met — a container over 100ml cannot go in the bag.

Before you fly

Rules can differ by airport and by country, and they can change over time. Some airports allow exceptions for medicine and baby food, and some have newer scanners with different rules — but you cannot count on an exception unless your airport says so. Check your airport's own rules before you fly, and check again if you are changing planes, because the rules at your connecting airport may not be the same.

When in doubt, the safe choice is the strict one: keep every container at 100ml or less, keep everything in one clear resealable bag, and put anything larger in your checked luggage.

Questions people ask

What is the 100ml rule?

The 100ml rule says each container of liquid in your carry-on must hold 100ml or less. All the containers must fit in one clear resealable bag, and each passenger may take one bag. The limit is on the size of the container, not on how much liquid is left inside it.

What counts as a liquid?

Liquids, gels, creams and pastes are usually treated the same way at security, so things like toothpaste, shampoo, sun cream and jam normally count as liquids. If you are unsure about an item, treat it as a liquid and put it in the bag, or check your airport's own rules before you fly.

Can I take more than one bag?

No, the usual rule is one clear resealable bag per passenger. If your containers do not all fit in one bag, the extra ones cannot go in your carry-on as liquids and should go in checked luggage instead. Some airports may have different rules, so check before you fly.

What happens if a container is over 100ml?

A container over 100ml usually cannot go through security in your carry-on and will have to be left behind. A half-empty 200ml bottle still counts as 200ml, because the limit is on the container's size. Put larger containers in your checked luggage, or leave them at home.
