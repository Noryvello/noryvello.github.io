7 Credit Card Mistakes That Can Cost You Money

Short answer: avoid common costs by checking payment dates, fees, interest, statements and account security; use the checklist to track your preparation.

Seven credit card mistakes that can cost money

  1. Paying only the minimum. A low required payment can leave a balance accruing interest for longer. Check the statement balance and payment due date.
  2. Missing a payment date. Late payments can trigger fees and may affect your credit record under local rules. Use a reminder or automatic payment if appropriate.
  3. Carrying a balance for rewards. Rewards are not a reason to pay interest. Compare the cost of borrowing with any reward value.
  4. Ignoring the full fee schedule. Review annual, late, cash-advance, foreign-transaction and other applicable fees before using a card.
  5. Using cash advances casually. Cash advances can have different fees and interest rules from purchases. Read the card terms first.
  6. Not checking statements. Review transactions promptly and contact the issuer through an official channel if something looks unfamiliar.
  7. Sharing card or verification details. Do not share passwords, one-time codes or full card details in response to unsolicited messages or calls.

Questions people ask

Is paying the minimum payment enough?

It may keep an account current if paid on time, but the remaining balance can continue to accrue interest. Review your statement and repayment options.

Do credit card rewards make borrowing worthwhile?

Rewards do not automatically offset interest, fees or overspending. Avoid carrying a balance just to earn rewards.

What should I do if I see an unfamiliar transaction?

Contact your card issuer promptly using the phone number or secure contact method on its official website or your card statement. Follow its instructions to secure the account.

Should I use a credit card for a cash advance?

Check the card agreement first. Cash advances may have separate fees, interest rates and interest-start rules.
