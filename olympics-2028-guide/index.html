LA Olympics 2028 Guide

The LA Olympics 2028 are the Games of the XXXIV Olympiad, hosted by Los Angeles from Friday 14 July to Sunday 30 July 2028. The opening ceremony is shared by the LA Memorial Coliseum and SoFi Stadium, and the closing ceremony takes place at the Coliseum. The announced programme lists 36 sports and 353 events.

The Olympic Games are the largest sporting event in the world, held every four years, bringing together athletes from around the globe to compete in dozens of sports. In 2028, the Games come to Los Angeles, California, for the Games of the XXXIV Olympiad.

This guide gives the key facts about the LA Olympics 2028: the dates, the venues for the opening and closing ceremonies, and the size of the announced sports programme. It is a guide to the Games as announced — it has no fixtures, no team lists, and no ticket information.

The opening ceremony

The LA Olympics 2028 open on Friday 14 July 2028 with an opening ceremony shared by two stadiums: the LA Memorial Coliseum and SoFi Stadium. The opening ceremony is the formal start of the Games, and it is traditionally one of the most-watched television events in the world.

The ceremony follows the familiar Olympic pattern: the parade of athletes, the lighting of the Olympic flame, and a celebration of the host city's culture. The details of the ceremony itself are announced closer to the Games.

The closing ceremony

The Games close on Sunday 30 July 2028 with the closing ceremony at the LA Memorial Coliseum. The closing ceremony marks the end of the competition, with the handover of the Olympic flag to the next host city and the extinguishing of the flame.

Venues

Two stadiums share the ceremonies of the LA Olympics 2028. The LA Memorial Coliseum hosts the closing ceremony and shares the opening ceremony. SoFi Stadium shares the opening ceremony.

Competition venues across Los Angeles and the surrounding region will host the 36 sports of the programme. The full venue plan is set out by the organisers, and this page does not list individual competition venues.

Sports and events

The announced programme for the LA Olympics 2028 lists 36 sports and 353 events.

The programme covers the full range of Olympic sport: athletics, swimming, gymnastics, team sports, combat sports, cycling, rowing, sailing, and more. The exact list of sports and events is set by the International Olympic Committee and the LA28 organisers, and the programme as announced is what this page reports.

This page does not list fixtures, schedules, or team selections. Those are confirmed closer to the Games, and the official LA28 channels are the right source for them.

The Paralympic Games

After the Olympic Games close, the Paralympic Games take place from Tuesday 15 August to Sunday 27 August 2028. The Paralympics bring together para athletes from around the world for two weeks of competition, and they are held in the same host city, using many of the same venues.

The torch relay

Before the Games open, the Olympic flame is lit in Olympia in Greece, the site of the ancient Games, and carried by torchbearers on a relay to the host city. The relay passes through towns and cities along the way, and the flame is carried into the stadium at the opening ceremony to light the Olympic cauldron.

The torch relay is one of the most recognisable traditions of the Olympic Games. It connects the modern Games to their ancient roots, and it brings the Games to people who will never sit in a stadium. The route of the 2028 relay is announced by the organisers closer to the Games.

What the Olympics are

The Olympic Games are held every four years, and they bring together athletes from around the world to compete in a wide range of sports. The modern Games were inspired by the ancient Olympic Games held in Olympia in Greece.

The Games are built around three values: excellence, respect, and friendship. Athletes compete for gold, silver, and bronze medals, and the Games are as much about the meeting of nations as they are about the competition itself. The Olympic motto is "Faster, Higher, Stronger — Together", and it captures the spirit of the Games: the pursuit of the best in sport, shared by all.

For the host city, the Games are a huge undertaking — years of preparation, new and upgraded venues, and millions of visitors. For the athletes, the Games are the pinnacle of their sport, the event they train towards for years. For everyone else, they are two weeks of sport at its very best.
