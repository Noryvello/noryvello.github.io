Battery Backup Time Calculator

Enter your battery capacity, the load in watts and the efficiency, and this calculator estimates how long the battery will last. It works for power banks and UPS units alike, and shows the working so you can see exactly how the runtime is calculated.

Efficiency is the share of stored energy that actually reaches your device. 85% is a typical value — lower it for an older battery, raise it if your setup is known to be efficient.

This is an estimate. Real runtime varies with battery age, temperature, changes in load and the true efficiency of your battery and circuits. Never drain a battery fully if you can avoid it — most devices shut down before the battery is empty, so treat the result as an upper estimate and plan with margin.

How the maths works

Runtime comes from the energy stored in the battery, divided by how fast the load uses it. The calculator works in watt-hours (Wh), which measure energy, so a capacity given in mAh is converted first:

Wh = mAh × 3.7 ÷ 1000

The conversion uses 3.7 V, the nominal voltage of a typical lithium battery cell. Then the efficiency factor is applied, because no battery delivers all of its stored energy to the load — some is lost as heat:

runtime (hours) = Wh × (efficiency ÷ 100) ÷ load (watts)

The result is shown in hours and minutes, with minutes rounded to the nearest whole minute.

Worked examples

Example 1 — 20000 mAh power bank, 10 W load, 85% efficiency. Convert to watt-hours: 20000 × 3.7 ÷ 1000 = 74 Wh. Runtime: 74 × 0.85 ÷ 10 = 6.29 hours = 6 hours 17 minutes.

Example 2 — 500 Wh battery, 100 W load, 85% efficiency. No conversion needed: the capacity is already in watt-hours. Runtime: 500 × 0.85 ÷ 100 = 4.25 hours = 4 hours 15 minutes.

Example 3 — 5000 mAh battery, 15 W load, 85% efficiency. Convert to watt-hours: 5000 × 3.7 ÷ 1000 = 18.5 Wh. Runtime: 18.5 × 0.85 ÷ 15 = 1.05 hours = 1 hour 3 minutes.

Power banks and UPS units

The same maths works for both, because both are batteries delivering power to a load.

  • Power banks are usually labelled in mAh. Pick the mAh unit, enter the labelled capacity and the power draw of the device you are charging. A phone charging at 10 W from a 20000 mAh power bank lasts about 6 hours 17 minutes at 85% efficiency.
  • UPS units are usually labelled in Wh, or in volt-amp-hours — check the label for the battery's watt-hours. Enter the total power draw of the equipment the UPS protects: a router and a monitor might draw 40 W together, while a desktop computer can draw several hundred watts.
  • Higher load, shorter runtime. Runtime falls in direct proportion to the load: doubling the watts halves the time. If you need longer backup, reduce the load first — switch off what you do not need.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between mAh and Wh?

mAh (milliamp-hours) measures charge, while Wh (watt-hours) measures energy. To compare batteries you need Wh, because energy also depends on voltage. This calculator converts mAh to Wh at 3.7 V, the nominal voltage of a typical lithium battery cell: Wh equals mAh times 3.7 divided by 1000.

Why does efficiency matter?

No battery delivers all of its stored energy to the load. Some is lost as heat in the battery itself and in the power conversion circuits. The efficiency factor accounts for that loss: at 85% efficiency, only 85% of the stored energy reaches your device. Lower efficiency means a shorter runtime.

Can I use this for a UPS?

Yes. A UPS is a battery with an inverter, so the same maths applies. Enter the UPS battery capacity and the total power draw of the equipment it powers, in watts. The result is the estimated time the UPS keeps that equipment running during a power cut.

Why is the real runtime shorter than this?

The calculator gives a best-case estimate at a steady load. In real use, batteries lose capacity with age, cold weather reduces performance, the load often varies, and most devices shut down before the battery is fully empty. Treat the result as an upper estimate and plan with margin.
