The Year of the Earth Monkey

Chinese New Year 2028 opens the Year of the Earth Monkey. The Chinese calendar runs on a twelve-year animal cycle, and each year takes the name of one of twelve animals in turn. The Monkey is one of the twelve, and it returns every twelve years. Each year also pairs its animal with an element, and in 2028 the Monkey is paired with Earth, which gives the year its full name: the Year of the Earth Monkey.

The animal cycle is a way of naming years, and it is part of the festival's tradition. This page sticks to the date and the festival itself: it carries no horoscope content, no fortune telling and no claims about personality.

What the Spring Festival is

Chinese New Year is also known as the Spring Festival, and it is the most important festival in the Chinese calendar. At its heart is family reunion: people travel home to share the new year meal with their families, and the festival is a time for coming together after time apart. Homes are cleaned and decorated in red, the colour of good fortune, and the streets fill with lanterns, fireworks and lion dances.

The celebrations run for days, with the new year day itself at the centre. Elders give red envelopes to younger family members, tables fill with dishes that carry good wishes for the year ahead, and the old year is seen out with noise and light. The festival closes with the Lantern Festival, when lanterns are lit and carried through the streets.

Why the date moves each year

Chinese New Year follows the lunar calendar, so its date on the Gregorian calendar moves from year to year. It always falls in late January or February, but the exact day changes each time. In 2028 it falls on Wednesday 26 January, an early date in the usual range. The countdown above tracks the day, counting down to the start of 26 January local time.
