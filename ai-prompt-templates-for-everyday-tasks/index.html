40 AI Prompt Templates for Everyday Tasks

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

This page gives you forty original prompt templates for everyday tasks, in eight groups of five, from writing and planning to travel, home, and fun. Each template is copy-ready, with square brackets marking the parts you fill in yourself. Copy one, replace the brackets with your own details, and send it.

A good prompt is often the same shape every time, with only the details changing. Once you have that shape written down, you never have to build it from scratch again. That is what a template is: a proven shape with blanks for your details, ready to copy and fill in.

The forty templates below are all original and written for everyday use. They are grouped by task so you can find the one you need quickly: writing, planning, learning, shopping decisions, travel, home, work, and fun. Each one works with any general AI chatbot, and each one can be adjusted to fit your situation.

Keep private information out of your prompts. Before you fill in a template, replace real names, addresses, account details, and other private facts with placeholders or made-up examples. Check any important answer before you act on it, especially facts, figures, and dates.

How to use these templates

Using a template takes three steps. First, pick the template that matches your task. Second, replace every square bracket with your own detail, and delete the brackets themselves. Third, read the finished prompt once to check it makes sense, then send it. If the answer is not quite right, follow up in the same chat and say what to change.

A few habits make templates work better. Be specific inside the brackets, because vague details produce vague answers. If a template asks for a number, give a real one. If it asks for a tone, pick one rather than leaving it open. And when a template works well for you, save your filled-in version in your own prompt library so you can reuse it next time without starting over.

Writing templates

These five templates cover the writing jobs that come up most often: messages, posts, stories, rewrites, and thank-you notes. Each one sets the tone and the length so the answer comes back in the shape you need.

  1. Template 1: Write a [short or long] [email, letter, or message] to [person] about [topic]. Keep the tone [friendly or formal] and keep it under [number] words.
  2. Template 2: Turn these rough notes into a clear [number]-paragraph post about [topic] for [audience]. Keep my main points and cut anything that repeats.
  3. Template 3: Write an opening paragraph for a story about [character] who [situation]. Set the scene in [place] and end the paragraph with a question.
  4. Template 4: Rewrite the paragraph below in a [warm, playful, or serious] tone. Keep the meaning the same and shorten any sentence longer than [number] words.
  5. Template 5: Help me write a thank-you note to [person] for [reason]. Mention [specific detail] and keep it warm, short, and sincere.

Planning templates

Planning templates turn a vague intention into a clear sequence of steps. Use them for days, projects, routines, choices, and goals that feel too big to start.

  1. Template 6: Make a day plan for [day] with these tasks: [list]. Order them by importance, add short breaks, and leave [number] minutes free in the evening.
  2. Template 7: Help me plan a [number]-week project to [goal]. Break it into weekly steps, name the first action for each week, and flag any step that depends on another.
  3. Template 8: Build a morning routine for me. I wake at [time], I need to leave by [time], and I want to fit in [activity]. Keep it realistic and under [number] steps.
  4. Template 9: Compare two options for me: [option A] and [option B]. List the strengths and weaknesses of each, then say which fits someone who values [priority] most.
  5. Template 10: Turn this goal into a checklist: [goal]. Split it into small steps I can finish in [number] minutes each, and put the easiest step first.

Learning templates

These templates turn a chatbot into a patient tutor. They work for new topics, exam revision, language practice, summaries, and getting unstuck without being handed the answer.

  1. Template 11: Explain [topic] to me like I am a complete beginner. Start with a one-sentence definition, then give one everyday example, then ask me two questions to check I understood.
  2. Template 12: Quiz me on [topic]. Ask one question at a time, wait for my answer, tell me if I am right, and explain the correct answer briefly.
  3. Template 13: Make a vocabulary list for [language] about [topic]. Give [number] words with a short example sentence for each, and mark the three most useful ones.
  4. Template 14: Summarize this chapter in [number] bullet points: [paste text]. Keep the main ideas and leave out examples that do not change the meaning.
  5. Template 15: I am stuck on [problem]. Do not give me the answer. Give me one hint, then ask me a question that helps me take the next step.

Shopping decision templates

Shopping templates help you compare options and think before you buy. They are built for careful decisions, not impulse purchases, and they keep the focus on what you actually need.

  1. Template 16: Help me choose between [product A] and [product B] for [use]. Compare them on [feature 1], [feature 2], and [feature 3], and say which fits my needs better.
  2. Template 17: I need to buy [item] for [purpose]. My budget is [amount] in my local currency, and I care most about [priority]. List the features I should check before buying.
  3. Template 18: I am thinking of buying [item]. List five questions I should answer first to decide whether I really need it, and what cheaper alternatives might do the same job.
  4. Template 19: Make a shopping list for [occasion] for [number] people. Group the items by shop section and add quantities for each item.
  5. Template 20: I want to compare [number] options for [item]. Make a table with one row per option and columns for [feature 1], [feature 2], [feature 3], and one honest drawback.

Travel templates

Travel templates cover trip planning, first visits, weekend plans, packing, and messages to hosts. Fill in the place, the dates, and your interests, and the plan shapes itself around them.

  1. Template 21: Plan a [number]-day trip to [place] for [number] people. Include one main activity per day, one free afternoon, and a simple packing list.
  2. Template 22: I am visiting [place] for the first time. Tell me the [number] things a first-time visitor should know, including how to get around and what to avoid.
  3. Template 23: Make a day-by-day plan for a weekend in [place]. I like [interest] and I want to avoid [thing]. Keep each day to three stops or fewer.
  4. Template 24: Help me pack for a [number]-day trip to [place] in [season]. List what to bring, grouped by clothes, documents, and extras, for a [bag size] bag.
  5. Template 25: Write a short message to my host in [place] asking about [question]. Keep it polite, mention my arrival date [date], and ask one follow-up question.

Home templates

Home templates help with cleaning plans, organizing, meals, decluttering, and seasonal upkeep. They break household jobs into steps that are small enough to actually finish.

  1. Template 26: Make a weekly cleaning plan for a home with [number] rooms. Spread the work across [number] days, keep each session under [number] minutes, and put the hardest room first.
  2. Template 27: Help me organize [room]. List the steps in order, starting with sorting, and suggest where to put the three most common clutter items.
  3. Template 28: Plan [number] dinners for this week using [ingredients I have]. Keep each meal under [number] minutes to cook and use the same ingredients twice where you can.
  4. Template 29: I have [space] that is cluttered. Give me a decluttering plan in three rounds: keep, donate, and throw away, with one simple rule for deciding each item.
  5. Template 30: Make a simple home maintenance checklist for [season]. List [number] tasks, say how often each one needs doing, and mark the ones to do first.

Work templates

Work templates cover agendas, meeting notes, customer replies, interview preparation, and status updates. They keep work writing short, clear, and professional.

  1. Template 31: Draft a meeting agenda for [topic] with [number] people. Keep it to [number] items, give each item a time, and end with a list of decisions we need to make.
  2. Template 32: Turn these meeting notes into a summary with action items: [paste notes]. List each action with the person responsible and the deadline.
  3. Template 33: Write a polite reply to a customer who [situation]. Acknowledge the problem, explain what we will do, and offer [solution]. Keep it under [number] words.
  4. Template 34: Help me prepare for an interview for [role]. List [number] likely questions, and for each one give a short hint about what a good answer should include.
  5. Template 35: Write a weekly status update for [project]. Cover what was finished, what is next, and one problem that needs help, in under [number] words.

Fun templates

Fun templates are for game nights, birthday messages, story ideas, movie nights, and surprise parties. They are lighter in tone but just as structured as the rest.

  1. Template 36: Suggest [number] game night ideas for [number] people at home. Mix quiet games and lively ones, and say what each game needs.
  2. Template 37: Write a short birthday message for [person] who loves [interest]. Make it warm and funny, mention [detail], and keep it under [number] words.
  3. Template 38: Invent a story prompt for me: a [character] finds [object] in [place]. Give me the opening line and three possible twists.
  4. Template 39: Plan a movie night for [number] people who like [genre]. Suggest [number] films, a snack idea for each, and one fun question to discuss after.
  5. Template 40: Help me plan a surprise party for [person]. List the steps in order, from the guest list to the day itself, and include one idea to keep the surprise safe.

Tips for better results from any template

A template gives you the shape, but the details you fill in decide the quality of the answer. The single best habit is to be specific inside the brackets. A template filled with real details, real numbers, and a real audience will always beat the same template filled with vague words. If you are unsure what to write in a bracket, write the most concrete version of it you can.

The second habit is to iterate. A template answer is a first draft, and a short follow-up usually improves it a lot. Ask for a shorter version, a simpler version, or a version with a different example. The third habit is to save what works. When a filled-in template gives you a great answer, save it with a short note about what it was for, and you will never have to build it again. Over time, your saved templates become a personal library that fits your life better than any list on the internet.

Questions people ask

How do I use these prompt templates?

Copy a template, replace each square bracket with your own detail, and send it to your AI chatbot. The brackets are the only parts you need to change, and the rest of the wording is ready to use.

Can I change a template after copying it?

Yes, and you should. A template is a starting point, so adjust the tone, the length, or the details until the answers match what you need, then save your edited version for next time.

Do these templates work with any AI chatbot?

Yes. The templates are written in plain language and do not depend on any one chatbot, so they work with any general AI chat tool.

What should I put in the square brackets?

Put your own details in the brackets: names, topics, numbers, dates, and limits. Be specific, because the detail inside the brackets is what makes the answer fit your situation.

What if the answer is not what I wanted?

Follow up in the same chat and say what to change, such as asking for a shorter answer, a different tone, or a new example. Most templates give a better answer on the second try than on the first.
