AC Size Calculator — Which BTU Range Does Your Room Need?

This calculator starts with room area, then adjusts the estimate for strong sunlight and additional occupants. The result is a rough BTU-per-hour planning range, not a professional load calculation.

Honest limit: An oversized unit wastes energy and cools badly; unusual rooms need a professional check.

How This BTU Estimate Is Made

The calculator uses a simple planning baseline of 20 BTU per hour per square foot, applies a 10% sunlight adjustment for strong direct sun, and adds 600 BTU per hour for each occupant beyond two. It displays a range around that estimate to make clear that room conditions vary.

These are simplified assumptions for an initial comparison, not a building-standard sizing method. Insulation, ceiling height, windows, air leakage, climate, appliances, room orientation, and adjoining spaces can materially change cooling load.

When to Get a Proper Cooling-Load Assessment

Ask a qualified HVAC professional to assess rooms with high ceilings, large glass areas, poor insulation, open-plan connections, unusual heat sources, or significant air leakage before selecting equipment.

Questions people ask

What does BTU mean for an air conditioner?

BTU per hour is a measure of cooling capacity. A higher BTU per hour rating means the unit can remove heat at a greater rate.

How does room size affect the AC BTU estimate?

The estimate starts with room floor area. Larger rooms generally require more cooling capacity, while sunlight and occupant adjustments can change the planning result.

Does this calculator replace an HVAC load calculation?

No. It uses simplified assumptions and cannot assess insulation, ceiling height, windows, air leakage, climate, or all heat sources. Unusual rooms need a professional check.

Is a bigger air conditioner always better?

No. An oversized unit can cool too quickly without removing enough humidity and may cycle inefficiently. Choose equipment based on a suitable load assessment.
