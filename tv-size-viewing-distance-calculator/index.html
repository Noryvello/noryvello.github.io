TV Size and Viewing Distance Calculator

The right TV size depends on how far you sit from the screen. Enter your viewing distance and this calculator recommends a screen-size range in inches, using the common 30° to 40° viewing-angle guidance. The working is shown, so you can see exactly how the range is found.

Measure from where your eyes are when you normally sit to the screen.

This is general guidance, not a rule. Preference matters, and the room, the light and where you sit all change what feels right. If you are between sizes, the smaller end of the range is the safer choice for a small room.

How the range is worked out

The method starts from the viewing angle — how much of your field of view the screen fills. For a given angle, the screen width is 2 × viewing distance × tan(angle ÷ 2). For a 16:9 screen, the diagonal is the width × 1.1473, and metres are converted to inches by dividing by 0.0254. The calculator works this out at 30°, the reference angle, and at 40°, and the two results give the recommended range. The 30° to 40° range is common viewing-angle guidance, not a building rule.

Worked examples:

  • 2.5 metres — 30° gives 60.52 inches (61 inches) and 40° gives 82.20 inches (82 inches), so the range is 61–82 inches.
  • 2 metres — 30° gives 48.41 inches (48 inches) and 40° gives 65.76 inches (66 inches), so the range is 48–66 inches.
  • 3 metres — 30° gives 72.62 inches (73 inches) and 40° gives 98.64 inches (99 inches), so the range is 73–99 inches.

Before you buy

  • Measure the space. Check the width and height where the TV will stand, not just the diagonal size. A TV's stand and bezel add to its footprint.
  • A bigger screen is not always better. A screen that is too big for the room can feel uncomfortable to watch, especially if you sit close. The smaller end of the range is often the better fit.
  • Think about where you sit. If you watch from more than one seat, measure from the seat you use most. If the seats are at very different distances, the range will be wide — pick a size that works from the main seat.
  • Check the light. A screen facing a bright window can be hard to watch whatever its size. The room and the light matter as much as the inches.

Questions people ask

Why does the calculator give a range instead of one size?

Because there is no single perfect size. The 30 degree angle is a comfortable reference point, and 40 degrees fills more of your view. Both are common viewing-angle guidance, so the range covers the sizes most people find comfortable. Pick within the range by taste and by how much space you have.

How do I measure my viewing distance?

Measure in a straight line from where your eyes are when you normally sit to the surface of the screen. Measure from your usual seat, not from the back of the sofa, and use the distance you sit at most often.

Does this work for any TV?

The calculation assumes a 16:9 widescreen, which almost all modern TVs are. The screen size is the diagonal measurement in inches, which is how TVs are sold. If your screen has a different shape, the numbers will not match exactly.

What if the recommended size is too big for my space?

Measure the space where the TV will stand and check the TV's width and height, not just its diagonal size. If the range is too big for the space, go with the smaller end of the range or the largest size that fits comfortably.
