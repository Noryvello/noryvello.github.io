Concrete Block Calculator

Enter your wall measurements and pick a block size, and this calculator works out how many concrete blocks you need, including a waste allowance, plus an estimate of the mortar volume. The working is shown so you can check every step.

This calculator estimates for a single-skin wall with 10 mm mortar joints. Load-bearing walls, walls with piers and walls that hold back soil need proper design, so follow local building practice and get professional advice where the wall carries weight.

How to use this calculator

Measure the wall length and height in metres and enter both. Pick the block size closest to the blocks you plan to buy. The picker lists common sizes with their length, height and width in millimetres. Set the waste allowance. Ten percent is the default, and you may want more if the wall has many cuts. Press Calculate to see the block count and the mortar estimate, with the working shown underneath.

How the calculation works

The wall area is the length multiplied by the height. Each block covers its face area plus a 10 mm mortar joint on each side, so the blocks per square metre are one divided by that face area. The total is the wall area multiplied by the blocks per square metre, plus the waste allowance. The mortar volume is the wall volume minus the volume of the blocks themselves, which leaves the volume taken up by the joints.

How to use the result

Order the full block count shown, including the waste allowance, because cutting blocks to fit uses up spares. Check the block size on the pack before ordering, because sizes vary by maker. Use the mortar figure as a rough guide and check it against your supplier's advice for the mix you plan to use. If the wall has openings for doors or windows, subtract their area from the wall area before entering it, or the count will be too high.

Questions people ask

How many blocks cover one square metre?

It depends on the block size. A standard 440 by 215 mm block with 10 mm joints covers about a tenth of a square metre, so about ten blocks cover one square metre. The calculator works this out for the size you pick.

Why is a waste allowance needed?

Cutting blocks to fit around corners and openings, plus the odd broken block, uses up spares. The default allowance is 10 percent, and you can change it.

What does single-skin mean?

A single-skin wall is one block thick. A double-skin wall has two layers and needs about twice as many blocks, so this calculator does not cover it.

Can I use this for a garden wall?

Yes, for a simple freestanding garden wall the maths is the same. A wall that holds back soil is a retaining wall, which needs different design, so see the retaining wall block calculator and get professional advice for tall walls.

Why check the block size?

Block sizes vary by maker and by country. The picker lists common sizes, so check the size printed on the pack and pick the closest option.
