Before you pair

Charge the headphones first, or at least make sure they have enough battery for pairing. Headphones with a nearly flat battery can fail to pair or drop the connection partway through, which looks like a bigger problem than it is. Keep the phone and the headphones close together, within a metre or so, while you pair them. Bluetooth works over a short range, and walls and other wireless devices can interfere.

It also helps to know how your headphones enter pairing mode. On most over-ear headphones, you hold the power button for several seconds until the light flashes. On earbuds, opening the case often starts pairing, or you hold a button on the case. The light usually flashes blue, or blue and red, when the headphones are ready. If the headphones connect to a device you used before instead of entering pairing mode, turn that device's Bluetooth off for a moment.

Pairing step by step

The pairing process is the same in outline on every phone. First, put the headphones in pairing mode, so they are visible to other devices. Then open the Bluetooth settings on your phone and wait for the headphones to appear in the list of nearby devices. Tap the headphone name to connect. The phone may ask you to confirm the pairing, and the headphones may play a short sound when the connection is made.

Once paired, the phone remembers the headphones, and they should connect automatically the next time both are switched on and in range. If they do not connect on their own, open the Bluetooth settings and tap the headphone name to connect by hand. You only need to pair once; after that, connecting is a single tap. If you pair the headphones with a different phone later, the first phone stays paired too, and the headphones will connect to whichever device they find first.

Using headphones with more than one device

Many people use the same headphones with a phone and a laptop, or with two phones. Some headphones support multipoint, which means they can stay connected to two devices at once and switch between them. If yours do, you can listen to music on a laptop and take a call on your phone without re-pairing. Check your headphone manual to see whether multipoint is supported, because not all models have it.

If your headphones do not support multipoint, they can only connect to one device at a time. To switch devices, disconnect from the first device — either by turning its Bluetooth off or by choosing disconnect in its Bluetooth settings — and then connect from the second device. This is not a fault; it is how single-connection headphones work. Forgetting the headphones on a device you no longer use keeps the list tidy and avoids surprise connections.

When you buy new headphones, it is worth pairing them with your main device first and testing a call as well as music, because call quality can differ from music quality. If the headphones have a companion app, the app may offer updates that fix pairing problems, so check for updates once in a while. Keep the headphones charged before a long journey, and carry the charging case for earbuds, because a flat battery away from home is the most common reason headphones stop working.

When pairing keeps failing

If pairing fails repeatedly, start fresh. Remove the headphones from your phone's Bluetooth list by choosing Forget This Device, then reset the headphones using the method in their manual, which usually involves holding a button combination for several seconds. Restart the phone as well. Then try pairing again from the beginning. A fresh start clears out half-finished pairings that can block a new connection.

Also check for interference. A room full of wireless devices, a microwave oven running nearby, or a phone case with metal in it can all weaken the Bluetooth signal. Move to a quieter spot and try again. If the headphones pair with one phone but not another, the problem is more likely in the phone's settings than in the headphones, and clearing the phone's Bluetooth cache or resetting its network settings may help.

These steps cover the usual pairing process on Android phones and iPhones. Exact button presses vary by headphone model, so check your headphone manual if the steps here do not match your model. Never pair headphones with a stranger's device, and ignore pairing requests you did not expect.

Questions people ask

Why are my Bluetooth headphones not showing up on my phone?

The headphones are probably not in pairing mode. Hold the power or pairing button until the light flashes, which means they are ready to pair. Also check that they are not already connected to another device nearby.

How do I put my headphones in pairing mode?

On most headphones, hold the power button for several seconds until the light flashes, or hold the dedicated pairing button. Earbuds often pair when you open the case or hold the button on the case. Check your headphone manual for the exact method.

Can Bluetooth headphones connect to two devices at once?

Some headphones can stay connected to two devices and switch between them, which is called multipoint. Others can only connect to one device at a time and must be disconnected from the first before pairing with a second.

Why do my headphones keep disconnecting?

Low battery, distance from the phone, and interference from other wireless devices are the usual causes. Charge the headphones, keep the phone nearby, and move away from crowded wireless areas such as a busy station.

How do I reset my Bluetooth headphones?

The reset method varies by model. It usually involves holding a button combination for several seconds until the light flashes in a pattern. Check your headphone manual, and remove the headphones from your phone's Bluetooth list before pairing again.
