Speaking Time Calculator

Enter the number of words in your speech or talk and pick a speaking pace, and this calculator works out how long it will take to deliver, in minutes and seconds. The working is shown, so you can see exactly how the time adds up.

The paces are typical ranges: slow 110, average 140 and fast 170 words per minute. If you have timed yourself before, pick "Custom pace" and enter your own words per minute.

This is an estimate. Real delivery varies with pauses, emphasis, audience reaction and nerves, so your actual time will usually be a little longer than the calculator shows. Rehearse with a timer for the real number, especially if your talk has a strict time limit.

How the maths works

The calculator divides your word count by your speaking pace in words per minute:

duration (minutes) = words ÷ words per minute

It then converts the result into minutes and seconds, with seconds rounded to the nearest whole second. The three paces are typical ranges: slow is 110 words per minute, average is 140, and fast is 170. A 500-word talk at the average pace takes 500 ÷ 140 = 3.57 minutes, which is 3 minutes 34 seconds.

Worked examples

Example 1 — 500 words at 140 words per minute. 500 ÷ 140 = 3.57 minutes. 0.57 minutes is 34 seconds, so the talk takes 3 minutes 34 seconds.

Example 2 — 1000 words at 110 words per minute. 1000 ÷ 110 = 9.09 minutes. 0.09 minutes is 5 seconds, so the talk takes 9 minutes 5 seconds.

Example 3 — 300 words at 170 words per minute. 300 ÷ 170 = 1.76 minutes. 0.76 minutes is 46 seconds, so the talk takes 1 minute 46 seconds.

What changes your pace

  • Nerves. Most people speak faster than usual when they are nervous, especially at the start. A talk that runs 3 minutes 34 seconds in practice can easily come in under 3 minutes on stage.
  • Pauses. Pauses for emphasis, for applause or for the audience to laugh add real time. The calculator counts words only, so a talk with many pauses will run longer than the estimate.
  • Emphasis and delivery. Slowing down for key lines, repeating a phrase or waiting for a reaction all stretch the time. A flat read-through is faster than a delivered speech.
  • Reading vs speaking from notes. Reading a script word for word is usually steadier and closer to the estimate. Speaking from notes means you will add, skip and rephrase as you go, so the time is harder to predict.
  • Audience reaction. Laughter, applause and questions all take time. If your talk invites a response, leave room for it in your plan.

Use the estimate to plan, then rehearse out loud with a timer. Time yourself twice: once at your natural pace and once with your pauses included. The second time is the one to trust.

Questions people ask

How fast do people usually speak?

Most speakers deliver about 110 to 170 words per minute. A slow, measured pace is around 110 words per minute, an average conversational pace is about 140, and a fast pace is around 170. These are typical ranges, not rules, and your own pace may sit anywhere inside them.

What if I pause a lot?

Pauses add time that the calculator does not count, because it only divides words by pace. If you pause for emphasis, wait for applause or let the audience laugh, your real time will be longer. Pick the slow pace, or add a minute or two, to leave room for pauses.

Should I use the slow or fast pace?

Use the average pace of 140 words per minute if you are unsure. Choose slow if you speak carefully, read from a script or want pauses for emphasis. Choose fast only if you know you speak quickly. If you have timed yourself before, use the custom pace box with your own words per minute.

How do I count my words?

Most word processors show a word count in the status bar or under the Tools or Review menu. If you wrote your talk by hand, count the words on a few lines, work out the average per line, and multiply by the number of lines. Count every word you plan to say, including the greeting and the closing.
