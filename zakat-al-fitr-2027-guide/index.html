Zakat al-Fitr 2027

Zakat al-Fitr, often called fitrana, is one of the closing duties of Ramadan. It is paid at the end of the month, before the Eid al-Fitr prayer, and it is owed for every person in the household. Because it is tied to the end of Ramadan, its timing follows the moon: Eid al-Fitr in 2027 is expected on Tuesday 9 March, subject to moon sighting, so the last days of Ramadan are the time to make sure it is paid.

What is Zakat al-Fitr?

Zakat al-Fitr, also known as fitrana, is the obligatory charity paid at the end of Ramadan, before the Eid al-Fitr prayer. It is a duty of the month itself: every Muslim who is able to pay it owes it, and it is paid once, at the close of Ramadan, rather than spread across the year.

The obligation is narrated from Ibn Umar (Sahih al-Bukhari 1503 and Sahih Muslim 984), who reported that the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) made Zakat al-Fitr obligatory. It is a separate duty from the annual zakat on wealth, and it follows its own rules for amount, timing, and who pays — the differences are set out in the last section of this page.

The payment is made in food, or in its cash value where that is permitted. The point of paying it before the Eid prayer is that it reaches those who need it in time for Eid itself.

How much is Zakat al-Fitr per person?

The measure is one sa' of staple food per person. A sa' is a traditional measure of volume, and in weight it works out at about 2.5 to 3 kilograms of staple food. The commonly given staples are wheat, rice, dates, and barley — the everyday staple foods of the community giving it.

The amount is per person, not per household. A household of five people owes five measures, one for each person. The food given should be the staple that people in the area actually eat, so rice is the natural choice in a rice-eating community and wheat where wheat is the staple.

Because the measure is defined by food, its weight is the fixed part of the rule. Any cash figure is only ever the local cash value of that food, and it changes from place to place and year to year — which is why this page gives no single cash amount.

Who has to pay Zakat al-Fitr?

Zakat al-Fitr is owed for every person, including children and newborns. There is no age limit: a child born before the Eid prayer is counted, and the duty covers every member of the household.

The head of the household pays for the household. In practice, that means one person works out how many people the household holds — adults, children, and any newborn — and pays one measure for each of them. A household of four, for example, pays four measures; a household of six pays six.

Because the duty is per person, it is worth counting carefully at the end of Ramadan, especially in a household that has grown during the year. A baby born during Ramadan is owed for, just like everyone else at the table.

When must Zakat al-Fitr be paid?

Zakat al-Fitr may be paid during Ramadan or 1–2 days before Eid, and it must be paid before the Eid prayer. The window is generous — any time in the last part of Ramadan is fine — but the deadline is firm: the payment must reach its recipients before the Eid prayer begins.

In 2027, Eid al-Fitr is expected on Tuesday 9 March, subject to the sighting of the moon. The date is expected, not confirmed, and it can shift by a day depending on the moon sighting, so follow your local mosque or authority's announcement for the confirmed date in your area.

The deadline matters because of what happens after it. Paid after the Eid prayer, the same gift counts only as ordinary charity (sadaqah), not as Zakat al-Fitr, according to the hadith of Ibn Abbas. The charity is still good — but it no longer discharges the duty of Zakat al-Fitr. That is why mosques and charities collect it in the last days of Ramadan, and why paying early in the last week is the safest course.

Can Zakat al-Fitr be paid in cash?

The Hanafi school permits paying the cash value of the food instead of the food itself. In places where the Hanafi position is followed, giving money is an accepted way to pay Zakat al-Fitr, and many mosques and charities collect it in cash and distribute food on the giver's behalf.

Cash rates are set locally each Ramadan. There is no universal cash amount, because the cash value of a measure of staple food differs from country to country and from year to year. Readers should use the rate their own local mosque or charity announces for 2027 — that announced rate is the figure that applies where they live.

As an example only, and for the UK alone: UK charities announced a rate of around 5 pounds per person. That figure is a UK-only example. It is not a worldwide amount, and it does not apply outside the UK. If you live elsewhere, ignore it and use your own local mosque or charity's announced 2027 rate.

Not every school of thought permits cash payment, so if you follow a school that requires food, give food. When in doubt, ask your local scholars or your mosque which method applies to you.

Zakat al-Fitr and annual zakat: how they differ

Zakat al-Fitr and annual zakat are two different duties, and it helps to keep them apart. Zakat al-Fitr is paid once, at the end of Ramadan, before the Eid prayer, and it is measured in food — one sa' per person, about 2.5 to 3 kilograms. It is owed for every person in the household, including children and newborns.

Annual zakat is different in every respect. It is 2.5% of wealth above the nisab threshold, paid once a year, and it is owed by the person who holds the wealth — not per head of household. It is worked out from savings and wealth, not from a measure of food, and its timing follows the lunar year of the wealth itself rather than the end of Ramadan.

The two can both fall due around the same time of year, and some people choose to pay their annual zakat in Ramadan, but one does not replace the other. Zakat al-Fitr must still be paid before the Eid prayer even if annual zakat has already been paid.
