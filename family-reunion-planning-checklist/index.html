This is a general planning checklist, and every family is different. Your venue's own rules, and your family's needs and traditions, come first — treat this page as a starting point, not a set of rules.

How the five phases work

The checklist is split into five phases so the work is spread out instead of landing all at once. The big decisions — the date, the venue and the guest list — come first, six months out. The last phase covers the day itself, from setting up the tables to tidying up together at the end.

How many tables do you need?

Allow 8 guests per table, then divide your guest count by 8 and round up to the next whole table. For example, 50 guests need 7 tables, because 50 divided by 8 is 6.25. Rounding up matters: a part-filled table still needs a table. The headcount helper above does the sum and shows the working.

Questions people ask

How far ahead should I plan a family reunion?

Six months is a good starting point for a larger reunion, especially if guests need to travel. For a small gathering, three months is often enough. The checklist on this page starts at six months out, so you can skip ahead if you have less time.

How many tables do I need for a family reunion?

Divide your guest count by 8 and round up. For example, 50 guests need 7 tables, because 50 divided by 8 is 6.25, and you round up to the next whole table. The headcount helper above does the sum for you.

What should be on a family reunion checklist?

A reunion checklist should cover the date and venue, the guest list and RSVPs, food and supplies, activities for all ages, and a plan for the day itself. This page lists 20 tasks across five phases, from six months out to the day.

How do I keep track of who is coming?

Ask everyone to reply by a set date and keep one list with names, numbers of guests and any dietary needs. A shared message thread or a simple list works well, and a reminder a month before the reunion helps catch anyone who forgot to reply.
