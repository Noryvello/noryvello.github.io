Rent vs Buy Calculator

Short answer: type your numbers below — the calculator compares the total cost of renting against the true cost of buying over the years you actually stay, and tells you which costs less on those numbers.

Information only — not financial advice. The comparison holds the home price flat, because no one can predict prices. Selling costs, fees, tax rules and rate changes are not included. Change every assumption to match your own situation before deciding anything.

How the comparison works

  • Renting cost = all rent paid over the years you stay, with rent rising each year by the percentage you set.
  • Buying cash out = down payment + all monthly loan payments + all extra yearly costs.
  • Equity left = home price − remaining loan balance (price held flat, as stated).
  • True buying cost = buying cash out − equity left. That is the fair number to compare against rent, because the equity is money you still hold in the home.

Worked example

Rent 1,200 a month rising 3% a year; home price 300,000; down payment 60,000; loan 6.5% over 30 years; stay 5 years; extra costs 3,000 a year:

  1. Monthly loan payment: 1,516.96. After 5 years the remaining balance is 224,666.35.
  2. Total rent over 5 years: 76,451.56.
  3. Buying cash out: 60,000 + 91,017.80 in payments + 15,000 in extra costs = 166,017.80. Minus equity of 75,333.65 → true buying cost 90,684.14.
  4. On these numbers, renting costs 14,232.59 less than buying over 5 years. Try 10 or 15 years in the calculator and watch the answer change — that is the whole point of the comparison.

The math works with any currency — type your numbers in your own currency and the answers come back in the same currency.

Questions people ask

Is renting cheaper than buying?

It depends on the numbers and on how long you stay. Over a short stay, renting usually costs less because buying has a down payment, loan interest, and extra yearly costs. Over a long stay, buying can cost less because each payment builds equity. This calculator compares your own numbers instead of guessing.

What does the rent vs buy calculator include?

Renting: total rent paid, with an annual rent increase you can edit. Buying: down payment, monthly loan payments, and yearly extra costs such as tax, insurance, and maintenance — minus the equity left in the home, assuming the price stays the same.

Does it assume the home price never changes?

Yes, and that is stated on the page. If the home gains value, buying looks better than shown; if it loses value, buying looks worse. No calculator can predict prices, so the honest comparison holds the price flat and says so.
