Diwali 2028 Date

Diwali 2028 falls on Tuesday 17 October 2028, the day of Lakshmi Puja and the heart of the five-day festival of lights. The festival runs from Dhanteras on Sunday 15 October to Bhai Dooj on Thursday 19 October. Dates follow the Hindu lunar calendar and can vary by a day in some regions, so check your local temple or calendar.

Diwali, the festival of lights, is one of the most widely celebrated festivals in the Hindu calendar. Homes are cleaned and decorated, rows of small oil lamps called diyas are lit, and families gather for prayers, sweets, and fireworks. The festival marks the victory of light over darkness and good over evil, and it is celebrated by Hindus, Jains, Sikhs, and many others around the world.

The main day of Diwali 2028 is Tuesday 17 October 2028. It is the third day of a five-day festival, and the day on which Lakshmi Puja is performed in most regions. The five days each have their own meaning and customs, and this page gives the date of each one, explains how the dates are set, and shows how celebrations vary from region to region.

How the date of Diwali is set

Diwali is set by the Hindu lunar calendar. The main day falls on the new moon day, called Amavasya, of the month of Kartika. Because a lunar month is shorter than a calendar month, the Gregorian date moves each year — Diwali usually falls in October or November.

The new moon is the darkest night of the month, which is why the festival of lights falls on it: the lamps are lit against the darkest night. The other four days of the festival are fixed around the main day — Dhanteras two days before it, Naraka Chaturdashi the day before, Govardhan Puja the day after, and Bhai Dooj two days after.

Because the lunar calendar is calculated in slightly different ways by different traditions, a small number of regional calendars can place a day differently. The dates on this page are the widely published 2028 dates, and they match the consensus of the major Hindu calendars.

How Diwali is celebrated

Preparations for Diwali usually begin well before the first day. Homes are cleaned and repainted, new clothes are bought, and sweets and snacks are made or ordered. On Dhanteras many families buy something new — often a utensil, a piece of jewellery, or something for the home — as a sign of prosperity for the year ahead.

On the main day, diyas are placed around the house, on windowsills, and along paths, and electric lights are strung across buildings. In the evening, families perform Lakshmi Puja, the worship of Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth and prosperity, and ask for her blessing on the home. Fireworks light up the sky after dark, and sweets are shared with family, friends, and neighbours.

Gift-giving is a big part of the festival. Boxes of sweets, dried fruit, and small gifts are exchanged between families, and many workplaces give gifts to staff. Children look forward to new clothes, sparklers, and the sweets that appear in every house.

Regional variation

Diwali is celebrated across India and the world, but the emphasis varies by region. In much of North India, the main day is Lakshmi Puja, and the story told is the return of Rama to Ayodhya after fourteen years in exile, when the people lit lamps to welcome him home. In South India, the morning of Naraka Chaturdashi carries more weight, and the story told is Krishna's victory over the demon Narakasura.

In Gujarat and parts of western India, the day after Diwali is the start of the new year, and Govardhan Puja is observed with particular enthusiasm. In Nepal, the festival is known as Tihar and runs over five days with its own customs, including days honouring crows, dogs, and cows. Jain families mark the day as the anniversary of Mahavira's attainment of nirvana, and Sikh families mark Bandi Chhor Divas, the release of Guru Hargobind.

Outside India, Diwali is a public holiday in several countries and is celebrated in cities around the world with lights, food festivals, and community gatherings. Wherever it is celebrated, the lamps, the sweets, and the family gathering stay at the centre.

Planning for Diwali 2028

Because the main day falls on a Tuesday in 2028, many families will take the surrounding days off to make a long break of it. Travel around Diwali is busy in India, so anyone planning a trip should book early and expect crowded trains and flights. If you are hosting, the cleaning and decorating usually start a week or two before Dhanteras.

Gifts and sweets are easier to arrange early. Sweet shops are busiest in the last few days before the festival, and popular items sell out. If you are sending gifts to family far away, allow extra time for delivery around the festival.

Fireworks are a joyful part of the night, but they need care. Keep children supervised, keep water nearby, and follow any local rules about where and when fireworks may be used. Pets should be kept indoors during the loudest hours.
