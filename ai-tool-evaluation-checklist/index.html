AI Tool Evaluation Checklist

Every AI tool promises a lot, and the differences only show up when you test them. This checklist covers the four things worth checking before you sign up: what the free tier really gives you, what happens to your data, how good the output is, and how hard it is to leave.

New AI tools appear every week, and they all sound impressive on their own websites. The only way to judge one is to test it against your own work, with a clear list of what to check. This page is that list: sixteen checks in four groups, with a counter to track your progress.

Work through the checklist with one tool at a time, and tick each check only when you have actually tested it. A tool that passes all sixteen is a tool you can trust with real work.

Free-tier checks

Most AI tools offer a free tier, and the free tier is where your test should happen. First, the limits should be stated in plain words: how many uses, how much output, and what happens when you reach the limit. A tool that hides its limits until you hit them is telling you something about how it treats its users.

Second, the free tier should allow enough use for a real test. A handful of tries is not enough to judge a tool; you need to run it against your own real work, several times, before you know whether it helps. Third, a free tier should not demand payment details to start — that is a trial dressed up as a free tier, and it can turn into a charge you did not expect. Finally, know when the limits reset: daily, weekly or monthly, and whether unused allowance carries over.

Privacy questions

Whatever you type or upload into an AI tool goes somewhere, and the privacy policy should say where. Read what happens to your inputs: are they stored, for how long, and who can see them? The most important question is whether your inputs are used to train the tool — many tools do this by default, and some let you opt out.

Check that you can delete your history and your account, and that deleting actually removes your data rather than merely hiding it. And ask whether the tool needs the personal detail it asks for: a writing tool does not need your phone number, and a tool that asks for more than it needs deserves a second look. Never paste private or sensitive material into a tool you have not checked.

Output-quality tests

Test with your own real work, not only with the examples on the tool's own website. The examples are chosen to show the tool at its best; your work will show it as it really is. Run the same task twice and compare the results: a tool that gives wildly different answers to the same question is a tool you cannot rely on.

Check the output for errors against a source you trust. AI tools can produce confident-sounding text that is wrong in the details — wrong dates, wrong names, wrong numbers — and the errors are easy to miss when the writing is smooth. Finally, ask the simplest test of all: is the output good enough that you would show it to someone whose opinion matters? If not, the tool is not ready for your real work.

Lock-in checks

Lock-in is what happens when leaving a tool costs more than staying. Before you invest time in a tool, check that you can export your work in a standard format — plain text, a common document format, or a file another tool can open. If export is only available on a paid tier, or only in the tool's own format, your work is trapped.

Check that you can cancel without losing your past work, and that an alternative tool could take over your workflow if this one changed its terms or shut down. A tool that passes these checks is a tool you chose; a tool that fails them is a tool you are stuck with.

This checklist helps you compare tools, but it cannot judge a tool for you. Test with your own real work, not only with the examples on the tool's own website, and never paste private or sensitive material into a tool you have not checked.

Questions people ask

What should I check first in an AI tool?

Start with the free tier and the privacy policy: what the free tier really allows, and what happens to the text you type in. Those two answers tell you most of what you need to know.

Is a free tier really free?

A genuine free tier costs nothing and asks for no payment details. If a tool demands payment details to start, it is a trial, not a free tier, and it can turn into a charge.

What happens to the text I paste into an AI tool?

It depends on the tool. Some store your inputs, and some use them to train the tool. The privacy policy should say plainly what happens, and whether you can opt out or delete your history.

How do I test output quality fairly?

Use your own real work, run the same task twice, and check the output for errors against a source you trust. Examples on the tool's own website show it at its best, not as it really is.

What is lock-in, and why does it matter?

Lock-in is when leaving a tool costs more than staying, usually because your work cannot be exported in a standard format. Check export, cancellation and alternatives before you invest time in a tool.
