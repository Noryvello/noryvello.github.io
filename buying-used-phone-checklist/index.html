What Should You Check Before Paying for a Used Phone?

Short answer: Before paying, inspect the phone in person, test its main functions, verify it can be reset and activated, and confirm the seller can transfer it without account locks. Do not rely on appearance alone.

What to inspect before payment

  • Identity: Match the model and storage shown in settings with the seller’s description. Check the device identifier through appropriate official channels where available.
  • Physical condition: Look for cracks, bends, moisture indicators where visible, loose buttons and damaged ports.
  • Core functions: Test touch, cameras, audio, charging, connectivity and sensors you rely on.
  • Battery: Review battery-health information if the operating system provides it; this may not be available on every phone.
  • Ownership and activation: Ask the seller to remove their account and any activation or remote-management lock. Complete a reset and setup check before handing over money.
  • Paper trail: Ask for a receipt or written record identifying the device and transaction.

Walk away when testing is blocked

If the seller will not allow basic checks, cannot remove an account lock or pressures you to pay before setup is verified, do not proceed.

Important limit: Test everything before paying; a deal that forbids testing is a warning sign.

Questions people ask

Should I pay before testing a used phone?

No. Complete the key physical, functional, account and setup checks before paying.

How can I check whether a phone is account locked?

Ask the seller to remove their account and device locks, then verify that the phone can be reset and activated during the handover.

Can I rely on the battery health percentage?

It can be useful when available, but not every phone reports battery health in the same way or exposes a percentage.

What if the seller refuses testing?

Treat refusal as a warning sign and do not pay unless you can verify the device and its ownership safely.
