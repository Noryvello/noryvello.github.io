How Do You Write a Complaint Letter That Gets Results?

Short answer: A useful complaint letter is specific, factual and easy to act on: identify the order or service, describe what happened and when, explain the impact, attach relevant evidence, and state the resolution you are requesting.

The five parts of an effective complaint

  1. Identify the issue. Include the product, service, account or reference number needed to locate the matter.
  2. Set out the timeline. State what happened, when it happened and who you contacted. Separate what you know from what you assume.
  3. Explain the impact. Briefly describe the inconvenience, loss of use or other practical effect.
  4. Ask for a specific outcome. Request a repair, replacement, correction, explanation or other realistic next step.
  5. Set a reasonable response date. Give the recipient a clear timeframe and a way to contact you.

Before you send it

  • Check names, dates, amounts and reference numbers.
  • Attach copies of relevant receipts, screenshots, messages or photographs—not irreplaceable originals.
  • Remove unrelated personal information from attachments where possible.
  • Keep the tone firm and respectful; avoid insults, threats or unsupported accusations.
  • Save a copy of the final letter and note how and when it was sent.
A note before you act: Keep copies of everything; this is writing guidance, not legal advice. If your situation involves legal rights or deadlines, consult an appropriately qualified local professional.

Questions people ask

What should the first paragraph of a complaint letter say?

Identify yourself and the product, service or transaction, then state the main problem in one or two clear sentences.

Should I include evidence with a complaint letter?

Yes. Include relevant copies such as receipts, order confirmations, photographs or earlier messages, and refer to each attachment in the letter.

How long should I give a company to respond?

Choose a reasonable date based on the issue and any published process. State the date clearly rather than assuming the recipient's timeframe.
