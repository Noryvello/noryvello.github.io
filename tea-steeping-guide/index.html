Tea Steeping Guide

Every tea needs its own water temperature and steeping time. Pick your tea below and this guide shows the typical time and temperature for it, with plain steps to follow — no guesswork, no bitter cups.

How to steep any tea

The steps are the same for every tea — only the temperature and the time change:

  1. Boil fresh water. Start with fresh cold water and bring it to the boil.
  2. Get the temperature right. For black and herbal tea, use the water just off the boil. For green, white and oolong tea, let the boiled water stand for a few minutes so it cools to the temperature shown.
  3. Pour over the leaves. Use about one teaspoon of loose tea per cup as a starting point, and adjust to taste.
  4. Steep for the time shown. Start timing as soon as the water hits the leaves.
  5. Remove the leaves and pour. Take the leaves out at the end of the time so the tea does not keep getting stronger.

Why temperature matters

Water that is too hot can make green and white tea taste bitter, because it pulls out more of the bitter flavours from the leaves. Water that is too cool leaves black tea weak and flat, because the leaves never give up their full flavour. Herbal tea is the opposite — it needs water at a full boil to draw the flavour out of the dried herbs, roots and fruit. Getting the temperature right is the simplest way to make a better cup.

Questions people ask

How long should I steep black tea?

Steep black tea for 3–5 minutes in water at 95–100°C, just off the boil. Three minutes gives a lighter cup, five minutes a stronger one. Taste it as you go and take the leaves out when it suits you.

Why does my green tea taste bitter?

Green tea turns bitter when the water is too hot or the leaves sit too long. Use water at 70–80°C and steep for only 2–3 minutes. Let freshly boiled water cool for a few minutes before you pour it over the leaves.

Can I steep tea for longer than the time shown?

Yes. The times in this guide are typical ranges, not rules. Steeping longer makes a stronger cup, but it can also turn bitter, especially for green and white tea. Adjust to your own taste.

Do I need a thermometer to make tea?

No. For black and herbal tea, use the water just off the boil. For green, white and oolong tea, let the boiled water stand for a few minutes so it cools down before pouring. A thermometer helps, but it is not needed.
