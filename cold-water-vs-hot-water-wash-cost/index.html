Cold Water vs Hot Water Wash Cost

Enter your loads per week, your machine's power and cycle length, the extra energy used to heat the water, and your energy rates, and this calculator shows the cost per load for cold and hot washes plus the yearly difference. All rates come from you, so the answer matches your own home.

These are estimates from the numbers you enter. Real energy use varies with the machine, the load size and the water temperature, so treat the result as a guide, not a bill. Check your machine manual and your energy bill for the most accurate figures.

How the comparison works

A cold wash uses energy only to run the machine: the motor, the pump and the controls. A hot wash uses that same machine energy, plus the energy needed to heat the water. The calculator works out the machine energy from the power and the cycle length, then adds your extra heating energy for the hot wash.

Each option then gets its own rate, because cold and hot water are not always heated by the same fuel. If your machine runs on electricity and your water heater runs on gas, the two rates will differ. If everything runs on the same supply, enter the same rate in both boxes.

The yearly figures multiply the cost per load by your loads per week and by 52 weeks. That turns a small difference per load into a number you can judge properly: a few cents per wash adds up over a year of laundry.

What changes the answer

The biggest factor is usually the extra heating energy. Heating water takes a lot of energy, so the hotter the wash and the more water the machine uses, the larger the gap between cold and hot. A machine that uses little water keeps the gap smaller, and a short cycle keeps it smaller still.

Your loads per week matter just as much. A household doing two loads a week sees a smaller yearly difference than one doing eight, even with the same machine. That is why the calculator asks for your own number of loads instead of assuming one.

The rates matter too, but only in proportion. Doubling both rates doubles both costs and doubles the difference, while the ratio between cold and hot stays the same. What decides the winner is the energy gap, not the rate.

Reading your result

Look at the cost per load first: it tells you what each wash costs on its own. Then look at the yearly difference, which tells you what the choice is worth over a year. If the difference is small, the choice hardly matters in money terms, and you can pick by what cleans best. If the difference is large, switching everyday loads to cold water is worth considering.

Remember that the result only covers energy. It does not cover detergent, water or wear on the machine, and it does not judge how clean the clothes come out. Many households mix the two: cold washes for everyday loads and hot washes for bedding, towels and heavily soiled items.

Questions people ask

Why does a hot wash cost more than a cold wash?

A hot wash uses the same machine energy as a cold wash, plus the energy needed to heat the water. Heating water takes a lot of energy, so the extra heating energy is usually the biggest part of the hot wash cost.

Where do I find my machine's watts?

Check the rating plate on the back or side of the machine, or the manual. It lists the power in watts. If you only know the energy per load, you can work out the watts from the cycle length instead.

What is the extra heating energy per hot load?

It is the energy your water heater uses to heat the water for one hot wash, in kilowatt-hours. If your machine heats its own water, check the manual for the energy per hot cycle. If you do not know it, enter your best estimate and treat the result as a guide.

Why are there two energy rates?

Cold and hot water may be heated by different fuels. A machine running on electricity and a water heater running on gas have different rates, so the calculator lets you enter a rate for each option.

Does cold water clean as well as hot water?

Modern detergents are made to work in cold water for everyday laundry. Heavily soiled items and bedding may still benefit from a hot wash, so many households mix cold washes for everyday loads with hot washes when they are needed.
