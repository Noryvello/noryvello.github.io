What a screenshot is good for

A screenshot is a picture of whatever is on your screen at that moment. People use screenshots to save a receipt, keep a copy of a message, show someone an error on their computer, or remember a recipe or a map. Because a screenshot captures exactly what you see, it is often the fastest way to share something that is hard to describe in words. It also works when there is no save or share button in the app you are using.

Screenshots are also useful for keeping records. If you book a ticket, pay a bill, or agree to something online, a screenshot of the confirmation page is a handy backup in case the email never arrives. Just remember that a screenshot is only a picture: it does not prove anything on its own, and it can be edited, so keep the original email or receipt as well when something matters.

Where screenshots are saved

Where your screenshot ends up depends on the device. On Windows, pressing the Windows key and Print Screen together saves the image to the Screenshots folder inside your Pictures folder. Pressing Print Screen on its own does not save a file at all — it copies the screen to the clipboard, and you need to paste it into an app such as Paint or a document to keep it. The Snipping Tool also saves to the same Screenshots folder unless you choose somewhere else.

On a Mac, screenshots are saved to the desktop by default, as image files with the date and time in the name. A small thumbnail appears in the corner of the screen for a few seconds after you take one, and clicking it opens the image so you can crop it or mark it up before it is saved. On Android phones and iPhones, screenshots go to the photo app, usually in a Screenshots album, where you can find them alongside your photos.

Editing and sharing a screenshot

Most devices let you crop a screenshot right after taking it, which is useful when you only need part of the screen. On a phone, tap the thumbnail that appears after the capture to open the editor, then drag the corners to crop and tap save. On a computer, open the saved image in your usual photo viewer or editor to crop it. Cropping is worth doing when the screenshot shows private details, such as a notification or an open tab, that you do not want to share.

Before sharing a screenshot, look it over for anything private: names, addresses, account numbers, or messages in the background. What seems harmless on your screen can be readable to someone else once the image is shared. If you need to hide a detail, cover it with a solid shape in the editor rather than blurring it lightly, because light blurring can sometimes be reversed. Then share the image the way you would share any photo.

Troubleshooting screenshots

If nothing happens when you press the keys, check that you are pressing them at the same time. On a Windows laptop, the Print Screen key is often shared with another function, and you may need to hold the Fn key as well. On a phone, press the two buttons together briefly rather than holding them down, because holding them can open a different menu. If the screen flashes or you hear a shutter sound, the screenshot was taken even if you cannot see it yet.

Some apps block screenshots, and the image comes out black. Video apps and banking apps often do this for copyright or privacy reasons, and there is no setting that overrides it. In that case, look for a share or save option inside the app itself. If your screenshots are saving to an unexpected place, check the settings of your screenshot tool or photo app, which usually let you choose the save location.

Another common problem is taking a screenshot at the wrong moment, such as while a menu is open or a notification is covering the screen. Wait for the screen to settle before you capture it, and dismiss any notification first. If you need to capture a menu that disappears when you press keys, use the timed capture option in your screenshot tool if it has one. On Windows, the Snipping Tool lets you delay a capture by a few seconds, which is useful for menus and pop-ups.

These steps use only the built-in screenshot tools on each device, with no software downloads. Some apps block screenshots for privacy or copyright reasons, and those cannot be captured this way. Always check a screenshot for private details before sharing it with anyone.

Questions people ask

Where do screenshots go on a Windows computer?

Pressing the Windows key and Print Screen together saves the screenshot to the Screenshots folder inside Pictures. Pressing Print Screen on its own only copies the screen to the clipboard, so you need to paste it into an app to keep it.

Where do screenshots go on a Mac?

By default, Mac screenshots are saved to the desktop as image files. A small thumbnail appears in the corner of the screen for a few seconds, and clicking it opens the image for editing.

How do I take a screenshot of only part of the screen?

On Windows, press Windows, Shift, and S, then drag to select the area. On a Mac, press Command, Shift, and 4, then drag to select the area. On a phone, take a full screenshot and crop it in the photo app.

Why is my screenshot blank or black?

Some apps and video players block screenshots for copyright or privacy reasons, and the image comes out black. Try a different app, or check whether the app has its own share or save option.

Do I need to install an app to take screenshots?

No. Windows, Mac, Android, and iPhone all have screenshot tools built in. The steps on this page use only those built-in tools, with no downloads.
