How a shower compares with a bath

A short shower usually uses less water than a bath, but the answer depends on your shower and your habits. A shower's water use is its flow rate multiplied by how long you stand under it, so a long shower under a powerful showerhead can easily pass the water in a bath. A bath uses a fixed volume instead: however long you soak, the water you use is the water you put in the tub.

What the numbers depend on

Two things decide how much water a shower uses: flow rate and time. A water-saving showerhead typically delivers far less per minute than an older, high-flow one, and a minute or two either way changes the total more than most people expect. Bath volume varies too, so enter the volume you actually use, not the tub's full capacity. Example: with an example flow rate of 8 litres a minute and an example 8-minute shower, the shower uses 64 litres — about half of an example 120-litre bath.

Thinking about cost

Water is only part of the cost — most of the money in a hot wash goes into heating the water. A shorter shower saves hot water, which saves both water and the energy used to heat it. If you want to see what your household actually pays, learning to read your water bill is a good first step. The same thinking applies to other hot-water jobs, such as washing dishes or doing the laundry.

Habits that cut water use

Small changes add up. Shortening a shower by a couple of minutes, turning the water off while you lather, and fixing a dripping showerhead all cut use without changing how clean you feel. Filling the bath a little less deep has the same effect for baths. The biggest single saving usually comes from time: the clock matters more than anything else.

Who each one suits

Showers suit busy mornings and anyone who wants a quick wash with the least water. Baths suit slow evenings, soaking sore muscles, and bathing small children. Neither is the wrong choice — the right one is the one that fits the moment.

Is a short shower always better than a bath?

Usually, but not always. A very short shower under a low-flow showerhead uses far less water than even a shallow bath — but a long, hot shower under a powerful showerhead can use more than a modest bath.

How can I find my shower's flow rate?

Hold a measuring jug or a marked bucket under the shower for 30 seconds, then double the amount collected to get litres per minute. Repeat it once or twice and take an average.

Questions people ask

How is shower water use calculated?

Multiply the shower flow rate in litres per minute by the shower duration in minutes.

How do I estimate bath water use?

Enter the approximate litres of water in the bath; the tool compares that volume with your shower estimate.

Can a long shower use more water than a bath?

Yes. Shower use rises with both flow rate and duration, so a long shower can exceed the bath volume you enter.

Does this include water used before the shower gets warm?

No. The calculation includes only the flow rate and duration you enter, not water run while waiting for temperature.
