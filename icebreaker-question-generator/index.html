How to use an icebreaker question

Pick the setting that matches your group, read out the question, and let everyone answer in turn. The follow-up prompt helps the conversation keep going — use it when an answer is short or when you want to hear more. One question is usually enough to warm a group up; if the energy is still low, tap "New question" for another one.

Picking the right question for your group

The right icebreaker depends on who is in the room. Work meeting questions suit colleagues who know each other a little, classroom questions suit students and learners, party questions suit relaxed social gatherings, and team-building questions suit groups that work together. Whatever the setting, skip any question that does not fit the people in front of you.

Questions people ask

What is an icebreaker question?

An icebreaker question is a short, easy question asked at the start of a meeting, class or gathering to help people relax and start talking. It gives everyone something light to answer before the main activity begins.

What makes a good icebreaker question?

A good icebreaker question is easy to answer, safe for everyone in the group, and open enough to start a conversation. It should invite a short story or opinion, not a one-word answer, and it should never make anyone uncomfortable.

How do I use icebreakers in a meeting?

Ask one question at the start of the meeting and let everyone answer in turn, or let people answer in pairs. Keep it short — a few minutes is enough — and let anyone pass if they would rather not answer.

What icebreaker questions should I avoid?

Avoid questions about money, health, politics or personal relationships, and anything that could embarrass someone in front of a group. If a question might make someone uncomfortable, skip it and pick another one.
