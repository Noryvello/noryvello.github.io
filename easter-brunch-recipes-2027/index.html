Easter Brunch Recipes 2027

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

Easter Sunday in 2027 falls on 28 March, and brunch is the perfect way to celebrate it. This page gives you egg dishes, pastries, and drinks for the table, plus a full brunch menu with a timing plan for the morning.

Brunch suits Easter Sunday perfectly. It is late enough for a lie-in and an egg hunt, early enough to leave the afternoon free, and relaxed enough for the whole family to gather around one table. A good Easter brunch mixes sweet and savoury, hot and cold, and something for everyone, from the smallest child to the grandparents.

The secret to a calm brunch is planning. Choose dishes that can be prepared ahead, set the table the night before, and write a short timing plan for the morning. This page gives you recipes for the table, ideas for drinks, and a full menu with timing, so you can enjoy the morning instead of spending it in the kitchen.

Ask guests about allergies before you cook, and label dishes that contain nuts, gluten, dairy, or eggs. Ovens vary, so check that egg dishes and baked goods are cooked right through before serving, and keep hot food hot and cold food cold.

Planning the Easter brunch menu

A good brunch menu has balance: something with eggs, something baked, something fresh, and something sweet. Start with the main dish, usually an egg dish, and build around it. Add a pastry or bread, a fruit dish, and a couple of drinks, and you have a table that feels generous without a dozen recipes.

Think about your guests before you choose. Children like simple, familiar food, while adults may enjoy something a little more special. Offer at least one dish without meat and one without common allergens, and keep the flavours gentle: brunch is a morning meal, and heavy spice is rarely welcome. Write your menu down and shop from a list, so nothing is forgotten.

Egg dishes for the table

Eggs belong at Easter, and a baked egg dish is the easiest way to serve them to a crowd. A simple frittata, made with eggs, milk, cheese, and whatever vegetables you have, can be baked in a large dish and cut into slices at the table. It can be prepared the night before and baked in the morning, which frees you to enjoy the egg hunt.

Scrambled eggs are another good choice for a crowd: cook them gently in a large pan, in batches if needed, and keep them warm while you serve. For something special, try baked eggs in individual dishes with cream and herbs, or a simple omelette bar where guests choose their own fillings. Whatever you choose, cook the eggs gently and serve them promptly, while they are soft and warm.

Boiled eggs deserve a mention too, especially at Easter. A bowl of warm boiled eggs, served in their shells with buttered toast soldiers, is simple, loved by children, and easy to scale up for a crowd. If you dyed eggs with the children the day before, serve those at the table: they are decoration and breakfast in one.

Pastries and breads

Fresh bread and pastries make a brunch table feel like a celebration. Hot cross buns are the classic Easter choice: buy them ready-made or bake them the day before, then warm them gently and serve with butter. Croissants, Danish pastries, and cinnamon rolls are all good alternatives, and a loaf of good bread with butter and jam is never out of place.

If you enjoy baking, a simple fruit loaf or a tray of scones can be made the day before and warmed in the morning. Serve pastries in a basket lined with a clean cloth, and keep butter and jam on the table so guests can help themselves. A little baking goes a long way: the smell of warm bread is part of the celebration.

Fresh and light dishes

Fresh dishes balance the richness of eggs and pastries. A large fruit salad, made with whatever is in season, is easy to prepare and looks beautiful in a glass bowl. Add a platter of sliced melon, berries, and grapes, and a bowl of yoghurt with honey and granola for guests who want something lighter.

A green salad with a simple dressing works well too, especially if you are serving a richer main dish. Keep the fresh dishes simple and let the ingredients speak: good fruit, fresh herbs, and a little lemon are all you need. Prepare fruit the night before and keep it covered in the fridge, so the morning is easy.

Drinks for brunch

Drinks deserve a little thought at brunch. Tea and coffee are essential, so make sure you have enough of both, with milk and sugar on the table. Add a jug of fresh juice, such as orange or apple, and a jug of water with lemon or mint. Children enjoy a special drink too: a fruit smoothie or a sparkling fruit punch feels festive.

If you want one special drink for the day, try a fruit spritzer: sparkling water with fresh fruit and a little juice, served in a large jug with ice. Keep drinks on a side table rather than the main table, so guests can help themselves without crowding the food. A pot of hot chocolate is a lovely touch for children on a cool spring morning.

Keep a jug of iced water on the table as well, with slices of lemon or cucumber. It costs nothing, it suits everyone, and it means nobody has to ask for a drink. Refill the jugs before you sit down to eat, so the table looks after itself while you enjoy the meal.

A full brunch menu with timing

Here is a full menu for a family brunch, with a timing plan for Easter Sunday morning. The menu serves a family table: a baked frittata, hot cross buns and pastries, a fruit salad, yoghurt with honey, and tea, coffee, and juice. The plan assumes brunch is served at eleven in the morning, after the egg hunt.

The night before: prepare the fruit salad and keep it covered in the fridge, set the table, and lay out plates and cutlery. At nine in the morning, put the frittata together and slide it into the oven. At half past nine, warm the pastries and buns. At ten, set out the fruit, yoghurt, and drinks, and make the tea and coffee. At eleven, serve the frittata from the oven and sit down to eat.

The plan is flexible: if the egg hunt runs long, the frittata will wait in a warm oven, and the cold dishes are already on the table. The point of a timing plan is not to rush the morning, but to make sure everything is ready when you sit down.

Make-ahead tips and leftovers

Almost everything on a brunch table can be prepared ahead. Fruit can be cut the night before, pastries can be bought or baked in advance, and egg dishes can be assembled and kept in the fridge until morning. Set the table the night before too, so the morning is free for cooking and for the children.

Leftovers are part of the pleasure of brunch. Leftover frittata is good cold for lunch, fruit salad keeps well for a day, and pastries can be warmed the next morning. Pack leftovers into clean containers and refrigerate them promptly, and do not leave food sitting out for hours. A calm host and a table full of food are what make an Easter brunch memorable.

Questions people ask

When is Easter in 2027?

Easter Sunday in 2027 falls on 28 March. Good Friday is 26 March and Ash Wednesday is 10 February. Brunch is usually served late on Easter Sunday morning.

What time should Easter brunch start?

Brunch is usually served late in the morning, often around eleven, after the egg hunt and any church service. A timing plan helps: prepare cold dishes the night before and cook hot dishes in the morning.

What should I serve for Easter brunch?

A good brunch has an egg dish, pastries or bread, something fresh such as fruit salad, and drinks. A baked frittata, hot cross buns, fruit, yoghurt, and tea and coffee make a full and balanced table.

Can I prepare brunch dishes the night before?

Yes, most dishes can be prepared ahead. Cut fruit and keep it covered in the fridge, assemble egg dishes ready to bake, and set the table the night before. Warm pastries and bake egg dishes in the morning.

How do I feed a crowd at brunch?

Choose dishes that serve many from one tray, such as a baked frittata, and set food in the middle of the table so guests can help themselves. Prepare extra bread, fruit, and drinks, which are easy to top up.

What drinks go well with Easter brunch?

Tea, coffee, fresh juice, and water with lemon cover most guests. A fruit spritzer or a smoothie adds a festive touch, and hot chocolate is a lovely option for children on a cool spring morning.
