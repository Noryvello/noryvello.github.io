What do Ramadan Mubarak and Ramadan Kareem mean?

"Ramadan Mubarak" means "Blessed Ramadan" — a wish that the month brings blessings to the person you are greeting. "Ramadan Kareem" means "Generous Ramadan" — a wish that the month is generous to them, with mercy, provision, and good things. Both greetings are widely used, and neither is more correct than the other. "Ramadan Mubarak" is the more common of the two in most places, while "Ramadan Kareem" is heard often in parts of the Arab world. You can use either one, to anyone, at any point in the month.

If someone greets you first, a simple "Ramadan Mubarak to you too" is a perfect reply — there is no special formula to remember. The greetings work in a text, on a card, at the start of a call, or face to face after prayer. What matters is not which phrase you choose, but that the wish is sincere. A greeting sent with genuine warmth, even a short one, is worth more than a long message sent out of duty.

When to send Ramadan greetings

The best time to send a Ramadan greeting is just before the month begins, so your message arrives as the first fast approaches. In 2027, Ramadan is expected to begin on Monday 8 February, subject to moon sighting, so the days leading up to that date are the natural moment. But a greeting is welcome at any point through the month — at the first iftar, in the middle of the month, or as the last ten nights begin. If you missed the start, send one anyway: a late greeting is still a kind one.

How to personalise a Ramadan message

A copied message is a fine start, but a personal line is what makes it land. Start with the person's name — a message that begins with a name already feels written for one person, not for everyone. Then mention something specific: their family, a shared memory from last Ramadan, something they are hoping for this year. You do not need many words; one true sentence is enough.

Keep it sincere and keep it short — a greeting is not the place for a long letter. And if you are writing to someone whose Ramadan is hard this year — far from family, unwell, or grieving — a gentle line matters more than a cheerful one. "I am thinking of you this Ramadan" says everything it needs to.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between Ramadan Mubarak and Ramadan Kareem?

Ramadan Mubarak means Blessed Ramadan and Ramadan Kareem means Generous Ramadan. Both are common greetings, neither is more correct than the other, and you can use either one to anyone at any point in the month.

What do I write in a Ramadan card?

Start with a short greeting such as Ramadan Mubarak, add one personal line with the person's name or something specific to them, and finish with a warm wish. Keep it sincere and short — one true sentence is worth more than a long generic message.

Is it OK to send Ramadan wishes to a colleague?

Yes. A short, respectful message is entirely appropriate, and colleagues usually appreciate being remembered. Keep it warm and professional, and the formal examples on this page are a good starting point.
