How the maths works

CPM means "cost per mille" — the amount advertisers pay per 1,000 ad views. The estimate is:

Estimated earnings = monthly views ÷ 1,000 × CPM

Worked example: 100,000 monthly views with a CPM of 4 gives 100,000 ÷ 1,000 × 4 = 400. At 1,000,000 views with a CPM of 2, the estimate is 1,000,000 ÷ 1,000 × 2 = 2,000. The per-video figure divides the monthly estimate by the number of videos you publish; the yearly figure multiplies the monthly estimate by 12 at the same pace.

CPM vs RPM — why the two numbers differ

CPM is what advertisers pay. RPM (revenue per 1,000 views) is what you actually receive per 1,000 views, and it is always lower, because the platform takes its share before you are paid, and because not every view carries a monetised ad. If your analytics show an RPM, enter that figure in the CPM box for an estimate closer to real pay.

YouTube vs TikTok

On YouTube, long-form videos earn through ads shown around your videos, while Shorts pay through a different revenue pool — Shorts views are generally worth far less per view than long-form views. On TikTok, creator payouts run through TikTok's creator rewards system rather than the same CPM auction as YouTube. For TikTok, treat the CPM box as your own tested per-1,000-views figure from TikTok analytics. This calculator never states a rate for either platform, because rates are not fixed facts.

Why estimates vary so much

  • Niche: finance, technology and business audiences attract higher-paying advertisers than general entertainment.
  • Audience country: advertisers pay different rates in different countries, so the same view count earns differently depending on where viewers live.
  • Season: advertising demand rises around holidays and shopping events, and falls in quiet months.
  • Format: long-form, Shorts, live streams and embedded views monetise differently.
  • Monetised-view rate: ad blockers, Premium viewers, age-restricted content and region restrictions all reduce the share of views that earn.

Questions people ask

How is YouTube revenue calculated?

The basic estimate is monthly views divided by 1,000, multiplied by CPM — the amount advertisers pay per 1,000 ad views. What a creator actually receives is measured by RPM, revenue per 1,000 views, which is lower than CPM because the platform takes its share, and because not every view shows a monetised ad. This calculator estimates from the CPM you enter; your analytics RPM is the figure closest to real pay.

What CPM should I enter in the calculator?

Enter your own figure from your channel analytics — this page never states a CPM as fact, because real CPMs vary widely by niche, audience country, season and video format. If you have no analytics yet, run the calculator with several different CPM values to see a range of outcomes instead of trusting any single number.

Does this calculator work for TikTok?

Yes, as an estimator. TikTok creator payouts work differently from YouTube ads — TikTok pays through its creator rewards system rather than the same CPM auction — so for TikTok, enter your own per-1,000-views earnings figure from your TikTok analytics instead of a YouTube CPM. The maths is the same: views divided by 1,000, multiplied by your figure.

Why is my real pay lower than the estimate?

The estimate assumes every view is monetised at the full CPM you entered. In reality the platform takes its share before you are paid, some viewers use ad blockers, some views are not monetised, Shorts pay through a different pool than long-form videos, and your audience's countries and the season change what advertisers pay. That is why RPM in your analytics is always lower than CPM.
