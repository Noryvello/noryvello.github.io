What SMART goals mean

SMART is a simple way to check a goal before you commit to it. It stands for Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. Specific means you know exactly what you will do; Measurable means you can tell when it is done; Achievable means it fits your time and resources; Relevant means it matters to you; Time-bound means it has a deadline.

For example, instead of "read more", a SMART goal is "read one chapter every evening and finish the book by 30 June". It says what you will do, how often, and when it will be done — and it fits neatly into this worksheet.

How to use this worksheet

Fill in each field in your own words, starting with the goal itself. Break the goal into three milestones — smaller steps in the order you will do them — then write the very first step, the one you can take today. Pick a check-in day, then print your plan and keep it somewhere you will see it.

Questions people ask

What is a goal setting worksheet?

A goal setting worksheet is a simple form that turns a vague wish into a clear plan. You write down your goal, why it matters, a deadline, three milestones and a first step, so the whole plan fits on one page.

What are SMART goals?

SMART is a way of checking a goal: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. A SMART goal says exactly what you will do, how you will know it is done, and when it is due.

How do I set a realistic goal?

Start with something you can actually fit into your week, then break it into small milestones. If a goal feels too big, shrink the first step until it feels easy to start, and adjust the plan as you go.

How often should I check in on my goal?

Once a week is a good rhythm for most goals: pick one day, look at your milestones, and decide the next small step. This worksheet lets you choose your own check-in day.
