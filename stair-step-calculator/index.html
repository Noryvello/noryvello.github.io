Stair Step Calculator — How Many Steps, and How High Is Each Riser?

Choose a practical riser count by dividing the total rise by a planning target, then calculate equal riser height by dividing total rise by that count. Divide the total horizontal going by the number of tread intervals to estimate tread depth.

Honest limit: Stair dimensions are regulated in most places — check local building codes before building.

How Stair Rise and Going Are Calculated

  1. Riser count: total rise ÷ target riser height, rounded to the nearest whole number, with at least one riser.
  2. Actual riser height: total rise ÷ riser count.
  3. Tread depth estimate: total horizontal going ÷ (riser count − 1), when there is more than one riser and the top floor acts as the final landing.

For a one-riser result, tread depth cannot be calculated from this layout assumption. Stair configurations differ, and landings, nosings, floor finishes, winders, headroom, handrails, and measurement conventions all matter.

Worked Example

For a 108-inch total rise and a 7.5-inch planning target, the calculator selects 14 risers. Each equal riser is 108 ÷ 14 = about 7.71 inches. With 120 inches of total horizontal going and 13 tread intervals, the average tread depth is about 9.23 inches. These figures are not a code approval.

Questions people ask

How many risers should a staircase have?

A planning estimate divides total vertical rise by a chosen target riser height and rounds to a whole number. The final count and dimensions must satisfy the applicable local building code.

How do I calculate stair riser height?

Divide the measured total vertical rise by the number of equal risers. Measure finished floor to finished floor and account for finish thickness in the final design.

Why are there usually fewer treads than risers?

In a common straight stair that ends at an upper floor, the upper floor serves as the final walking surface, so the number of separate tread intervals can be one less than the number of risers. Landings and other layouts differ.

Can I build stairs using this calculator alone?

No. It provides arithmetic estimates only. Stair dimensions, headroom, handrails, landings, guards, and construction details are regulated in many places and require a code-compliant design.
