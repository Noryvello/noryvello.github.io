Hiking Time Calculator

Enter the distance of your hike and the total elevation gain, and this calculator estimates your walking time using Naismith's rule: 5 km per hour on the flat, plus 1 hour for every 600 m of climbing. The working is shown, so you can see exactly how the time adds up.

Leave the elevation gain at 0 for a flat walk. The time shown is walking time only — breaks are not included.

This is an estimate for average fitness on decent paths, not a promise. Your fitness, the weather, the state of the path and the weight of your pack all change the real time. Breaks are not included, so add your own break time and plan with margin, especially on a route you have never walked before.

How the maths works

Naismith's rule estimates walking time from two numbers: the distance and the total climb. It allows 5 km per hour on flat ground, and adds 1 hour for every 600 m of ascent:

time (hours) = distance ÷ 5 + ascent ÷ 600

The two parts are worked out separately and added together. A flat 10 km walk takes 10 ÷ 5 = 2 hours. Add 600 m of climbing and the rule adds 600 ÷ 600 = 1 hour, giving 3 hours in total. The calculator converts the total into hours and minutes, with minutes rounded to the nearest whole minute.

Worked examples

Example 1 — 10 km, 600 m of ascent. Flat walking: 10 ÷ 5 = 2.00 hours. Climbing: 600 ÷ 600 = 1.00 hours. Total: 2.00 + 1.00 = 3.00 hours = 3 hours 0 minutes.

Example 2 — 8 km, 300 m of ascent. Flat walking: 8 ÷ 5 = 1.60 hours. Climbing: 300 ÷ 600 = 0.50 hours. Total: 1.60 + 0.50 = 2.10 hours = 2 hours 6 minutes.

Example 3 — 15 km, 900 m of ascent. Flat walking: 15 ÷ 5 = 3.00 hours. Climbing: 900 ÷ 600 = 1.50 hours. Total: 3.00 + 1.50 = 4.50 hours = 4 hours 30 minutes.

What the estimate assumes

  • Average fitness. The rule is pitched at a steady walker, not a runner and not someone out for a slow stroll. Faster walkers will beat it; slower walkers, or a group with children, will take longer.
  • Decent paths. It assumes you are walking on paths you can keep a steady pace on. Mud, loose rock, thick forest or scrambling all slow you down.
  • No breaks. The time is pure walking time. Stops for food, water, photos and rests come on top — add your own break time when you plan the day.
  • Climb only. The rule counts the total ascent. Long descents are not added separately, though steep downhills can be slow too, so allow extra time on routes with a big descent.

Use the estimate as a starting point. If the route is new to you, or the weather looks changeable, add margin — it is better to finish early than to be caught out by darkness.

Questions people ask

What is Naismith's rule?

Naismith's rule is a simple way to estimate walking time. It allows 5 km per hour on flat ground, plus 1 hour for every 600 m of climbing. So a 10 km walk with 600 m of ascent takes about 3 hours: 2 hours for the distance and 1 hour for the climb.

Does it work for very steep hills?

It gets less accurate on very steep or rough ground. The rule assumes decent paths and average fitness, so on steep climbs, muddy tracks or scrambling sections, allow extra time beyond what the calculator shows.

Does the time include breaks?

No. The calculator gives walking time only. Add your own break time on top: a short rest every hour or two, plus time for lunch, photos and looking at the view, all add to the length of your day.

How accurate is the estimate?

It is a planning estimate, not a promise. Fitness, weather, path quality, pack weight and how often you stop all change the real time. Use it as a starting point and plan with margin, especially on a route you have never walked before.
