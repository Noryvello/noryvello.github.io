Painting Room Mistakes: 7 Common Errors & Prep Checklist

Short answer: most bad paint jobs come from rushed prep, skipped primer and overloaded rollers. Follow the seven mistakes and the prep checklist below, and use the calculator to estimate paint before you buy.

Estimate only. The calculator gives an estimate from the dimensions and coverage you enter. Real needs vary with surface texture, paint brand and application method — and doors and windows are not subtracted, which leaves a small safety margin.

7 painting mistakes to avoid

  1. Skipping surface cleaning and sanding. Paint peels off dirty or glossy walls.
  2. Wrong paint finish for the room's purpose — kitchens and bathrooms need washable finishes.
  3. Skipping primer on new or repaired walls, where paint soaks in unevenly.
  4. Over-loading the roller, causing drips and uneven texture.
  5. Not allowing drying time between coats — check the can's recoat time.
  6. Neglecting ventilation, leading to strong fumes and uneven drying.
  7. Failing to protect trim, floors and fixtures before painting.

Prep checklist

  • Remove or mask furniture and fixtures.
  • Clean walls with mild detergent; rinse and dry.
  • Repair holes and cracks; sand smooth.
  • Apply appropriate primer where needed.
  • Protect floors with drop cloths.
  • Gather quality brushes, rollers, and painter's tape.
  • Ensure good ventilation and keep windows open.

Questions people ask

How many coats of paint should I apply?

Typically two coats give even coverage; a third may be needed on dark walls.

Do I need a primer on previously painted walls?

Only if the existing paint is glossy, damaged, or a different color family; otherwise a light sanding may suffice.

What is the best way to avoid roller drips?

Load the roller evenly, roll off excess on a grid, and work in sections using a W pattern.

Can I paint over wallpaper?

It is possible, but the wallpaper must be firmly adhered; otherwise it may peel after painting.
