How Much Does a Different Tire Size Change Your Speedometer?

Short answer: Enter both tire sizes in the common width/aspect-ratio/rim format. The calculator estimates diameter and circumference change, then shows the approximate actual speed when the speedometer is calibrated for the first tire.

Enter tire sizes as section width in mm, aspect ratio in percent and wheel diameter in inches.

How tire size is calculated

A tire’s nominal sidewall height is its section width multiplied by its aspect ratio. Overall diameter is twice that sidewall height plus the wheel diameter converted from inches to millimetres. Circumference is estimated from diameter multiplied by pi.

  • The calculation uses nominal size markings, not measurements of a mounted tire.
  • Actual dimensions can vary by tire model, tread, load and inflation.
  • The speed comparison assumes the speedometer was calibrated for the original size and ignores other vehicle factors.
  • Check manufacturer fitment guidance before changing sizes.
Important limit: Changing tire size can affect handling and legality; check your vehicle manual.

Questions people ask

How is overall tire diameter calculated?

The calculator doubles the nominal sidewall height and adds the rim diameter converted to millimetres.

What does a positive diameter difference mean?

A positive difference means the new tire has a larger calculated nominal diameter than the original tire.

How does tire size affect the speedometer estimate?

If the new tire is larger, it travels farther per revolution; the tool estimates actual speed from the diameter ratio.

Are the calculated dimensions exact?

No. They are nominal estimates based on the size code; real tire dimensions vary by model, wear, load and inflation.
