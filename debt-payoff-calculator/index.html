Debt Payoff Calculator

Short answer: type your balance, your interest rate and your monthly payment — the calculator shows how many months until you are debt-free, the total interest you will pay, and what a little extra each month saves.

Information only — not financial advice. The simulation assumes a fixed rate, no new charges, no fees and no missed payments. Real balances change when rates move, fees apply, or new spending is added. This page only shows the arithmetic of the numbers you type.

Worked example

  1. Balance 5,000 at 18%, paying 200 a month: debt-free in 32 months, total interest 1,313.96.
  2. Same balance, paying 250 a month (50 extra): debt-free in 24 months, total interest 989.13.
  3. The extra 50 a month saves 8 months and 324.83 in interest — because every extra payment shrinks the balance that next month's interest is charged on.

The trap to watch: a payment smaller than the interest

If your monthly payment is smaller than one month's interest, the balance grows even while you pay. On a 5,000 balance at 18%, one month's interest is 75 — so any payment of 75 or less never reduces the debt at all. The calculator warns you the moment your numbers fall into that trap.

The math works with any currency — type your numbers in your own currency and the answers come back in the same currency.

Questions people ask

How does the debt payoff calculator work?

It simulates your balance month by month: each month interest is added at one twelfth of the annual rate, then your payment is subtracted. It counts the months until the balance reaches zero and adds up all the interest paid along the way.

What if my payment is smaller than the monthly interest?

Then the balance never falls — each month's interest alone is bigger than the payment, so the debt grows instead of shrinking. The calculator warns you when that happens, and the only fix inside the arithmetic is a larger payment or a lower rate.

Does paying a little extra really help?

Yes, and the calculator shows exactly how much. On a 5,000 balance at 18%, paying 250 a month instead of 200 finishes 8 months sooner and saves about 325 in interest — because every extra payment cuts the balance that future interest is charged on.
