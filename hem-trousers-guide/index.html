How to Hem Trousers

Hemming trousers means shortening the legs to the right length and finishing the raw edge so it does not fray. This guide shows you how to measure, pin and sew a hem by hand or by machine, with a picker for the hem style that fits your trousers.

Trousers rarely come in exactly the right length. New trousers are often sold long so they can be hemmed to fit, and a worn hem can come undone after years of wear. Hemming is one of the most useful sewing skills to learn, because it turns an almost-right pair of trousers into a pair that fits.

The job has three parts: measuring the right length, pinning the fold in place, and sewing the hem. Measuring matters most, because a hem sewn at the wrong length is hard to fix. Take your time with the measuring and pinning, and the sewing itself is straightforward.

Measure twice and cut once. Cutting a leg too short cannot be undone, so pin the hem, try the trousers on with the shoes you will wear, and check both legs before you cut anything. If you are unsure, practise on an old pair first.

Pick a hem style

The hem style depends on the trousers. Pick the one that fits yours to see the steps for it.

Measuring

Put the trousers on with the shoes you will wear with them. The shoe changes where the hem falls, so measuring in bare feet or in different shoes gives the wrong length. Stand straight on a flat floor, with your weight even on both feet.

Fold one leg up to the length you want and pin it in place. The hem should just touch the top of the shoe at the front and fall a little lower at the back, though the right length depends on the style of the trousers and on what you like. Walk a few steps and check the fold in a mirror before you settle on the length.

Measure the pinned leg from the fold to the raw edge, and mark the same length on the other leg with tailor's chalk. Measure both legs separately rather than copying one to the other, because most people stand slightly unevenly and trousers do not always hang the same on both sides. Allow enough fabric below the fold for the hem itself: a deeper hem hangs better and is easier to sew.

Pinning

Take the trousers off and lay them flat on a table. Fold each leg up to the marked line and press the fold with an iron. A pressed fold stays in place while you sew and gives a crisp edge to the finished hem.

Pin the fold at intervals around the leg, with the pins pointing in the same direction so they are easy to pull out as you sew. Check that the fold is even all the way around, with the same depth at the inner seam and the outer seam. An uneven fold shows at the ankle, where everyone can see it.

Turn the trousers right side out and try them on once more before you sew. Check the length in the mirror, walk a few steps, and sit down to check that the hem does not ride up too far. This is the last easy chance to change the length, so take it.

Sewing by hand

Hand sewing suits a blind hem, where the stitches should not show on the outside. Thread a needle with thread that matches the trousers, and knot the end. Work from the inside of the leg, taking small stitches that catch only a few threads of the outer fabric with each stitch.

Keep the stitches even and not too tight. A stitch pulled too tight puckers the fabric and shows on the outside, while a stitch that is too loose lets the hem drop. Aim for stitches that hold the fold firmly but let the fabric lie flat.

Work all the way around the leg, then finish with a few small stitches on top of each other to lock the thread. Try the trousers on before you trim the thread, in case the hem needs adjusting.

Sewing by machine

Machine sewing suits a topstitched hem, where a visible line of stitching is part of the look. Press the fold first, then pin it well, because fabric shifts under the presser foot more than you expect.

Sew close to the folded edge, all the way around the leg, keeping the edge lined up with the same mark on the machine foot. Go slowly around the seams, where the fabric is thicker, and use the hand wheel to walk the needle through the thickest spots rather than forcing it.

Backstitch at the start and the end to lock the thread, then trim the thread ends. Press the hem once more from the inside to flatten the stitching and set the fold.

Pressing and finishing

Pressing is what makes a hem look finished rather than homemade. Press the hem from the inside first, then lightly from the outside with a pressing cloth between the iron and the fabric. The cloth stops the iron from leaving a shine on the fabric.

Trim any loose threads, then try the trousers on and check both legs in a mirror. The hems should sit at the same length, the folds should lie flat, and the stitching should be even all the way around.

If a hem looks uneven, it is usually the pressing rather than the sewing. Press the fold again, easing the fabric into place with your fingers as you go. A good press fixes most small unevenness without any unpicking.

Questions people ask

How long should trousers be?

It depends on the style and the shoes. As a rough guide, the hem should just touch the top of the shoe at the front and fall a little lower at the back. Always measure while wearing the shoes you will wear with the trousers.

Should both legs be the same length?

Measure both legs, because most people stand slightly unevenly and trousers do not always hang the same on both sides. Pin each leg separately and check the length while standing straight.

Can I hem trousers without a sewing machine?

Yes. A blind hem sewn by hand is invisible from the outside and holds well on most fabrics. It takes longer than machine sewing, but the result looks neater on smart trousers.

What if I cut the legs too short?

A leg cut too short cannot be lengthened back to its old length. If only a little was cut, a narrower hem may save some length. Otherwise the trousers may need a facing or a cuff to add length back.

Do I need to finish the raw edge?

Yes, or the edge will fray in the wash. Fold the raw edge under before you sew, or finish it with a zigzag stitch or an overlock stitch if your machine has one.
