Engagement Congratulations Messages

An engagement is wonderful news, and the right message makes it even sweeter. This page offers 25 original engagement messages, grouped by tone: short, warm and formal. Pick one, add a personal line, and share in their happiness.

An engagement is one of the happiest announcements a couple can share, and a warm message makes it even better. The messages below are all original, written for this page.

Choose a tone that fits your relationship with the couple. Short messages suit a quick text, warm messages suit close friends and family, and formal messages suit colleagues and acquaintances.

Keep the focus on the couple and their happiness. Avoid jokes about the wedding, questions about the ring or the cost, and any comment about how long they waited.

How to personalise an engagement message

A personal line makes your congratulations stand out. Mention how you heard the news, a memory of the couple together, or something you admire about their relationship. One detail only you could write is worth more than a long general message.

Keep it warm and simple. You do not need to write a speech or offer advice about marriage. A few sincere lines, sent promptly, say everything that matters.

When to send your congratulations

Send your congratulations as soon as you hear the news. A message on the day of the announcement feels warm and immediate, and the couple will be reading every note.

If you hear about it later, send your message anyway. A late congratulation is always welcome, and you can add that you were delighted to hear the news.

Questions people ask

What is a good short engagement message?

A short message works best when it is warm and direct. Say congratulations, mention how happy you are for them, and wish them well. One or two sentences is enough.

Should I congratulate both people?

Yes. Address the message to the couple, even if you know one of them better. It shows respect for both of them and for the partnership.

Is it okay to send congratulations by text?

Yes, a text is fine for friends and family, especially on the day of the announcement. A card suits a formal congratulation or a colleague.

What should I avoid in an engagement message?

Avoid asking about the ring, the wedding cost or the date. Keep the focus on their happiness and your good wishes for their future.

Do I need to mention the wedding?

No. A congratulation is about the engagement itself. You can look forward to the wedding, but there is no need to ask about plans.
