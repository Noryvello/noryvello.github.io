Wedding Toast Builder

A good wedding toast has four parts: an opening, a story, a wish and a closing. Pick your role, fill in three short prompts, and this builder turns your own words into a 3–5 minute toast outline you can practise out loud.

This page builds an outline from your own words. Practise it out loud, and keep the toast kind, short and about the couple.

How the toast is built

Every toast on this page follows the same four parts. The opening introduces you and your role, so the room knows who is speaking. The story shares one short memory and says what you admire about the couple. The wish looks ahead to their life together. The closing raises a toast to the couple and brings the speech to a clean end.

Length guide: 3–5 minutes

Aim for 3 to 5 minutes in total. As a rough guide, give the opening about 30 seconds, the story 1 to 2 minutes, the wish about a minute, and the closing about 30 seconds. If your toast runs long, cut a second story rather than rushing — one good story is enough.

Delivery tips

  • Speak slowly. Nerves make everyone speed up, so leave a pause between each part.
  • Practise out loud, not just in your head — words that read well can trip you up when spoken.
  • Keep it short. If you are over 5 minutes, cut the weakest line, not the wish.
  • Look up from your notes now and then, and pause before the final toast so the room can raise their glasses.

Questions people ask

How long should a wedding toast be?

Aim for 3 to 5 minutes. That is long enough to tell one short story and raise a toast, and short enough that the room stays with you. If you are unsure, practise with a timer and cut anything that does not add to the story.

What should I say in a wedding toast?

Say who you are and how you know the couple, share one short memory, say what you admire about them, and finish with a wish and a toast. Keep it kind, keep it short, and keep the focus on the couple, not on yourself.

How do I start a wedding toast?

Introduce yourself and your role, then thank the couple for asking you to speak. A simple opening works best: who you are, how you know them, and one warm line about the day. Save the story for the middle of the toast.

What should I avoid in a wedding toast?

Avoid jokes at the couple's expense, stories about past relationships, anything embarrassing, and anything only a few people in the room would understand. If a line could make anyone uncomfortable, leave it out.
