Coffee at Home vs Cafe Cost

The cost of a daily coffee depends on where it is made and how many cups you drink. Enter how many cups you drink a day and the cost per cup at home and at a cafe, and this page shows the weekly and yearly totals for each.

This compares the cup costs you enter only. It leaves out the cost of a coffee machine or equipment, milk and extras bought separately, and the trip to the cafe.

How it works

The calculator works out the cost of one day first: the cups you drink each day times the cost of one cup. Then it multiplies the daily cost by 7 days for the weekly total, and the weekly total by 52 weeks for the yearly total. It does the same for both sides, so the gap between home and cafe is shown for a week and for a year.

The cost of a cup at home is the coffee, milk and anything else in one cup, on average. Divide a bag of coffee and a carton of milk by the cups they make if you want a fair figure. The cost of a cup at a cafe is what you pay for one cup, including any tip you usually leave.

How to use the result

Start with the weekly totals — they show the gap for one ordinary week of coffee. Then look at the yearly totals, which show what that weekly gap adds up to over a year of drinking the same way. A small gap per cup can become a large gap over a year when you drink several cups a day.

If you drink coffee at home on some days and at a cafe on others, the calculator still helps: it shows what the whole week would cost if every cup were made at home, and what it would cost if every cup came from a cafe. Your real spending sits somewhere between the two.

What this comparison leaves out

Money is only one part of the choice. The calculator does not count the cost of a coffee machine or equipment, the trip to the cafe, or the time spent making coffee at home. It also does not count pastries or snacks bought with a cafe coffee — if you usually buy one, include it in the cafe cup cost.

If you want the cost of a coffee machine counted, divide its cost by the cups you expect it to make and add that amount to your home cup cost before running the comparison.

Questions people ask

What counts as the cost of a cup at home?

The coffee, milk and anything else in one cup, on average. Divide a bag of coffee and a carton of milk by the cups they make.

Should I include the cost of my coffee machine?

The calculator counts running cost only. If you want the machine counted, divide its cost by the cups you expect it to make and add that to your home cup cost.

What if I drink a different number of cups on different days?

Enter your average cups per day across the week. The weekly and yearly figures follow your average.

Why multiply by 7 and then 52?

Seven days make a week and 52 weeks make a year, so the daily total times 7 is weekly, and weekly times 52 is yearly.

Does the cafe side include tips?

Only if you include them in the cost per cup you enter. Add a usual tip to the cafe cup cost so the comparison counts it.
