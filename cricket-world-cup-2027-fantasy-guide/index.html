Cricket World Cup 2027 Fantasy Guide

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

Fantasy cricket turns the Cricket World Cup 2027 into a game of judgement: pick eleven players, choose a captain, and score points from their real performances. This guide explains how to build a balanced team and avoid the common mistakes.

The Cricket World Cup 2027 runs from 2 October to 21 November 2027, with 14 teams playing 57 matches across South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia. That is seven weeks of cricket, which makes it one of the longest fantasy contests in the sport, and one where patience and planning matter as much as picking stars.

Fantasy cricket is simple in outline: you pick a team of players, they score points based on what they do in real matches, and your total is compared with other players. The details differ from platform to platform, so always check the rules of the one you play on. This guide covers the strategy that applies everywhere.

Fantasy platforms each have their own rules, scoring and deadlines. This guide gives general strategy; always check the rules of the platform you play on, and treat fantasy as entertainment.

How fantasy cricket works

On most fantasy platforms you pick a team of eleven players from the two teams playing a match, or from across the tournament, depending on the format. Players score points for runs, wickets, catches, stumpings and run-outs, and some platforms award bonus points for milestones such as half-centuries or three-wicket hauls. You choose a captain, who on most platforms scores double points, and often a vice-captain, who scores one and a half times.

The exact scoring, the number of players you can pick from each team, and the deadlines for changes all vary by platform, so read the rules before you play. Some platforms run daily contests for single matches, while others run season-long games across the whole tournament. The strategy in this guide applies to both, but the details are yours to check.

Building a balanced team

The most common mistake in fantasy cricket is picking eleven stars. Stars are expensive in fantasy terms, and a team of stars leaves no room for the players who actually win fantasy contests: the all-rounders. All-rounders score points in two disciplines, with bat and ball, which makes them the most reliable fantasy picks over a long tournament. A team with two or three genuine all-rounders has a higher floor than a team built only on batters and bowlers.

Balance your team across roles: top-order batters who face the most balls, at least one wicketkeeper who bats high in the order, fast bowlers who bowl at the start and the end of an innings, and spinners who bowl in the middle overs. Bowlers who bowl in the death overs tend to take more wickets, and batters who open or bat at number three face more balls than those lower down. Pick players for their role, not just their reputation.

Captain and vice-captain choices

The captain choice is the single most important decision in fantasy cricket, because the captain's points are doubled on most platforms. A good captain is a player with two ways to score: an all-rounder who bats in the top order and bowls a full spell, or a top-order batter in strong form. A bad captain is a player picked on reputation alone, or a bowler who may bowl only a few overs.

Form matters more than reputation. A player who has scored runs or taken wickets in recent matches is a better captain pick than a bigger name who is out of form. Check team news before the deadline: a captain who does not play scores nothing, and that single mistake can ruin a whole round. When in doubt, pick the in-form all-rounder.

Using the format to your advantage

The 2027 format shapes fantasy strategy. The group stage has 30 matches, and the Super 7 has 21, which means players from teams that reach the Super 7 play more matches and have more chances to score. Teams that are likely to go far are therefore worth weighting your team towards, but only if their players are in form.

Think in stages. In the group stage, pick players from the strongest teams and the best all-rounders. As the Super 7 approaches, favour players from the teams that have qualified, because they will play the most cricket. In the knockouts, the pool shrinks to four teams, and the best picks are the in-form players from those sides. Planning by stage beats picking the same eleven players all tournament.

Conditions and venues

The tournament is played at 12 venues across three countries, and conditions vary widely. South African pitches often offer pace and bounce, which suits fast bowlers, while some venues may turn more as the tournament goes on, which helps spinners. Weather, time of day and the state of the pitch all matter, and they change from match to match.

The practical advice is to check the conditions before each match: read the pitch report, check the weather, and look at the teams. A fast bowler on a green pitch is a better pick than the same bowler on a flat one, and a spinner on a dry, used pitch is worth more than on a fresh one. Fantasy rewards players who do their homework, not just those who pick the biggest names.

Common mistakes

The most common mistakes are easy to list and easy to make. Picking only star players leaves no room for all-rounders. Chasing last match's hero ignores the fact that form is temporary and match-ups change. Forgetting the deadline means playing with last week's team. Not checking team news means picking players who are rested or injured. And picking too many players from one team means one bad match can sink your whole round.

Another mistake is ignoring the toss and the conditions. A batter picked for a match where his team bats second in dew may score differently from one picked for a dry afternoon match. None of these mistakes is complicated, but together they are the difference between a good fantasy season and a frustrating one.

Playing responsibly

Fantasy cricket is entertainment, and it should be treated that way. Many platforms offer free games, and some offer paid contests. If you play paid contests, set a limit before the tournament begins and stick to it, and never spend more than you can afford. The fun of fantasy is in the judgement, the research and the competition with friends, not in the money.

Set a routine that fits your life: check team news once before each deadline, make your changes, and then enjoy the match. Fantasy should add to your enjoyment of the World Cup, not take it over. The tournament lasts seven weeks, and the players who enjoy it most are the ones who pace themselves.

Transfers and changes during the tournament

Most fantasy platforms let you change your team between rounds, and how you use those changes matters as much as your first picks. A long tournament rewards patience: do not use all your changes in the first week chasing last match's hero. Save changes for injuries, for players who lose their place, and for the shift between stages, when the Super 7 begins and the pool of teams narrows.

React to what the tournament tells you. A young player who breaks out in the group stage may be worth bringing in before everyone else notices. A star who is out of form for three matches in a row is worth replacing, however famous the name. And always check the deadline: changes made after it do not count, and a missed deadline can leave you fielding a player who is not even in the side. The best fantasy players treat changes as a resource to be spent wisely, not a panic button.

Questions people ask

How many players do I pick in fantasy cricket?

Usually eleven, but the exact number and the rules for how many you can pick from each team vary by platform. Check the rules of the platform you play on.

Who makes a good captain?

An in-form all-rounder who bats in the top order and bowls a full spell is usually the best captain pick, because they have two ways to score. Form matters more than reputation.

Should I pick players from every team?

Balance matters more than spreading thin. Pick players for their role, their form and their likely number of matches, and favour players from teams likely to reach the Super 7.

Do fantasy points carry across the tournament?

It depends on the platform. Some games run across the whole tournament, while others are daily contests for single matches. Check the format of the game you are playing.

What if my player does not play?

On most platforms a player who does not play scores nothing, which is why checking team news before the deadline matters so much. Some platforms allow substitutes; check your platform's rules.

Is fantasy cricket free to play?

Many platforms offer free games, and some offer paid contests. Check the platform you use, set a limit before you start, and treat fantasy as entertainment.
