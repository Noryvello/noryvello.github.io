How to Clean a Dishwasher

A dishwasher needs cleaning because food, grease and detergent residue build up in the filter, the spray arms and the door seal, and that build-up is what causes smells and cloudy glasses. This guide shows you how to clean each part, with a monthly checklist and notes on keeping smells away.

A dishwasher that smells, leaves grit on plates or turns glasses cloudy is usually not broken. It is dirty. Food particles wash off the plates and gather in the filter, grease coats the spray arms, and the warm damp interior gives smells a place to grow. The machine keeps running, but the water cannot circulate or drain as it should, so each wash gets a little worse.

The fix is a proper clean of the three places where dirt gathers: the filter at the bottom, the spray arms that throw the water, and the seal around the door. None of it needs tools, and the whole job takes less time than a normal wash cycle. A monthly routine keeps the machine working the way it should.

Never mix cleaning products, and use only products made for dishwashers. Watch for sharp items such as broken glass or cutlery when you reach into the filter area, and follow your machine manual for the filter and the hottest cycle.

The filter

The filter sits at the bottom of the dishwasher, under the lower rack. It catches food so it cannot block the drain, which means it is also the first place to smell. Start here, because a clean filter makes the rest of the clean work better.

  1. Pull out the lower rack and set it aside.
  2. Find the filter at the bottom. It usually twists out with a quarter turn, and your manual will show the exact direction.
  3. Lift the filter out and tap off loose food into the bin.
  4. Rinse the filter under warm running water and scrub it gently with a soft brush until the mesh looks clear.
  5. Check the opening where the filter sits and wipe away any food or grit around it.
  6. Put the filter back and twist it until it locks firmly into place.

If the filter will not twist, do not force it. Some models have a clip or a different turn direction, and your manual will show how yours works. A filter that is not locked back in properly lets food straight into the drain, so check that it sits flush before you run the next wash.

The spray arms

The spray arms spin and throw water over the dishes, and their small holes block easily with food and limescale. When the holes block, the water cannot reach every plate, and the arm may stop spinning evenly.

Most spray arms lift or unclip from their mount without tools. Take the lower arm off first, then the upper arm if you can reach it. Hold each arm up to the light and look through the holes. Clear blocked holes with a soft brush or a wooden toothpick, working gently so you do not widen the holes or push the blockage further in.

Rinse the arms under running water and shake them to clear any loose bits inside. Put them back and spin each one by hand to check it turns freely. If an arm catches on a rack or a tall item, adjust the rack height or move the item, because an arm that cannot spin leaves half the load unwashed.

The door seal and the interior

Wipe the rubber seal around the door with a damp cloth, paying attention to the folds at the bottom where water and food collect. Wipe the edges of the door itself and the inside of the door panel, which the spray arms do not reach. A cloth dampened with warm water is enough for the seal; harsh cleaners can damage the rubber.

Next, run an empty hot cycle with a dishwasher cleaner made for the job, used exactly as its label says. The hot water and cleaner work through the pipes and the parts you cannot reach, clearing grease and residue from the whole system. Take the racks out first if the cleaner label says to, and put them back when the cycle is done.

When the cycle finishes, leave the door ajar until the inside is dry. A dishwasher that is closed up while still damp will smell again quickly, however well you cleaned it. If the smell is still there after one cleaning cycle, run a second empty hot cycle with fresh water only before you look for a deeper cause.

Keeping smells away

Scrape plates before you load them. The filter is there to catch food, but less food in the machine means less food rotting in the filter between cleans. Bones, large scraps and coffee grounds belong in the bin, not in the dishwasher.

Run the machine regularly rather than letting dirty dishes sit for days. A dishwasher full of dirty plates in a warm kitchen will smell before the cycle even starts. If you only run a full load every few days, run a short rinse cycle in between to keep food from drying onto the plates and the filter.

Use the right amount of detergent and keep the rinse aid topped up if your machine has a rinse aid dispenser. Too much detergent leaves a film on everything, and too little leaves grease behind. The label on the detergent and your machine manual will tell you the right dose for a normal load.

Questions people ask

Why does my dishwasher smell?

Food and grease collect in the filter, the spray arms and the door seal, and the warm damp inside lets smells grow. Cleaning the filter and running an empty hot cycle usually clears it.

Why are my glasses cloudy after washing?

Cloudy glasses often come from a dirty filter or spray arms that cannot spin freely, so the water does not rinse properly. Clean the filter and check the spray arm holes, then run a cycle and see if the glasses come out clear.

Can I use washing-up liquid in a dishwasher?

No. Washing-up liquid foams far too much for a dishwasher and can flood the kitchen floor. Use only detergent made for dishwashers, and only in the amount the label suggests.

How often should I clean the filter?

Check it every few weeks and clean it whenever you see food caught in it. Homes that run the dishwasher daily will need to clean the filter more often than homes that run it a few times a week.

Should I rinse plates before loading them?

Scrape food off, but a full rinse is not needed. Dishwasher detergent works best when it has a little grease and food to grip, and the filter catches what comes off during the wash.
