Roof Shingle Calculator

Enter your roof measurements and pitch, and this calculator works out the roof area, the number of roofing squares and the bundles of shingles to buy, including a waste allowance. The working is shown so you can check every step.

This calculator estimates for a simple pitched roof. Valleys, hips, dormers and skylights all add cutting and waste, and working on a roof is dangerous, so follow local building practice and get professional help for the fitting itself.

How to use this calculator

Measure the roof length along the ridge and the roof width from the eave to the ridge, measured flat, in metres. Pick the roof pitch closest to yours. The pitch is given as the rise for every 12 units of run. Enter the shingle exposure, which is the part of each shingle left showing after the next row overlaps it. Set the waste allowance, then press Calculate to see the roof area, squares and bundles.

How the calculation works

The flat area is the length multiplied by the width. The pitch factor adjusts for the slope: it is the square root of one plus the pitch squared, so a steeper roof has more surface than its flat measurements suggest. One roofing square is 100 square feet, or 9.29 square metres. Bundles are worked out at three bundles per square, which is the usual count for standard three-tab shingles, plus the waste allowance. The courses figure is the slope length divided by the exposure.

How to use the result

Use the bundle count as your ordering figure, and check the bundles-per-square count on your shingle pack, because it varies by product. The courses figure tells you how many rows of shingles run up the slope, which helps when planning the layout. Add extra waste for a roof with valleys, hips or skylights, and keep a spare bundle for later repairs.

Questions people ask

What is a roofing square?

A roofing square is a unit of roof area equal to 100 square feet, or 9.29 square metres. Shingles are often sold and estimated by the square, so the calculator shows it alongside the area.

How many bundles are in a square?

Three bundles per square is the usual count for standard three-tab shingles, and this calculator uses that figure. Check your pack, because thicker or larger shingles may pack differently.

What is shingle exposure?

Exposure is the part of each shingle that stays visible after the next row overlaps it. It is printed on the pack or in the fitting guidance, and it decides how many rows run up the slope.

How do I find my roof pitch?

The pitch is the rise for every 12 units of run. If the roof rises 6 units over a 12-unit run, the pitch is 6 in 12. Pick the closest option in the picker.

Why add a waste allowance?

Cutting shingles to fit edges, valleys and around chimneys uses up spare shingles. Ten percent is the default, and a complex roof may need more.
