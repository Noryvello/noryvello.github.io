24-Hour Clock Guide

The 24-hour clock runs from 00:00 at midnight to 23:59 just before the next midnight, with no need for am or pm. Morning hours stay the same as on a 12-hour clock, afternoon hours add 12, and midnight and noon are the two times that trip most people up.

The 24-hour clock is the way most of the world tells the time. Train timetables, hospital charts, flight boards and weather reports use it, because it never leaves any doubt about whether a time is morning or afternoon. Once the two simple rules click, reading it becomes as natural as reading any clock.

How to read the 24-hour clock

The 24-hour clock counts the hours of the day from 0 to 23 instead of counting 1 to 12 twice. Midnight is 00:00, the start of a new day. The morning hours run 01:00 to 11:00, noon is 12:00, and the afternoon and evening hours run 13:00 to 23:00. The last minute of the day is 23:59, and one minute later the clock turns back to 00:00.

The leading zero matters. Nine in the morning is written 09:00, not 9:00, so that every time has the same shape and lines up neatly in timetables. The minutes work exactly as they do on a 12-hour clock: half past nine in the morning is 09:30, and a quarter to seven in the evening is 18:45.

Because each time of day has only one number, there is nothing to confuse. On a 12-hour clock, 7:30 could be morning or evening, and only the am or pm tells you which. On the 24-hour clock, 07:30 is morning and 19:30 is evening, and nobody has to ask.

Converting in both directions

Converting from 12-hour to 24-hour time follows two rules. For morning times, the hour stays the same, except for 12 am, which becomes 00. For afternoon and evening times, add 12 to the hour, except for 12 pm, which stays 12. So 9:15 am is 09:15, 3:45 pm is 15:45, 12:30 am is 00:30, and 12:30 pm is 12:30.

Going the other way is just as simple. If the hour is 00, it is 12 am. If the hour is 01 to 11, it is the same hour in the morning. If the hour is 12, it is 12 pm. If the hour is 13 to 23, subtract 12 and add pm. So 08:20 is 8:20 am, 13:20 is 1:20 pm, and 23:50 is 11:50 pm.

The minutes never change in either direction. Only the hour changes, and only by the rules above. That is the whole conversion, and it is worth practising until it is automatic, because the two special cases, midnight and noon, are where nearly every mistake happens.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is swapping midnight and noon. Twelve am is midnight, the start of the day, and it becomes 00:00. Twelve pm is noon, the middle of the day, and it stays 12:00. A simple way to remember is that the day starts at 0, so the midnight hour is the zero hour, and noon is twelve hours into the day.

The second most common mistake is adding 12 to 12 pm and getting 24:00. Noon is the exception to the add-12 rule: 12 pm is already 12:00, and adding 12 would give a time that does not exist as a time of day. The number 24:00 does appear in some timetables, but only to mean the very end of a day, and midnight itself is written 00:00.

The third mistake is dropping the leading zero and writing 9:00 when 09:00 is meant. In everyday writing nobody will misunderstand, but in timetables and official records the leading zero is part of the format, and leaving it out can cause confusion in lists where times need to line up. The fourth mistake is mixing the two systems, writing something like 14:30 pm. The 24-hour time already says it is afternoon, so the pm is not needed and makes the time wrong.

Where the 24-hour clock is used

Most of the world uses the 24-hour clock as the standard way of writing the time. It is the format on train and flight timetables, on hospital charts, in military communications, and in weather reports. It is also the format computers and phones use internally, even when they display the time in 12-hour form.

The reason is always the same: the 24-hour clock removes ambiguity. A train that leaves at 18:40 leaves in the evening, and there is no way to read it as a morning train. A medicine given at 02:00 is given in the early morning, and a dose written as 14:00 cannot be mistaken for a night dose. When the cost of a mistake is high, the clock that cannot be misread wins.

Learning it takes only a little practice. Start with the table above, use the checker to test yourself on random times, and try reading the times you see during the day in 24-hour form. Within a few days, 15:30 will feel as natural as half past three.

Midnight belongs to the day that is starting: 00:00 on Monday is the very beginning of Monday, not the end of it. If a timetable or ticket says 00:00, check which date it belongs to, because an early-morning time after midnight is part of the new day.

Questions people ask

What is 12 am in 24-hour time?

12 am is midnight, and in 24-hour time it is written 00:00. It is the start of the day, so the midnight hour is the zero hour.

What is 12 pm in 24-hour time?

12 pm is noon, and in 24-hour time it stays 12:00. Noon is the exception to the add-12 rule, so do not turn it into 24:00.

How do I convert pm times to 24-hour time?

Add 12 to the hour for pm times from 1 pm to 11 pm, so 3 pm becomes 15:00 and 9 pm becomes 21:00. The minutes stay the same, and 12 pm stays 12:00.

Is 24:00 a real time?

Midnight is written 00:00. The number 24:00 appears in some timetables to mean the very end of a day, but as a time of day midnight is 00:00.

Why do timetables use the 24-hour clock?

Because each time of day has only one number, so a time can never be confused between morning and evening. A train at 18:40 is clearly an evening train.
