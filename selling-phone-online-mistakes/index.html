Mistakes to Avoid When Selling Your Phone Online

The biggest mistakes when selling a phone online are forgetting to sign out of cloud activation locks before wiping data, leaving SIM or memory cards inside, hiding cosmetic flaws or battery wear, and accepting unverified payment links. Complete the six preparation steps below before handing over your phone.

Honest limit: Even when you follow every listing and handover safeguard, local marketplace disputes and carrier IMEI database delays can still occur. Always meet in a well-lit public location for local sales or ship only through tracked, insured services.

1. Wiping the Phone Without Disabling Activation Lock

  • What to do: Sign out of your Apple ID, Google account, and manufacturer cloud account before initiating a factory reset.
  • Why: Security locks remain active on the hardware even after a reset, making the phone unusable for the buyer and forcing a return or dispute.
  • How to check it: After the reset finishes, walk through the initial setup screens without signing in—if it reaches the home screen without asking for your old password, the lock is off.

2. Forgetting Two-Factor Authentication Apps and Physical Cards

  • What to do: Transfer authenticator apps and banking tokens to your new device, and eject both the SIM tray and any microSD card.
  • Why: Wiping your old phone before moving authenticator apps can lock you out of email and financial accounts, while an unremoved SIM exposes your phone number to SMS interception.
  • How to check it: Open each critical account on your replacement phone first, then physically shine a light into the empty SIM slot to confirm no cards remain.

3. Hiding Scratches, Screen Burn-In, or Battery Health

  • What to do: Photograph the screen against a plain white background and disclose every dent, repair history, and current battery capacity percentage.
  • Why: Undisclosed wear gives buyers valid grounds to demand partial refunds or return the device at your expense.
  • How to check it: Open Settings > Battery to read the reported maximum capacity, and take close-up photos under bright daylight before publishing your listing.

4. Listing the Wrong Storage Size, Model Variant, or Carrier Status

  • What to do: Verify the exact model number, internal storage capacity, and whether the device is factory-unlocked or locked to a specific mobile carrier.
  • Why: Two phones that look identical outside can have different storage tiers or network locks, causing immediate buyer complaints.
  • How to check it: Read the exact model string in Settings > About Phone and test a SIM card from a different network if advertising the phone as unlocked.

5. Falling for Fake Payment Screenshots or Off-Platform Links

  • What to do: Confirm funds directly inside your bank app or the official marketplace dashboard rather than trusting emailed receipts or SMS alerts.
  • Why: Scammers routinely send spoofed payment confirmations or ask you to click external courier links to enter card details.
  • How to check it: Never ship or hand over the phone until your own banking app shows the transfer as settled—not pending—or you hold verified cash in hand.

6. Handing Over the Device Without Recording Proof of Condition

  • What to do: Record the phone's IMEI number and film a short continuous video showing the working touchscreen, cameras, and speakers right before packing or meeting.
  • Why: If a dishonest buyer claims the phone arrived cracked or non-functional, dated pre-handover evidence protects your position.
  • How to check it: Dial *#06# on the phone keypad to display the IMEI on screen and capture it clearly in your pre-sale photos.

Questions people ask

Why is signing out of your Apple ID or Google account critical before resetting a phone?

Modern smartphones use Activation Lock or Factory Reset Protection tied to your cloud account. If you erase the phone without signing out first, the next owner cannot activate the device without your password.

Does a factory reset erase data on a removable microSD card?

Usually no. A standard factory reset wipes internal phone storage, so you must physically eject any microSD card and SIM card before handing over the device.

Should I accept an overpayment or courier-pickup escrow link from a buyer?

No. Fake payment emails, overpayments asking you to refund the difference, and unfamiliar courier escrow links are common fraud tactics used to steal phones without paying.

What details should I record before shipping or handing over a sold phone?

Record the device IMEI or serial number, take clear photos and a short video showing the screen and exterior condition working properly, and save the buyer conversation.
