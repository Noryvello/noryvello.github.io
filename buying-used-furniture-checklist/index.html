Used Furniture Buying Checklist

This checklist covers the inspection steps worth doing before you pay for used furniture, from joints and smells to pest signs and measurements. Tick each item off as you inspect the piece, and the counter keeps your progress.

Take this list with you to the viewing, and tick each check off as you inspect the piece.

This page is general buying guidance only. If a piece shows signs of pests, strong damp or structural damage, walk away: repairs can cost more than the piece is worth. Always lift with help, and never carry heavy furniture on your own.

How to use this checklist

Take the list with you to the viewing, on your phone or printed out. Start with the structure — joints, legs, drawers — before you fall in love with how the piece looks. A pretty piece with loose joints is a repair job, not a bargain, and it is much easier to spot that before you have paid and carried it home.

Give yourself time. A proper inspection takes about ten minutes for a single piece, and a rushed one misses the things that matter: the smell inside a drawer, the wobble in a leg, the tiny holes that mean pests. If the seller hurries you, treat that as information too.

The inspection, step by step

Joints tell you most of what you need to know. Press on them, wiggle them and listen: a solid joint is silent and firm, a failing one creaks and moves. Check where legs meet the frame, where arms meet the back, and where shelves meet the sides. A little glue can fix a loose joint, but a joint that has been repaired badly is harder to trust.

Smells are the second thing to check, and the one people are shy about. Smell the piece up close, including inside drawers and under cushions. Smoke, damp and pet smells sink deep into wood and fabric, and some never fully leave. A piece that smells of damp may also have damage you cannot see, so treat a strong damp smell as a reason to walk away.

Pest signs are small but easy to spot once you know what to look for: tiny round holes, fine dust in crevices, or small droppings in joints and underneath. Check the underside and the back, not just the front. If you see any of these signs, do not bring the piece home.

Measure twice, move once

The most common regret with used furniture is not the piece itself but the journey home. Measure the piece, then measure every doorway, stair turn and hallway it has to pass through, and measure the space where it will live. A tape measure takes a minute; a sofa stuck on a landing takes an afternoon.

Plan the transport before you pay. Know the vehicle size, bring blankets to protect the piece and your car, and bring a second pair of hands for anything you cannot lift alone. Ask whether the piece comes apart, and if it does, put every screw and fitting in a labelled bag so nothing goes missing.

After it arrives

Clean the piece before it comes inside properly, especially fabric and the insides of drawers. A wipe-down with a cloth, a vacuum of upholstery and a day of fresh air will take care of most surface dust and mild smells. Then let it settle in its new spot for a day before you load it up, and check the joints once more after the move.

Questions people ask

What are the signs of pests in used furniture?

Look for tiny round holes, fine dust in crevices, or small droppings in joints and underneath. Check the back and underside, not just the front. If you see any of these signs, do not bring the piece home.

How do I get rid of a smell in used furniture?

Start with fresh air and a thorough clean: wipe wood down, vacuum fabric, and leave drawers open for a day. Mild smells usually fade. A strong smell of damp or smoke that stays after cleaning is a sign the piece may not be worth keeping.

Should I buy used upholstered furniture?

You can, if you inspect it carefully. Press the cushions, check the seams, smell the fabric and look underneath for stains or pest signs. Avoid pieces with sagging cushions, torn seams or a smell you cannot identify.

What should I measure before I buy?

Measure the piece itself, then every doorway, stair turn and hallway it must pass through, and the space where it will live. Note the height as well as the width: tall pieces often fail on a stair turn, not at the front door.

Can I return used furniture?

That depends on the seller, so ask before you pay. Private sellers usually sell as seen, while shops may offer a short return window. Agree the terms in writing if you can, and inspect the piece thoroughly before money changes hands.
