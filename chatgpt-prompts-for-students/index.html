30 ChatGPT Prompts for Students

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

This page gives you 30 original ChatGPT prompts for students, organised into five groups: studying, writing help, exam prep, research, and learning new topics. Every prompt comes with a one-line note on how to adapt it to your own subject.

A good prompt turns ChatGPT from a search box into a study partner. Instead of asking for an answer to copy, you can ask for an explanation, a quiz, a plan, or feedback on your own work. The prompts below are written for exactly that kind of use.

Each prompt is a starting point, not a script. Change the topic, the level, and the length to fit what you are studying, and read the adaptation note under each one for ideas. The more specific you are about your subject, the more useful the answer will be.

ChatGPT can be wrong, and a wrong answer in study notes can cost you marks. Check facts, dates, and worked steps against your course material before trusting them, and never submit AI-written work as your own unless your school or college allows it.

How to Use These Prompts

Replace the words in square brackets with your own topic, subject, or limits before you send a prompt. One prompt at a time works best: send it, read the answer, and then follow up with a question instead of pasting five prompts at once. If an answer is too long, ask for a shorter version; if it is too hard, ask for a simpler one.

Use these prompts to learn, not to skip learning. A quiz prompt helps only if you try to answer before reading the answers. A summary prompt helps only if you compare the summary with your notes. The prompts below are designed so that you stay the one doing the thinking, with ChatGPT doing the explaining, testing, and organising.

Studying

Use these prompts to understand a topic, test yourself, and plan your study time.

  1. "Explain [topic] to me as if I am 12 years old, then explain it again at university level, and point out what changes between the two versions."
    Adapt it: swap the topic and the two levels to fit your course.
  2. "Turn these notes into a set of 10 questions I can use to test myself, and do not include the answers until I ask for them."
    Adapt it: paste your own notes in place of the words these notes.
  3. "Give me a simple memory trick for remembering [list of items], and explain why the trick works."
    Adapt it: use any list you need to memorise, from dates to formulas.
  4. "Summarise this chapter in five bullet points, then list the three ideas I am most likely to be tested on."
    Adapt it: paste the chapter text or your own notes.
  5. "Create a one-week study plan for [subject], with 45 minutes a day, and mix review days with practice days."
    Adapt it: change the subject, the time, and the number of days.
  6. "Explain the difference between [concept A] and [concept B] using a table and one everyday example for each."
    Adapt it: pick any two ideas you keep mixing up.

Writing Help

Use these prompts to improve your own writing, not to replace it.

  1. "Read my paragraph and tell me which sentence is the weakest, and why, without rewriting the whole thing."
    Adapt it: paste one paragraph at a time for careful feedback.
  2. "Help me turn this rough idea into a clear thesis sentence, and give me two versions to choose between."
    Adapt it: paste your rough idea in place of the words this rough idea.
  3. "List five stronger words I could use instead of repeating [word] in my essay."
    Adapt it: pick any word you notice yourself overusing.
  4. "Check this introduction for flow: does each sentence lead into the next, and where does it jump?"
    Adapt it: paste any introduction or section of your draft.
  5. "Turn these bullet points into a short paragraph in my own voice, keeping the facts exactly as written."
    Adapt it: paste your own bullet points.
  6. "Give me three possible opening lines for an essay about [topic], each in a different style."
    Adapt it: change the topic and ask for more styles.

Exam Prep

Use these prompts to practise under exam-like conditions.

  1. "Quiz me on [topic] one question at a time, and wait for my answer before asking the next."
    Adapt it: name any topic and set the difficulty you want.
  2. "Make a practice test of 10 questions on [topic], half multiple choice and half short answer."
    Adapt it: change the mix and the number of questions.
  3. "Explain the three mistakes students most often make with [topic], with one example of each."
    Adapt it: name the topic you find hardest.
  4. "Turn this list of terms into flashcards: give me the term first, then the definition only when I ask."
    Adapt it: paste your own list of terms.
  5. "Walk me through a worked example of [problem type], one step at a time, and let me try each step first."
    Adapt it: name the problem type you need to practise.
  6. "Give me a five-minute revision summary of [topic] I can read the morning of the exam."
    Adapt it: name the topic and the time you have.

Research

Use these prompts to narrow a topic, find sources, and organise what you find.

  1. "Help me narrow this broad topic into a research question I can answer in 2,000 words."
    Adapt it: state your topic and your word limit.
  2. "Suggest five search terms I can use to find academic sources about [topic]."
    Adapt it: name your topic and the kind of sources you need.
  3. "Explain this abstract in plain language and tell me the paper's main finding in one sentence."
    Adapt it: paste any abstract you find hard to read.
  4. "Compare these two viewpoints on [issue] and list the strongest argument on each side."
    Adapt it: name the issue you are researching.
  5. "Check my bibliography entries for consistency: same style, same order, no missing details."
    Adapt it: paste your reference list.
  6. "Turn my research notes into an outline with three main sections and two supporting points each."
    Adapt it: paste your notes and change the structure.

Learning New Topics

Use these prompts when you are starting a subject from zero.

  1. "Teach me [topic] from zero in five short lessons, and quiz me after each lesson."
    Adapt it: name any topic and change the number of lessons.
  2. "Give me a real-world example of [concept] that I can picture in my daily life."
    Adapt it: name any abstract concept that feels hard to grasp.
  3. "Explain [topic] using only words a 10-year-old would know, then add the technical terms one by one."
    Adapt it: pick any topic with heavy jargon.
  4. "What should I learn first, second, and third if I want to understand [subject]?"
    Adapt it: name the subject you are starting.
  5. "Pretend you are my tutor. Ask me what I already know about [topic], then fill in the gaps."
    Adapt it: name the topic and answer honestly.
  6. "Create a glossary of the 10 most important terms in [topic], with a one-line definition for each."
    Adapt it: name the topic and change the number of terms.

Checking Answers Before You Trust Them

ChatGPT can explain a topic beautifully and still get a fact wrong. When an answer matters for an exam or an assignment, check it against your textbook, your lecture notes, or a trusted source. If the AI gives you a date, a figure, or a quotation, treat it as unverified until you have confirmed it.

A useful habit is to ask the AI to show its working. For a maths problem, ask for each step. For a summary, ask which part of your notes each point came from. When the reasoning is visible, mistakes are easier to spot, and you learn more from the answer even when it is right.

Making Prompts Your Own

The best prompts are the ones you shape for yourself. Start with a prompt from this page, then change the details: the topic, the length, the level, and the format. Notice which changes improve the answer and which do not, and keep a note of the versions that work best for each subject.

Over time you will build a small set of prompts you trust for studying, writing, and revision. That set is worth more than any list, because it matches how you learn. Use this page as the raw material, and your own experience as the editor.

Questions people ask

Is it cheating to use ChatGPT for studying?

It depends on how you use it and on your school rules. Using AI to explain a concept, quiz you, or give feedback on your own draft is study help. Submitting AI-written work as your own is cheating at most schools, so check your school policy first.

How do I adapt a prompt to my subject?

Replace the bracketed words with your own topic, level, and limits. If a prompt asks for a five-minute summary and you have ten minutes, say so. Small changes like these make a big difference to the answer.

What if ChatGPT gives me a wrong answer?

Ask it to explain its reasoning step by step, then check the steps against your textbook or notes. If the answer still looks wrong, trust your course material and ask your teacher.

Can ChatGPT help me with maths problems?

Yes, it can walk through worked examples and explain methods. Ask it to show each step and let you try each step first, and always check the final answer by working it out yourself.

Should I paste my whole essay into ChatGPT?

It is better to paste one paragraph at a time and ask for specific feedback, such as which sentence is weakest. Short pieces get more careful answers, and your work stays in your hands.
