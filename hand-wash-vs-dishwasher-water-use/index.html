Which uses less water, and when?

Whichever side's litres multiplied by times per week is lower wins. The dishwasher wins when it runs full loads with little water per cycle while hand washing uses many litres each time. Hand washing wins when you wash small amounts with little water and would run the dishwasher often for only a few items.

How the maths works

Weekly water use = litres per wash × washes per week. This is worked out separately for hand washing and for the dishwasher.

Yearly water use = weekly water use × 52. A year is counted as 52 weeks.

The method that uses less water is the one with the lower weekly total. The saving is the higher total minus the lower total, per week and per year.

Worked examples

Each example below uses the same formula as the calculator. Results are rounded to 2 decimal places.

Example 1 — Hand wash 20 litres, 7 loads a week; dishwasher 12 litres per cycle, 5 cycles a week.
Hand wash: 20 × 7 = 140.00 litres per week; 20 × 7 × 52 = 7280.00 litres per year.
Dishwasher: 12 × 5 = 60.00 litres per week; 12 × 5 × 52 = 3120.00 litres per year.
Difference per week: (20 × 7) − (12 × 5) = 80.00 litres. Difference per year: (20 × 7 × 52) − (12 × 5 × 52) = 4160.00 litres. The dishwasher uses less water, by 57.1% (80 ÷ 140 × 100 = 57.1, rounded to 1 decimal place).

Example 2 — Hand wash 10 litres, 5 loads a week; dishwasher 15 litres per cycle, 7 cycles a week.
Hand wash: 10 × 5 = 50.00 litres per week; 10 × 5 × 52 = 2600.00 litres per year.
Dishwasher: 15 × 7 = 105.00 litres per week; 15 × 7 × 52 = 5460.00 litres per year.
Difference per week: (15 × 7) − (10 × 5) = 55.00 litres. Difference per year: (15 × 7 × 52) − (10 × 5 × 52) = 2860.00 litres. Hand washing uses less water, by 52.4% (55 ÷ 105 × 100 = 52.4, rounded to 1 decimal place).

Hand wash: 12 × 5 = 60.00 litres per week; 12 × 5 × 52 = 3120.00 litres per year.
Dishwasher: 10 × 6 = 60.00 litres per week; 10 × 6 × 52 = 3120.00 litres per year.
Difference per week: (12 × 5) − (10 × 6) = 0.00 litres. Difference per year: (12 × 5 × 52) − (10 × 6 × 52) = 0.00 litres. Both methods use exactly the same amount of water.

What to check before you decide

Run full loads. A dishwasher cycle uses roughly the same water whether the machine is full or only partly full, so full loads get more washed per litre. The same goes for hand washing: a full basin shared across a pile of dishes uses less water per item than washing a few things at a time.

Basin or running tap? Washing in a basin uses a fixed amount of water, while washing under a running tap keeps adding water the whole time you wash. If you wash by hand, the basin is the part of your habit that most changes the result.

Count what you actually wash. If your household washes up once a day, that is 7 loads a week; if you wash after every meal, it is more. Count your real washes for a week before you enter a number, rather than guessing.

Remember what this comparison leaves out. It counts water only. It does not count electricity, detergent, your time, or the water used to make the appliances — so it answers the water question, not the whole question of which method is better overall.

Questions people ask

Which uses less water, hand washing or a dishwasher?

It depends on your numbers. Whichever side uses fewer litres multiplied by times per week wins, so a dishwasher with a low water use per cycle can beat hand washing, and careful hand washing with little water can beat a dishwasher that runs often. Enter your own litres and washes per week above to see which uses less for your home.

How much water does a dishwasher use per cycle?

It varies a lot by machine, cycle and load size, so there is no single figure that fits every home. Check your machine's manual or label for its water use per cycle and enter that figure above. This page does not guess one for you, because a guessed figure would give you a wrong answer.

How much water does hand washing use?

It varies by habit. Washing in a basin uses less than washing under a running tap, because the basin holds a fixed amount of water while a running tap keeps adding water. Measure your own basin or jug once to get your figure, and enter it above.

Does the calculator include electricity or detergent?

No. It compares water use only, based on the litres and washes per week you enter. Electricity, detergent, time and the water used to make the appliances are not included.
