7 CV Mistakes That Could Hide Your Best Experience

Short answer: check relevance, accuracy, readability, specific achievements, dates, contact details and the application format before sending your CV.

Seven CV mistakes to check

  1. Typos and inconsistent formatting: proofread names, dates, punctuation and headings.
  2. A generic opening: make the summary relevant to the role, or leave it out if it adds nothing.
  3. Unclear achievements: explain what you did and, where accurate, the result or scale.
  4. Long blocks of text: use concise bullets and clear section headings.
  5. Unexplained gaps or dates: make the timeline easy to follow without inventing details.
  6. Irrelevant detail: prioritise skills and experience that relate to the vacancy.
  7. Unprofessional contact details or file naming: use a suitable email address and a clear file name.

Make each bullet clearer

Start with an action, describe the task or scope, and include a measurable result only when you can support it. For example: “Coordinated weekly stock checks for a five-person team” is more informative than “Responsible for stock.”

Important limit: Hiring norms differ by country and industry. Check the employer’s instructions and local expectations rather than assuming one CV format fits every application.

Questions people ask

How long should a CV be?

There is no single length that suits every country, career stage or industry. Follow the employer’s instructions and keep relevant information clear and concise.

Should I include a photo on my CV?

Photo expectations vary by country, industry and employer. Check local conventions and the application instructions before including one.

Should I tailor my CV for every job?

It is usually useful to prioritise the experience and skills that match each role, while keeping every statement accurate.

What is the most important CV check?

Check that the CV is accurate, relevant to the role and easy to scan. Also verify contact details and the requested file format.
