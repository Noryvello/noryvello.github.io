What makes a good podcast name

A good podcast name does three things: it tells a listener roughly what the show is about, it is easy to say and spell, and it sticks in the memory. Short names do this best. A name of two to four words is easy to remember, easy to fit on cover art, and easy to mention at the end of an episode. Long names get shortened by listeners anyway, so you may as well start short.

The name should also fit the tone of the show. A true-crime podcast and a comedy podcast can both be excellent, but their names should sound different — a listener should be able to guess the mood from the name alone. If your show is warm and friendly, pick a name that sounds warm and friendly. If it is serious and investigative, pick a name that sounds like it.

How to use the ideas

Use the generator as a starting point, not a final answer. Pick a topic and look at the five ideas it returns. If one jumps out at you, try it on for size: say it aloud, imagine it on cover art, and picture yourself saying it at the start of every episode. If none of them feels right, mix and match — take a word from one idea and a word from another, and see what you get.

Do not rush the decision. A podcast name is hard to change once listeners know it, so it is worth a day or two of thought. Write your shortlist down, come back to it the next day, and see which name you still like. The name-check checklist above walks you through the checks worth doing before you commit.

Check before you commit

Before you settle on a name, search for it. Check that no other podcast already uses the same name or something very close to it, and check that the name is not already a well-known brand. A quick search now saves an awkward rename later. Also check that listeners can spell the name when they hear it — if they cannot find your show by typing what they heard, they will give up.

Finally, think about the long term. A name tied to a single narrow topic can box you in if the show grows. A name with a little room in it lets the show change direction without changing its name. Pick a name you will still be happy saying in a year's time.

Questions people ask

How long should a podcast name be?

Two to four words is a good length. Short names are easier to remember, easier to say at the start of an episode, and easier to fit on cover art. Long names get shortened by listeners anyway.

Should my podcast name say what the show is about?

It helps. A listener browsing for a new show should be able to guess roughly what yours covers from the name alone. You do not need to spell it out — a hint of the topic or the mood is enough.

What if the name I like is already taken?

Pick another one. Two podcasts with the same name confuse listeners and can cause trouble later. Search the name before you commit, and have a shortlist of favourites rather than a single name.

Can I change my podcast name later?

You can, but it is disruptive — listeners, cover art, and links all need updating. It is worth taking a day or two to choose well now, using the name-check checklist above, rather than renaming later.

Do I need a unique name for my podcast?

Yes, as far as you can. A unique name is easier to search for, easier to remember, and less likely to clash with another show. The generator above gives you original ideas to start from.
