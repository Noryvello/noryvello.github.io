House Sitter Instructions Checklist

Use this checklist to get your home ready for a house sitter, from keys and alarms to plants, bins and emergency contacts. Tick each item as you go, and print the finished list so your sitter has everything in one place.

A good house sit starts before you leave. The sitter needs to know how the house works: which key fits which door, how the alarm behaves, when the bins go out and who to call if something goes wrong. Writing it all down once saves a dozen phone calls later.

This checklist covers the essentials in four parts: keys and access, alarms and security, plants and bins, and contacts and handover. Work through it the day before you leave, then print the list and leave it with your handover notes.

Keep alarm codes and spare keys out of written notes left in plain sight. Share codes with your sitter directly, and change any code you shared once the sit is over.

Before the sitter arrives

Walk through the house with your sitter, ideally a day or two before you leave. Show them the alarm, the heating controls, the stopcock and the fuse box, and let them try the keys in every lock. Things that are obvious to you are not obvious to someone visiting for the first time.

Write the handover notes in plain words and keep them short. A page of clear notes beats a long chat that the sitter has to remember. Include the quirks: the window that sticks, the tap that needs a firm turn, the light switch that does nothing.

On handover day

Go through the checklist together one last time and tick off anything still to do. Agree how often the sitter will message you, and what counts as urgent. A short daily message keeps you relaxed and lets the sitter know you are easy to reach.

Tell a trusted neighbour that a sitter is staying, so the house does not look empty and someone nearby knows who should be there. Leave a spare key with the neighbour too, in case the sitter locks themselves out.

Questions people ask

How early should I prepare for a house sitter?

Start the day before you leave, and walk the sitter through the house a day or two before that if you can. A trial visit answers questions that no checklist can.

What should the sitter do if the alarm goes off?

Write down the alarm company's number and the steps to reset the alarm. Tell the sitter to call you first, then the alarm company, if the alarm will not reset.

Should I tell my neighbours about the sitter?

Yes. A neighbour who knows a sitter is staying can keep an eye out and help if the sitter locks themselves out or needs a hand.

What about mail and deliveries?

Ask the sitter to bring mail inside each day so the house does not look empty. Leave a note for deliveries, or ask the sitter to accept parcels on your behalf.
