Make it personal

  • Say what you noticed rather than relying on vague superlatives.
  • Focus on a choice, effort, skill or action the person can recognize.
  • Keep the compliment appropriate to your relationship and setting.
  • Do not use praise to pressure someone or expect something in return.
A note before you act: A compliment lands best when it is specific and sincere. Avoid comments about sensitive personal traits or appearance unless you know they are welcome and appropriate.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a compliment feel sincere?

A sincere compliment is based on something you genuinely noticed. Mention a specific action, effort, skill or quality instead of using exaggerated praise.

How do I compliment someone at work?

Focus on a relevant contribution, such as clear communication, helpful teamwork or careful problem-solving. Keep it professional and specific.

What if I feel awkward giving compliments?

Keep it short and natural. Say what you noticed and why it mattered, without expecting a particular response.

Can a compliment be too personal?

Yes. Consider the relationship, setting and the other person's comfort. When unsure, focus on a person's work, choices or helpful actions rather than private traits.
