Air Fryer Time Guide

Pick a food from the list and this guide shows the temperature range, the time range, whether to shake or turn it, and how to tell when it is done. It covers 20 everyday foods, from chicken and fish to fries and vegetables.

Pick a food from the list to see its cooking time and temperature.

Times are guides, not guarantees. Air fryers vary in power, and thick pieces or a crowded basket take longer. Always check food is cooked through before serving, and follow your air fryer's manual.

How to use this guide

Choose your food and read the card. The temperature is given in Celsius with Fahrenheit beside it, and the time is a range because no two air fryers behave exactly alike. Start at the low end of the range, check the food, and add a few minutes if it needs more. That habit matters more than any single number on the card.

Each card also tells you whether to shake the basket or turn the food, and what done looks like. A shake halfway through matters most for small pieces like fries and vegetables, where the pieces on top brown first. Larger pieces like a chicken breast or a steak do better with one careful turn.

Getting the best results from your air fryer

Give food room. A single layer with a little space between pieces lets the hot air move, which is what makes things crisp. If you pile food up, the pieces in the middle steam instead of browning, and the cooking time stretches. Cook in two batches when the basket is full rather than forcing everything in at once.

Pat food dry before it goes in, especially fish, chicken skin and vegetables. Surface moisture turns to steam and works against crisping. A light brush of oil helps browning on vegetables and on foods without much fat of their own. Too much oil, on the other hand, drips, smokes and makes a mess, so a thin coat is enough.

Cut pieces to a similar size so they finish together. If some wedges are twice as thick as others, the thin ones burn while the thick ones stay raw in the middle. A few extra seconds with the knife at the start saves a disappointing plate at the end.

When times need adjusting

Frozen food needs more time than fresh food of the same kind, and dense food needs more time than thin food. A thick chicken breast takes longer than a thin one, and a whole potato wedge takes longer than a slim fry. The ranges on the cards already allow for this, so check early and add time in small steps.

Your air fryer's size matters too. A small basket cooks a small load faster than a large basket cooking the same load, because the hot air is more concentrated. If you switch models or cook a much bigger batch than usual, treat the first cook as a test run and note what worked for next time.

Questions people ask

Do I need to preheat my air fryer?

Many models cook well without preheating, but a few minutes of preheating gives more even results for foods that need a crisp finish. Check your own model's manual for what it recommends.

Why do the times here show a range?

Air fryers differ in power, and food size and basket load change cooking time. Start at the low end of the range and check doneness, then add a few minutes if the food needs it.

Should I shake or turn the food?

Yes, when the guide says so. Shaking or turning halfway helps food brown evenly, because the hot air reaches the side that was facing down.

Can I cook frozen food straight from the freezer?

Yes for foods made for it, such as frozen fries, but add a few extra minutes and check doneness before serving. Spread frozen pieces in a single layer so they crisp instead of steaming.

How do I know when food is done?

Use the doneness check on each card. For example, fish should flake easily, chicken should have no pink meat and clear juices, and vegetables should be tender with crisp edges.
