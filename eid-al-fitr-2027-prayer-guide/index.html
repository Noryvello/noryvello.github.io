Eid al-Fitr 2027 Prayer Guide

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

Eid al-Fitr in 2027 is expected on Tuesday 9 March, and the morning begins with the Eid prayer, prayed in congregation at the mosque or in a large open space. This guide explains when and where the prayer happens, how it works, and what to wear and bring. It also covers etiquette for the day, so first-timers can feel at ease.

Eid al-Fitr marks the end of Ramadan, and its morning begins with prayer. After a month of fasting, Muslims gather — in mosques, halls, parks and open grounds — to pray together, to greet one another, and to begin a day of celebration. For many people, the Eid prayer is the happiest congregation of the year.

This guide covers the practical side of the day: when and where the prayer happens, how it works, what to wear and bring, and how to behave. Whether it is your first Eid prayer or your fiftieth, the details here will help the morning run smoothly.

Eid dates depend on the sighting of the moon, so Tuesday 9 March 2027 is an expected date. Prayer times and arrangements vary by mosque, so check your local mosque for the details. This page is general guidance, not religious instruction.

When and where Eid prayer happens

Eid prayer is prayed in the morning, after sunrise, and the exact time is set by each mosque or community. Most mosques hold the prayer in the mid-morning, and larger communities often hold several sessions to fit everyone in. Your local mosque will announce its times in the last days of Ramadan, so check its notice board, website or announcements.

The prayer is often held in a large space so the whole community can gather. Some mosques use their main hall, others hire a sports hall or a community centre, and in many places the prayer is held outdoors in a park or open ground, following the traditional practice. Wherever it is held, the sight of a whole community praying together is one of the great moments of the year.

Arrive early. Eid prayer is the busiest congregation of the year, and the best places fill quickly. Arriving with time to spare lets you find a place calmly, greet friends, and settle before the prayer begins. If you are driving, allow extra time for parking, and consider walking if the mosque is near.

The morning of Eid: step by step

The Eid morning has a happy routine of its own. It begins with a bath and your best clothes, and it is customary to eat something sweet before leaving for the prayer — often dates, in odd numbers. The sweet bite marks the difference from Ramadan, when the morning began with a fast.

Before leaving, make sure Zakat al-Fitr has been given. This is the charity due at the end of Ramadan, traditionally given before the Eid prayer so that it reaches people in need in time for the day. Many people give it through their mosque in the last days of Ramadan.

On the way to the prayer, it is customary to recite the takbir — the declaration that God is greatest — softly or aloud. Families often walk together, greet neighbours on the way, and arrive in a cheerful crowd. The walk itself is part of the celebration.

How the Eid prayer works

The Eid prayer is two rakats, prayed in congregation behind an imam, and it has a distinctive feature: extra takbirs, the saying of "Allahu Akbar", at the start of each rakat. The number of additional takbirs varies by tradition — some mosques add six, others twelve — so simply follow your imam. There is no call to prayer, or adhan, before Eid prayer, and no Sunnah prayer immediately before it in most traditions.

After the prayer, the imam gives a short sermon, called the khutbah. Unlike the Friday sermon, the Eid khutbah comes after the prayer, and it is customary to stay and listen, though it is not obligatory in the same way. The sermon usually speaks about gratitude, generosity and community, and it is a fitting end to the month.

If you are new to Eid prayer, the simplest advice is to follow the person next to you and the imam. Stand when they stand, bow when they bow, and do not worry if you miss a takbir. The congregation is forgiving, and everyone was new once.

What to wear and bring

Eid is the day to wear your best. Clean, smart clothes are the custom, and many people wear new outfits or traditional dress. Children are often dressed up specially, and the sight of families in their finest is part of the day's joy. Comfort matters too: you will be sitting on the ground, so choose clothes you can move in easily.

Bring little with you. A prayer mat is useful if the prayer is outdoors or the hall is crowded, and a small bag for your shoes keeps things tidy. A bottle of water is sensible on a warm day. Leave valuables at home, and keep your phone silent during the prayer.

If the prayer is outdoors, dress for the weather. Early March can be cool in the morning in many places, so a layer you can remove later is wise. Check the forecast the night before so the morning holds no surprises.

Eid prayer etiquette

A few courtesies make the morning pleasant for everyone. Greet people warmly — "Eid Mubarak" is the greeting heard everywhere. Keep the area in front of the congregation clear, and do not walk in front of someone who is praying. If you arrive late, join the rows at the back rather than pushing forward.

Children are welcome and expected at Eid prayer, and a little noise from them is part of the day. Keep young children close, and bring something quiet to occupy them if they grow restless during the sermon. The congregation is a family occasion, and patience with children is part of its spirit.

After the prayer, the greetings begin in earnest. Shake hands, hug family and friends, and wish everyone well. It is customary to greet people you have not seen for a while, and to make peace with anyone you have fallen out with. Eid is a day for mending as well as celebrating.

After the prayer: visits and greetings

The prayer ends and the visiting begins. In most families, Eid day is a round of visits: parents first, then grandparents, relatives, friends and neighbours. Each visit brings food, sweets and conversation, and children collect Eidi — money or small gifts — at every stop.

Pace the day. It is easy to promise visits to everyone and end up exhausted by the afternoon. Agree a short list of the most important visits, and save the rest for the days that follow Eid. A relaxed visit is worth more than a rushed one.

Do not forget those who are alone. A neighbour who lives by themselves, an elder without family nearby, or a friend far from home will value a visit or a call on Eid day. Including them is one of the finest traditions of the festival.

If you miss the congregational prayer

Sometimes the morning does not go to plan: illness, travel, work, or a late arrival can mean missing the congregation. If that happens, do not let it spoil the day. Eid is a whole day of celebration, not a single prayer, and there is still much to enjoy.

In many traditions, the Eid prayer can be prayed at home, alone or with family, following the same form as the congregational prayer. Some scholars hold that it should be prayed in congregation, while others allow it at home. If you are unsure, ask your local imam or a scholar you trust.

Even if you miss the prayer entirely, the day is still Eid. Visit family, share food, give to those in need, and enjoy the celebration. The prayer is the best start to the day, but it is not the whole of it.

Questions people ask

What time is Eid prayer?

Eid prayer is prayed in the morning, after sunrise. Each mosque sets its own time, and larger communities often hold more than one session. Check your local mosque for its announced times.

How many rakats is Eid prayer?

Eid prayer is two rakats, with additional takbirs at the start of each rakat. The number of extra takbirs varies by tradition, so follow your imam.

Should I eat before Eid prayer?

It is customary to eat something sweet before leaving for Eid al-Fitr prayer, often dates. This marks the end of the fast and the start of the celebration.

Do I need to pay Zakat al-Fitr before the prayer?

Zakat al-Fitr is traditionally given before the Eid prayer, so that it reaches people in need in time for the day. Many people give it through their mosque in the last days of Ramadan.

Can children attend Eid prayer?

Yes. Children are welcome at Eid prayer, and it is a family occasion. Keep young children close and bring something quiet to occupy them during the sermon.

What should I wear to Eid prayer?

Your best clean clothes. Many people wear new or traditional outfits for Eid. Choose something comfortable enough to sit and pray in.
