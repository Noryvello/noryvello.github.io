How Much Food and Drink Do You Need for a Party?

Short answer: choose the number of guests, meal style and event length to calculate estimated food portions and drink servings for planning.

Planning assumptions

These are planning estimates, not catering standards. The calculator uses per-person starting amounts: light refreshments use smaller food portions; a buffet uses a moderate meal portion; a full meal uses a larger portion. Drink planning rises with event length. Adjust for weather, activity, children, alcohol-free preferences and the menu.

Practical food and drink checklist

  • Confirm vegetarian, vegan, allergy and religious dietary needs.
  • Offer water as well as other drinks.
  • Include a mix of filling items and lighter options.
  • Plan serving utensils, plates, cups, napkins and safe food storage.
  • Keep a small contingency for late arrivals or unexpectedly hungry guests.
Important limit: Quantities are estimates; dietary needs change them.

Questions people ask

How much food should I plan per party guest?

It depends on whether the event serves snacks, a buffet or a full meal. Use the calculator as a starting estimate and adapt it to the menu and guest needs.

How many drinks should I provide per person?

A useful planning estimate is around 2 servings per person for a short event, with more for longer events or hot conditions. Provide water and adjust to your guests.

Does a buffet need more food than snacks?

Usually a buffet is intended to function as a meal, so plan more substantial portions than for light refreshments.

Should I plan separate food for dietary needs?

Yes. Ask guests about allergies and dietary requirements in advance, label dishes clearly and avoid assuming one menu suits everyone.
