Car Dashboard Warning Lights Finder

Dashboard lights use colour and symbols to tell you what needs attention, from a loose fuel cap to a fault that needs a mechanic. Pick the light's colour and symbol below to see what it generally means and what to do next.

Pick the colour of the light, then the symbol that looks closest, and the finder will explain what it generally means and the safe next step.

This finder gives general meanings only: the exact meaning of a light varies by car. Always read your car's manual for your model, and for any red light, pull over safely and seek a mechanic rather than guessing. If a light is flashing, or the car feels wrong, stop driving.

How the colours work

Dashboard lights follow a simple colour code, and the colour tells you how urgent the message is before you even read the symbol. Red means act now: stop safely, switch off, or do not drive until the problem is checked. Amber or yellow means check soon: the car can usually be driven gently, but the problem needs attention and should not be ignored for long. Green and blue are information: something is switched on and working, such as headlights or cruise control.

Colour is only the first half of the message. The symbol tells you which system is involved, and the same colour can mean very different things in different systems. A red oil light and a red seat belt light both need attention, but only one of them means the engine is in danger. That is why this finder asks for both the colour and the symbol.

Red lights: act now

Red lights are the ones to take seriously at once. The oil pressure light, the coolant temperature light and the brake system light all mean something that can damage the car or make it unsafe to drive. If one of these comes on while you are driving, slow down, pull over somewhere safe, and switch the engine off. Do not try to drive to the next town to save time: a few more minutes of driving with no oil pressure or an overheating engine can turn a repair into a much bigger one.

Not every red light means danger to the car. A red seat belt light means a belt is unfastened, and a red door light means a door, boot or bonnet is open. Both are quick to fix. The rule is simple: if you do not know what a red light means, treat it as urgent, pull over safely, and check the manual before you drive on.

Amber lights: book a check soon

Amber lights mean something needs attention, but usually not this minute. The check engine light, the tyre pressure light and the ABS light are the common ones. You can usually drive on gently, avoid hard acceleration and high speeds, and book a check as soon as you can. Do not ignore an amber light for weeks: it is a warning, and warnings that are ignored tend to become red lights.

One exception is a flashing amber check engine light. A steady light means a fault to check soon; a flashing one usually means a serious misfire that can damage the car quickly. If the engine light is flashing, slow down and get the car checked without delay.

Green and blue: information

Green and blue lights are not warnings at all. They tell you that a system is on: headlights, indicators, cruise control, or main beam headlights, which are usually blue. If a green or blue light comes on, nothing is wrong. The only one worth a second thought is the blue main beam light, which is a reminder to dip your headlights for other drivers.

What to do if a light comes on while driving

First, stay calm and keep your eyes on the road. Note the colour and the symbol if you can, and notice how the car feels: any new noise, smell, vibration or loss of power matters. For a red light, pull over safely and switch off. For an amber light, drive gently and plan a check. When you are safely stopped, look the light up in the car's manual, which will tell you the exact meaning for your model. If several lights come on at once, or the car feels wrong in any way, do not guess: pull over and call for help.

Questions people ask

What does a red warning light mean?

Red usually means act now: stop safely, switch the engine off, or do not drive until the problem is checked. Some red lights are simple, like a seat belt or an open door, but if you do not know what a red light means, treat it as urgent and check the manual.

Can I keep driving with an amber light on?

Usually yes, gently. Amber means check soon rather than stop now. Avoid hard acceleration and high speeds, and book a check as soon as you can. If the light is flashing, or the car feels wrong, slow down and get it checked without delay.

Why do the lights come on and then go off when I start the car?

That is the car's self-check. When you turn the key or press start, the dashboard lights up so you can see that the bulbs work, then the lights go out one by one. A light that stays on after the engine is running is the one to pay attention to.

What if my light is not listed in this finder?

Use your car's manual, which lists every light for your exact model and year. Car makers change symbols between models, so the manual is the only source that is certain for your car.

What should I do if several lights come on at once?

Pull over safely and switch the engine off. Several lights at once often means a single electrical fault, such as a failing battery or alternator, but it can also mean more than one problem. Do not keep driving to find out which.
