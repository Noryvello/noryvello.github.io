Buy vs Rent Camping Gear

Whether to buy or rent camping gear depends on how often you go camping. Enter the purchase cost, the rental cost per trip, how many trips you take a year and how long you expect to keep the gear, and this page shows the break-even point and the yearly cost of each option.

This compares purchase and rental cost only. It leaves out cleaning, repairs, storage, transport of the gear, and the fact that rented gear is usually checked and cleaned between trips — and it assumes the rental cost stays the same.

How it works

The break-even point is the number of trips at which renting has cost as much as buying. It is the purchase cost divided by the rental cost per trip, rounded up to a whole trip. If you go camping more often than that over the gear's life, buying costs less overall. If you go less often, renting costs less.

The yearly figures spread the costs over one year. Buying per year is the purchase cost divided by the years you expect to keep the gear. Renting per year is the rental cost per trip times the trips per year. Your total trips over the gear's life are the trips per year times the years of use.

How to use the result

Compare your total trips with the break-even point first. That tells you which side wins over the whole life of the gear. Then look at the yearly costs, which show the same comparison one year at a time.

Be honest about how often you camp. Gear bought for one holiday and then left in storage rarely reaches its break-even point, while gear used several times a year usually passes it quickly. If you are unsure, run the calculator twice — once with a low number of trips and once with a high number — and see where the answer changes.

What this comparison leaves out

Buying costs more than the price tag. The calculator does not count cleaning, repairs or storage, and it does not count getting the gear to and from the campsite. Rented gear is usually checked and cleaned between trips, so it arrives ready to use.

It also assumes the rental cost stays the same and that you can always hire the gear when you need it. In busy seasons the gear you want may be booked out, so renting has a cost in planning and hassle that no number on this page captures.

Questions people ask

What is the break-even point?

The number of trips at which renting has cost as much as buying. Go camping more often than that and buying costs less overall. Go less often and renting costs less.

What if I rent different gear each trip?

Enter the rental cost of the same gear you would buy, so the two sides match.

Does buying include cleaning and repairs?

No. The calculator counts the purchase cost only, so allow for cleaning and repairs yourself if the gear needs them.

What if I camp more some years than others?

Enter your average trips per year. The yearly figures follow your average.

Why do I enter years of use?

To spread the purchase cost across the gear's life for the yearly comparison. Gear kept longer costs less per year.
