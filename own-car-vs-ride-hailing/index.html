Own Car or Ride-Hailing: Which Costs Less Each Month?

Short answer: enter your monthly car costs and expected ride-hailing use to compare both monthly totals in the same currency.

What to include in the comparison

Enter your own monthly costs in one consistent currency. Use either a monthly car loan payment or an estimated monthly depreciation allowance, not both. Add insurance, maintenance, parking, fuel, tolls and other recurring costs that apply to you. For ride-hailing, use your actual average fare and expected trips.

How to interpret the result

The calculator compares the monthly totals you enter. It does not assign a value to convenience, travel time, reliability, resale proceeds or the ability to use a car for trips that ride-hailing does not suit. If your driving changes seasonally, compare a typical month and a high-use month.

Important limit: Include insurance, maintenance, and parking in ownership cost or the comparison lies.

Questions people ask

Is owning a car always more expensive than ride-hailing?

No. The result depends on your ownership costs, trip frequency, ride fares, parking and driving needs. Enter your own figures to compare the two options.

What car costs should I include?

Include financing or depreciation, insurance, maintenance, repairs, parking, fuel, tolls, registration and other recurring costs relevant to your location.

Should I include the car purchase price?

For a monthly comparison, use a monthly loan payment or a clearly estimated monthly depreciation allowance. Avoid counting the full purchase price and monthly loan payments together.

Does this calculator include time or convenience?

No. It compares the monetary inputs only. Waiting time, flexibility, availability, comfort and trip suitability are personal trade-offs to consider separately.
