Eid al-Fitr Menu Ideas

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

Eid al-Fitr in 2027 is expected on Tuesday 9 March, subject to the sighting of the moon. This page gives you a full day of menu ideas for the celebration, with festive dishes for breakfast, lunch and dinner, plus planning and serving tips that keep the day relaxed.

Eid al-Fitr marks the end of Ramadan, a month of fasting, and food sits at the heart of the celebration. After weeks of early mornings and evening meals, the day itself is a chance to cook generously, gather the family, and share dishes that feel special. A good menu does not need to be complicated: it needs a little planning, a few dishes made ahead, and a table that welcomes everyone who drops in.

The pages below follow the shape of the day. Breakfast comes first, often sweet and light after the Eid prayer. Lunch is usually the biggest meal, when the whole family sits down together. Dinner can be lighter, built around leftovers and desserts, because by evening everyone is full and happy. Use the ideas as a starting point and adjust them to your family, your kitchen, and the number of guests you expect.

The date of Eid al-Fitr depends on the sighting of the moon, so Tuesday 9 March 2027 is expected rather than certain. Check with your local mosque for the confirmed date. Ask guests about allergies and dietary needs before you cook, and keep hot food hot and cold food cold through the day.

Planning your Eid menu around the day

Start by thinking about how your day will run. Most families begin with the Eid prayer in the morning, then come home to breakfast and visitors. Guests often arrive in waves rather than all at once, so food that can sit on the table and be served at any time works better than a meal that must be eaten the moment it is ready. Dishes that hold well, such as rice dishes, slow-cooked curries, and baked trays, are the backbone of an Eid menu.

Write your menu down a few days ahead and shop from a list. Decide which dishes you will cook fresh on the day and which you can prepare the day before. Anything that improves with resting, such as marinated meat or a chilled dessert, is a good candidate for the day before. Keep one or two simple dishes in reserve for unexpected guests, and remember that drinks, dates, and small snacks on the table cover the gaps between meals.

  • Plan the day in three parts: breakfast after the prayer, a main lunch, and a light dinner.
  • Choose dishes that hold well so guests can eat whenever they arrive.
  • Cook what you know well and try at most one new dish, so the day stays calm.
  • Keep a list of what is in the fridge so nothing is forgotten.

Eid breakfast ideas

Breakfast on Eid morning is a happy moment: the first morning meal in daylight after a month of fasting. Many families start with something sweet, such as dates and a milky dessert, before moving on to a fuller spread. Sheer khurma, a warm vermicelli pudding made with milk, dates, and nuts, is a much-loved Eid breakfast in many homes, and it can be made the night before and gently reheated.

For a bigger breakfast table, add pastries, fresh bread, eggs cooked any way your family likes, and a platter of fruit. Small savoury bites, such as samosas or stuffed pastries, work well for guests who arrive hungry from the prayer. Keep breakfast relaxed rather than grand: the main feast comes later, and a light start leaves everyone room to enjoy the rest of the day.

A festive Eid lunch

Lunch is usually the centrepiece of the Eid table, and a rice dish often anchors it. A fragrant biryani or pilaf, served in a large dish in the middle of the table, feeds a crowd and looks generous without much effort. Around it, add one or two slow-cooked dishes, such as a rich curry or roast meat, and keep the flavours balanced: something mild, something with a little heat, and something fresh.

Freshness matters on a rich table. A crisp salad, a bowl of yoghurt with herbs, and pickled vegetables cut through heavy dishes and give guests a choice. Bread is worth thinking about too: warm flatbreads or rolls are easy to keep coming from the kitchen and suit every dish on the table. Serve lunch in stages if your table is small, starting with salads and breads, then the rice and mains.

Eid dinner ideas

By dinner time, most people have eaten well and the mood is relaxed, so dinner can be lighter and simpler. This is the time for leftovers served with fresh bread and salad, or for a simple grilled dish with rice. Some families turn dinner into a second gathering, with a fresh pot of something slow-cooked for evening visitors.

If you expect guests in the evening, one easy option is a soup or stew that can simmer gently and be served whenever people arrive. Another is a platter of grilled meats and kebabs with flatbread, which feels festive without much cooking. Whatever you choose, keep the evening easy on yourself: you have already cooked all day, and a simple dinner is no less welcoming than a grand one.

Desserts and sweet treats

No Eid table is complete without something sweet. Many families bake or buy a mix of treats: syrup-soaked pastries, milk-based sweets, cakes, and biscuits that guests can take with tea. A large tray of one dessert is often easier than many small ones, and it looks generous on the table.

If you are making dessert yourself, choose something that can be prepared the day before, such as a baked custard, a chilled pudding, or a tray of biscuits. Keep a fruit platter on the table as well: fresh fruit balances the sweetness and gives children and lighter eaters something to enjoy. Small boxes or bags of sweets make a thoughtful gift for guests leaving in the evening.

Drinks and table touches

Drinks deserve a little thought, because a busy day means many cups of tea and many glasses of something cold. Keep a jug of water with lemon or mint on the table, alongside tea and coffee, and add one special drink for the day, such as a rose or mango lassi, a fruit punch, or sparkling water with fresh fruit. Children enjoy having their own special drink, and a decorated jug makes the table feel festive.

Small touches make the table feel like Eid without much cost or effort. A clean tablecloth, a bowl of dates in the middle, fresh flowers if you have them, and plates stacked ready for guests all help. If children are helping, let them fold napkins or arrange the fruit: small jobs make them part of the day.

Serving tips and make-ahead planning

The secret to a calm Eid is doing as much as possible before the day. Marinate meat the night before, chop vegetables and store them covered, cook rice dishes early and reheat them gently, and set the table the evening before if you can. Write a short plan for the day itself: what goes in the oven when, and what is served at each meal.

Serve food in the middle of the table so guests can help themselves, and keep serving spoons in every dish. Label dishes if any contain nuts, gluten, or dairy, so guests with allergies can choose safely. Finally, save some containers for the end of the day: sending guests home with a little food is one of the kindest Eid traditions, and it saves you from a fridge full of leftovers.

Questions people ask

When is Eid al-Fitr in 2027?

Eid al-Fitr in 2027 is expected on Tuesday 9 March, but the date depends on the sighting of the moon. Your local mosque will confirm the date, so check there before you finalise your plans.

What do people usually eat on Eid al-Fitr?

Many families start with something sweet, such as dates or a milk pudding, then share a festive lunch built around rice, meat, and salads. Desserts and tea follow through the day. The exact dishes vary from family to family and from country to country.

How far ahead should I plan my Eid menu?

A few days ahead is enough for most families. Write the menu, shop from a list, and prepare what you can the day before, such as marinades, desserts, and chopped vegetables. This leaves the day itself for cooking fresh dishes and enjoying your guests.

How do I cater for guests with different diets?

Ask ahead about allergies and dietary needs, and label dishes that contain nuts, gluten, or dairy. Offer at least one dish without meat and one without common allergens, and keep salads and fruit on the table so everyone has something to enjoy.

Can I prepare Eid dishes the day before?

Yes, many dishes are better for it. Marinades, slow-cooked dishes, chilled desserts, and baked treats can all be made ahead. Cook rice and fried foods fresh on the day where you can, and reheat prepared dishes gently.

What is sheer khurma?

Sheer khurma is a warm milk pudding made with fine vermicelli, dates, and nuts. It is a much-loved Eid dish in many South Asian homes and is often served for breakfast on Eid morning, sometimes made the night before.
