Flat Rate vs Reducing Balance

Short answer: at the same quoted rate, a flat-rate loan almost always costs more than a reducing-balance loan — because flat interest is charged on the full amount for the whole term. Type your numbers below and see both totals before you sign anything.

Information only — not financial advice. This comparison shows standard interest arithmetic only. Real loans add processing fees, insurance, late charges and early-repayment terms that change the final cost. Always read the lender's own schedule before signing.

Why the same rate costs different amounts

  • Flat rate: interest = principal × rate × years. You pay interest on the full original amount in every month, even in the last month when almost everything is repaid.
  • Reducing balance: interest each month = remaining balance × monthly rate. As you repay, the balance — and the interest — falls.
  • The trap: a flat 10% and a reducing 10% sound identical. They are not. The worked example below shows the gap on the same loan.

Worked example

Loan 100,000 at 10% per year for 12 months:

The flat-rate loan costs 4,500.94 more in interest — on the same amount, the same rate, and the same term. That is why the method matters more than the headline rate.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between flat rate and reducing balance?

On a flat rate, interest is charged on the full original loan amount for the whole term, even as you repay. On a reducing balance, interest is charged only on the amount you still owe, so the interest falls as the balance falls. At the same quoted rate, a flat-rate loan almost always costs more in total interest.

Why does a flat rate look cheaper than it is?

Because the quoted flat percentage is applied to the full principal for the entire term, while a reducing-balance rate applies to a shrinking balance. A 10% flat rate on a 12-month loan costs roughly the same total interest as a reducing-balance rate near 18%. The headline numbers are not comparable until both totals are worked out.

Which loan type is better?

Neither label is automatically better — only the total amount you repay matters. Compare the total interest and the monthly payment on the same principal and term, and check fees and early-repayment terms as well. This page only shows the arithmetic.
