General stain rules

  • Blot, do not rub. Rubbing pushes the stain deeper and can damage fibres.
  • Start with cold water for most stains. Heat can set protein stains such as blood, milk and egg.
  • Work from the outside in so the stain does not spread.
  • Check the care label before using any cleaner, and never mix bleach with other cleaners.

Questions people ask

Should I use hot or cold water on a stain?

Start with cold water for most stains, especially blood, milk and egg, because heat can set protein stains. Check the care label first — some fabrics and stains have their own rules.

What if the stain has already dried?

Dried stains are harder but often still treatable. Rewet the area with cold water, apply the recommended treatment, and allow extra soaking time before laundering. Repeat gently rather than scrubbing hard.

Can I use bleach on any stain?

No. Bleach can damage colours, silk, wool and many synthetics, and it can set some stains permanently. Use it only on bleach-safe whites when the care label allows it, and never mix bleach with other cleaners.

What should I do with silk or wool?

Treat silk and wool gently: blot, use mild detergent and cold water, and avoid wringing or harsh rubbing. For valuable or heavily stained items, a professional cleaner is the safer choice.
