The Day of Arafah

The Day of Arafah, Thursday 4 May 2028, is the most important day of the Hajj. On this day, pilgrims gather on the plain of Arafat, near Makkah, and spend the day in prayer and reflection. For pilgrims, standing at Arafat is the heart of the Hajj.

For Muslims who are not on Hajj, the Day of Arafah is a day of fasting and prayer. Fasting on this day is a widely observed voluntary fast, and the day is considered one of the best days of the year for dua.

What is Eid al-Adha?

Eid al-Adha commemorates the willingness of the Prophet Ibrahim to sacrifice his son in obedience to Allah, and Allah's mercy in providing a ram to sacrifice instead. The story is told in the Quran, and it is the reason for the sacrifice of Qurbani on Eid al-Adha.

The day begins with the Eid prayer in the morning. After the prayer, those who are able offer Qurbani — the sacrifice of an animal — and the meat is shared in three parts: for the family, for friends and relatives, and for those in need. The emphasis is on sharing: no one should go without meat on Eid.

Eid al-Adha is celebrated for four days in total — the day of Eid and the three days of Tashreeq that follow it. In 2028, the celebrations run from Friday 5 May through Monday 8 May, subject to the confirmed dates.
