How much less energy does an LED use, and why?

In plain words: an LED uses less energy because it needs fewer watts to produce light for the same hours. Energy use is wattage multiplied by time, so if the LED's wattage is lower and the hours are the same, the energy use is lower by the same proportion.

With the starting example above, a 9 watt LED uses 85% less energy than a 60 watt incandescent bulb, because (60 − 9) ÷ 60 = 0.85. That is 16.43 kWh a year for the LED, against 109.50 kWh a year for the incandescent bulb, at 5 hours a day for 365 days.

Why? An incandescent bulb makes light by heating a wire until it glows, so most of its electricity becomes heat. An LED makes light directly, with far less heat, so far fewer watts are needed for light. The calculator does not need to know how the light is made — it only needs the wattage and the hours — but that difference in how the bulbs work is why the wattages are so far apart.

How the maths works

Energy use in kilowatt-hours (kWh) is: watts × hours per day × days per year ÷ 1000. The ÷ 1000 turns watts into kilowatts.

Running cost is: energy (kWh) × your cost per kWh. Enter your own cost per kWh from your bill. The calculator uses no currency symbol, so the result is in whatever currency your rate is in.

Saving is: incandescent energy minus LED energy. Percentage less is: saving ÷ incandescent energy × 100.

Change any input and the results update at once. Use the reset button to go back to the starting example.

Worked examples

Each example below uses the same formula as the calculator. Results are rounded to 2 decimal places.

Example 1 — 9 watt LED vs 60 watt incandescent, 5 hours a day, 365 days a year, cost 1 per kWh.
LED: 9 × 5 × 365 ÷ 1000 = 16.425 kWh, shown as 16.43 kWh. Cost: 16.425 × 1 = 16.425, shown as 16.43.
Incandescent: 60 × 5 × 365 ÷ 1000 = 109.50 kWh. Cost: 109.50 × 1 = 109.50.
Saving: 109.50 − 16.425 = 93.075 kWh, shown as 93.08 kWh, which is 85% less energy. Cost saving: 93.075 × 1 = 93.075, shown as 93.08.

Example 2 — 12 watt LED vs 75 watt incandescent, 4 hours a day, 300 days a year, cost 2 per kWh.
LED: 12 × 4 × 300 ÷ 1000 = 14.40 kWh. Cost: 14.40 × 2 = 28.80.
Incandescent: 75 × 4 × 300 ÷ 1000 = 90.00 kWh. Cost: 90.00 × 2 = 180.00.
Saving: 90.00 − 14.40 = 75.60 kWh, which is 84% less energy. Cost saving: 180.00 − 28.80 = 151.20.

Example 3 — 7 watt LED vs 40 watt incandescent, 3 hours a day, 365 days a year, cost 1.5 per kWh.
LED: 7 × 3 × 365 ÷ 1000 = 7.665 kWh, shown as 7.67 kWh. Cost: 7.665 × 1.5 = 11.4975, shown as 11.50.
Incandescent: 40 × 3 × 365 ÷ 1000 = 43.80 kWh. Cost: 43.80 × 1.5 = 65.70.
Saving: 43.80 − 7.665 = 36.135 kWh, shown as 36.14 kWh, which is 82.5% less energy. Cost saving: 65.70 − 11.4975 = 54.2025, shown as 54.20.

Which is better, and when?

For running cost, the lower-wattage bulb is better whenever both bulbs are used for the same hours — and in this comparison that is the LED, because its wattage is lower. The saving grows with more hours per day and more days per year: a bulb left on for 5 hours a day saves more in a year than the same bulb used for 1 hour a day, because the wattage gap is multiplied by more hours.

The LED is the clear choice for a bulb that is on for several hours a day, every day. For a bulb that is rarely on — for example, a cupboard light used for a few minutes a week — the yearly saving is small in absolute terms, even though the percentage saving is the same, so the running-cost advantage matters less. The calculator shows both the percentage and the actual yearly saving, so you can judge the size of the saving for your own use.

This verdict is about running cost only. It does not say which bulb to buy, because buying cost, brightness, fitting and how long a bulb lasts are not part of this calculation. If the two wattages you enter are equal, the calculator will show no saving, which is correct: equal watts for equal hours means equal energy use.

Questions people ask

How much less energy does an LED use than an incandescent bulb?

It depends on the wattages, but a common swap is a 9 watt LED replacing a 60 watt incandescent bulb, which uses 85% less energy for the same hours of light. The saving in percent is simply the wattage difference divided by the old bulb's wattage, so enter your own bulbs above to see your exact figure.

Why does an LED cost less to run?

An LED turns more of its electricity into light, while an incandescent bulb turns most of its electricity into heat. Because the LED needs far fewer watts to give light for the same hours, it uses fewer kilowatt-hours over the year, so the running cost is lower.

What cost per kWh should I enter?

Enter the rate from your own electricity bill, in your own currency, without a currency symbol. The maths works with any currency, and the calculator uses your rate only to turn kilowatt-hours into a running cost.

Does this calculator include the price of the bulbs?

No. It compares running cost only, based on wattage, hours per day and days per year. It does not include buying the bulbs, fitting, or any other costs.
