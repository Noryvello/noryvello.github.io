Budget Trip Cost Comparison

This page compares the total cost of two trip styles, using numbers you enter yourself. Enter transport, stay and food costs for each style, and the calculator shows both trip totals with a breakdown of where the saving comes from.

How the comparison works

A trip total is really three totals added together: getting there, staying there and eating while you are there. The calculator works out each style the same way: transport plus stay cost times nights plus food cost times days. Because the two styles can have different lengths, you enter nights and days separately for each, so a short comfortable trip can be compared fairly with a longer budget one. The result shows both totals and the difference, and the saving breakdown splits that difference into transport, stay and food, so you can see which part of the trip is doing the saving. Every figure comes from your own quotes, so the comparison matches the trip you are actually planning.

This page uses no live fares and no stated prices. Totals depend entirely on the numbers you enter, and a cheaper total is not always the better trip. Activities, insurance and visas are not included, so add them to your own planning if they apply.

How to use the saving breakdown

The breakdown matters more than the headline total. If most of the saving comes from the stay, a cheaper room on the dearer style may close the gap without changing how you travel or eat. If transport is the gap, a different route or travel time may be worth a look. Try changing one figure at a time: raise the food budget on the cheaper style to see whether the saving survives a few good meals, or shorten the dearer trip by a night to see what the saving becomes. The calculator is a planning aid, not a booking tool, so use it to shape the trip before you commit to any of the numbers.

What the totals do not include

Three costs sit outside both totals, and they can be large. Activities and entry fees vary so much by destination that they belong in your own planning rather than in a general comparison. Travel insurance and visa costs apply to some trips and not others, so they are left out too. Small daily extras, such as local transport, tips and souvenirs, add up over a trip and are easy to forget. If you want them in the comparison, add your best estimate to the transport box of each style, and the breakdown will carry it through.

Questions people ask

What counts as transport cost?

Enter everything that gets you there and back, such as fares or fuel, plus transfers if you know them. If you are comparing two ways of travelling, put each option under its own style.

Should nights and days be the same number?

Not always. A trip of 3 nights usually has 4 days of food. Enter the numbers as they are for each style, even if the two styles differ.

Can I compare two different destinations?

Yes. The calculator compares any two sets of numbers, so you can compare two destinations, two travel styles or two times of year.

Why are there no fares or hotel prices here?

Fares and room prices change constantly and vary by route and date. Entering your own quotes keeps the comparison true to the trip you are planning.

Does the cheaper style always win?

No. The calculator only adds up money. Comfort, time, safety and what you enjoy matter too, and only you can weigh them.
