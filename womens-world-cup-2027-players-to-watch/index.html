Women's World Cup 2027 Players to Watch

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

The Women's World Cup 2027 in Brazil will showcase the best players in the women's game, from Spain's celebrated midfield to the United States' new generation of attackers. This guide introduces the stars and rising names to follow, with the reminder that final squads are named closer to the tournament, so no place is confirmed yet.

The tournament runs from 24 June to 25 July 2027, with 32 teams playing 64 matches across eight Brazilian cities. The final draw takes place in December 2026, and qualification is still running, with the last places decided by an inter-confederation play-off in March 2027. Until the squads are named, nobody can say for certain who will be on the plane to Brazil.

That is what makes a watchlist useful. The players below are established international stars and widely known rising names whose club and international form is worth following in the months before the tournament. They are grouped by what they do best, and described in general terms: their role, their style, and their reputation. Where a player is carrying an injury or fighting for a place, form and fitness will decide what happens next.

The playmakers who run the game

Every great team has a player who sets the rhythm, and the 2027 tournament should be rich in them. Spain's midfield has set the standard in recent years, built around players who keep the ball under pressure, find passes through tight spaces, and control the pace of a match. Aitana Bonmatí is the best known of them, a midfielder whose close control and ability to arrive in the box have made her one of the most decorated players of her generation. Alongside her, Alexia Putellas brings experience, leadership and a habit of scoring important goals from midfield.

Japan's Yui Hasegawa offers a different kind of control: tidy, intelligent and always available for a pass, she is the sort of player teammates look for when a game needs calming down. Watch for how these playmakers are used. Some teams build everything through one midfielder; others share the job across two or three. The teams that protect their playmakers best, and give them runners ahead of the ball, usually go furthest.

The goalscorers

Goals decide tournaments, and the list of forwards to watch is long. Spain's Salma Paralluelo combines pace with a directness that frightens defenders, and she has already scored on the biggest stages while still young. England's Alessia Russo leads the line with strength and clever movement, holding the ball up for teammates as well as finishing herself. Australia's Sam Kerr, when fit, is one of the most feared finishers in the game, a striker whose heading and movement in the box have produced goals in every competition she has played.

Zambia's Barbra Banda brings raw power and speed, and has shown she can score against the best defences in the world. The point about all of these players is not just that they score, but how they score: some thrive on crosses, some on through balls, some by creating chances out of nothing. How each team's forwards match up against the defences they meet is one of the great stories of any World Cup.

The wingers and wide threats

Modern women's football is played at high speed out wide, and the 2027 tournament will feature some of the fastest players in the game. The United States' Trinity Rodman is a winger who attacks defenders one against one and creates chances for herself and others. France's Kadidiatou Diani offers a similar threat with her pace and direct running, and has been one of her country's most reliable attackers for years.

Colombia's Linda Caicedo is one of the most exciting young attackers in the world game, a player who glides past opponents and scores goals that look effortless. Australia's Mary Fowler, still young, has already shown she can change a match from wide areas or from behind the striker. Wide players matter because they stretch defences, win the corners and free kicks that lead to goals, and give their teams a way out when the middle of the pitch is crowded.

The defenders and goalkeepers who win tournaments

Attackers win headlines, but defenders and goalkeepers win tournaments. The United States' Naomi Girma has become one of the most respected centre-backs in the game, a defender who reads play early, rarely gets beaten, and starts attacks with calm passing from the back. England's Lucy Bronze brings experience and attacking energy from full-back, a player who has played in the biggest matches club and international football can offer.

France's Wendie Renard, one of the tallest and most commanding defenders in the game, remains a threat at both ends of the pitch, especially from set pieces. In goal, the tournament will showcase keepers whose shot-stopping and command of the box decide tight knockout matches; Nigeria's Chiamaka Nnadozie and Germany's Ann-Katrin Berger are among the established names whose form is worth following. A goalkeeper in form can carry a team through a tournament almost single-handed.

The rising names

Every World Cup produces a player the wider world had not yet noticed. The Netherlands' Wieke Kaptein is a young midfielder whose passing range and composure have already marked her out as a future star. Germany's Jule Brand offers pace and creativity from wide areas and is still improving. Keep an eye, too, on the younger players coming through in the United States and England, where deep squads mean competition for places is fierce and a strong club season can earn a young player a seat on the plane.

Rising players matter for another reason: they change how opponents prepare. A team built around one famous forward can be planned against; a team with two or three young attackers nobody has yet solved is much harder to defend. The group stage is often where these players announce themselves, and the knockout rounds are where they become stars.

Brazil's home hopes

Brazil will carry the hopes of a host nation, and the home crowd at the Maracanã for the opening match on 24 June 2027 will be one of the great occasions of the tournament. Brazil's most famous player, Marta, said farewell to international football after the Paris 2024 Olympics, so the home tournament is expected to belong to a new generation. Debinha, a skilful and experienced attacker, is among the senior players whose creativity Brazil will lean on, while Kerolin and Geyse offer pace and goals.

Playing at home is both a gift and a burden. The support is enormous, but so is the expectation, and Brazil's group matches in Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo and Brasília will be played in front of crowds that demand attacking football. How the home side handles that pressure, and how quickly its younger players settle, will shape the whole tournament.

How to follow the stars before the tournament

The best way to follow these players is through their club football in the months before June 2027, when form is made and lost week by week. Domestic leagues and continental club competitions are where players build rhythm, and where injuries and loss of form can change a squad picture in a matter of weeks. International friendlies and the remaining qualifiers, including the play-off in March 2027, are the other place to watch, because national coaches use them to test combinations.

After the final draw in December 2026, the picture sharpens: groups are known, routes to the final can be traced, and the match-ups between star players start to take shape. Squad announcements follow closer to the tournament. Until then, enjoy the guessing, but remember that a watchlist is a starting point, not a promise.

Questions people ask

Squads are named closer to the tournament, in the weeks before it begins on 24 June 2027. No squad places are confirmed yet, and selection depends on form, fitness and each coach's plans.

No favourite can fairly be named before the final draw in December 2026 sets the groups. The goalscorers to watch include established finishers such as Sam Kerr, Alessia Russo and Barbra Banda, and rising scorers such as Salma Paralluelo.

Will Marta play at the 2027 World Cup?

Marta said farewell to international football after the Paris 2024 Olympics, so she is not expected to play in Brazil. The home tournament is expected to belong to a new generation of Brazilian players.

Which young players could break out at the tournament?

Names to follow include Linda Caicedo of Colombia, Mary Fowler of Australia, Wieke Kaptein of the Netherlands and Jule Brand of Germany. Every World Cup also produces a breakout star few people saw coming.

How does the draw affect which stars meet each other?

The final draw in December 2026 decides the eight groups, which in turn decides the possible routes to the final. Until the draw, nobody knows which star players will meet in the group stage or the knockouts.

Where can I watch the matches?

Broadcasters differ from country to country, and schedules are confirmed closer to the tournament. Check your local listings and the official tournament channels as June 2027 approaches.
