Cloud Storage or an External Drive: Which Costs Less Per Year?

Short answer: enter the storage you need and your own plan and drive costs to compare estimated yearly spending and capacity.

How the yearly estimate works

Cloud cost is the monthly plan cost multiplied by 12, plus any yearly extras you enter. External-drive cost is the purchase cost divided by the useful-life years you enter. The calculator checks whether the drive capacity meets your stated storage need; it does not price multiple drives or RAID systems.

What cost cannot tell you

  • Cloud storage may include syncing, sharing and access from multiple devices.
  • An external drive may work without an internet connection but needs physical care.
  • Upload speeds, download limits, privacy terms and recovery options differ by provider and device.
  • Budget for a second copy if the files matter.
Important limit: Drives fail and accounts get locked; important files need two copies in two places.

Questions people ask

How much cloud storage should I compare?

Choose the amount of storage you actually need, then enter a cloud plan cost and an external drive capacity and cost that can hold at least that amount.

Does the calculator include an external drive's replacement cost?

It spreads the drive purchase cost over the useful-life years you enter. This is an estimate and does not predict when a drive will fail.

Are cloud subscriptions and external drives directly equivalent?

Not completely. Cloud plans can include syncing and remote access, while a drive is local storage. Compare the features and access needs as well as the annualized cost.

Is one copy of my files enough?

No single storage method is risk-free. Keep important files in two copies in two places, and periodically check that backups can be opened.
