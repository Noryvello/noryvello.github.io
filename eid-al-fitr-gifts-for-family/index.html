Eid al-Fitr Gifts for Family

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

Eid al-Fitr is a time for generosity, and the best gifts are the ones chosen with the person in mind rather than the price tag. This guide has gift ideas for parents, siblings, children and elders, organised by budget. Every idea is simple to find or make, with tips for wrapping and giving.

Giving is woven into Eid al-Fitr. After a month of fasting and charity, the festival is a time to be generous with the people closest to you — and the gifts do not need to be grand. A thoughtful present, chosen with care, means more than an expensive one chosen in a hurry.

This guide is organised by person: parents, siblings, children and elders, with ideas for small, modest and generous budgets. Use it as a starting point, and let your knowledge of each person do the rest. Eid al-Fitr 2027 is expected on Tuesday 9 March, so there is time to choose well.

This page never states prices, because prices change and differ everywhere. Set a budget you are comfortable with, and check the seller before you buy. Eid al-Fitr 2027 is expected on Tuesday 9 March, depending on the sighting of the moon.

What makes a good Eid gift

A good Eid gift has three qualities: it suits the person, it suits the occasion, and it was chosen with thought. A gift that shows you noticed what someone likes — their favourite scent, a book they mentioned, a colour they wear — will always beat a generic present, whatever it costs.

Think about what the person would enjoy rather than what is easy to buy. A parent who loves tea, a sibling who is always cold, a child who is mad about drawing: each of them has a gift waiting in what you already know about them. The best Eid shopping starts with a little thought, not a trip to the shops.

Presentation matters too. A gift wrapped with care, with a short handwritten note, feels twice as generous as the same gift handed over in a bag. Eid is a festival of beauty as well as generosity, and the wrapping is part of the gift.

It also helps to start thinking about gifts early in Ramadan, rather than in the last few days. A list made in the first week — one line per person, with an idea or two beside each name — turns gift shopping from a rush into a pleasure. You can buy a little at a time, spread the effort across the month, and still have the final days free for worship and family. If an idea does not come for someone straight away, leave the line blank and come back to it; the right idea often arrives when you are not looking for it.

Finally, remember that Eid gifts are not only for family. A neighbour, a friend far from home, a colleague who is fasting alone — a small gift for someone outside the family circle is one of the kindest gestures of the festival. Generosity that reaches beyond the people you already love is the generosity Ramadan teaches.

Gifts for parents

Parents are often the hardest people to buy for, because they say they want nothing. The answer is to give them comfort and pleasure: something that makes their days nicer. A beautiful prayer mat, a good quality shawl, a set of fine teas or coffees, or a framed family photograph are all gifts that parents treasure.

Experiences make fine gifts too. A meal out together, a day trip, or an afternoon spent with the family can mean more than any object. If your parents live far away, a long call on Eid morning, a parcel sent in good time, or a video call with the grandchildren will brighten their day.

For parents who have everything, consider a gift in their name: a donation to a cause they care about, or a tree planted, or a contribution to a community project. Tell them about it in a card, and the gift becomes a story they can share.

Gifts for siblings

Siblings are the easiest people to shop for and the hardest to surprise. The trick is to buy for who they are now, not who they were as children. A sibling who has taken up cooking will love good ingredients or a useful kitchen tool. A sibling who travels will appreciate something light and practical. A sibling who reads will always welcome a book.

Shared memories make good gifts. A photograph from childhood, framed; a playlist of songs from family holidays; or a small item that recalls a family joke — these are the gifts siblings keep. They cost little and mean a great deal, because only a sibling could have chosen them.

If your siblings have children, a gift for the family can be better than a gift for one person: a board game, a puzzle, or a baking set the whole household can enjoy. Family gifts are practical, generous and easy to choose.

Gifts for children

Children are the heart of Eid giving. Money — Eidi — is the classic gift, given in a small envelope or a decorative pouch, and children love it. But a chosen gift alongside the money makes the day special: a book, a craft set, a toy they have been wanting, or something to wear.

Choose by age and interest rather than by what is fashionable. A young child loves anything they can hold, build or draw with. An older child appreciates something that respects their growing tastes — a book in a series they follow, a sports item, or a creative kit. Ask their parents if you are unsure; they will be glad you asked.

Keep it joyful and simple. One good gift, beautifully wrapped, beats a pile of things. And remember that children watch how gifts are given: a warm word, a hug, and genuine pleasure in their excitement are part of the present.

Gifts for elders

Elders deserve gifts chosen with respect and warmth. Comfort is the theme: a soft shawl, warm slippers, a comfortable cushion, or a set of good teas. Practical gifts are welcome too — a large-print book, a simple device that helps them stay in touch, or help with something around the house.

Time is the greatest gift for many elders. A visit on Eid day, a shared meal, or an afternoon of conversation will be remembered long after objects are forgotten. If distance makes visiting hard, a parcel and a long call can carry the same warmth.

Include elders in the celebration rather than giving from a distance. Ask them to tell stories, involve them in the cooking, and let the children spend time with them. Eid is a festival of generations, and the elders are its heart.

Gifts by budget: small, modest and generous

A small budget is no barrier to a lovely Eid gift. Homemade treats, a handwritten card, a small plant, a scented candle, or a carefully chosen book all make delightful presents. What matters is the thought and the wrapping, not the size of the gift.

A modest budget opens up more choice: a good quality item of clothing, a set of something the person enjoys, a board game for the family, or a beautiful household item. This is the range where most Eid gifts sit, and it is more than enough for a generous celebration.

A generous budget calls for something lasting: a piece of jewellery, a fine garment, a quality item for the home, or a shared experience. Even then, the thought matters most. A generous gift chosen carelessly is worth less than a small gift chosen with love.

Wrapping and giving with heart

Wrapping is part of the Eid gift. Use paper in festive colours, add a ribbon, and tuck in a short handwritten note. A note that says why you chose the gift — "I saw this and thought of you" — turns an ordinary present into a keepsake.

Give gifts at a moment when they can be enjoyed. After the Eid meal, when the family is together and relaxed, is the natural time. Let children open theirs with excitement, and give the adults theirs with a word of thanks. The giving itself is a small ceremony, and it deserves an unhurried moment.

Finally, remember that giving is not a competition. There is no need to match what others give, and no need to give to everyone. Give what you can, with a glad heart, and the festival will be generous in return.

Questions people ask

How much should I spend on Eid gifts?

Whatever fits your budget comfortably. Thought matters far more than cost, and a small gift chosen with care will always be appreciated. Families often agree on spending limits to keep things relaxed.

Is money a good Eid gift?

Yes. Money, known as Eidi, is a much-loved Eid tradition, especially for children. Presented in a small envelope or decorative pouch, it is a gift every child enjoys.

What if I live far from my family?

Send gifts early so they arrive before Eid, and follow up with a call or video call on the day. A parcel sent with love, plus your voice on the phone, carries the celebration across any distance.

Should children get gifts from everyone?

It is kind, but not required. Many families agree that each child receives one gift from each household, or that the adults give money and one relative gives a chosen gift.

Are handmade gifts a good idea?

Yes. Handmade gifts are often the most remembered Eid presents. Baking, crafts, a knitted item, or a handmade card all carry a personal touch that shop-bought gifts cannot match.

When should I buy Eid gifts?

Start early in Ramadan to avoid the last-week rush. Buying a little at a time through the month spreads the cost and the effort, and leaves the final days free for worship and family.
