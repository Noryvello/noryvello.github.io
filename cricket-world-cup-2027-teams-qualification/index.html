Cricket World Cup 2027 Teams and Qualification

Fourteen teams will play at the 2027 Cricket World Cup in South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia, from 2 October to 21 November 2027. Ten teams qualified directly, as hosts or through the ICC ODI rankings, and four more come through the World Cup Qualifier. This page lists the two groups, explains how each team got in, and explains the Super Series that settles the final group-stage place.

The 2027 Cricket World Cup is hosted by South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia under the brand "Three Nations, One Heartbeat". It is the first men's Cricket World Cup staged in Africa since 2003, and 14 teams will take part across 57 matches. Ten of those teams qualified directly, and four more come through the World Cup Qualifier, so the field is a mix of host nations, the highest-ranked sides in one-day international cricket, and the teams that fought through the qualifying tournament.

Qualification for the tournament is built around the ICC ODI rankings. Eight of the direct places went to the highest-ranked teams on 30 September 2026, the cut-off date, and the two host nations, South Africa and Zimbabwe, took the other two direct places. The remaining four places are decided by the World Cup Qualifier, which also decides the last two names in the group stage: the Qualifier winner takes the place marked Qualifier A, and the winner of the Super Series takes the place marked Qualifier B.

Qualifier A and Qualifier B are placeholders, not team names. They stay in the group lists until the World Cup Qualifier and the Super Series are decided, and they will be replaced by the teams that earn those places.

How teams qualify for 2027

Ten teams qualify directly. Two of those places belong to the hosts: South Africa and Zimbabwe qualified automatically because they are hosting the tournament alongside Namibia. The other eight direct places went to the highest-ranked teams in the ICC ODI rankings at the cut-off date of 30 September 2026. A team's ranking on that single date decided whether it qualified directly or had to go through the Qualifier, so the cut-off was the most important date in the whole qualifying process.

The remaining four places come through the World Cup Qualifier. The Qualifier is a separate tournament in which the teams that did not qualify directly play for the last places in the field. The Qualifier winner goes straight into the group stage, where it takes the place marked Qualifier A. The teams that finish 2nd, 3rd and 4th in the Qualifier do not go straight through: they go to the Super Series, a round robin played from 2 to 6 October in Windhoek, and only the winner of that round robin advances to the group stage, as Qualifier B.

That means the road to the group stage has three routes: hosting, the rankings, and the Qualifier. Ten teams took the first two routes, and four teams took the third, which makes the 14-team field. The two placeholder names in the groups, Qualifier A and Qualifier B, are the last two pieces of the puzzle, and they are filled by the Qualifier winner and the Super Series winner.

The two groups

The 12 group-stage teams are split into two groups of six. Group A contains India, Australia, Pakistan, Afghanistan, Zimbabwe and Qualifier A. Group B contains New Zealand, South Africa, Sri Lanka, England, Bangladesh and Qualifier B. Each group has five named teams and one placeholder, so one place in each group stays open until the Qualifier and the Super Series are decided.

The hosts are split between the groups: Zimbabwe plays in Group A and South Africa plays in Group B. The group stage runs from 7 to 24 October and has 30 matches, with each team playing the other five teams in its group. The two placeholders sit in different groups, so the Qualifier winner and the Super Series winner do not meet each other in the group stage.

How each team got in

Every named team in the groups reached the tournament through one of the three routes. Here is how each one got in:

  • India — qualified directly through the ICC ODI rankings at the cut-off of 30 September 2026.
  • Australia — qualified directly through the ICC ODI rankings at the cut-off of 30 September 2026.
  • Pakistan — qualified directly through the ICC ODI rankings at the cut-off of 30 September 2026.
  • Afghanistan — qualified directly through the ICC ODI rankings at the cut-off of 30 September 2026.
  • Zimbabwe — qualified directly as a host nation.
  • New Zealand — qualified directly through the ICC ODI rankings at the cut-off of 30 September 2026.
  • South Africa — qualified directly as a host nation.
  • Sri Lanka — qualified directly through the ICC ODI rankings at the cut-off of 30 September 2026.
  • England — qualified directly through the ICC ODI rankings at the cut-off of 30 September 2026.
  • Bangladesh — qualified directly through the ICC ODI rankings at the cut-off of 30 September 2026.
  • Qualifier A — the winner of the World Cup Qualifier, who goes straight into the group stage.
  • Qualifier B — the winner of the Super Series in Windhoek, who advances to the group stage.

Counting the routes gives the full picture: two hosts, eight teams through the rankings, and four teams through the Qualifier, which adds up to 14. The eight rankings places went to India, Australia, Pakistan, Afghanistan, New Zealand, Sri Lanka, England and Bangladesh, while the two host places went to South Africa and Zimbabwe. Namibia, the third host country, does not take a host place in the field: the host places went to South Africa and Zimbabwe only.

The Super Series

The Super Series is the last step on the road to the group stage. It is a round robin played from 2 to 6 October 2027 in Windhoek, and it involves the three lowest qualifier teams: the teams that finished 2nd, 3rd and 4th in the World Cup Qualifier. Each of the three teams plays the other two, and the winner of the round robin advances to the group stage as Qualifier B.

The Super Series is played just before the group stage begins on 7 October, so the last group-stage place is settled only days before the group stage starts. For the three teams involved, it is a second chance: they missed out on the direct route through the Qualifier, because only the winner goes straight through, but one of them still reaches the group stage by winning the round robin. The other two teams go home, and the tournament continues with the 12 group-stage teams.

The format in brief

The group stage runs 7–24 October and has 30 matches. The top 3 in each group plus the best 4th-place team advance, and points reset for the Super 7 stage. The full format is covered in the format guide linked in the Related section below.
