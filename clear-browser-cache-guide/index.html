What the cache is

When you visit a website, your browser downloads images, style files, and scripts so it can show you the page. To save time on your next visit, it keeps copies of those files on your device. That store of copies is the cache. The next time you open the same site, the browser loads the files from the cache instead of downloading them again, which is why familiar pages open quickly.

The cache is meant to be invisible, and most of the time it is. Problems start when a site changes a file but your browser keeps using the old copy. Then a page can look broken, show yesterday's content, or refuse to load a new feature. The same thing can happen if a cached file is damaged. Clearing the cache forces the browser to download fresh copies, which fixes most of these problems at once.

What clearing the cache does and does not do

Clearing the cache removes the saved copies of files. It does not remove your bookmarks, your history, or your saved passwords, unless you tick those boxes too. The clear-browsing-data panel in every browser lists several kinds of data with tick boxes, and you choose which to remove. For most problems, ticking only cached images and files is enough, and it is the safest choice.

What clearing does not do is log you out of websites. Signing in is handled by cookies, which are a separate kind of data. If you tick cookies and site data as well, you will be signed out of most sites and will need to sign in again, and any shopping baskets or site preferences stored in cookies will be lost. If you only want to fix a broken page, leave cookies alone.

What you lose when you clear it

The main thing you lose is speed, and only for a short while. The first time you visit each site after clearing, the browser has to download everything again, so pages load a little more slowly. After that first visit the cache fills up again and pages are fast as before. On a slow connection this first load can be noticeable, so it is better to clear the cache when you have a little time, not in the middle of something urgent.

You may also lose offline access to pages you had open. Some sites work offline from the cache, and clearing it removes those saved pages. If you tick more than the cache box, you can lose more: history, cookies, saved form entries, and site settings. That is why it is worth reading the tick boxes before you press clear, rather than ticking everything and hoping for the best.

After you clear the cache

Close the browser completely and open it again after clearing, so the change takes full effect. Then visit the page that was giving trouble and check whether it looks right. If it does, the stale cache was the cause. If the page still looks wrong, the problem is probably with the site itself or with your connection, and clearing the cache again will not help.

Give the browser a little time on the first visit to each site, since it is downloading fresh files. If you cleared cookies as well, sign back in to the sites you use. If a site still misbehaves, try it in a private window: if it works there, an extension or a saved setting may be the cause rather than the cache.

Clearing the cache only removes saved copies of files. It does not remove viruses, fix a slow connection, or make your computer faster in general. If your browser is slow on every site, the cause is more likely your connection, an extension, or a full device, and clearing the cache will not solve it.

Questions people ask

Will clearing my cache delete my passwords?

No, if you only clear cached images and files. Saved passwords are a separate option in the clear-browsing-data panel. Only tick the password box if you mean to remove saved passwords.

Will clearing the cache log me out of websites?

Clearing cached images and files alone does not log you out. If you also tick cookies and site data, you will be logged out of most sites and will need to sign in again.

How often should I clear my browser cache?

There is no fixed schedule. Clear it when a page looks broken, shows old content, or will not load after an update. Clearing it every day is not needed and makes pages load more slowly at first.

Does clearing the cache make my browser faster?

It can help if the cache is very large or has a damaged file in it, but it usually makes the next visit to each site a little slower, because the browser has to download everything again. It is a fix for problems, not a speed boost.

What is the difference between cache and cookies?

The cache stores copies of files like images so pages load faster. Cookies store small pieces of information a site sets, such as whether you are signed in or what is in your basket. They are cleared separately.
