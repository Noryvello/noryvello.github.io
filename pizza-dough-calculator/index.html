How baker's percentages work

Bakers weigh ingredients as a share of the flour, with flour always at 100 percent. Water at 65 percent means 65 g of water for every 100 g of flour. Salt at 2 percent means 2 g of salt for every 100 g of flour. Because everything is tied to the flour, a recipe scales cleanly: double the flour and every other weight doubles with it.

This calculator works backwards from the size of your pizzas. It first works out how much dough each pizza needs from its area, then splits that dough into flour, water, salt and yeast using the percentages. The working below the result shows each step, so you can see exactly where the numbers come from.

Choosing your hydration

Hydration is the share of water relative to flour, and it changes how the dough feels and how the crust bakes. At 60 percent, the dough is firm and easy to shape, which suits beginners and anyone shaping by hand for the first time. At 65 percent, the dough is softer and the crumb is more open. At 70 percent, the dough is wet and sticky to handle, but the baked crust is light and airy.

Your flour matters as much as the number. Strong bread flour absorbs more water than plain flour, so the same hydration can feel different from one bag to another. If your dough feels too sticky to shape, add flour a little at a time. If it feels stiff and tears, add water a spoonful at a time. Trust your hands over the numbers.

Using your dough

Mix the flour, water, salt and yeast, then knead until the dough is smooth and springs back when poked. Cover it and let it rise until doubled in size, then divide it into balls of the weight shown and let them rest again before shaping. A rested dough stretches without tearing and without springing back.

Shape each ball by pressing from the centre outwards, leaving a thicker rim for the crust. Avoid a rolling pin if you want an airy edge: pressing by hand keeps the air in the dough. Top lightly, since too much topping weighs the base down, and bake on the hottest setting your oven allows.

Questions people ask

What are baker's percentages?

Baker's percentages express every ingredient as a share of the flour weight, with flour always at 100 percent. Water at 65 percent means 65 g of water for every 100 g of flour, which makes any recipe easy to scale.

Which hydration should I pick?

Pick 60 percent if you are new to pizza dough, since it is easy to handle. Pick 65 percent for a softer crumb, and 70 percent for a light, airy crust once you are comfortable with sticky dough.

What flour works best for pizza dough?

A strong bread flour or a flour sold for pizza works well, because it builds the gluten that gives the crust its chew. Plain flour makes a softer crust and absorbs less water.

How much yeast does the calculator use?

The calculator uses instant yeast at 0.5 percent of the flour weight. If you use active dry yeast, dissolve it in a little of the water first. For a long, slow rise, use less yeast and give the dough more time.

Can I make the dough ahead of time?

Yes. After mixing and kneading, the dough keeps in the fridge for a day or two in a covered bowl or oiled bag. Bring it back to room temperature before shaping.
