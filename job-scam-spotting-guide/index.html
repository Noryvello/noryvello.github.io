Job Scam Spotting Guide

Fake job offers look real until they cost you money or personal documents. This page lists the 7 red flags of job scams and gives you a quick yes-or-no questionnaire that checks any offer and gives a plain risk verdict.

Check a job offer

Answer all 7 questions about the job offer, then press the button for your risk verdict. Nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

1. Did they ask you to pay any fee to get the job?

2. Is the pay far higher than normal for this kind of work?

3. Were you offered the job with no interview at all?

4. Did they ask for personal documents (passport, ID or bank details) very early?

5. Is all contact through a free email address or a chat app only?

6. Are they pressuring you to accept quickly, with a short deadline?

7. Is the job description vague about the duties, hours or pay?

This page is general education only, not legal advice. The questionnaire gives a guide based on common warning signs, not a certainty — a low count does not prove an offer is safe, and a high count does not prove it is a scam. Always verify the company independently before you accept any offer, pay anything, or share documents.

7 red flags to watch for

Scammers use the same tricks again and again. If you spot one of these signs, slow down and check the offer carefully. If you spot several, treat the offer as risky.

  1. Upfront fees. A real employer never asks you to pay for a job, training, a uniform, or background checks before you start. Any fee is the strongest warning sign.
  2. Too-good pay. Pay far above the normal rate for the work, especially for little experience or few hours, is a classic lure to get you to act fast.
  3. No interview. A genuine job usually includes at least one interview. An instant offer with no interview, no questions about your experience, and no meeting is a warning sign.
  4. Personal documents requested early. Being asked for your passport, ID, or bank details before you have even met the employer or signed a contract puts your identity at risk.
  5. Unofficial email or chat-only contact. Contact only through a free email address or a chat app, with no official company email, website, or phone number, is a red flag.
  6. Pressure to decide fast. "Accept today or lose the offer" is designed to stop you thinking and checking. A real employer gives you time to decide.
  7. Vague job description. If the role, duties, hours, and pay are unclear, keep changing, or are never written down, the job itself may not be real.

How to verify a company is real

Never pay for a job, and never take the offer at face value. Check the company yourself, using sources the sender did not give you:

  • Find the company's official website yourself, by searching its name — do not click links in the job message.
  • Call the official phone number listed on that website and ask whether the job and the person who contacted you are real.
  • Check the company in your country's official company register, and search the company name together with the word "scam" to see if others have reported it.
  • Compare the job details with the listing on the company's own careers page. If the job is not listed there, ask the company directly.

If you think it is a scam

If the offer feels wrong, trust that feeling and act on it:

  • Stop replying to the sender and do not pay any money, whatever reason they give.
  • Do not share any more personal documents or details.
  • Keep records of everything: save the messages, emails, and any phone numbers or account details.
  • If you already paid money or shared documents, contact your bank at once if bank details were involved, and report what happened to your local authorities.
  • Warn others — tell friends or family, and report the listing on the site where you found it, if there is one.

Questions people ask

What is the biggest red flag in a job offer?

Being asked to pay money to get the job. A real employer pays you, never the other way round. Any request for a fee for training, a uniform, background checks or equipment before you start is the strongest sign the offer is a scam.

Should I ever pay to get a job?

No. Never pay for a job, a job offer, an interview or a work visa arranged by the employer. Genuine employers cover their own hiring costs. If someone asks you to pay, stop and verify the company through official channels before doing anything else.

How do I verify a company is real?

Find the company's official website yourself instead of clicking links in the message, call the official phone number listed there, and check the company in your country's official company register. Search the company name together with the word scam to see if others have reported it.

What do I do if I already shared personal documents?

Act quickly. If you shared bank details, contact your bank at once. Keep copies of every message, watch your accounts for unusual activity, and report what happened to your local authorities. Do not send any more documents or money.
