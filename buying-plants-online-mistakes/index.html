7 Mistakes to Avoid When Buying Plants Online

The most common mistake when buying plants online is choosing a plant for its photo instead of your light, space and routine. This guide covers the seven mistakes buyers make most often, plus a seller-check and unboxing checklist.

Buying plants online opens up far more choice than any local shop, and it is easy to see why so many people do it. But a plant is a living thing, not a parcel — it travels in a box, arrives in a new home, and needs the right light and care to settle in. A little care before and after delivery makes all the difference.

The mistakes below are the ones that cost buyers the most plants. Most of them are simple: checking the seller, reading the description properly, and giving the plant time to settle once it arrives.

This guide is general buying advice. Plants vary by type and seller, so check the care needs of the exact plant you buy and follow the guidance that comes with it.

The 7 mistakes to avoid

  1. Buying for the photo, not your home. A plant that looks wonderful in a bright, airy photo may need far more light than your room offers, or grow far larger than your shelf allows. Before you buy, check the light the plant needs, how big it grows, and whether your routine suits it. A plant that matches your home will thrive with little fuss; a plant that does not will struggle no matter how much you want it to work.
  2. Ignoring the seller. The seller matters as much as the plant. Check reviews from other buyers, look at how long the seller has been selling plants, and read their policy for plants that arrive damaged. A good seller packs plants carefully, answers questions, and sorts out problems when they happen. A seller with no reviews and no clear policy is a risk, however tempting the price.
  3. Not checking what you actually get. Listings can be unclear about what is included. Check whether the plant comes in a pot or bare-root, how big the plant actually is, and whether the photo shows the exact plant you will receive or a sample of the type. A small plant in a listing photo can look large, and a plant without a pot needs one ready at home. Read the description fully before you order.
  4. Forgetting about delivery. Plants suffer in transit — days in a dark box, bumps, heat and cold all take their toll. Check how the plant will be packed, how long delivery takes, and whether the seller avoids sending in very hot or cold weather. If you can, choose a faster delivery option and make sure you will be home when the plant arrives, so it does not sit in a box longer than it has to.
  5. Skipping the unboxing check. When your plant arrives, unpack it promptly and look it over: check the leaves, the stems and the soil, and look for broken stems or pests. Take a photo if anything looks damaged, and contact the seller straight away — most sellers need to hear about damage quickly to help. Leaving a plant in its box while you get around to it only adds to the stress of travel.
  6. Repotting too soon. A new plant has just travelled in a box and moved to a strange new home. Repotting it straight away adds more stress at the worst possible time. Let the plant settle first — give it a spot with the light it needs and leave it alone for a while. Repot later, once the plant looks settled and is growing, and only if it actually needs a bigger pot.
  7. Watering on a schedule, not by need. Watering every few days because the calendar says so is one of the fastest ways to harm a houseplant. Always check the soil first: if it is still damp, wait. Most houseplants suffer more from too much water than from too little, so when in doubt, hold off. Learn what your plant needs and water by the soil, not by the day of the week.

Before you buy

Once you know the plant you want, do a final check of your home. Find the spot where it will live and check the light at different times of day, because a sunny morning windowsill can be dark by afternoon. Make sure you have a pot with drainage if the plant needs one, and a saucer to catch drips. And start with one or two plants rather than a whole order — learning how plants behave in your home is easier with a few than with many.

Questions people ask

Is it safe to buy plants online?

Yes, if you buy from a seller with good reviews, clear photos and a fair policy for damaged arrivals. Check how the plant will be packed and how long delivery takes before you order.

What should I do when my plant arrives?

Unpack it promptly, check the leaves, stems and soil, and give it a little water only if the soil is dry. Put it in a spot with the light it needs and let it settle before you move or repot it.

Why do leaves drop after delivery?

Plants often drop a few leaves after the stress of packing and travel, and after moving to a new home with different light. Give the plant time to settle, and check its light and water needs.

Should I repot a new plant right away?

It is usually better to wait and let the plant settle in its new home first. Repotting straight away adds more stress, so wait until the plant looks settled and is growing.

How do I know if a plant seller is reliable?

Look for clear photos of the actual plants, honest descriptions of size, good reviews from other buyers, and a clear policy for plants that arrive damaged. A seller who answers questions is a good sign.
