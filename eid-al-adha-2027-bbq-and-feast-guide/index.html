Eid al-Adha 2027 BBQ and Feast Guide

By the Noryvello team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

Eid al-Adha in 2027 is expected on Sunday 16 May, subject to the sighting of the moon. This guide helps you plan the Eid barbecue and feast, from the grill and marinades to side dishes and hosting a crowd.

The Eid al-Adha feast is one of the great meals of the year. After the prayer and the Qurbani, families gather around the grill and the table, and the smell of cooking meat fills the air. A barbecue suits the day perfectly: it feeds a crowd, it keeps the cook outside with the guests, and it turns the meal into a celebration in itself.

A good Eid barbecue is less about skill and more about planning. The meat needs marinating in advance, the grill needs time to reach the right heat, and the side dishes need to be ready before the first skewer comes off the fire. This guide walks through the whole day, from planning the menu to serving the last guest.

Planning the feast around the day

The feast follows the prayer and the Qurbani, so the day has a natural rhythm. Start the grill only when you are ready to cook: a barbecue that starts too early burns fuel and patience, while one that starts too late leaves guests hungry. A good plan is to have the meat marinated and the sides ready before the prayer, so the afternoon can be given over to cooking and eating.

Think about numbers early. Count the adults and children you expect, and plan the menu around them, with a little extra for unexpected guests. Decide who is cooking and who is serving: one person on the grill and one on the table works well, and guests are usually happy to help. Write a short list of what needs to be done and when, and the day will run itself.

Choosing what to grill

Eid al-Adha often means fresh Qurbani meat, and a barbecue is a natural way to cook it. Choose cuts that suit the grill: pieces that cook quickly over direct heat, such as chops, steaks, and skewered cubes, and larger pieces that can cook slowly to one side. Mix the meat across the grill so that something is always ready to serve.

A mix of meats keeps the table interesting. If you have lamb, beef, or chicken, offer more than one, and add a vegetarian option for guests who do not eat meat, such as grilled vegetables or paneer skewers. Cut the meat into even pieces so it cooks at the same pace, and keep the pieces big enough that they do not fall through the grill.

Marinades and flavour ideas

Marinades do the heavy work of flavour, and they need time. A simple marinade of oil, lemon, garlic, and herbs works for almost any meat, while yoghurt-based marinades keep meat tender and suit spices well. Marinate for a few hours at least, or overnight in the fridge for the best flavour, and keep the meat covered and cool while it marinates.

Keep the flavours balanced across the table. Offer one mild marinade and one with more heat, and let guests choose. Save a little marinade, kept separate and untouched by raw meat, to brush over the meat as it cooks. A squeeze of lemon and a scatter of fresh herbs at the end lifts the flavour more than any extra spice.

Side dishes that feed a crowd

Side dishes make a barbecue a feast. Rice is the classic partner: a large pot of fragrant rice, served in the middle of the table, feeds everyone and soaks up the juices from the grill. Salads add freshness: a chopped salad with cucumber and tomato, a bowl of yoghurt with herbs, and pickled vegetables all cut through rich meat.

Bread is essential. Warm flatbreads, rolls, or pitta are easy to keep coming and suit every dish on the table. Add one or two cooked sides, such as roasted vegetables or grilled corn, and keep the sides simple: the grill is the star, and the sides should support it rather than compete with it. Set the sides out before the meat is ready, so guests can start while the grill heats up.

A simple potato dish is another crowd-pleaser: roasted potatoes with herbs, or a warm potato salad, both hold well and suit grilled meat. Whatever sides you choose, make them in large bowls and keep serving spoons in each one, so guests can help themselves without waiting for the host.

Setting up the grill and the table

Set up the grill in an open, safe place, away from the house, trees, and anything that can catch fire. Give the cook room to work, with a table nearby for raw meat, tongs, and a tray for cooked food. Keep children and pets away from the hot grill, and keep a bucket of water or sand nearby, just in case.

The table should be ready before the cooking starts: plates, cutlery, napkins, and drinks in place, with space for the dishes as they arrive. Serve food in the middle of the table so guests can help themselves, and keep serving spoons in every dish. A shaded table matters on a warm day, so set up under cover or a tree where you can.

Hosting a crowd without stress

Hosting a crowd is easier when you share the work. Ask a family member to look after drinks, another to manage the table, and let guests bring a dish or a dessert if they offer. Cook in batches rather than trying to grill everything at once: serve the first batch while the next one cooks, and nobody waits long.

Pace yourself as the host. Eat something before the guests arrive, keep water nearby, and take a break from the grill when you need one. The day is for enjoying, not for standing over a fire from morning to night. If something goes wrong, a calm host and a table full of food will cover it.

End the day with something sweet and a pot of tea. A tray of pastries or a bowl of fruit, served after the main feast, gives the meal a gentle close and keeps guests at the table a little longer. The best feasts end slowly, with conversation rather than a rush to clear up.

Food safety on a feast day

Warm weather and outdoor cooking need care. Keep raw meat cool until it goes on the grill, and never put cooked meat back on a plate that held raw meat. Cook meat right through, with no pink in the middle for minced meat, and use a food thermometer if you are unsure. Keep salads and cold dishes in the shade or on ice, and cover food to keep insects away.

Leftovers need attention too. Pack leftover cooked meat into clean containers and refrigerate it soon after the meal, and do not leave food sitting out for hours in the heat. When in doubt, throw it out: a feast is no place for a stomach upset. A little care keeps the day happy for everyone.

How much meat should I prepare for a crowd?

Plan a generous portion per guest with a little extra for big eaters and unexpected visitors. A meat quantity calculator can help you work out amounts for your guest list, and it is better to have a little too much than too little.

How do I keep meat safe in warm weather?

Keep raw meat cool until it goes on the grill, keep cooked and raw meat apart, and do not leave food sitting in the sun. Refrigerate leftovers soon after the meal and cover dishes to keep insects away.

What side dishes go well with grilled meat?

Fragrant rice, warm flatbreads, a chopped salad, yoghurt with herbs, and pickled vegetables all suit grilled meat. Add a cooked side such as roasted vegetables or grilled corn, and keep the sides simple so the grill stays the star.

Can I prepare marinades the day before?

Yes, marinades are better for it. Marinate meat overnight in the fridge, covered and cool, and keep a little separate marinade, untouched by raw meat, to brush over the meat as it cooks.

How do I time the cooking for a crowd?
