How to use this converter

Find the cook time in your recipe — the time it would take in an oven or on the hob — and enter it in minutes above. The converter looks the time up in the standard table and shows the slow cooker equivalents on LOW and on HIGH. For example, a recipe that normally takes 30 minutes converts to 1.5–2.5 hours on HIGH or 4–6 hours on LOW.

The conversion table

The table below is the standard slow cooker conversion table. It is a guide — use it as a starting point and check the food before serving.

Normal cook timeOn HIGHOn LOW
15–30 minutes1.5–2.5 hours4–6 hours
35–45 minutes2–3 hours6–8 hours
50 minutes–3 hours4–6 hours8–12 hours

Times under 15 minutes or over 3 hours fall outside the standard table, so the converter shows a message instead of a result for those.

Use less liquid

Slow cookers lose very little liquid, because the lid stays on and traps the steam. Use less liquid than the original recipe calls for — a good starting point is about a third less. You can always add more liquid near the end if the dish looks dry, but you cannot easily take it out again.

Questions people ask

How do I convert a recipe to a slow cooker?

Take the normal oven or hob cook time from the recipe, enter it in minutes above, and use the LOW or HIGH time the converter shows. Use less liquid than the original recipe, because a slow cooker loses very little liquid as steam.

How long is low on a slow cooker?

It depends on the recipe. On the standard table, low ranges from 4 to 6 hours for a recipe that normally takes 15 to 30 minutes, up to 8 to 12 hours for one that normally takes 50 minutes to 3 hours.

Can I cook on high instead of low?

Yes, in most cases. High cooks the same dish in roughly half the time of low, so a dish that takes 8 to 12 hours on low usually takes 4 to 6 hours on high. Check the food is cooked through before serving.

Should I use less liquid in a slow cooker?

Yes. A slow cooker loses very little liquid, because the lid traps the steam, so use less liquid than the original recipe calls for. Start with about a third less and add more only if the dish looks dry.
