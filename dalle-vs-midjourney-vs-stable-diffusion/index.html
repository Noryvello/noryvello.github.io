DALL-E vs Midjourney vs Stable Diffusion: How They Differ

By the Noryvello team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

DALL-E, Midjourney, and Stable Diffusion are the three best-known AI image generators, and they differ most in how you reach them, how much control they give you, and what their pictures look like by default. DALL-E is the simplest to start with, Midjourney is the most artistic out of the box, and Stable Diffusion is the most flexible for people who like to tinker. This guide compares them in plain terms so you can pick the one that fits your needs.

All three tools do the same basic job: you write a description, and the tool generates a new image. But the experience of using them is very different. One lives inside a chatbot, one began inside a chat app and grew its own website, and one you can run on your own computer. One favours clean, literal images, one favours dramatic art, and one can be shaped into almost anything if you are willing to learn it.

There is no single winner. The right choice depends on what you want to make, how much time you want to spend learning, and how much control you need over the result. This guide compares the three on access, ease of use, style, editing, and fit, so you can decide with clear eyes.

All three tools change often: features, models, plans, and prices are updated regularly, and new versions can change how images look. Treat this comparison as a general guide to how the three differ, and check each tool's current pages before you commit time or money to one of them.

The three tools at a glance

DALL-E is made by OpenAI, the company behind ChatGPT, and it lives inside the ChatGPT chat window. You ask for an image in plain conversation, the same way you would ask a question, and it returns pictures you can refine by chatting. It is the most conversational of the three, and the one that feels most like talking to an assistant.

Midjourney is made by an independent research lab of the same name. It began on Discord, the chat app, and now also has its own website. You write short prompts, often with settings added at the end, and it returns a grid of four images that you can upscale or vary. It is known for a rich, artistic default look that needs very little prompting to produce striking pictures.

Stable Diffusion is an openly available image model, originally released by Stability AI, and it works differently from the other two. Instead of one official app, there are many interfaces, websites, and apps built on the same model, and you can download the model and run it on your own computer. That openness makes it the most flexible of the three, and also the one that asks the most of you.

How you access each one

Access is the first real difference, and for many people it decides the choice on its own. DALL-E is reached through ChatGPT, so if you already use ChatGPT, you are one message away from making an image. There is nothing new to install and no new interface to learn; you simply ask for a picture in the same window where you ask questions.

Midjourney needs its own account. You can use it on its website or through Discord, and either way you sign up, choose a plan, and start generating. The website feels like a normal app, while the Discord route means working inside a chat server, where your images in public channels can be seen by others. Neither route needs any installation, but both need an account and an active plan.

Stable Diffusion is the outlier. Because the model is openly available, you can run it on your own computer with a free interface, which means no subscription and no sending your prompts to a company server. The catch is that running it well needs a capable computer with a strong graphics card, and setting it up takes some technical comfort. If that sounds like too much, there are also websites and apps that run Stable Diffusion for you, so you can use it in a browser like the other two.

Ease of use

DALL-E is the easiest of the three to learn. You describe what you want in plain words, and if the result is not right, you say so in the next message: "make it warmer", "add a dog", "try a watercolour style". The conversation carries the work, and you never need to learn commands, settings, or codes. That makes it the best starting point for someone who has never used an image generator before.

Midjourney takes a little more learning. Its prompts are shorter and more structured, and its best features live in settings added to the end of a prompt, like the aspect ratio or the style level, plus buttons under each image for upscaling and variations. None of this is hard, but the first session feels unfamiliar, especially on Discord, where the tool lives inside a chat channel. Most people feel comfortable after a few sessions, and the public gallery of other people's images and prompts is a fast way to learn.

Stable Diffusion asks the most of a beginner. Even the friendlier interfaces present a panel of settings with unfamiliar names, and the results depend heavily on which model version and settings you pick. The payoff is control: once you learn it, you can shape almost every part of the result. But the learning curve is real, and the first hour is mostly setup and experimentation rather than finished images.

Style strengths

Each tool has a default character, and it shows in the images even when your prompt is short. Midjourney's default look is its biggest strength: rich colour, strong composition, and a painterly or cinematic finish that makes even a simple prompt look like art. It is the tool most likely to surprise you with a beautiful image from a few words, which is why illustrators and concept artists favour it.

DALL-E's default look is cleaner and more literal. It follows the prompt closely, handles unusual combinations well, and is good at following instructions like "a diagram of" or "a poster that says". Its images can look a little polished and tidy, which suits illustrations, explainer images, and playful ideas, but it rarely produces the dramatic, moody look that Midjourney reaches by default.

Stable Diffusion has no single default look, because the look depends on which model you use. The base model produces capable, fairly plain images, and the real variety comes from the many community models trained for specific styles, from anime to photorealism to watercolour. That means Stable Diffusion can match either of the others in style, but only if you choose and set up the right model first.

Editing and control

Making the first image is only half the job. The other half is changing it: fixing a detail, swapping a background, or trying a new version of a picture you mostly like. The three tools handle this very differently.

DALL-E edits by conversation. You can ask it to change part of an image, and it will regenerate with your change applied. You can also select an area of an image and describe what should replace it, which is useful for fixing a single detail without starting over. The editing is easy to use, though the result is still a fresh generation, so other parts of the image may shift too.

Midjourney edits through variations and region tools. You can make new versions of an image you like, keeping the composition while changing the details, and you can select part of an image and describe a change for that area. The variation workflow is its strongest editing feature: it is fast, it keeps you close to an image you already like, and it rewards patience.

Stable Diffusion offers the deepest control of the three. You can fix the starting point of a generation so results are repeatable, control how strongly the prompt is followed, blend images together, extend an image beyond its edges, and train the model on a specific style or subject. There are also add-ons that let you pose a figure, copy a composition, or match the layout of a reference image. None of this is beginner-friendly, but for a project that needs precise, repeatable control, nothing else in this comparison comes close.

Who each one fits

DALL-E fits people who want images quickly, inside a tool they already use. It suits teachers making illustrations, writers brainstorming covers, small business owners making social media images, and anyone who wants to describe an idea and get a picture without learning a new app. It is also the best choice when the image needs short readable text, like a sign or a label.

Midjourney fits people who care most about how the image looks. It suits artists, designers, and hobbyists making concept art, illustrations, mood boards, book covers, and atmospheric images. If you enjoy the process of generating, varying, and refining until an image feels right, Midjourney rewards that patience with striking results.

Stable Diffusion fits people who want control, privacy, or a custom workflow. It suits hobbyists who enjoy tinkering, developers building image features into their own projects, and anyone who wants to run image generation on their own computer without a subscription. It also suits people who need a consistent style across many images, because a chosen model and fixed settings can repeat a look reliably.

Many people end up using more than one. A common pattern is to brainstorm with DALL-E, make the beautiful version with Midjourney, and do precise edits or batch work with Stable Diffusion. The tools are not rivals in practice; they are different instruments, and knowing which one to reach for is a skill in itself.

How to choose

Start with one question: what will you make most often? If the answer is quick illustrations and ideas in a chat, start with DALL-E. If it is beautiful, artistic images, start with Midjourney. If it is a custom project, a consistent style, or private on-device generation, start with Stable Diffusion.

Then ask how much learning you are willing to do. DALL-E needs almost none, Midjourney needs a few sessions, and Stable Diffusion needs real time and patience. There is no shame in picking the easiest tool; the best image generator is the one you actually use.

Finally, remember that all three change often. New versions arrive, features move, and plans shift, so a comparison written today will drift out of date. Pick the tool that fits you now, check its current pages before you pay, and do not be afraid to switch later. The prompting skills you learn on one tool transfer to the others, because the language of describing an image is the same everywhere.

Questions people ask

Which of the three is easiest for a beginner?

DALL-E is usually the easiest to start with, because it works inside a normal chat window and needs no special setup. Midjourney takes a little learning because of its commands and settings, and Stable Diffusion needs the most technical setup.

Which one is best for artistic illustrations?

Midjourney is the usual pick for artistic and painterly images, because its default style leans toward rich, dramatic, illustration-like results. DALL-E and Stable Diffusion can make illustrations too, but they need more style words in the prompt to get there.

Can I run any of these on my own computer?

Stable Diffusion can run on your own computer, because its model is openly available and there are free interfaces for it. DALL-E and Midjourney run on their makers' servers, so you need an internet connection and an account to use them.

Which one handles text inside images best?

DALL-E is generally the strongest of the three for short readable words inside an image, such as a sign or a label. Midjourney and Stable Diffusion have improved at this, but garbled letters are still common, so keep any text short and expect several attempts.

Do I need to pay for all three?

All three have different access models, and plans and prices change often, so check each current pricing page before you decide. Stable Diffusion itself can be used without a subscription, though running it still needs a capable computer or a paid cloud service.
