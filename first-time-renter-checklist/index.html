First-Time Renter Checklist: What to Check Before You Sign

Before signing your first lease, inspect seven practical areas: water pressure and leaks, electrical outlets and appliances, window and door locks, mobile signal and internet wiring, signs of pests or mold, which utilities are included in rent, and the exact lease rules for deposits, repairs, and move-out notice. Use the checklist below during your walkthrough.

Honest limit: Landlord-tenant laws, security deposit deadlines, and habitability rules vary significantly by city and jurisdiction. This checklist covers practical physical and contract checks—not formal legal advice.

1. Test Water Pressure, Drainage, and Under-Sink Plumbing

  • What to do: Run both cold and hot water in the kitchen and bathroom during your viewing.
  • Why: Weak water pressure, slow drains, and hidden pipe leaks are daily frustrations that are much easier to get repaired before you sign.
  • How to check it: Turn on the bathroom sink and shower together, wait for hot water to arrive, watch how fast the basin drains, and feel the cabinet floor under every sink for moisture.

2. Check Electrical Outlets, Switches, and Included Appliances

  • What to do: Test wall outlets, light switches, refrigerator cooling, stove burners, and heating or air conditioning.
  • Why: Dead outlets or broken appliances discovered after move-in can lead to delays or arguments over who caused the fault.
  • How to check it: Bring a small phone charger in your pocket during the tour and plug it into outlets in the bedroom, living room, and kitchen.

3. Verify Door Deadbolts, Window Locks, and Entry Security

  • What to do: Lock and unlock every exterior door and ground-floor or balcony window yourself.
  • Why: Warped door frames or broken window latches compromise personal security and contents safety from day one.
  • How to check it: Confirm the main entry door has a working deadbolt, check whether intercom buzzers work, and ask whether locks are re-keyed between tenants.

4. Test Cell Reception and Broadband Internet Options

  • What to do: Check your phone's signal bars inside the bedroom and living area, and locate the wall cable or fiber jack.
  • Why: Thick concrete walls or basement layouts can block mobile reception, and some buildings only support a single wired internet provider.
  • How to check it: Load a web page using mobile data inside the apartment and ask the landlord which internet providers currently service the unit.

5. Inspect Corners, Closets, and Baseboards for Dampness or Pests

  • What to do: Look closely at ceiling corners, bathroom grout, inside kitchen cabinets, and behind appliances.
  • Why: Peeling paint, water rings, or small dark droppings inside cabinets point to roof leaks, humidity problems, or active pests.
  • How to check it: Open under-sink cabinets with your phone flashlight and check window sills for condensation or mildew odor.

6. Confirm Every Utility and Monthly Add-On in Writing

  • What to do: Ask for an itemized list of which utilities (water, electricity, gas, heating, trash, internet) are covered by rent and which you must open in your own name.
  • Why: Unclear utility splits or mandatory parking and trash fees can push your monthly housing outlay well above the advertised base rent.
  • How to check it: Verify that every promised inclusion—such as water, trash collection, or an assigned parking spot—is written directly into the lease document.

7. Read the Lease Clauses on Repairs, Deposits, and Notice

  • What to do: Read the entire lease contract from start to finish before signing or transferring a deposit.
  • Why: Verbal promises from an agent or landlord do not override written contract clauses about early termination penalties or deposit deductions.
  • How to check it: Highlight the lease end date, how many days' written notice you must give before moving out, how maintenance requests are submitted, and the exact conditions for full security deposit return.

Questions people ask

Why should I document pre-existing apartment damage on move-in day?

Timestamped photos and a signed move-in inspection sheet prove that scratches, stains, or broken fixtures were already present before your tenancy began, protecting your security deposit when you move out.

What clauses should I check first in a residential lease agreement?

Check the exact lease start and end dates, total monthly rent due date, grace period and late-fee rules, security deposit return timeline, notice period required to move out, and subletting or guest restrictions.

How can I check water pressure and plumbing during a quick apartment tour?

Turn on the bathroom shower and kitchen tap at the same time, run the hot water for a minute to test the water heater, flush the toilet, and look under sinks for damp wood or musty odors.

Should I verify who owns or manages the rental property before paying a deposit?

Yes. Confirm that the person collecting the application fee or deposit is the legal property owner or an authorized property manager, and never pay a deposit before touring the actual unit in person or via live video.
