Before your pet arrives

Get the basics ready before your pet comes home: a sleeping spot, food and water bowls, and a quiet corner they can retreat to. A calm first day starts with a prepared home.

The first day

Keep the first day quiet. Show your pet where to eat, drink and sleep, then let them explore at their own pace. Short, gentle periods together are better than long, excited ones.

Feeding in the first week

Feed the food your pet is used to, at regular times, and keep fresh water available. Note what and when your pet eats, so you can tell your vet if anything changes.

When to call the vet

Book a first vet visit in the first week. Call sooner if your pet will not eat or drink, seems in pain, or you are worried about anything at all. It is always better to ask than to wait.

Questions people ask

How long does it take a new pet to settle in?

Every animal is different. Some settle in a day or two, others take a week or more. A quiet routine, regular feeding times and patience help more than anything.

Should I change my pet's food straight away?

No. Keep the food your pet is used to for the first days, and change it gradually if you plan to switch. Sudden changes can upset a new pet's stomach.

When should I book the first vet visit?

Book a first visit in the first week, even if your pet seems healthy. It is a good chance to ask questions and to get your pet's records started.

What should I do on the first night?

Keep the first night calm and quiet. A warm sleeping spot near a familiar scent helps, and a short, gentle routine before bed sets a good pattern.

What if my pet will not eat?

A new pet may skip a meal or two while settling in. Offer food at regular times and keep water available. If your pet still will not eat after a day, or seems unwell, contact a vet.
