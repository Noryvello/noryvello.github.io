7 Mistakes to Avoid When Buying a Coffee Machine

The most common mistake when buying a coffee machine is choosing a machine for the coffee you imagine drinking instead of the coffee you actually make every day. This guide covers the seven mistakes buyers make most often, with a brew-method picker and a counter-space checklist.

A coffee machine is a daily companion, which means small annoyances add up fast. A machine that is hard to clean, slow to heat up or awkward to fill will quietly push you back to instant coffee — while a machine that fits your routine becomes part of the morning you look forward to.

The secret is to buy for your everyday drink, your real counter space and the cleaning you will actually do. Work through the mistakes below and the picker, and you will end up with a machine that earns its place on the counter.

This guide is general buying advice, not a product review. Machines vary between models and makers, so check the details of any machine before you buy.

The 7 mistakes to avoid

  1. Buying for a drink you rarely make. A machine that makes elaborate milk drinks is wonderful if you drink them every day, and a waste of counter space if you mostly drink a simple black coffee. Think honestly about the drink you make most mornings, and choose the machine that makes that drink well. The machine that matches your everyday habit is the one you will love using.
  2. Ignoring how much cleaning it needs. Some machines need daily rinsing, weekly deep cleaning and regular descaling, while others need very little. Before you buy, find out what cleaning the machine involves and ask yourself whether you will actually do it. A machine that is hard to clean slowly makes worse coffee, because old oils and scale build up inside it. Easy cleaning is not a luxury — it is what keeps the coffee good.
  3. Forgetting the counter space. Coffee machines are bigger than they look in photos, and they need more room than their footprint suggests. Measure the height under your cabinets, the depth of the counter, and the space needed to open the lid, slide out the water tank and pull out the drip tray. A machine that has to be dragged out every morning is a machine that gets used less.
  4. Overlooking the water tank. A small water tank means frequent refills, which is a daily annoyance in a busy household. Check the size of the tank and whether it removes easily for filling at the tap — a fixed tank that must be filled in place is awkward under low cabinets. Also check whether you can see the water level easily, so you are not caught out mid-brew.
  5. Skipping the grinder question. Freshly ground beans taste better, but a grinder — built in or separate — adds cost, counter space, noise and cleaning. Decide before you shop whether you want a built-in grinder, a separate one, or ready-ground coffee. Each choice is fine, but it should be a choice you make on purpose, not something you discover after the machine arrives.
  6. Choosing pods without checking the ongoing cost. Pod machines are quick, clean and consistent, which is genuinely valuable on a busy morning. But pods cost more per cup over time than beans or ground coffee, and they limit your choice of beans. If you drink several cups a day, that difference adds up over a year. Weigh the convenience against the ongoing cost before you commit.
  7. Not checking the noise. Grinders and pumps can be loud, and a loud machine at dawn is no joke in a shared home. If early mornings matter to you, check reviews for noise before you buy, and think about where the machine sits in relation to bedrooms. A quiet machine is easy to overlook as a feature, but you will notice it every single morning.

Before you buy

Once you know the type you need, take a final look at the running costs. Check the price of the coffee it uses — beans, ground coffee or pods — and think about how that adds up over a year of daily cups. Check the warranty and the returns policy, and read a few reviews from people who have used the machine for months, not days. Then take it home, read the manual once, and enjoy the first cup — a machine chosen with care makes the morning better from day one.

Questions people ask

Espresso or filter coffee — which machine should I buy?

Choose the machine that makes the coffee you drink every day. Espresso machines suit small, strong drinks and milk coffees, while filter machines suit larger amounts of milder coffee.

Do I need a grinder with my coffee machine?

Freshly ground beans taste better, but a grinder adds cost, counter space and cleaning. Some machines have one built in, while others work fine with ready-ground coffee.

How often should I clean a coffee machine?

Follow the guidance from the maker of your machine. Daily rinsing of the parts that touch milk and coffee, plus regular descaling, keeps the coffee tasting good.

Are pod coffee machines worth it?

Pod machines are quick, clean and consistent, which suits busy mornings. The trade-off is that pods cost more per cup over time and give you less choice of beans.

What size coffee machine do I need?

It depends on how many cups you make a day and how much counter space you have. A small household needs only a compact machine, while a busy household needs a larger tank and a machine that heats up quickly.
