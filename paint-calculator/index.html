Paint Calculator — How Much Paint Do I Need for a Room?

To find how much paint a room needs, multiply the wall perimeter by the ceiling height, subtract doors and windows, multiply by the number of coats, and divide by the coverage rate printed on your paint container. Enter your room measurements below to see the exact net wall area, total paint volume, and step-by-step math.

Honest limit: This calculator measures flat four-wall rooms and assumes uniform paint absorption. Rough or textured plaster, deep color changes, ceiling painting, trim work, and brush or roller waste can increase actual paint usage beyond the mathematical estimate.

How the Paint Formula Works

Every room paint estimate follows four straightforward steps:

  1. Gross wall area: 2 × (Length + Width) × Height
  2. Net paintable area: Gross Wall Area − Doors and Windows Area
  3. Total coat area: Net Paintable Area × Number of Coats
  4. Paint required: Total Coat Area ÷ Coverage Rate per Container Unit

The math works identically with any volume unit (liters, gallons, or quarts) and any currency when budgeting your purchase—just match the area unit in your dimensions with the area unit printed on the paint tin.

3 Worked Examples

  • Example 1 — Standard Bedroom (Feet & Gallons): A room is 12 ft long, 10 ft wide, and 9 ft high, with 36 sq ft of doors and windows. Gross wall area is 2 × (12 + 10) × 9 = 396 sq ft. Subtracting 36 sq ft leaves 360 sq ft. For 2 coats, total coverage needed is 360 × 2 = 720 sq ft. At 350 sq ft per gallon, you need 720 ÷ 350 = 2.06 gallons (round up to 3 gallons or 2 gallons plus 1 quart).
  • Example 2 — Small Home Office (Feet & Liters): A room is 10 ft long, 8 ft wide, and 8 ft high, with 28 sq ft of openings. Gross wall area is 2 × (10 + 8) × 8 = 288 sq ft. Net area is 288 − 28 = 260 sq ft. For 2 coats, total area is 260 × 2 = 520 sq ft. At 100 sq ft per liter, you need 520 ÷ 100 = 5.20 liters (buy 6 liters).
  • Example 3 — Living Room in Metric (Meters & Liters): A room is 5 m long, 4 m wide, and 3 m high, with 6 sq m of doors and windows. Gross wall area is 2 × (5 + 4) × 3 = 54 sq m. Net area is 54 − 6 = 48 sq m. For 2 coats, total area is 48 × 2 = 96 sq m. At 12 sq m per liter, you need 96 ÷ 12 = 8.00 liters.

Questions people ask

How do I calculate paintable wall area for a room?

Add the room length and width, multiply by 2 to get the perimeter, and multiply by the wall height to find gross wall area. Then subtract the total area of doors and windows that will not be painted.

Should I use feet or meters in this paint calculator?

You can use either feet or meters as long as your wall dimensions, opening area, and paint coverage rate use the same unit system. For example, enter dimensions in feet if your paint label lists coverage in square feet per liter or gallon.

How many coats of paint should I enter?

Enter 2 coats for most standard repaints over a similar color. Use 3 coats if you are painting a light color over a dark wall or covering raw, porous plaster.

Why should I round up to whole cans or buckets?

Paint is sold in fixed container sizes, and having extra paint from the same batch makes future touch-ups match cleanly without color differences.
